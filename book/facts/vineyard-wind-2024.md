# Vineyard Wind 1: July 2024 blade failure and project financing

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Vineyard Wind and GE Vernova settle all litigation, 16 September 2026)

## Summary

Vineyard Wind 1 is an approximately 800 MW offshore wind farm about 15 miles (24 km) south of Martha's Vineyard, Massachusetts. Its 62 GE Haliade-X turbines total 806 MW. It is the first commercial-scale US offshore wind project.

- **Ownership:** a 50/50 joint venture of Avangrid Renewables (Iberdrola group) and funds managed by Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners (CIP).
- **Offtake:** long-term PPAs with Massachusetts utilities National Grid, Eversource and Unitil.
- **Financing:** financial close on 15 September 2021 with nearly USD 2.3 billion of senior debt from nine banks, advised by Santander. A first-of-its-kind USD 1.2 billion tax equity package followed in October 2023 (J.P. Morgan, Bank of America and Wells Fargo).
- **First power:** 2 January 2024.

On the evening of Saturday 13 July 2024, during commissioning and before Vineyard Wind had taken operational control, a blade on turbine AW-38 failed. Debris washed onto Nantucket beaches, and on 17 July a further part of the blade broke away.

- **Regulatory response:** the Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE) suspended operations. Updated orders allowed towers and nacelles to be installed from mid-August 2024 and new blades from 22 October 2024, subject to conditions.
- **Root cause:** GE Vernova's root cause analysis preliminarily identified "a manufacturing deviation" as the primary cause, one that "should have been identified through the quality assurance process at the blade manufacturing facility". Remediation included re-reviewing over 8,300 ultrasound records per blade, internal crawler inspections, new real-time fibre-optic monitoring, and removing some blades.
- **Second shock:** the project was then caught in the federal lease "pause" of 22 December 2025. A Massachusetts federal court stayed it on 27 January 2026.
- **Contractor dispute:** a commercial dispute with GE Vernova, including a GE notice of termination, was settled on confidential terms on 16 September 2026. By then all turbines were capable of producing power.

The case shows OEM technology and quality risk at scale on a new turbine platform, how a single component failure becomes a regulatory shutdown of a whole wind farm, and the value of contractor warranty and liability terms.

## Verified facts

1. **Ownership, location and offtake.** Vineyard Wind 1 is an 800 MW project 15 miles off Martha's Vineyard, 50 percent owned by CIP funds and 50 percent by Avangrid Renewables. It sells under long-term PPAs with National Grid, Eversource and Unitil. The company projected about USD 1.4 billion of ratepayer savings over the first 20 years. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. **Senior debt.** On 15 September 2021 the project reached financial close, which Vineyard Wind described as the first for a commercial-scale US offshore wind farm. The nine banks provided nearly USD 2.3 billion of senior debt for construction: Bank of America, J.P. Morgan, BBVA, NatWest, Santander, Crédit Agricole, Natixis, BNP Paribas and MUFG. Santander advised, and Norton Rose Fulbright was lead financing counsel. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Tax equity.** On 25 October 2023 the project closed a USD 1.2 billion tax equity package with J.P. Morgan Chase, Bank of America and Wells Fargo. The sponsors called it the largest single-asset tax equity financing and the first for a commercial-scale offshore wind project. CCA Group, Santander and Kirkland & Ellis advised. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. **Construction milestones:**
    - onsite construction began in late 2021;
    - steel in the water in June 2023;
    - the first US offshore substation completed in July 2023;
    - first turbine installed in October 2023;
    - first power at 11:52 pm on 2 January 2024, from one turbine delivering about 5 MW.

    At completion the project would have 62 turbines and 806 MW. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3, 14]
5. **The failure.** On the evening of Saturday 13 July 2024 a blade on a turbine in the lease area was damaged. No personnel were nearby. Vineyard Wind set a safety perimeter, and GE, as turbine and blade manufacturer and installation contractor, led the root cause analysis. On 17 July 2024 a further "compromise to the integrity" of the blade released new debris. A 500 m safety zone had been in place since 13 July. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
6. Vineyard Wind stated that the blade failed during GE's commissioning of the turbine, before Vineyard Wind assumed operational control, and that GE Vernova was responsible for offshore debris recovery. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
7. **Root cause and remediation** (9 August 2024). GE Vernova's root cause analysis had "preliminarily determined that a manufacturing deviation is considered to be the primary root cause of the blade event, which should have been identified through the quality assurance process at the blade manufacturing facility". Conditions for any installed turbine to start or restart power production:
    - re-processing of over 8,300 ultrasound records per blade to confirm bond requirements;
    - internal visual inspection by crawler drones;
    - a new real-time monitoring algorithm using existing fibre-optic sensors.

    Blade removal followed a four-task plan with salvage firm Resolve Marine, under BSEE's superseding order. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
8. **Partial restart, August 2024.** On 13 August 2024 BSEE issued an updated Suspension Order allowing limited activities, including installation of towers and nacelles, but not blade installation or power production. BSEE relied in part on an independent structural load analysis by DNV, the project's certified verification agent. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
9. **Blade installation approved, October 2024.** By 23 October 2024 eight new towers and nacelles had been installed. On 22 October 2024 the companies received approval to resume installing new blades once safety and operational conditions were met. GE Vernova intended to remove some blades and strengthen others. Power production would resume only after further approvals. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
10. **Return to generation and completion.** Secondary reporting states that:
    - complete turbines were allowed to generate again in January 2025;
    - 17 turbines were operating by July 2025 and 30 by October 2025;
    - the last turbine was completed on 13 March 2026.

    [confidence: medium (secondary source)] [source: 10]
11. **Federal pause and court stay.** DOI paused Vineyard Wind's lease (OCS-A 0501), among five projects, on 22 December 2025. Vineyard Wind sought a TRO and preliminary injunction in the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts on 15 January 2026. On 27 January 2026 the court stayed BOEM's suspension order, allowing full activities to resume. [confidence: high] [source: 11, 12] (See facts/us-offshore-wind-2025.md.)
12. **GE Vernova settlement.** On 16 September 2026 Vineyard Wind and GE Vernova announced an amicable settlement of "all outstanding litigation". GE Vernova withdrew its notice of termination, both parties agreed to dismiss all pending claims, and the terms are confidential. The release states that all turbines are capable of producing power. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
13. **The dispute before settlement.** Secondary reporting says Vineyard Wind sued GE Vernova in April 2026 after GE said it would terminate its service and maintenance contract. GE claimed about USD 300 million owed for work performed. Vineyard Wind claimed about USD 545 million for costs and delays from the blade failure. Separately, GE Vernova reportedly settled with the Town of Nantucket for USD 10.5 million in July 2025. [confidence: medium (secondary; not verified in court filings)] [source: 10]

## Financing and structure details

- **Sponsors:** Avangrid Renewables (50 percent) and CIP funds (50 percent). [1, 2]
- **Senior debt:** nearly USD 2.3 billion construction financing from nine banks (Bank of America, J.P. Morgan, BBVA, NatWest, Santander, Crédit Agricole, Natixis, BNP Paribas, MUFG), arranged at financial close in September 2021. [1]
- **Tax equity:** USD 1.2 billion (J.P. Morgan Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo), October 2023. [2]
- **Revenue:** long-term PPAs with National Grid, Eversource and Unitil. [2]
- **Key contract:** GE (GE Vernova) as turbine and blade supplier and installation contractor, with service and maintenance obligations. This is the contract that was later disputed. [4, 13]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **GE Vernova (preliminary root cause):** a manufacturing deviation that factory quality assurance should have caught. Secondary reports describe it as insufficient bonding. [7, 10]
- **Regulator approach (BSEE via company releases):** activities were restarted in stages, each depending on independent analysis (DNV as certified verification agent) and inspection results. Construction resumed before generation. [8, 9]
- **What helped:** the turbine supplier, not the owner, was commissioning the turbine and responsible for debris recovery. That framed responsibility and supported Vineyard Wind's claims. The eventual settlement kept the OEM engaged for service. [6, 13]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 71 (Offshore wind):** Use AW-38 to teach serial-defect risk in large new turbine platforms. One blade failure led to fleet-wide inspection, blade removals and months without generation. Map the contractual allocation between owner and OEM (commissioning responsibility, warranty, liquidated damages, termination rights), and show how lenders rely on a creditworthy OEM and on the certified verification agent. Then layer on 2025–2026 political risk (the federal pause) to show two independent shocks hitting one financed asset. Note the capital stack (USD 2.3 billion senior debt plus USD 1.2 billion tax equity) as a US template.
