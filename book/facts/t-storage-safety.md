# Battery storage safety standards and insurance (NFPA 855, UL 9540, UL 9540A)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest items covered: NFPA 855 2026 edition current, next edition 2029; UL 9540A 5th and 6th editions in use; Vistra Moss Landing insurance disclosures to August 2026, via facts/moss-landing.md)

## Summary

In North America, battery energy storage system (BESS) safety rests on three layers:

1. **Installation standard:** NFPA 855, *Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems*. Editions are 2020, 2023 and 2026 (current), with the next due in 2029.
2. **Product standard:** UL 9540, *Energy Storage Systems and Equipment*.
3. **Fire test method:** UL 9540A, *Test Method for Evaluating Thermal Runaway Fire Propagation in Battery Energy Storage Systems*. It is run at cell, module, unit and installation level, and is used when a design or installation exceeds the limits set by NFPA 855, NFPA 1 or the International Fire Code (IFC).

The 2026 edition of NFPA 855 puts weight on large-scale fire testing, with a new Annex G.11 on fire spreading from one BESS to another. UL 9540A's 6th edition changes the installation-level test to assume a developed fire after a deflagration. It evaluates enclosure design, separation distances and, for indoor systems, building fire suppression.

States add operating rules on top. California's CPUC proposed in January 2025, just after the Moss Landing fire, to amend General Order 167 to set BESS maintenance and operation standards (implementing SB 1383) and to require emergency response and action plans (SB 38).

For financing, these standards feed:
- the independent engineer's diligence;
- permitting and authority-having-jurisdiction (AHJ) approval;
- insurers' underwriting, which considers propagation test results, spacing, suppression and chemistry.

Moss Landing showed the gap between insurance limits and the true economic loss. Vistra's combined business-interruption and property limits were USD 500 million net of deductibles, fully collected by February 2026. Disclosed write-offs, impairments and clean-up costs already exceeded approximately USD 700 million.

## Verified facts

1. **NFPA 855 editions.** NFPA lists editions for 2020, 2023 and 2026. The 2026 edition is current and the next is scheduled for 2029. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **NFPA 855 (2026): large-scale fire testing.** According to UL Solutions, the 2026 edition "introduces significant revisions that emphasize the importance of large-scale fire testing". A new Annex G.11 sets expectations for tests where fire could spread from one BESS to another. It gives guidance on initiating a developed fire condition, evaluating separation distances and considering active fire protection. [confidence: high for the edition and the Annex as described by UL Solutions; the NFPA text itself was not reviewed] [source: 2]
3. **UL 9540A scope.** UL 9540A is the American and Canadian national standard for assessing fire propagation from thermal runaway in BESS. It provides a method for testing safety behaviour where the design or installation exceeds limits set by NFPA 855, NFPA 1, the IFC or the International Residential Code. UL Solutions says it is the only consensus standard explicitly cited in NFPA 855 for large-scale fire testing. It is recognised in UL 9540, the IFC, NFPA 855 and various state and local codes. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. **UL 9540A test levels (5th edition, UL 9540A:2025):**
    - **Cell:** thermal runaway characteristics and gas composition and flammability.
    - **Module:** propagation tendency, heat and gas release, ignition and deflagration hazard.
    - **Unit:** fire spread between modules, heat and gas release, deflagration and re-ignition.
    - **Installation:** effectiveness of fire protection, heat and gas release, deflagration and re-ignition.

    [confidence: high] [source: 2]
5. **UL 9540A 6th edition.** It updates the installation-level test with "a challenging fire scenario", assuming a post-deflagration condition, to evaluate enclosure design, separation distances and (indoors) building suppression. Unit-level testing is still required for residential BESS and for non-residential BESS with an active Thermal Runaway Propagation Prevention System. UL Solutions tests to both the 5th and 6th editions. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. **California (CPUC).** On 27 January 2025 the CPUC issued a proposal to modify General Order 167 that would:
    - implement SB 1383 by adding maintenance and operation standards for BESS;
    - make explicit the requirement, under SB 38, that BESS owners have emergency response and emergency action plans;
    - set logbook standards.

    It was set for the 13 March 2025 voting meeting. The CPUC said California's battery storage grew from 500 MW in 2019 to over 13,300 MW in 2024. [confidence: high for the proposal; adoption not verified here] [source: 3]
7. **Moss Landing insurance outcome** (cross-reference facts/moss-landing.md):
    - Vistra's claims ran against policies with combined business-interruption and property limits of USD 500 million net of deductibles, fully collected by February 2026.
    - In 2025 Vistra booked a USD 425 million property insurance receivable against incident expenses of USD 502 million, and USD 71 million of business-interruption proceeds in revenue.
    - Disclosed losses (approximately USD 400 million write-off, approximately USD 155 million impairment and approximately USD 175 million clean-up estimate) exceeded approximately USD 700 million before lost revenue and litigation.

    [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
8. Moss Landing 300 was a 300 MW indoor lithium-ion system in a converted building with about 100,000 modules. The fire of 16 January 2025 damaged about 55 percent of them and stopped all co-located facilities. As of Vistra's latest filings (August 2026) no cause had been published. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]

**Where the current rule is found:**
- NFPA 855, current edition (adopted by reference in state and local fire codes; the adopted edition varies by jurisdiction);
- IFC Section 1207 (energy storage systems) as adopted locally;
- UL 9540 and UL 9540A (UL Standards & Engagement);
- state utility regulators' rules, for example CPUC GO 167 in California;
- the local AHJ (fire marshal).

Which edition applies depends on the code edition the jurisdiction has adopted. Diligence must confirm it project by project.

**Indicative insurance market practice (qualitative; not verified quantitatively):** Underwriters and lenders' insurance advisers look at the following. Drivers that move terms are chemistry (for example LFP or NMC), indoor or containerised layout, spacing, UL 9540A results, suppression and deflagration venting, fire-department plans, loss history (Moss Landing 2025), and market capacity.
- UL 9540A results and propagation behaviour;
- separation between enclosures;
- detection, deflagration control and suppression;
- maximum foreseeable loss, including co-located assets sharing a building or site;
- debris removal and pollution clean-up sub-limits;
- business-interruption indemnity periods;
- supplier warranties and their credit.

## Financing and structure details

- **Conditions precedent and covenants:** typical BESS loan documents require compliance with applicable codes. They tie insurance requirements to the independent engineer's and insurance adviser's recommendations, often requiring evidence of UL 9540 listing and UL 9540A test reports for the specific product and configuration. [practice description; no single source verified]
- **Limit adequacy:** Moss Landing shows a one-building, multi-facility site exhausting combined limits. Lenders should test maximum foreseeable loss across co-located units, and add clean-up and regulatory-order exposure (an EPA administrative order there) to property and BI limits. [4, 5]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Standards have moved toward large-scale, fire-spread testing.** The 2026 NFPA 855 Annex G.11 and UL 9540A Ed. 6 respond to AHJ and fire-safety concerns about real-world failure conditions, according to UL Solutions. [2]
- **Insurance vs economic loss (Vistra filings, analysed in facts/moss-landing.md):** limits were sized below a total loss plus remediation. [4, 5]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 73 (Storage):** Treat safety standards as part of technology and permitting risk. Explain what UL 9540A tests and why NFPA 855 now emphasises fire spread between units. Have the reader set the "standards stack" a lender's engineer should check (product listing, propagation test, installation standard edition adopted by the AHJ, state operating rules), and link indoor versus containerised layouts to loss severity.
- **Ch 27 (Insurance):** Use Moss Landing's USD 500 million combined limit against losses above approximately USD 700 million to teach maximum foreseeable loss, limit sizing, BI versus property allocation, and clean-up sub-limits. Show how propagation test data and spacing feed underwriting and premium. Treat market pricing as cyclical and driven by loss events.
