# Interest limitation rules: BEPS Action 4, EU ATAD, UK corporate interest restriction (incl. public infrastructure exemption), US section 163(j)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: IRS 163(j) Q&A updated 19 August 2026; UK Finance Act 2026 changes to CIR reporting-company administration for periods ending on or after 31 March 2026)

## Summary

Highly leveraged project companies depend on interest deductions, so the rules that cap those deductions bear directly on cash flow available for debt service and on equity returns. In October 2015 the OECD's BEPS Action 4 report recommended an earnings-based "fixed ratio" cap on net interest. The cap is set as a share of EBITDA (the OECD recommended a 10–30% range), with an optional group-ratio rule, a de minimis threshold and an optional exclusion for third-party interest on public-benefit projects. The report was updated in December 2016.

The EU made this mandatory through Article 4 of the Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive (ATAD), Council Directive (EU) 2016/1164 of 12 July 2016:

- a 30%-of-tax-EBITDA cap;
- an optional EUR 3 million safe harbour;
- optional exclusions, including one for loans funding long-term public infrastructure projects inside the EU.

The UK applied it through the Corporate Interest Restriction (CIR) in Part 10 of TIOPA 2010, from 1 April 2017. The CIR has a 30% fixed ratio, a GBP 2 million de minimis, a group ratio and debt cap, and an elective Public Infrastructure Exemption (PIE) for "qualifying infrastructure companies".

The US equivalent is section 163(j). It caps net business interest at 30% of adjusted taxable income. From tax years beginning after 31 December 2024, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) restored the EBITDA-style definition of that income. Regulated public utilities and electing real property businesses are excepted trades or businesses.

## Verified facts

### OECD BEPS Action 4

1. **Publication.** The OECD's Action 4 report ("Limiting Base Erosion Involving Interest Deductions and Other Financial Payments") was published in interim form in 2014 and in final form in October 2015, with an updated version in December 2016. The final BEPS reports were released on 5 October 2015. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 2]
2. **Recommended approach.** The best-practice approach has four elements:
   - a fixed-ratio rule limiting net interest to a percentage of EBITDA;
   - an optional group-ratio rule allowing a higher ratio for groups that are highly leveraged with third-party debt;
   - a de minimis threshold;
   - an optional exclusion for interest paid to third-party lenders on loans funding public-benefit projects, subject to conditions.

   [confidence: high] [source: 4]
3. **The 10–30% range.** The European Commission's ATAD proposal states that the OECD recommended a scale of 10 to 30% for the deductibility ratio. The EU chose "the top of the scale" because the directive sets a minimum standard. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. **Infrastructure rationale.** HMRC's manual records the OECD's view in the 2015 Action 4 report that infrastructure financing presents little or no base erosion or profit-shifting risk. This is the policy basis for public-benefit exclusions. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### EU: ATAD Article 4 (current law; text on EUR-Lex)

5. **Directive.** Council Directive (EU) 2016/1164 of 12 July 2016 was published in OJ L 193 on 19 July 2016. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. **The cap.** "Exceeding borrowing costs" (net borrowing costs) are deductible only up to 30% of the taxpayer's EBITDA. EBITDA here is taxable income with tax-adjusted net borrowing costs, depreciation and amortisation added back, and tax-exempt income excluded. Member states may apply the rule at the level of a domestic tax group. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
7. **Optional reliefs.** Member states may allow:
   - full deduction up to EUR 3,000,000, applied per group where the group option is used;
   - full deduction for a standalone entity, meaning one not in a consolidated group with no associated enterprise or permanent establishment.

   [confidence: high] [source: 2]
8. **Optional exclusions.** Member states may exclude two kinds of borrowing costs:
   - (a) costs on loans concluded before 17 June 2016, though not on later modifications;
   - (b) costs on loans used to fund a long-term public infrastructure project where the project operator, borrowing costs, assets and income are all in the EU.

   A "long-term public infrastructure project" is a project to provide, upgrade, operate and/or maintain a large-scale asset that a member state considers in the general public interest. Where exclusion (b) applies, the project's income is also excluded from EBITDA. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
9. **Group escapes.** Member states may offer either:
   - an equity escape, which gives full deduction if the entity's equity-to-total-assets ratio is equal to or higher than the group's, with a 2-percentage-point tolerance;
   - a group ratio, which applies the group's ratio of third-party net borrowing costs to group EBITDA to the entity's EBITDA.

   [confidence: high] [source: 2]
10. **Carry-forward options.** Member states choose one of three:
    - carry disallowed costs forward without time limit;
    - carry them forward without time limit and back up to three years;
    - carry them forward without time limit, plus unused interest capacity forward for up to five years.

    Financial undertakings may be excluded. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
11. **Timing.** Member states had to transpose by 31 December 2018 and apply the rules from 1 January 2019. Member states that had equally effective national targeted rules at 8 August 2016 could keep them until the end of the first full fiscal year after the OECD published agreement on a minimum standard for Action 4, but at the latest until 1 January 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

### UK: Corporate Interest Restriction (TIOPA 2010 Part 10; HMRC Corporate Finance Manual CFM95000–CFM99500)

12. **Commencement and de minimis.** The CIR applies to periods of account starting on or after 1 April 2017. Groups with less than GBP 2 million a year of net interest expense and other financing costs suffer no restriction. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 1]
13. **Fixed ratio and debt cap.** The basic limit is 30% of tax-EBITDA (taxable earnings before tax-interest, depreciation and amortisation). It is subject to a debt cap that limits net tax-interest by reference to the worldwide group's net external interest. A group ratio method may be elected instead. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
14. **Carry-forward.** Disallowed interest is carried forward indefinitely and can be reactivated when there is spare interest allowance. Unused interest allowance can be carried forward for up to five years. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
15. **Public Infrastructure Exemption: purpose and election.** The PIE (TIOPA 2010 Part 10 Chapter 8, sections 432–449) is intended to ensure that private investment in public infrastructure is not deterred by unwarranted restrictions on interest deductibility. A company must elect to be a qualifying infrastructure company (QIC). The election is made before the end of the accounting period it is to cover and continues for later periods. It cannot be revoked with effect for any period beginning within five years of the election taking effect, and after revocation no new election can take effect for five years. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 8, 9]
16. **QIC conditions (s.433).** A QIC must meet three conditions:
    - all, or all but an insignificant proportion, of its income must come from qualifying infrastructure activities, shares in QICs, or loans to QICs;
    - substantially all of its asset value must come from qualifying assets: tangible infrastructure assets, service-concession assets, financial assets, shares in or loans to QICs, and certain others;
    - it must be fully taxed in the UK, with every source of income within the charge to corporation tax and no foreign-branch exemption election or double tax relief claim.

    [confidence: high] [source: 8]
17. **Public infrastructure assets (s.436).** A qualifying asset is either:
    - a tangible asset forming part of UK infrastructure, or of the UK continental shelf, that meets a public benefit test (procured by a relevant public body, or used in a regulated activity overseen by a listed regulator), has an expected economic life of at least 10 years, and is on a group balance sheet; or
    - a building let on a short-term basis (effective duration of 50 years or less) to unrelated persons as part of a UK property business, also with an economic life of at least 10 years.

    Qualifying activities include providing such assets and activities ancillary to, or facilitating, their provision. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 6]
18. **Which interest is excluded (s.438).** Interest of a QIC is excluded from tax-interest expense where the creditor is not a related party, or is itself a QIC, and the creditor's recourse is limited to the income, assets, shares or debt of QICs. Certain guarantees are disregarded: those given before 1 April 2017, those from non-related parties, and those from relevant public bodies. This is how classic non-recourse project debt fits the exemption. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
19. **Grandfathering of related-party debt (s.439).** Loan relationships entered into on or before 12 May 2016 can qualify where, at that date, at least 80% of the company's future qualifying infrastructure receipts for the qualifying period were highly predictable by reference to qualifying public contracts. The qualifying period runs to the loan's expiry or 10 years from 12 May 2016, whichever is earlier. Later increases in principal or term lose the protection. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
20. **Effect on the group.** QICs are treated as having nil tax-interest income and expense amounts and nil tax-EBITDA (ss.440–442). This removes them from the group's ordinary CIR calculation. [confidence: medium (summary of sections)] [source: 8]
21. **2026 administrative change.** Finance Act 2026 changed how reporting companies are appointed under the CIR. For periods ending on or after 31 March 2026, groups appoint a reporting company without notifying HMRC, and the appointment must be made for each period with no carry-forward. [confidence: medium] [source: 10, 11]

### US: section 163(j) (Internal Revenue Code; IRS Form 8990 and IRS Q&A)

22. **The limitation.** Deductible business interest expense is capped at the sum of:
    - business interest income;
    - 30% of adjusted taxable income (ATI);
    - floor plan financing interest.

    Disallowed interest carries forward indefinitely. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
23. **OBBBA: ATI basis restored.** For tax years beginning after 31 December 2024, OBBBA restored the add-back of depreciation, amortisation and depletion in computing ATI, which makes the cap EBITDA-based rather than EBIT-based. [confidence: high] [source: 12, 13]
24. **OBBBA: later changes.** For tax years beginning after 31 December 2025:
    - Subpart F inclusions, net CFC tested income (the successor to GILTI) and related items are excluded from ATI;
    - the 163(j) limitation is computed before interest capitalisation provisions (other than sections 263(g) and 263A(f)), and allowable interest is applied first to capitalisable interest.

    [confidence: high (IRS confirms the capitalisation ordering; law firms describe both)] [source: 12, 13]
25. **Small business exemption.** Taxpayers whose average annual gross receipts over the prior three years fall under an inflation-indexed threshold are exempt. The threshold is USD 30 million for 2024, USD 31 million for 2025 and USD 32 million for 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
26. **Excepted trades or businesses.** The following are outside the limitation:
    - performing services as an employee;
    - electing real property trades or businesses;
    - electing farming businesses;
    - certain regulated public utilities.

    [confidence: high] [source: 12]

## Financing and structure details

Not a transaction. Structural points:

- **What each regime caps.** All four regimes cap net interest by reference to a tax-adjusted EBITDA measure, so a project with high leverage, a long construction period (no EBITDA) or low early EBITDA can face disallowances that reverse only later through carry-forward. [source: 2, 7, 12]
- **Construction-phase interest.** Treatment of capitalised interest during construction differs by regime and must be checked. In the US from 2026, the 163(j) limit is computed before capitalisation (fact 24). [source: 13]
- **Non-recourse SPV debt and the reliefs.** The infrastructure carve-outs (EU option (b), UK PIE) are built around third-party, non-recourse project debt on public-benefit assets. Shareholder loans generally fall outside the UK PIE unless grandfathered. The EU and UK reliefs are elective or optional, so the member state's transposition and the company's election must be checked. [source: 2, 8]
- **US utility exception.** The regulated-utility exception means many US regulated utility businesses fall outside 163(j). Merchant or contracted generation is not automatically excepted. [source: 12]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Why infrastructure gets carve-outs.** The OECD and HMRC accept that third-party infrastructure financing carries little BEPS risk. That justifies the public-benefit exclusions, which preserve the economics of highly geared PPP and regulated-asset financings. [source: 4, 6]
- **Why the EU chose 30%.** The Commission says it set 30%, the top of the OECD range, because the ATAD is a floor; member states may go stricter. [source: 3]
- **Why the UK changed CIR administration.** The ICAEW and KPMG report that CIR administration caused practical problems when groups failed to appoint a reporting company on time, which could invalidate returns and elections. This was the motivation for the 2026 change. [source: 10, 11]

## Teaching angles by chapter

**Chapter 41 (Modeling operations, tax and working capital).** Model the interest cap as a period-by-period calculation:

1. compute tax-EBITDA;
2. set allowable interest at 30% of tax-EBITDA, or at the de minimis or exemption amount;
3. carry the disallowed amount into a carry-forward balance;
4. reactivate it when capacity appears;
5. check any limit on how long unused capacity can be carried forward.

Show how back-ended EBITDA in early operations creates temporary disallowances, lowers early cash flow available for debt service, and moves equity IRR. Flag the regime and election as model inputs.

**Chapter 67 (Tax structuring).** Compare the EU, UK and US designs and show how they shape capital structure: third-party versus shareholder debt, standalone-entity relief, and PIE eligibility, including its full-UK-taxation and recourse-limitation tests. Teach readers to check the current text (EUR-Lex plus national transposition, legislation.gov.uk and the HMRC CFM manual, the IRC and IRS guidance) and to treat thresholds and dates as as-of facts.

## Do not state

- That all EU member states adopted the EUR 3 million safe harbour, the infrastructure exclusion or the grandfathering. These are member-state options; check national law.
- That the UK PIE covers all infrastructure debt, including shareholder loans. Related-party debt generally qualifies only if the creditor is a QIC or the loan is grandfathered.
- Any OECD recommendation on the group-ratio uplift percentage. Not verified here.
- That US regulated utilities are fully exempt in all cases. "Certain regulated public utilities" is the IRS framing; check the statutory definition.
- Detail of the UK statutory instrument SI 2026/352. Not verified.
- Inflation-adjusted 163(j) thresholds for years after 2026. Not yet published in the sources consulted.
