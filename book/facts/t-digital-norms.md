## Summary

Digital infrastructure is financed against contracted or highly recurring cash flows: tower leases with fixed escalators, fibre customer contracts, and data-centre colocation or hyperscale leases. In the US the dominant long-term instrument is the "whole-business-style" securitisation. A bankruptcy-remote issuer, secured on the assets and contracts, issues fixed-rate notes with an anticipated repayment date (ARD) of typically 3–10 years and a legal final maturity of about 30 years. If the notes are not refinanced by the ARD, cash flow is swept and step-up interest of at least 5% applies. Towers pioneered the form. SBA's Tower Securities (USD 7.2 billion, 9,498 sites at end-2025) trap excess cash at DSCR of 1.30x or below and amortise below 1.15x. Its coupons show the rate cycle: 1.6–2.6% for 2020–2021 issues, 6.599% in November 2022 and 4.65–4.83% in October 2024. Fibre ABS followed from 2023, with Frontier (USD 1.59 billion; A-2 at 6.60%, C at 11.50%), Tucows/Ting, Uniti (the first enterprise fibre ABS, February 2025, about 6.5% weighted yield) and Shentel (December 2025, A-2 at 5.64%). Data-centre ABS has become a repeat market. Brookfield-controlled Csquare issued USD 885 million to USD 1.1 billion per deal in 2024–2025, with senior A-2 coupons of 6.40–7.31% and five-to-seven-year ARDs. Construction-stage data-centre debt is mostly bank and private-credit term loans and delayed-draw facilities priced over SOFR (see t-market-norms.md). Very large hyperscale campuses have moved to lease-backed investment-grade SPV bonds (Hyperion). The drivers that move these terms are base rates, tenant credit and contract length, churn and penetration (fibre), and how stable the asset's cash flow is once built.

## Verified facts

### A. Tower securitisation (US; current terms in each issuer's latest 10-K "Debt" note)

1. SBA Communications' Secured Tower Revenue Securities ("Tower Securities"):
   - USD 7.2 billion is issued through a trust whose sole asset is a non-recourse mortgage loan to SBA subsidiaries (the Borrowers), with one loan tranche matching each security;
   - the loan is paid from the cash flow of 9,498 tower sites (31 December 2025);
   - it is secured by mortgages on a substantial portion of the sites, a security interest in sites and personal property, the Borrowers' rights under tenant leases, and proceeds. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. SBA's triggers:
   - if the DSCR (net cash flow over the next 12 months' interest, servicing and trustee fees) is 1.30x or lower at any quarter-end, all excess cash flow is trapped in a reserve account;
   - below 1.15x an "amortization period" starts and trapped cash prepays the loan;
   - if any class is not repaid by its ARD, cash flow is trapped and applied to interest at the original rate, reserves and then principal, with additional interest accruing. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. SBA Tower Securities outstanding at 31 December 2025 (issue date, amount, coupon, ARD):
   - 2020-1C (July 2020), USD 750 million, 1.884%, ARD January 2026 (repaid 9 January 2026 from the revolver);
   - 2020-2C, USD 600 million, 2.328%, January 2028;
   - 2021-1C, USD 1,165 million, 1.631%, November 2026;
   - 2021-2C, USD 895 million, 1.840%, April 2027;
   - 2021-3C, USD 895 million, 2.593%, October 2031;
   - 2022-1C (23 November 2022), USD 850 million, 6.599%, January 2028;
   - 2024-1C (11 October 2024), USD 1,450 million, 4.831%, October 2029;
   - 2024-2C (11 October 2024), USD 620 million, 4.654% all-in after a treasury lock (5.115% before the hedge), October 2027.

   Final maturities run to 2050–2056. Interest is paid monthly. Issuance fees ranged from about USD 5.5 million to USD 12.9 million per series. To meet Regulation RR risk retention, an SBA subsidiary holds "R" securities (for example 2024-1R, USD 108.7 million at 6.252%). [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### B. Fibre securitisation (US)

4. Frontier Communications (secured fibre network revenue notes; Frontier Issuer LLC, bankruptcy-remote):
   - Series 2023-1 (8 August 2023; Dallas metro fibre assets and contracts): Class A-2 USD 1,119 million at 6.60%, Class B USD 155 million at 8.30%, Class C USD 312 million at 11.50%. ARD 20 July 2028; final maturity 20 August 2053;
   - Series 2024-1 (1 July 2024; adding North Texas): A-2 USD 530 million at 6.19%, B USD 73 million at 7.02%, C USD 147 million at 11.16%. ARD 20 May 2031 (seven years); final maturity June 2054; weighted average yield about 7.4%;
   - if the notes are not refinanced by the ARD, additional interest is the greater of 5.00% a year and a Treasury-based excess spread formula;
   - cash sweeps are triggered by failing a DSCR or "a certain minimum penetration rate in the markets that were securitized";
   - a USD 500 million variable funding note (VFN) facility, with ARD July 2026 and two one-year extensions, was later cut to USD 0;
   - Verizon completed its acquisition of Frontier on 20 January 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3, 4]
5. Tucows (Ting Issuer LLC), 4 May 2023: Class A-2 USD 168.4 million at 5.95%, Class B USD 23.3 million at 7.40%, Class C USD 46.9 million at 9.95%. ARD April 2028. After the ARD, step-up interest is the greater of 5.00% and the 10-year Treasury yield + 5.00% + 3.50% (A-2), 5.00% (B) or 7.82% (C), less the original coupon. The notes are secured on fibre network agreements, assets and customer contracts held by bankruptcy-remote subsidiaries. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
6. Uniti Group, 3 February 2025: USD 589 million of fibre securitisation notes, described as "the first true enterprise fiber securitization". Class A-2 USD 426 million at 5.9%, B USD 65 million at 6.4%, C USD 98 million at 9.0%. ARD April 2030; weighted average yield about 6.5%. The notes are secured on fibre assets and customer contracts in Florida and the Gulf Coast. The issuers are unrestricted subsidiaries, and part of the proceeds redeemed USD 125 million of 10.50% secured notes. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
7. Shentel, 5 December 2025: USD 567.4 million of secured fibre network revenue term notes. A-2 USD 489.1 million at 5.64% and B USD 78.3 million at 6.03%, ARD December 2030, plus up to USD 175 million of Class A-1 variable funding notes (Bank of America as administrative agent and letter-of-credit provider). [confidence: high] [source: 7]

### C. Data-centre ABS and related facilities

8. Csquare, Inc. (formerly BIF III US Aggregator (Delaware) LLC; majority owned by Brookfield-managed funds; carrier-neutral colocation data centres in 21 metro areas in the US, Canada and the UK):
   - ABS issues: USD 885 million (17 October 2024), USD 940 million (20 March 2025), USD 815 million (21 August 2025) and USD 1,100 million (4 December 2025). It also acquired USD 743 million of 2020–2022 series in an October 2025 portfolio acquisition;
   - coupons (ARD in brackets): 2024 A-2 notes at 7.07% (October 2029) and 7.31% (October 2031), and B at 7.77%; 2025 A-2 notes at 6.60%/6.88% (March 2030/2032), 6.46%/6.78% (August 2030/2032) and 6.40%/6.40%/6.60% (December 2029/2030/2032), with B notes at 6.90–7.39%; older 2021–2022 series at 5.27–8.39%;
   - collateral is multi-tenant data centres held in fee simple;
   - a USD 100 million Series 2024-1 VFN carries SOFR + 2.45%, a 0.50% undrawn fee and a 2.00% letter-of-credit fee. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

### D. Indicative ranges (market, period, source) and drivers

9. Indicative senior (A-2 or equivalent) coupons:
   - US tower ABS: about 1.6–2.6% (2020–2021), about 6.6% (late 2022), about 4.7–4.8% (late 2024) [source: 1];
   - US fibre ABS: 5.6–6.6% (2023–2025) [source: 2, 5, 6, 7];
   - US data-centre ABS: 6.4–7.3% (late 2024–2025) [source: 8].

   Subordinate classes price 0.4–5 percentage points wider (for example Frontier C at 11.50% against A-2 at 6.60%). ARDs run from about 3 years (SBA 2024-2C) to 10 years (SBA 2021-3C), with most around 5 to 7 years, and legal finals of about 28–30 years. [confidence: high for the individual data points; medium for the ranges as market generalisations]
10. Drivers, as the documents show them:
    - the base rate (SBA coupons track Treasury yields; the 2024-2C was hedged with a treasury lock);
    - asset maturity (towers have long contracted leases and low churn, and price tightest);
    - fibre penetration risk (a penetration-rate sweep trigger at Frontier);
    - enterprise against consumer contracts (Uniti's enterprise pool);
    - the sponsor's own credit (Uniti used ABS to refinance 10.50% secured notes; issuers are unrestricted subsidiaries);
    - refinancing risk at the ARD, managed by step-up interest and cash traps. [confidence: medium (synthesis of cited terms)] [source: 1, 2, 5, 6, 8]

## Financing and structure details

- Standard US digital ABS architecture: an asset entity holds the towers, fibre or data centres and the customer contracts. A bankruptcy-remote issuer issues senior (A-2), mezzanine (B) and junior (C) fixed-rate term notes, often with a revolving A-1 VFN. An affiliated manager runs the assets under a management agreement. Reserve accounts, DSCR-based cash traps and amortisation, ARD step-ups and risk-retention notes complete the structure. [source: 1, 2, 5, 7, 8]
- Construction phase: ABS needs stabilised cash flow, so new builds are financed first by warehouse facilities, bank or private-credit term loans or delayed-draw facilities (Frontier set up a warehouse facility for Tampa Bay fibre on 31 December 2024). The assets are then "termed out" into ABS once customers are connected. [source: 3; for data-centre construction loans see t-market-norms.md items 28–30]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The ABS market stayed open through the 2022 rate shock but repriced. SBA's coupons rose from under 2% (2021) to 6.6% (2022) and settled near 4.7–4.8% (2024). [source: 1]
- Fibre issuers used ABS to lower funding costs against their corporate debt. Uniti said this directly, redeeming 10.50% notes. [source: 6]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 82 (Telecoms, fiber, towers and data centers):** Teach the securitisation template once, using SBA's 1.30x cash trap and 1.15x amortisation triggers, then show how it adapts. Fibre adds penetration triggers and higher subordinate coupons. Data centres add fee-simple real-estate collateral and VFNs for working capital. Have students build a DSCR test and an ARD refinancing scenario, using Frontier's 5% step-up. Compare a USD 1.1 billion Csquare deal at about 6.5% with the much larger investment-grade Hyperion SPV bond (hyperion-meta.md) to discuss tenant credit against asset diversity.
