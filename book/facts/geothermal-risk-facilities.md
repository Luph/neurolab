# Geothermal exploration risk mitigation facilities (GRMF East Africa, Indonesia GREM, Turkey RSM)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest documents covered: GRMF POWER Developer Manual V15 (2025) and 8th round timeline; World Bank ISR for Indonesia GREM, 1 October 2024; World Bank Turkey restructuring paper, 16 December 2024, and ISR, 16 January 2025)

## Summary

Geothermal projects carry resource risk at the start: surface studies and exploration or confirmation wells cost tens of millions of dollars and can find no commercially viable steam. Commercial lenders will not fund this stage, and equity prices it expensively. Public facilities therefore share the risk. Three contrasting designs:

- **GRMF (Geothermal Risk Mitigation Facility for Eastern Africa).** A grant facility of approximately USD 60 million:
    - set up by the African Union Commission (AUC) with Germany's BMZ and the EU-Africa Infrastructure Trust Fund through KfW;
    - covers 15 eligible countries;
    - pays up-front, non-reimbursable cost-share grants: 80 percent of surface study costs, 40 percent of exploration drilling costs, 20 percent of infrastructure costs, plus a contingent "Continuation Premium";
    - is awarded through competitive application rounds (the 8th POWER round opened in January 2025).
- **Indonesia GREM (Geothermal Resource Risk Mitigation Project).** Approved by the World Bank in September 2019:
    - a USD 455 million facility run by state infrastructure financier PT Sarana Multi Infrastruktur (PT SMI);
    - financed by IBRD, the Green Climate Fund (GCF) and the Clean Technology Fund (CTF);
    - lends for exploration drilling, with the possibility of grant conversion if drilling fails;
    - as of October 2024, five years after approval, had approved no sub-projects. Its IBRD loan was 0 percent disbursed, and the World Bank downgraded the planned multiphase programme to a standalone project.
- **Turkey Risk Sharing Mechanism (RSM).** Part of the World Bank's Turkey Geothermal Development Project, approved November 2016:
    - a USD 39.8 million CTF contingent recovery grant administered by the development bank TKYB;
    - repays an agreed share of well costs (60 percent of acceptable costs in Round 1) only if wells fail pre-agreed success criteria;
    - collects a "success fee" from developers if wells succeed;
    - sits alongside USD 550 million of IBRD credit lines through TKYB and TSKB for later-stage drilling and plants.

    In Round 1 both funded projects failed and the RSM paid out USD 5.1 million. The loan facility had mobilised over USD 630 million of private capital by December 2024.

Together they show the main design choices: grant or loan or contingent grant; who administers; how success is defined; and how the facility links to later debt.

## Verified facts

**GRMF (Eastern Africa)**

1. GRMF provides non-reimbursable grant funding for geothermal projects in 15 eligible East African countries: Burundi, Comoros, DRC, Djibouti, Eritrea, Eswatini, Ethiopia, Kenya, Malawi, Nigeria, Rwanda, Somalia, Tanzania, Uganda and Zambia. There are two programmes, GRMF POWER (power) and GRMF HEAT (direct use). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The AUC, Germany's Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ) and the EU-Africa Infrastructure Trust Fund, through KfW, agreed to establish GRMF. The facility's size is approximately USD 60 million from BMZ and the EU-ITF, and it is open to other donors. Grant agreements are concluded between the AUC and applicants. An Oversight Committee includes the AUC and KfW. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
3. **GRMF POWER grant ceilings** (shares of approved allowable costs):

    | Cost item | Grant share |
    |---|---|
    | Infrastructure for surface studies or drilling | 20 percent |
    | Surface studies (excluding infrastructure) | 80 percent |
    | Exploration drilling and testing of reservoir confirmation wells (excluding infrastructure) | 40 percent |
    | Continuation Premium | up to 30 percent of eligible expended continuation costs, or 30 percent of the developer's share of initial drilling costs, whichever is lower |

    Disbursement is based on actual costs. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. **Eligible drilling programmes:** up to two full-size reservoir confirmation wells (last casing or liner of 5 inches or more), or up to three slim holes, or two slim holes plus one full-size well. They may include a feasibility study aimed at securing finance for later wells. Continuation Premium activities include an additional full-size well, wellhead units, at least six months of discharge testing, a reservoir evaluation update and a feasibility study, with feasibility study costs capped at USD 100,000. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
5. **Selection:** two stages. Expressions of interest must score over 70 percent to be short-listed. There is then a mandatory pre-bidding workshop, including training on GRMF's financial model for drilling applicants, and full applications must also score over 70 percent. Drilling budgets must follow Anticipated Well Cost guidelines that cap allowable cost per metre. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
6. The 8th GRMF POWER round opened its expression-of-interest phase on 22 January 2025, with an EoI deadline of 9 April 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 5]

**Indonesia GREM (World Bank P166071)**

7. The World Bank approved GREM on 25/26 September 2019; the ISR gives 25 September and the project database 26 September. The borrower is the Government of Indonesia, the implementing agency is PT SMI, and the closing date is 31 October 2029. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
8. **Financing:**
    - Component 1, the Geothermal Resource Risk Mitigation Facility, costs USD 455 million.
    - Component 2, technical assistance, costs USD 10 million: USD 2.5 million each from GCF, CTF, ESMAP and the Global Infrastructure Facility.
    - The project abstract lists for Component 1: USD 150 million IBRD loan, USD 97.5 million GCF loan and reimbursable grant, CTF loan and contingent recovery grant, and a Government of Indonesia (PISP) loan. It expects USD 60 million of private developer equity to be leveraged.

    [confidence: high for totals and the IBRD and GCF amounts; the CTF line is internally inconsistent in the abstract] [source: 6]
9. **Status in the ISR of 1 October 2024:**
    - Progress toward the development objective and implementation progress were both rated Moderately Unsatisfactory.
    - PT SMI had received two pre-proposals, both under the Public Window, in December 2022 and January 2023. One awaited safeguards instruments and the other could not proceed without more geoscientific data.
    - There were no pre-proposals under the Private Window.
    - Indicators for MW reaching financial close and for approved proposals were zero, against a target of 1,000 MW by 2029 (600 MW for Phase 1).
    - The IBRD loan of USD 150 million was 0 percent disbursed.
    - The Bank noted it was "downsizing the ambition of an MPA to an standalone project".

    [confidence: high] [source: 7]

**Turkey RSM (World Bank P151739 and Additional Financing P172827)**

10. **Project financing:**
    - The Turkey Geothermal Development Project was approved on 1 November 2016 (effective February/April 2017), with USD 250 million of IBRD loans to TKYB and TSKB.
    - Additional Financing of USD 300 million was approved on 16 December 2021 (effective June 2022, closing December 2027).
    - A USD 39.8 million CTF grant to TKYB funds the RSM, of which USD 37 million is for the RSM and USD 2.8 million for training and operating costs.
    - A USD 350,000 ESMAP grant also supports the project.

    [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9]
11. **How the RSM works:**
    - TKYB runs rounds and signs Beneficiary Agreements (BAs) that set technical success criteria for exploratory wells.
    - If tests fail the criteria, TKYB disburses a predetermined share of well costs from the CTF grant.
    - If they succeed, the developer pays a success fee into TKYB's RSM account.
    - In February 2021 the success fee was cut from 10 percent to 5 percent of eligible drilling expenditure for the first three wells, and from 25 percent to 10 percent for up to two additional wells.

    [confidence: high] [source: 9]
12. **RSM results to December 2024:**
    - **Round 1** (launched 5 July 2018, closed 31 December 2022): seven shortlisted, two BAs signed. Both found unviable resources, received 60 percent of acceptable well costs, and the payout totalled USD 5.1 million.
    - **Round 2** (launched 21 January 2021): 16 subprojects, 12 withdrew. Three BAs had been signed, with combined full well costs of USD 14.2 million for 23.4 MW of potential resources.
    - **Round 3** (launched April 2023): 13 full proposals, none with BAs.
    - **Round 4** (launched July 2024): drew 14 full proposals, contingent on extending the CTF grant.
    - **Overall:** 34 full proposals across Rounds 1–3 and 6 BAs, but zero MW confirmed by the RSM. Withdrawals were due to land acquisition, licensing and high drilling costs.

    [confidence: high] [source: 9]
13. **Restructuring and indicators:**
    - The December 2024 restructuring extended the CTF grant closing date to the end of 2027.
    - The January 2025 ISR rated progress Moderately Satisfactory and implementation Moderately Unsatisfactory.
    - Private capital mobilised was USD 631 million against a target of USD 555 million.
    - Geothermal capacity built was 277.25 MW against a target of 383 MW, with 207.25 MW developed through capacity drilling under the loan facility.
    - Direct financing catalysed by CTF was USD 8.6 million against a target of USD 78 million.

    [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9]
14. **Feed-in tariff context** (World Bank):
    - Turkey's first geothermal feed-in tariff (FiT) paid USD 105/MWh for 10 years for plants commissioned 2011 to June 2021.
    - Version 2 (June 2021 to April 2023) paid around USD 72/MWh in lira.
    - Version 3 (from May 2023) pays about USD 102/MWh for 15 years in lira, indexed mostly to USD and EUR exchange rates.

    The Bank links the 2021–2023 low-FiT period to the pause in pipeline. [confidence: high] [source: 9]

**Where the current rule is found:** the GRMF Developer Manual (current version) and round documents at grmf-eastafrica.org; PT SMI's GREM Operations Manual and Developers Manual; World Bank project documents (PAD, ISRs, restructuring papers) for P166071, P151739 and P172827; TKYB RSM round documents.

## Financing and structure details

| | GRMF | Indonesia GREM | Turkey RSM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument | Up-front cost-share grant | Loan facility (public and private windows) with concessional or contingent-grant elements | Contingent recovery grant, paid only on failure; success fee on success |
| Size | approximately USD 60 million | USD 455 million (facility) | USD 37 million (RSM) plus USD 550 million loan facility |
| Administrator | AUC with KfW | PT SMI | TKYB (RSM); TKYB and TSKB (loans) |
| Funders | BMZ, EU-ITF | IBRD, GCF, CTF, GoI | CTF (RSM), IBRD (loans), ESMAP |

[sources: 2, 3, 6, 8, 9] The loan facility half of the Turkish design (capacity drilling and plant construction) is what mobilised most private capital. [8, 9]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Turkey (World Bank):** RSM uptake was slow because of FiT changes, land, licensing and drilling costs. The contingent design makes disbursement an uncertain measure of impact, since money flows only when wells fail. Meanwhile the IBRD credit lines through local development banks performed well against the private capital target. [9]
- **Indonesia (World Bank ISR):** five years after approval, no sub-projects had reached proposal stage and the private window had no pre-proposals. The ISR records low uptake and lack of geoscientific data. It does not, in the pages reviewed, give a full diagnosis. [7]
- **GRMF:** a grant model with competitive rounds, technical standards (AWC guidelines, the AU drilling code) and a contingent continuation premium. Outcome statistics (wells drilled, MW confirmed) were not verified here. [2, 3]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 72 (Hydropower and geothermal):** Use the three facilities to show how public money buys down exploration risk and why the instrument matters. Up-front grants (GRMF) lower cost but not outcome risk. Contingent grants (Turkey) act like insurance with a premium (the success fee) and payouts on failure. Loan-based facilities (GREM) leave the developer with debt unless forgiveness is triggered. Contrast Turkey's strong private mobilisation through credit lines with GREM's zero uptake. Ask the reader to price the Turkish RSM as an insurance contract: 60 percent cover against a 5 percent success fee.
