# Energy transition investment gap: global volumes, regional distribution and the cost of capital

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest items covered: IEA World Energy Investment 2026, released about 28 May 2026, reported via secondary sources; IRENA/CPI Global Landscape of Energy Transition Finance 2025, 17 November 2025; IRENA Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024, July 2025)

Cross-references: data-centre power demand and the IEA's data-centre investment estimates are in facts/t-frontier-data.md. DFI instruments and blended finance are in facts/t-dfis.md.

Access note: iea.org returned HTTP 403 to automated requests during this research, so IEA World Energy Investment (WEI) figures below come from press coverage compiled by Carbon Brief. They are marked medium confidence. Authors should check them against the IEA report pages before publication.

## Summary

Global energy investment is at record levels, but it is concentrated in China and the advanced economies.
- **IEA.** According to press coverage of WEI 2025, global energy investment was set to reach about USD 3.3 trillion in 2025, of which about USD 2.2 trillion was clean energy, roughly twice the amount going to fossil fuels. Coverage of WEI 2026 (late May 2026) reports total investment rising slightly to about USD 3.4 trillion in 2026, oil investment falling below USD 500 billion, and gas spending at a ten-year high.
- **IRENA and CPI** put energy transition investment at a record USD 2.4 trillion in 2024, up 20% on the 2022–23 average. China and the advanced economies accounted for 90% of it (44% and 46%). Least developed countries received less than 0.22%, and Sub-Saharan Africa about 2.3% of renewable energy investment. In 2024, investment in renewable power, grids and battery storage (USD 1.19 trillion) exceeded fossil fuel investment (USD 1.13 trillion).
- **The gap.** Against IRENA's 1.5°C pathway, renewable power investment must almost double to USD 1.4 trillion a year in 2025–30, grid investment must reach USD 671 billion a year (1.9 times 2024), and efficiency faces the largest gap, more than USD 1.8 trillion a year.
- **Cost of capital** explains much of the regional imbalance. IRENA's 2024 WACC assumptions run from 3.8% in Europe to 12% in Africa. Onshore wind costs about the same per kWh in Africa and Europe (USD 0.051 against 0.052), but in Africa most of that cost is financing rather than capex.

## Verified facts

### A. Global volumes (IEA World Energy Investment; global; check iea.org/reports for the current edition)

1. WEI 2025 (released about 5 June 2025), as reported by Reuters, the FT and Bloomberg via Carbon Brief:
   - global energy investment set to reach a record USD 3.3 trillion in 2025;
   - clean energy technologies (including renewables, nuclear and storage) attracting about USD 2.2 trillion, twice the amount expected for fossil fuels;
   - upstream oil and gas investment down 4% to just under USD 570 billion;
   - oil production spending down 6%, the largest fall outside the Covid years since 2016;
   - solar investment about USD 450 billion in 2025.
   [confidence: medium; secondary reports of the IEA report] [source: 1, 2]
2. WEI 2026 (released about 28 May 2026), as reported by Bloomberg and Reuters via Carbon Brief:
   - overall energy investment expected to rise slightly to about USD 3.4 trillion in 2026, mostly to grids, storage, low-emissions fuels, nuclear, renewables and electrification;
   - global oil investment to fall below USD 500 billion in 2026, a third successive annual decline;
   - spending on gas to reach a ten-year high.
   [confidence: medium] [source: 3]
3. WEI 2026, as reported by Carbon Brief directly from the IEA data:
   - investment in grid upgrades, power equipment and generation to support data centres reached USD 105 billion in 2025, more than total energy-sector investment across Africa;
   - global orders for new gas power plants rose to 130 GW in 2025, a 25-year high;
   - US gas-power investment tripled in 2025, and the IEA expects US investment in fossil-fuelled power plants to overtake China's in 2026.
   [confidence: medium-high; Carbon Brief analysis of IEA data] [source: 4]

### B. Distribution and gap (IRENA and CPI, Global Landscape of Energy Transition Finance 2025, published 17 November 2025; data year 2024)

4. Global investment in energy transition technologies reached a record USD 2.4 trillion in 2024, 20% above the 2022–23 annual average. The scope covers renewables, grids, storage, EVs and charging, CCS, green hydrogen, efficiency and heat pumps. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
5. In 2024, investment in renewable power, grids and battery storage was USD 1.19 trillion, against USD 1.13 trillion for fossil fuels. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
6. Breakdown of 2024 investment:
   - renewable energy: USD 807 billion (up 22% on 2022–23);
   - power grids: USD 359 billion (up 14%);
   - battery storage: USD 54 billion (up 73%);
   - energy efficiency: USD 346 billion (up 3%);
   - green hydrogen: USD 8 billion (down 20%).
   [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. China and the advanced economies made up 90% of energy transition investment in 2024 (44% and 46% respectively). LDCs accounted for less than 0.22% of energy transition investment and about 2.2% of renewable energy investment. Regional shares of 2024 renewable energy investment:
   - China: USD 352 billion, 44%;
   - Sub-Saharan Africa: USD 18 billion, 2.3%, or about USD 15 per head against USD 229 in Europe;
   - Latin America and the Caribbean: 5.4%;
   - Middle East and North Africa: 2.6%.
   [confidence: high] [source: 5]
8. Finance mix: in 2022–23, renewable energy investment was about 50% equity, 48.4% market-rate debt and 1.6% low-cost debt and grants. Equity was under 30% of investment in LDCs, against 50% elsewhere. Hydropower (95%), geothermal (81%) and onshore wind (62%) were debt-dominated, and solar PV equity-dominated (59%). [confidence: high] [source: 5]
9. Investment needed under IRENA's 1.5°C Scenario for 2025–30:
   - renewable power: USD 1.4 trillion a year, almost double current levels;
   - grids: USD 671 billion a year, 1.9 times 2024;
   - efficiency: nearly USD 2.6 trillion a year, a gap of more than USD 1.8 trillion a year and a more than seven-fold increase.
   [confidence: high] [source: 5]

### C. Cost of capital (IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024, July 2025)

10. IRENA's 2024 WACC assumptions for renewable power ranged from 3.8% in Europe to 12% in Africa, reflecting country risk and macroeconomic conditions in 2023. IRENA's regional chart shows African onshore wind and solar PV at the top of the range (about 11% to 12%) and South America at about 7.7%. [confidence: high for the 3.8%–12% range stated in the text; medium for values read from the chart] [source: 6]
11. IRENA: the weighted average LCOE of onshore wind in 2024 was USD 0.051/kWh in Africa and USD 0.052/kWh in Europe. In Europe the LCOE is driven mainly by capex. In most African countries, financing costs make up the majority of it. IRENA estimates financing cost as LCOE at the given WACC minus LCOE at a 0% discount rate. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
12. IRENA reports that renewable WACC fell in 2024 compared with 2023 because of monetary easing, especially in China, where state-owned developers have access to low-cost debt. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
13. A July 2025 UN report on the economics of renewables, as reported by Carbon Brief, said the cost of capital for a large-scale solar PV project in an emerging or developing economy is "well over twice as high" as in advanced economies. [confidence: medium; secondary] [source: 7]

## Financing and structure details

The drivers of the gap that a financier can act on:
- **Country risk premium in WACC** (fact 10). Every percentage point matters more for capital-heavy renewables than for fuel-heavy thermal plants.
- **Equity scarcity in LDCs** (fact 8).
- **Currency mismatch.**
- **Concessional and blended capital.** IRENA notes that only 1.6% of renewable investment is concessional (fact 8).

Tools that address these include DFI guarantees, local-currency facilities and political risk cover. These are covered in facts/t-dfis.md and the Chapter 88 local-currency and blended solutions section.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- IRENA (2025): investment is concentrated where the cost of capital is lowest, so the regions with the greatest needs, such as Sub-Saharan Africa at about USD 15 per head, receive the least. IRENA notes that LDCs depend on international and public funds because of limited fiscal capacity and high cost of capital. [source: 5]
- IRENA (2025): Africa's onshore wind is as cheap as Europe's only because its better resource offsets far higher financing costs. Lowering the WACC is therefore the largest single lever for cutting tariffs. [source: 6]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 88 (The frontier)**: Separate the volume story (record USD 2.4–3.4 trillion totals) from the distribution story (90% in China and advanced economies). Then work the cost-of-capital arithmetic with the reader. Take a project with identical capex and output and change only the WACC from 4% to 12%. Show how much of the tariff becomes financing cost, and how a DFI guarantee or local-currency facility that cuts the WACC by a few points changes bankability. State all figures with their edition and year, and tell readers that the IEA (WEI, each May or June) and IRENA/CPI (each autumn) update them every year.

## Do not state

- Do not give WEI 2025 or WEI 2026 figures for emerging and developing economies' share of clean energy investment, Africa's share, or the IEA's estimate of the EMDE cost-of-capital premium (it is often quoted as "two to three times"). The IEA site blocked automated access, so these were not verified.
- Do not quote the WEI 2026 clean-energy total for 2026. Only the USD 3.4 trillion overall figure was found, and only in secondary coverage.
- Do not treat IEA, IRENA/CPI and BNEF totals as comparable. Scopes differ: IEA includes fossil supply and efficiency, while IRENA/CPI counts transition technologies and supply chains separately.
- Do not use Carbon Brief's "USD 2.2bn" wording for WEI 2025 clean-energy investment. It is a typographical slip for USD 2.2 trillion.
- Do not cite IRENA's World Energy Transitions Outlook 2026 investment-needs figures. Not checked.
