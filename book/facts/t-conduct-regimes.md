# Personal conduct and information rules for deal professionals: UK MAR, US Regulation FD and insider trading, NDAs and clean teams, FCA Conduct Rules

As of: 2026-10-03 (UK MAR text as shown on legislation.gov.uk on 2026-10-03; latest amendment covered: UK insider dealing maximum sentence raised to ten years, 1 November 2021; EU gun-jumping fine on Altice, 24 April 2018)

Cross-references: sanctions and anti-bribery rules are in facts/t-anticorruption-sanctions.md, and loan-document confidentiality provisions are in facts/t-loan-docs.md.

## Summary

Project finance is mostly private, but deal professionals routinely handle price-sensitive information. Sponsors and offtakers are often listed. Project bonds and listed infrastructure funds trade. Bidders in consortia are competitors. Four rule sets govern personal conduct.

- **UK MAR.** The retained Market Abuse Regulation defines inside information as precise, non-public and likely to have a significant price effect. It bans insider dealing, recommending or inducing it, and unlawful disclosure. Disclosure is lawful only "in the normal exercise of an employment, a profession or duties". Issuers and their advisers must keep insider lists, retain them for at least five years, and get everyone on the list to acknowledge their duties in writing. Criminal insider dealing under the Criminal Justice Act 1993 now carries up to ten years' imprisonment.
- **US Regulation FD and Rule 10b-5.** Regulation FD (effective 23 October 2000) bars US issuers from selectively disclosing material non-public information to market professionals and holders. It exempts recipients who owe a duty of confidence or who expressly agree to keep the information confidential, which is why NDAs matter. Insider trading liability rests on Rule 10b-5, including the misappropriation theory upheld in United States v. O'Hagan (1997) and the duties of trust or confidence defined in Rule 10b5-2.
- **Competition law.** Information shared between competing bidders or merger parties must be ring-fenced through clean teams. The CMA's 2023 horizontal guidance names clean teams and confidentiality rules for bidding consortia. The European Commission fined Altice €124.5 million in 2018 for gun-jumping, including receiving commercially sensitive target information outside any confidentiality agreement.
- **FCA Conduct Rules (COCON).** These apply to individuals at UK-authorised firms. Rule 1 is "You must act with integrity" and Rule 5 is "You must observe proper standards of market conduct".

## Verified facts

### A. UK Market Abuse Regulation (UK MAR: retained Regulation (EU) No 596/2014; current text on legislation.gov.uk)

1. Article 7(1)(a) defines inside information as information "of a precise nature, which has not been made public, relating, directly or indirectly, to one or more issuers or to one or more financial instruments, and which, if it were made public, would be likely to have a significant effect on the prices" of those instruments or related derivatives. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Information is precise (Article 7(2)) if it indicates circumstances or an event that exists, has occurred or may reasonably be expected to, and is specific enough to draw a conclusion about its possible price effect. Article 7(3) provides that an intermediate step in a protracted process is itself inside information if it meets the criteria. Under Article 7(4), information likely to have a significant price effect means information "a reasonable investor would be likely to use as part of the basis of his or her investment decisions". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. Article 14 provides that a person shall not (a) engage or attempt to engage in insider dealing, (b) recommend or induce another person to do so, or (c) unlawfully disclose inside information. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. Article 10(1) provides that unlawful disclosure arises where a person with inside information discloses it to any other person, "except where the disclosure is made in the normal exercise of an employment, a profession or duties". [confidence: high] [source: 3]
5. Article 18 sets out the insider list rules:
   - issuers, and persons acting on their behalf or account (for example advisers and accountants), must each draw up a list of all persons with access to inside information who work for them;
   - the list records the identity of each person, the reason for inclusion, the date and time of access, and the date the list was drawn up;
   - the list must be promptly updated when a reason changes or a person gains or loses access;
   - lists must be kept for at least five years after being drawn up or updated;
   - the list-keeper must take all reasonable steps to ensure that each listed person acknowledges in writing the legal and regulatory duties involved and is aware of the sanctions. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. Criminal insider dealing in the UK falls under Part V of the Criminal Justice Act 1993. Under s.61(1)(b), the maximum on conviction on indictment is a fine, imprisonment for up to ten years, or both. The ten-year figure was substituted with effect from 1 November 2021 by the Financial Services Act 2021, s.31. In England and Wales, proceedings need the consent of the Secretary of State or the DPP (s.61(2)). [confidence: high] [source: 5]

### B. United States: Regulation FD and insider trading

7. The SEC adopted Regulation FD together with Rules 10b5-1 and 10b5-2 in Release Nos. 33-7881 and 34-43154 (15 August 2000). They took effect on 23 October 2000. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
8. Regulation FD's general rule (17 CFR 243.100(a)) applies when an issuer, or a person acting on its behalf, discloses material non-public information to certain persons. The issuer must then make public disclosure simultaneously if the disclosure was intentional, and promptly if it was not. The covered recipients (243.100(b)(1)) are broker-dealers, investment advisers and institutional managers, investment companies, and security holders where it is reasonably foreseeable they will trade on the information. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
9. Regulation FD does not apply (243.100(b)(2)) to disclosures to:
   - persons who owe the issuer a duty of trust or confidence, such as attorneys, investment bankers or accountants;
   - persons who "expressly agree to maintain the disclosed information in confidence";
   - certain disclosures in connection with registered securities offerings.
   [confidence: high] [source: 7]
10. Regulation FD definitions (243.101):
    - a disclosure is "intentional" when the person knows, or is reckless in not knowing, that the information is material and non-public;
    - "promptly" means as soon as reasonably practicable, and in no event after the later of 24 hours or the start of the next day's trading on the NYSE, after a senior official learns of the disclosure;
    - public disclosure is ordinarily made by filing or furnishing a Form 8-K, or by another method reasonably designed for broad, non-exclusionary distribution.
    [confidence: high] [source: 8]
11. Rule 10b5-1 provides that a person trades "on the basis of" material non-public information if aware of it when trading. It provides affirmative defences for trades made under a contract, instruction or written plan put in place before the person became aware of the information. Rule 10b5-2 sets out three non-exclusive situations in which a duty of trust or confidence arises for misappropriation purposes:
    - an agreement to keep information confidential;
    - a history or pattern of sharing confidences that gives a reasonable expectation of confidentiality;
    - information received from a spouse, parent, child or sibling, subject to an affirmative defence.
    [confidence: high] [source: 6]
12. United States v. O'Hagan, 521 U.S. 642, decided 25 June 1997. The Supreme Court upheld the misappropriation theory: a person who trades on confidential information in breach of a duty owed to the source of the information may be liable under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5. [confidence: high] [source: 9]

### C. NDAs, clean teams and gun-jumping (competition law)

13. CMA guidance on horizontal agreements (CMA184, published 16 August 2023), on information exchange, paragraph 8.72: "A clean team generally refers to a restricted group of individuals within an undertaking who are not involved in the undertaking's commercial operations and are bound by strict confidentiality obligations with regard to the competitively sensitive information." A trustee is an independent third party used for the same purpose. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
14. On bidding consortia, the same guidance says that only information necessary for preparing the bid and performing the contract should be disclosed between consortium members, and that circulation should be restricted to relevant staff. Members may reduce competition risk by "providing for clean teams or confidentiality rules". [confidence: high] [source: 10]
15. On 24 April 2018 the European Commission fined Altice €124.5 million for implementing its acquisition of PT Portugal before notification or clearance under the EU Merger Regulation. This breached both the notification requirement and the standstill obligation. The Commission found that:
    - purchase agreement provisions gave Altice veto rights over PT Portugal's ordinary business;
    - Altice gave PT Portugal instructions on a marketing campaign;
    - Altice sought and received "detailed commercially sensitive information about PT Portugal outside the framework of any confidentiality agreement".
    Fines can reach 10% of aggregate turnover. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

### D. FCA Conduct Rules (UK; FCA Handbook COCON 2.1)

16. The Individual Conduct Rules are:
    - Rule 1: "You must act with integrity."
    - Rule 2: "You must act with due skill, care and diligence."
    - Rule 3: "You must be open and cooperative with the FCA, the PRA and other regulators."
    - Rule 4: "You must pay due regard to the interests of customers and treat them fairly."
    - Rule 5: "You must observe proper standards of market conduct."
    - Rule 6: "You must act to deliver good outcomes for retail customers."
    [confidence: high] [source: 12]

## Financing and structure details

Not a financing. The practical structures are:
- **NDAs or confidentiality undertakings** at the teaser or information memorandum stage;
- **wall-crossing** (market soundings) before a public bond or a listed sponsor's announcement;
- **insider lists** kept by the issuer and by each adviser;
- **clean-team agreements** for competitor consortium members, and for sell-side data rooms where the buyer is a competitor;
- **information barriers** within banks between private-side and public-side staff.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Altice (2018) shows that pre-closing integration, veto rights over ordinary business, and receipt of sensitive data without a confidentiality framework can each be treated as implementing the deal early. The Commission treated the breach as at least negligent because Altice knew its obligations. [source: 11]
- Regulation FD's confidentiality exclusion (fact 9) explains a market practice: lenders and potential bond investors sign NDAs or are wall-crossed before a US-listed sponsor shares unannounced project information with them. [source: 7]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 85 (Screening deals and reading data rooms)**: Before opening any data room, ask four questions. Am I under an NDA, and with whom? Is anything here inside information about a listed sponsor, offtaker or bond issuer, applying the UK MAR Article 7 tests? Am I on an insider list, and have I acknowledged it? Am I a competitor of the seller or of another bidder, so that I should only see clean-team material? Use the CMA's definition of a clean team and the Altice facts to show what not to request.
- **Chapter 87 (The practitioner's craft and career)**: Present conduct rules as career-limiting-mistake territory. Personal trading in a sponsor's shares while working on its project. Telling a friend or relative, which is the Rule 10b5-2 family-member example. Forwarding a model to someone outside the wall. Link these to COCON Rules 1 and 5 and to the ten-year criminal maximum. The point to drive home is that "normal exercise of a profession" is a narrow gateway, not a licence to share with anyone on the deal.
