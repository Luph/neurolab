# Corporate PPA market: volumes, structures and price indices, 2020–2026 (US and Europe)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: LevelTen Q2 2026 North American PPA Price Index commentary, July 2026; BNEF 1H 2026 Corporate Energy Market Outlook, 19 February 2026; Pexapark Renewables Market Outlook 2026, 27 January 2026)

Cross-references: for fixed-shape hedge and proxy-revenue-swap losses in Winter Storm Uri, use facts/winter-storm-uri.md. For Article 19a of Regulation (EU) 2024/1747 (PPA promotion) and the two-way CfD obligation, use facts/t-eu-support-schemes.md. For US tax credit changes (OBBBA), use facts/t-us-tax-credits.md.

## Summary

BloombergNEF (BNEF) counts publicly announced corporate clean-power purchase agreements. On its count, volumes grew every year from 2016 to 2024: 23.7 GW in 2020, 31.1 GW in 2021, 36.7 GW in 2022 (later revised to about 41 GW) and 46 GW in 2023. BNEF called 2024 a record, at an implied figure of about 62 GW. Volumes then fell for the first time in nearly a decade, to 55.9 GW in 2025. Big Tech dominates: Meta, Amazon, Google and Microsoft accounted for 49% of 2025 activity, including 4.7 GW of nuclear. The US hosted a record 29.5 GW in 2025, yet the number of unique US corporate buyers halved, to 33. Corporate PPA volumes in Europe, the Middle East and Africa (EMEA) fell 13% to 17 GW. Pexapark, which tracks only European disclosed PPAs (corporate and utility), recorded 13.1 GW in 247 deals in 2025, down from 15.3 GW in 2024. Spain was again the largest market (3.9 GW), and Germany fell steeply as solar cannibalisation removed any overlap between buyers' bids and sellers' offers. Prices moved in opposite directions. In North America, LevelTen's P25 offer index rose about 13% for solar and 24% for wind in the year to Q1 2026, driven by tariffs, permitting and the tax-credit cut-offs under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA). In Europe, solar PPA prices fell for four straight quarters, down 8% year on year to Q1 2026, as negative-price hours grew. In structure, the market is moving away from simple pay-as-produced solar contracts towards hybrid (solar plus storage), baseload-like and firm-power contracts. Two forces drive this: hourly matching in the proposed revision of the GHG Protocol's Scope 2 guidance, and the value lost to cannibalisation.

## Verified facts

### A. Global and regional volumes (BNEF counts publicly announced PPAs; figures are revised in later outlooks)

1. 2020: corporations bought a record 23.7 GW (up from 20.1 GW in 2019 and 13.6 GW in 2018). US 11.9 GW (down from 14.1 GW); EMEA 7.2 GW (from 2.6 GW), including Spain 4.2 GW; APAC 2.9 GW; Latin America 1.5 GW. Amazon was the top buyer (5.1 GW). BNEF's next release restated 2020 as 25.1 GW. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. 2021: a record 31.1 GW, up nearly 24%. The Americas accounted for 20.3 GW (US 17 GW, of which virtual PPAs 12 GW and green tariffs 3.2 GW) and Europe a record 8.7 GW. Amazon bought 6.2 GW; AES was the top seller (just under 3 GW). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. 2022: a record 36.7 GW, up 18%. Americas 24.1 GW; APAC 4.6 GW; EMEA 8.1 GW (down 7%, owing to the energy crisis). Amazon bought 10.9 GW. Microsoft disputed BNEF's 1.3 GW figure for it, saying it had signed 6.7 GW; BNEF counts only publicly announced deals. BNEF later restated 2022 as 41 GW. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
4. 2023: a record 46 GW, up 12% on 41 GW in 2022. Americas 20.9 GW (45%), of which US 17.3 GW (down 16%); Europe 15.4 GW (up 74%). Spain, Germany, the UK and the Netherlands made up over half of Europe's deals. Amazon bought 8.8 GW; Engie was the top seller (2.4 GW). Cumulative volume since 2008 was 198 GW. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
5. 2025: 55.9 GW, down 10% from the 2024 record (implying about 62 GW in 2024, computed). Meta, Amazon, Google and Microsoft were 49% of global activity; Meta and Amazon together contracted 20.4 GW, including 4.7 GW of nuclear. US: a record 29.5 GW, but unique buyers fell 51% to 33. EMEA: 17 GW (down 13%). APAC: 6.9 GW (from 10.7 GW). Engie was the top seller (3.6 GW). "Baseload-like" products (co-located solar plus storage, hybrid solar-wind, nuclear) totalled 5.2 GW, and co-located or hybrid deals 5.8 GW. [confidence: high for stated figures; medium for the computed 2024 figure] [source: 6]
6. Europe (Pexapark disclosed PPAs, which include utility offtakes): 13.1 GW in 247 deals in 2025, down from 15.3 GW in 2024. Spain 3.9 GW, Italy 1.8 GW, Poland 1.5 GW. Utilities raised PPA offtake by over 200% year on year. Iberdrola was the top seller (1,088 MW, 13 deals); Amazon was the top corporate buyer (711 MW, five deals). [confidence: high] [source: 7]
7. US (Pexapark): overall US PPA volumes fell 42% year on year in 2025. The information technology sector accounted for 82% of US corporate PPA volume. ERCOT solar and wind PPA "Fair Values" rose 8% and 16% over the year. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
8. Flexibility purchase agreements (European battery tolls and optimisation deals): nearly 12 GW / 23 GWh contracted in 2025, about triple 2024. Utilities took 77% of contracted FPA volume. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

### B. Price indices and price trends

9. LevelTen PPA Price Index methodology: built from developers' PPA offers on the LevelTen Marketplace; includes bundled RECs or guarantees of origin; tenors of 10–19 years; financial settlement at regional hubs on real-time prices; only standard flat, as-generated offers; reported mainly as the 25th percentile (P25) by market. Q2 2026 data: 266 offers from 185 projects in six North American markets (AESO, CAISO, ERCOT, MISO, PJM, SPP), and 110 offers from 92 projects in 16 European countries (plus 94 hybrid offers). Full reports are subscription-only. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
10. North America, Q1 2026: P25 Market-Averaged Continental solar index up 4.6% quarter on quarter and over 13% year on year; wind up nearly 8% quarter on quarter and nearly 24% year on year. LevelTen attributed this to tariffs, insurance and labour costs, federal permitting bottlenecks (including FAA turbine-height demands) and OBBBA tax-credit cuts. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
11. North America, Q2 2026: Market-Averaged solar prices fell 4.8% quarter on quarter, the first decline in two years (1.8% excluding CAISO). Wind rose 5.5% quarter on quarter and 17.5% year on year. LevelTen notes the 4 July 2026 tax-credit qualification cut-off imposed by the OBBBA. Projects that qualified must still be placed in service by the end of 2030. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
12. Europe, Q1 2026 (LevelTen): solar PPA prices fell for a fourth consecutive quarter, down 8% year on year. Negative-price risk and cannibalisation are pushing buyers to hybrid (storage plus generation) deals and to structures that limit negative-price settlements. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
13. Europe, Q3 2025 (Pexapark "Transactable Price Range", the overlap of top buyer bids and lowest seller offers): no overlap in Germany (only three announced deals, 169 MW, in the quarter) or in Great Britain for solar and onshore wind. In GB, inflation-linked CfD prices anchor seller expectations above corporate willingness to pay. Italy onshore wind traded at around EUR 72.6/MWh. In GB, about 70% of new solar and wind projects in H1 2025 were co-located with batteries. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
14. Europe, 2022 (BNEF survey): corporate PPA prices rose 7.2% on average since H2 2021 and 13.5% since H2 2020, with prices up in every market and technology. The cheapest were Spanish solar (EUR 37/MWh) and Swedish wind (EUR 34.5/MWh). Annual baseload contracts carried a premium of EUR 1.7/MWh (Finland) to EUR 6.4/MWh (UK) over pay-as-produced deals, owing to balancing costs. Shorter (5–10 year) and longer (15–20 year) terms carried premiums of up to EUR 2.3/MWh over the standard 10–15 years, because developers mainly need to cover their 10–15 year financing period. In the US, by contrast, longer terms attract a discount. [confidence: high] [source: 13]

### C. Structures (definitions as used in these sources)

15. Virtual (financial) PPA: the project sells into the wholesale market and the buyer settles the difference between the fixed PPA price and the market price, usually at a hub. It dominates the US market (12 GW of 17 GW US deals in 2021). [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
16. Pay-as-produced (as-generated) versus baseload or shaped: baseload deals require the seller (or a utility intermediary) to firm the profile, which adds balancing and hedging cost (see item 14). Cross-border virtual PPAs, for example in Spain, let buyers offset load elsewhere in Europe. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 13]
17. Regulatory driver: proposed GHG Protocol Scope 2 revisions could require hourly matching and tighter geographic boundaries, which favours hybrid and firm deals. BNEF says 100% renewable claims "will become harder to justify" under hourly tracking. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

- 2021: 31.1 GW; US virtual PPAs 12 GW.
- 2022: European energy crisis; EMEA volumes dip; European PPA prices up 13.5% in two years.
- 2023: 46 GW; Europe up 74%.
- 2024: record (about 62 GW implied); Pexapark Europe 15.3 GW.
- 2025: 55.9 GW, first fall in nearly a decade; Pexapark Europe 13.1 GW; Q3 Germany no transactable range.
- Q1 2026: US P25 solar up 13% and wind up 24% year on year; Europe solar down 8% year on year.
- 4 July 2026: OBBBA tax-credit qualification cut-off.
- Q2 2026: first US solar decline in two years (−4.8% quarter on quarter); wind +17.5% year on year.

## Financing and structure details

- Bankability of a corporate PPA for project lenders depends on four things: buyer credit (investment grade or credit support), tenor relative to debt tenor (BNEF: developers price to cover a 10–15 year financing period), settlement point (hub basis risk stays with the seller under a hub-settled virtual PPA), and shape (pay-as-produced passes volume and shape risk to the buyer; baseload or fixed-shape keeps it with the seller, which was fatal in Uri).
- Utilities as intermediaries: Pexapark's 2025 data show utilities taking a larger share of offtake and of battery FPAs because they can absorb shape and volatility on a portfolio basis.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Europe 2025: Pexapark attributes the slowdown to lower capture-price expectations. Sellers anchor to cost and to auction benchmarks (EEG, CfD), while buyers price in cannibalisation and negative prices, so the price ranges stop overlapping. [source: 7, 12]
- US 2025–2026: BNEF and LevelTen both describe a "tale of two markets". Hyperscalers procure at scale, including nuclear and firm power, while smaller corporates hesitate because of high prices and the pending GHG Protocol revisions. [source: 6, 10]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 20: Use BNEF volumes and LevelTen and Pexapark trends to show that a corporate PPA price is set by the gap between developer cost of capital and buyer value (expected capture price). When that gap is negative, as in Germany in Q3 2025, deals stop. Walk through the structure menu (physical/virtual, pay-as-produced/baseload, hub/node settlement, floors and collars, hybrid). For each, show which party holds volume, shape, basis and negative-price risk.
- Ch 70: For onshore wind and solar, connect the European solar price decline to capture-rate erosion, and the US wind price rise to permitting and tax credits. Ask how a lender should haircut merchant tail value when PPA tenors (10–19 years in LevelTen's index) are shorter than asset life.
