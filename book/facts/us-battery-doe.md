# US DOE loans for EV battery manufacturing: Ultium Cells, BlueOval SK and StarPlus Energy

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Ford's 10-Q for the quarter to 30 June 2026, filed 29 July 2026, and Stellantis's H1 2026 report, filed 30 July 2026)

## Summary

Between 2022 and 2024 the US Department of Energy's Loan Programs Office (LPO) used the Advanced Technology Vehicles Manufacturing (ATVM) program to make three very large direct loans, funded through the Federal Financing Bank, to automaker–cell-maker battery joint ventures. They were Ultium Cells (GM/LG Energy Solution, USD 2.5 billion, closed late November 2022), BlueOval SK (Ford/SK On, up to USD 9.63 billion, closed December 2024) and StarPlus Energy (Stellantis/Samsung SDI, USD 7.54 billion, closed December 2024). The loans were construction financings for single-purpose plants whose output was sold almost entirely to the sponsoring automaker, and they were backed by sponsor guarantees and support agreements. That made them sponsor-credit loans dressed as project loans. When US EV demand expectations fell in 2025, the three loans went different ways. Ultium prepaid its DOE loan in full in May 2025 using a USD 1.8 billion loan from GM. Ford and SK On dissolved BlueOval SK (agreement December 2025, closing May 2026). Ford took the Kentucky plants and assumed USD 3.8 billion of DOE debt directly at 4.814% to 2040, and it recorded billions of dollars of charges. StarPlus remained in place, with Stellantis guaranteeing 49% of drawn debt. Meanwhile DOE renamed LPO the Office of Energy Dominance Financing (EDF). EDF says it is restructuring, revising or eliminating more than USD 83.6 billion of Biden-era loans and commitments.

## Verified facts

1. Ultium Cells LLC (a GM/LG Energy Solution joint venture) received a USD 2.5 billion ATVM direct loan for cell plants in Lordstown (Ohio), Spring Hill (Tennessee) and Lansing (Michigan). The loan was issued in late November 2022 and announced on 12 December 2022. DOE estimated 6,000 construction and 5,100 operations jobs. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. In May 2025 GM lent USD 1.8 billion to Ultium Cells LLC to fund full voluntary prepayment of its ATVM loans. GM's loan accrues interest at 5.7% a year, matures in April 2030 and is prepayable without penalty. GM funded part of it from a May 2025 bond issue. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
3. GM recorded USD 364 million of impairment charges in equity income in Q2 2026 in connection with its EV strategic realignment. Its share of equity income from Ultium Cells Holdings was insignificant in H1 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
4. On 13 December 2024 BlueOval SK, LLC (BOSK, a 50/50 joint venture of Ford and SK On/SK Battery America) entered into a Loan Arrangement and Reimbursement Agreement. Under it DOE arranged for the Federal Financing Bank to purchase notes and advance up to USD 9,633,040,000 for one Tennessee plant and two Kentucky plants. DOE announced the closing on 16 December 2024, calling it the largest ATVM loan, with up to 7,500 operations jobs and more than 120 GWh a year of capacity. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]
5. Under a Sponsor Support, Share Retention and Subordination Agreement dated 13 December 2024 (with DOE, BOSK, Ford, SK On, SKBA and SK Innovation), Ford guaranteed 50% of BOSK's payment obligations under the DOE loan. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
6. Advances of USD 7,835,540,000 were made to BOSK. After its DOE draws, BOSK distributed USD 3.1 billion to Ford as returns of capital, including USD 1.7 billion in the first quarter of 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 7]
7. On 9 December 2025 Ford, SK On, SKBA and BOSK signed a Joint Venture Disposition Agreement. Ford recorded a USD 3.2 billion pre-tax impairment of its BOSK investment in Q4 2025 because the liabilities it would assume were expected to exceed the value of the assets received. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
8. Closing took place on 20 May 2026. Ford's membership interest was redeemed and its obligation to contribute up to USD 6.6 billion to BOSK was terminated. Ford Energy Battery LLC acquired BOSK's two Kentucky plants subject to existing DOE liens, and Ford was released from the support agreement. Ford assumed BOSK's obligations under the USD 3,805,040,000 DOE note for the single Kentucky plant for which advances had been made. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
9. Ford's new DOE loan carries interest at 4.814% a year, with quarterly interest only until 15 January 2030 and amortisation from 15 April 2030 to final maturity on 15 July 2040. Its covenants mirror Ford's corporate credit agreement, including a minimum Available Liquidity covenant of USD 4 billion. Cross-default and cross-acceleration thresholds are USD 1 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
10. On closing Ford recognised pre-tax special charges of USD 3.6 billion, including about USD 500 million of cash, and acquired assets with a fair value of USD 0.9 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
11. By arithmetic from Ford's figures, about USD 4.03 billion of the USD 7.84 billion advanced to BOSK was not assumed by Ford (USD 7,835.54 million minus USD 3,805.04 million). Ford's 10-K says it would be released from the guarantee relating to the Tennessee plant. [confidence: medium] (arithmetic; the treatment of that balance with SK On was not verified) [source: 5, 8]
12. StarPlus Energy LLC (a joint venture of FCA US LLC, a Stellantis subsidiary, and Samsung SDI) closed a USD 7.54 billion ATVM loan (USD 6.85 billion principal plus USD 688 million capitalised interest), announced on 17 December 2024, for up to two cell and module plants in Kokomo, Indiana. DOE expected about 67 GWh a year of capacity and up to 2,800 operations jobs, and had been evaluating the application since July 2023. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
13. Stellantis's 2025 Form 20-F reports a guarantee to third parties of 49% of StarPlus's drawn debt under a loan facility of EUR 6,383 million (USD 7,500 million). Drawn debt was EUR 2,373 million (USD 2,788 million) at 31 December 2025, against EUR 888 million at end-2024. At 30 June 2026 Stellantis guaranteed EUR 1,395 million, 49% of a total commitment of EUR 2,848 million. Stellantis holds 49% of StarPlus. [confidence: high] [source: 10, 11]
14. In January 2026 EDF's director stated that DOE and EDF leadership were "restructuring, substantially revising, or eliminating" more than USD 83.6 billion of loans and conditional commitments, over 80% of the Biden-era portfolio. Of USD 104 billion of Biden-era principal obligations, EDF said more than USD 29.9 billion (about 29%) was being de-obligated and USD 53.6 billion (about 51%) revised. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
15. EDF reported, as of 31 August 2026, USD 132.0 billion of loans and guarantees issued, USD 53.1 billion disbursed, USD 18.1 billion of principal repaid and actual and estimated losses of USD 1.03 billion (2.0% of disbursements). [confidence: high] [source: 13]

## Financing and structure details

- Instrument: ATVM direct loans funded through the Federal Financing Bank. Headline amounts include capitalised interest (StarPlus: USD 6.85 billion principal plus USD 688 million capitalised interest). [source: 5, 9]
- Credit support: several (not joint) sponsor guarantees pro rata to ownership (Ford 50% of BOSK; Stellantis 49% of StarPlus), plus share retention and subordination of sponsor claims. [source: 5, 10]
- Distributions: BOSK upstreamed USD 3.1 billion of returns of capital to Ford after DOE draws. This was permitted leakage that later made the unwind costlier for the sponsor. [source: 7]
- Exit paths seen: sponsor-funded prepayment (Ultium); JV dissolution with the sponsor assuming its plant-specific debt on corporate terms (BOSK to Ford); continuation with a growing drawn balance (StarPlus).

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Ford attributes its charges to changes in EV adoption expectations and the regulatory and market environment. Its 2025 10-K describes about USD 13.8 billion of Q4 2025 charges for its updated EV strategy and the expected BOSK disposition. [source: 8]
- The structures worked as credit protection for DOE: because sponsors guaranteed their shares, the unwinds converted project exposure into investment-grade corporate exposure (Ford) or into full prepayment (Ultium), rather than into losses. This reading follows the filings. DOE has not published a loss figure for these loans.
- Concentration: each plant had a single captive customer, so plant economics moved one-for-one with that automaker's EV programme.

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 77 (Manufacturing/gigafactory finance): Contrast these captive-offtake, sponsor-guaranteed DOE loans with Northvolt (facts/northvolt.md), a merchant gigafactory with no investment-grade parent. Show how the BOSK unwind allocated plants and debt between JV partners, why the assumed loan migrated to corporate-style covenants (liquidity floor, cross-default), and how returns of capital drawn from loan proceeds raise sponsor exposure on exit.
- Chapter 88 (Frontier): Use the 2025–26 DOE/EDF portfolio review to teach political and policy risk in state-backed lending. A change of administration renamed the office, re-scoped eligibility and re-opened signed loans. Students should judge new public-finance programmes by their durability across election cycles as well as by their pricing.

## Do not state

- Do not state that DOE cancelled or forced the BOSK, StarPlus or Ultium loans. The filings describe sponsor-driven prepayment and disposition. DOE's role in negotiating them is not described beyond the new loan agreement.
- Do not state what happened to the remaining approximately USD 4.0 billion of BOSK advances (Tennessee plant) or SK On's arrangements with DOE. Not verified.
- Do not state the interest rates on the original BOSK, StarPlus or Ultium DOE loans (only the 4.814% Ford loan is verified).
- Do not state that the Stellantis-reported StarPlus "loan facility" has been amended by DOE, or give StarPlus production status. The 20-F does not label the facility; its size matches the DOE loan.
- Do not state the status of the Lansing Ultium plant or plant-level idlings. Not verified here.
