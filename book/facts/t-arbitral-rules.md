# Arbitral rules in current use: ICC 2021, LCIA 2020, UNCITRAL 2013/2021, ICSID 2022, SIAC 2025; English Arbitration Act 2025

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Arbitration Act 2025 in force 1 August 2025; ICC, LCIA and ICSID rules pages checked October 2026 showing the 2021, 2020 and 2022 rules still current)

## Summary

Project contracts and finance documents usually choose one of a small set of institutional or ad hoc rules. Checked against the institutions' own pages in October 2026, the current versions are:

- **ICC Arbitration Rules:** in force 1 January 2021.
- **LCIA Arbitration Rules 2020:** effective 1 October 2020.
- **UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules:** the 2010 revised text. Article 1(4) was added in 2013 to incorporate the Rules on Transparency for treaty-based investor-State arbitration (treaties concluded on or after 1 April 2014). Article 1(5) was added in 2021 to incorporate the Expedited Arbitration Rules, which apply only where the parties so agree.
- **ICSID 2022 Regulations and Rules:** approved by member States on 21 March 2022, in effect from 1 July 2022.
- **SIAC Rules 2025 (7th edition):** in force 1 January 2025.

The 2020–2025 revisions follow a common pattern:

- disclosure of third-party funding (ICC art. 11(7), ICSID 2022, SIAC r.38);
- tools for multi-contract, multi-party projects (consolidation and joinder in ICC and LCIA, SIAC "coordinated proceedings");
- early or summary disposal (LCIA "Early Determination", SIAC "Preliminary Determination");
- expedited tracks (ICC expedited procedure for agreements from 1 January 2021 up to US$3 million; SIAC Streamlined Procedure below S$1 million and Expedited Procedure up to S$10 million);
- express authority for remote hearings.

For seats in England and Wales (and Northern Ireland), the **Arbitration Act 2025** (Royal Assent 24 February 2025; in force 1 August 2025) amended the Arbitration Act 1996. The main changes:

- a default rule that the law of the seat governs the arbitration agreement unless the parties expressly choose otherwise;
- a statutory duty of disclosure for arbitrators;
- an express power to make awards on a summary basis;
- recognition of emergency arbitrators' peremptory orders;
- a tighter procedure for s.67 jurisdiction challenges.

Treaty and enforcement frameworks (ICSID Convention, New York Convention, ECT, sovereign immunity) are covered in facts/t-arbitration-treaties.md and are not repeated here.

## Verified facts

### ICC (Paris; International Court of Arbitration)

1. The ICC Rules of Arbitration in force entered into force on 1 January 2021 and govern cases received by the ICC Court from that date. Where to find the current rule: iccwbo.org, "2021 Arbitration Rules". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Third-party funding (art. 11(7)):** each party must promptly disclose to the Secretariat, the tribunal and the other parties the existence and identity of any non-party that has an arrangement to fund claims or defences and has an economic interest in the outcome. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Equal treatment in constitution (art. 12(9)):** notwithstanding any agreement on the method of constitution, in exceptional circumstances the Court may appoint each member of the tribunal to avoid a significant risk of unequal treatment and unfairness that may affect the validity of the award. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **Treaty cases (art. 13(6)):** where the arbitration agreement arises from a treaty, no arbitrator may have the same nationality as any party unless the parties agree otherwise. The emergency arbitrator provisions do not apply to treaty-based arbitration agreements (art. 29(6)(c)). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. **Counsel changes (art. 17(2)):** once constituted, the tribunal may take any measure necessary to avoid an arbitrator conflict of interest arising from a change in party representation, including excluding new representatives in whole or in part. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. **Remote hearings (art. 26(1)):** after consulting the parties, the tribunal may decide that a hearing will be held in person or remotely by videoconference, telephone or other appropriate means. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. **Expedited procedure (Appendix VI):**
   - The opt-out threshold is US$3,000,000 for arbitration agreements concluded on or after 1 January 2021. It is US$2,000,000 for agreements concluded between 1 March 2017 and 1 January 2021.
   - The Court may appoint a sole arbitrator notwithstanding any contrary provision of the arbitration agreement.
   - The standard time limit for the final award is six months (art. 31(1)).

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. **Multi-contract claims and consolidation:** claims arising out of more than one contract may be made in a single arbitration whether under one or several arbitration agreements under the Rules (art. 9), and the Court may consolidate pending ICC arbitrations (art. 10). [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### LCIA (London)

9. The LCIA Arbitration Rules 2020 are effective 1 October 2020. Where to find the current rule: lcia.org. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
10. **Early Determination (art. 22.1(viii)):** the tribunal may determine that any claim, defence, counterclaim or cross-claim is manifestly outside its jurisdiction, inadmissible or manifestly without merit, and issue an order or award to that effect. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
11. **Consolidation and concurrent conduct (art. 22A):** the tribunal may, with LCIA Court approval, consolidate arbitrations or order that they be conducted concurrently. Composite Requests and Responses covering several arbitrations are allowed (arts 1.2, 2.2). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
12. **Emergency arbitrator (art. 9B):** available before the tribunal is formed, but not if the arbitration agreement was concluded before 1 October 2014 without written opt-in, or if the parties have opted out. Recourse to state courts for interim measures remains available (art. 9.13). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
13. **Award timing (art. 15.10):** the tribunal must endeavour to make its final award no later than three months after the last submission. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

### UNCITRAL

14. UNCITRAL lists the versions of its Arbitration Rules as:
    - 1976;
    - 2010 (revised);
    - 2013, which adds art. 1(4) incorporating the Rules on Transparency for arbitrations under investment treaties concluded on or after 1 April 2014 and is otherwise unchanged from 2010;
    - 2021, which adds art. 1(5) incorporating the UNCITRAL Expedited Arbitration Rules as an appendix.

    The words "where the parties so agree" in art. 1(5) mean the Expedited Rules need the parties' express consent. Where to find the current rule: uncitral.un.org. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
15. The UNCITRAL Rules are used in purely ad hoc proceedings, investor-State and State-to-State disputes, and commercial cases administered by institutions. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### ICSID (Washington; World Bank Group)

16. ICSID member States approved amendments to the ICSID rules on 21 March 2022. The 2022 Regulations and Rules took effect on 1 July 2022. The applicable Institution Rules are those in effect on the date the request is filed. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
17. Key features of the 2022 amendments, per ICSID:
    - mandatory timeframes for orders and awards;
    - new expedited arbitration rules, which ICSID says would cut case time in half where the parties adopt them;
    - new mediation and fact-finding rules;
    - Additional Facility access where one or both parties is not an ICSID Contracting State, and for regional economic integration organisations such as the EU;
    - greater publication of orders and awards;
    - for the first time, an ongoing duty to disclose third-party funding, including the funder's name and address. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

### SIAC (Singapore)

18. SIAC released the SIAC Rules 2025 (7th edition) on 9 December 2024. They came into force on 1 January 2025. The siac.org.sg rules page returned HTTP 403 when checked, so these facts come from two law-firm summaries. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
19. **Streamlined Procedure (r.13, Schedule 2):** applies if the amount in dispute is below S$1 million (unless the SIAC President decides otherwise on a party's application) or if the parties agree before the tribunal is constituted. The award is due within three months of constitution. Tribunal and SIAC administrative fees are capped at 50% of the maximum in the Schedule of Fees. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
20. **Expedited Procedure (r.14, Schedule 3):** the threshold rose from S$6 million to S$10 million (approximately US$7.4–7.5 million). The award is due within six months of constitution. Per Reed Smith, the Streamlined and Expedited Procedures expressly override inconsistent terms in the arbitration agreement. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
21. **Preliminary Determination (r.46):** any issue can be determined finally at a preliminary stage if the parties agree, if doing so is likely to save time and costs, or if the circumstances otherwise warrant it. The decision is due within 90 days of the application. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
22. **Third-party funding (r.38):** parties must disclose any funding agreement and the funder's identity and contact details. The tribunal may order further disclosure of the funder's interest in the outcome and whether it has committed to adverse costs. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
23. **Emergency arbitrator (r.12, Schedule 1):** an emergency arbitrator can be appointed before the Notice of Arbitration is filed (per Reed Smith, the Notice must then follow within seven days). Parties may apply ex parte for a protective preliminary order. The emergency arbitrator must decide on it within 24 hours of appointment, and per Reed Smith it expires after 14 days. [confidence: high for pre-notice and 24 hours (two sources); medium for 7 and 14 days (one source)] [source: 6, 7]
24. **Coordinated Proceedings (r.17):** where the same tribunal is constituted in two or more arbitrations raising a common question of law or fact, a party may apply for them to be conducted together, heard with aligned procedures, or for one to be suspended pending another. This is designed for related-contract disputes that cannot be consolidated. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### English Arbitration Act 2025

25. The Arbitration Act 2025 (2025 c. 4) received Royal Assent on 24 February 2025. It amends the Arbitration Act 1996 and extends to England and Wales and Northern Ireland. Sections 16–18 came into force on Royal Assent. The rest came into force on 1 August 2025 under the Arbitration Act 2025 (Commencement) Regulations 2025 (S.I. 2025/905, made 16 July 2025). [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9, 10]
26. **Transitional rule (s.17(4)):** the amendments do not apply to:
    - arbitral proceedings commenced before the relevant section came into force;
    - court proceedings connected with those arbitrations;
    - other court proceedings commenced before that date.

    Otherwise they apply to arbitration agreements whenever made. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
27. **Law of the arbitration agreement (new 1996 Act s.6A, inserted by 2025 Act s.1):** the arbitration agreement is governed by the law the parties expressly agree applies to it or, failing that, the law of the seat. A choice of law for the main contract is not, by itself, an express choice for the arbitration agreement. The rule does not apply to arbitration agreements derived from standing offers in treaties or in non-UK legislation (investor-State cases). [confidence: high] [source: 12]
28. **Duty of disclosure (new s.23A, inserted by s.2):**
    - Prospective and appointed arbitrators must disclose, as soon as reasonably practicable, circumstances that might reasonably give rise to justifiable doubts about their impartiality.
    - They are treated as aware of what they ought reasonably to be aware of.
    - The duty is mandatory (added to Schedule 1).

    [confidence: high] [source: 13]
29. **Summary awards (new s.39A, inserted by s.7):** unless the parties agree otherwise, the tribunal may, on a party's application, make an award on a summary basis on a claim or issue if a party has no real prospect of succeeding on the claim or issue, or in defending it. The parties must be given a reasonable opportunity to make representations first. [confidence: high] [source: 14]
30. **Emergency arbitrators (new s.41A, inserted by s.8):** where the parties have agreed to rules that provide for an emergency arbitrator, the emergency arbitrator may make a peremptory order if a party fails without sufficient cause to comply with its order. The court may enforce that order under s.42. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
31. **Section 67 jurisdiction challenges (s.11):** rules of court may provide that, where a party that took part in the arbitration challenges a jurisdiction ruling, then unless the court rules otherwise in the interests of justice:
    - new grounds may not be raised unless they could not have been discovered with reasonable diligence;
    - new evidence may not be considered unless it could not with reasonable diligence have been put before the tribunal;
    - evidence the tribunal heard is not re-heard.

    [confidence: high] [source: 16]
32. Other sections of the 2025 Act (from its table of contents):
    - arbitrator immunity on removal applications and on resignation (ss.3–4);
    - court determination of jurisdiction (s.5);
    - power to award costs despite no substantive jurisdiction (s.6);
    - court powers in support of arbitration against third parties (s.9);
    - remedies and time limits on challenges (ss.10, 12);
    - appeals (s.13);
    - repeal of the unused domestic arbitration agreement provisions (s.15). [confidence: high] [source: 8]

## Timeline

## Financing and structure details

- **Project document suites:** EPC, O&M, offtake, shareholder and finance documents often choose different rules or seats. The tools most relevant to multi-contract project disputes are:
  - ICC art. 9 (multi-contract claims) and art. 10 (consolidation);
  - LCIA art. 22A;
  - SIAC r.17 (coordinated proceedings).

  Drafters still need compatible clauses (same rules, compatible seats) for these to work [source: 1, 2, 6].
- **Funder disclosure:** ICC, ICSID and SIAC all now require third-party funding to be disclosed. This matters for sponsors that fund claims through litigation funders or monetise awards [source: 1, 4, 6].
- **Seat in England:** after 1 August 2025, a contract governed by, say, New York law with a London seat will, absent an express choice for the arbitration clause, have an arbitration agreement governed by English law (s.6A) [source: 12].

## What went wrong or right, and why

- ICSID described its 2022 package as designed to reduce time and cost, increase transparency and enhance disclosure, after a multi-year process involving State officials and users [source: 4].
- Latham & Watkins described the SIAC Streamlined and Expedited changes as aimed at lower-value disputes and efficiency. It also noted that SIAC's funding disclosure goes further than HKIAC 2024 art. 44, which requires only the existence of the funding agreement and the funder's identity [source: 6].
- The 2025 Act's s.6A default (law of the seat) replaces, for English-seated arbitrations, the common-law approach under which the main-contract law usually governed the arbitration clause. The leading case was the UK Supreme Court's 2020 *Enka v Chubb* decision. That characterisation of prior law is standard commentary and was not separately checked here.

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 10 (law for financiers):** Arbitration rules are a menu, not boilerplate. Show how picking ICC 2021 versus LCIA 2020 versus SIAC 2025 changes concrete outcomes: the expedited threshold, early or summary disposal, and funder disclosure. Note that the law of an English-seated arbitration agreement now defaults to English law unless chosen expressly.
- **Ch 54 (governing law and disputes):** Build the dispute clause for Case P by matching rules to enforcement (link to facts/t-arbitration-treaties.md). Use consolidation and coordination tools for the multi-contract project. Choose ICSID 2022 or UNCITRAL 2013/2021 for investor-State claims. Draft an express choice of law for the arbitration agreement. Remember that ICC emergency arbitrator provisions do not apply to treaty claims.

## Do not state

- The SIAC rule numbers and details beyond those above (for example, the 90-day draft-award deadline) without checking the SIAC Rules 2025 text. siac.org.sg returned HTTP 403 and was not read directly.
- An exact effective date for the UNCITRAL Expedited Arbitration Rules (often given as 19 September 2021). Not verified here.
- Any new ICC, LCIA or ICSID rules revision after those listed. None appeared on the institutions' pages in October 2026, but recheck before publication.
- That the Arbitration Act 2025 applies in Scotland. Scotland has its own Arbitration (Scotland) Act 2010.
- Caseload statistics for any institution. Not gathered for this sheet.
