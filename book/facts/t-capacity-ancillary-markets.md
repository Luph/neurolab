# Capacity and ancillary-service markets: GB Capacity Market (T-4), PJM Base Residual Auctions 2025/26–2028/29, ERCOT ancillary services (RRS, ECRS)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: PJM 2028/2029 BRA report, 14 July 2026; DESNZ auction-parameters letter to NESO, 14 July 2026; Potomac Economics 2025 State of the Market Report for ERCOT, June 2026; GB T-4 auction for 2029/30, 10 March 2026)

Cross-references: for ERCOT pricing during Winter Storm Uri and the post-Uri offer cap, use facts/winter-storm-uri.md. For LDES and interconnector revenue floors, use facts/t-cap-and-floor.md.

## Summary

Capacity markets pay for megawatts that are available when the system is stressed, separately from payment for energy. In Great Britain the Capacity Market runs descending-clock auctions four years ahead (T-4) and one year ahead (T-1) of each delivery year. Multi-year agreements for new build make the GB T-4 a bankable revenue line for batteries, gas peakers and demand response. T-4 clearing prices rose from about £16–18/kW/year (delivery years 2023/24–2024/25) to £63–65/kW/year (2026/27–2027/28) and £60 (2028/29). They then fell to £27.10/kW/year in the March 2026 auction for 2029/30, which procured 40.1 GW. PJM's Base Residual Auction (BRA) moved the other way. The RTO price jumped from $28.92/MW-day (2024/25) to $269.92 (2025/26). It then cleared at a negotiated cap in three successive auctions: $329.17 (2026/27), $333.44 (2027/28) and $325.00 (2028/29). The last two cleared about 6.5–6.8 GW (UCAP) short of the reliability requirement, at a 14.4% reserve margin against a 20% target, largely because of data-centre load growth. Texas (ERCOT) has no capacity market. Scarcity value instead comes through energy prices and ancillary services: regulation, Responsive Reserve Service (RRS), ERCOT Contingency Reserve Service (ECRS, introduced June 2023) and Non-Spinning Reserve (NSRS). The market monitor estimated that ECRS deployment practices cost about $12 billion in 2023, falling to $720 million in 2024 and $90 million in 2025. On 5 December 2025 ERCOT went live with real-time co-optimisation (RTC). As batteries flooded the ancillary markets (over 17,000 MW and 31,500 MWh by end-2025), ancillary-service costs fell to $0.39/MWh of load in 2025, and battery revenue per kW fell 37%. Ancillary revenue is a shallow, quickly saturating pool, and project lenders should not treat it as a long-term base case.

## Verified facts

### A. Great Britain Capacity Market (current rules: Capacity Market Rules and Regulations; results via NESO as EMR Delivery Body and DESNZ annual scheme updates)

1. Purpose and design: providers compete in auctions for Capacity Market Agreements and receive payments to be available at times of system stress, with penalties for non-delivery. Auctions are held one year (T-1) and four years (T-4) ahead of delivery. Existing generation, new build, demand-side response and interconnectors compete. Auctions use descending-clock bidding rounds from the price cap down. As at December 2025, DESNZ was consulting on a "Multiple Price Capacity Market" with a higher price cap for new-build dispatchable, enduring units from the 2026 prequalification window. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. T-4 clearing prices (GBP per kW of de-rated capacity per year, nominal; auction name, date, delivery year, capacity awarded):
   - 2019 T-4, 5 March 2020, for 2023/24: £15.97; 43,749 MW
   - 2020 T-4, 9 March 2021, for 2024/25: £18.00; 40,820 MW
   - 2022 T-4, 21 February 2023, for 2026/27: £63.00; 43,001 MW
   - 2023 T-4, 27 February 2024, for 2027/28: £65.00; 42,831 MW
   - 2024 T-4, 11 March 2025, for 2028/29: £60.00; 43,055 MW
   - 2025 T-4, 10 March 2026, for 2029/30: £27.10; 40,108.6 MW across 691 units (clearing round 10)
   [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]
3. The 2028/29 T-4 (March 2025) had 44.7 GW enter, of which 96.4% won agreements. Unabated gas secured 27.3 GW; batteries 1.8 GW (new build); DSR 1.8 GW; pumped hydro 1.6 GW; nuclear 0.9 GW; interconnectors about 6.6–6.8 GW (DESNZ gives both figures in the same document). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. T-1 results: 2024/25 cleared at £35.79/kW/year (20 February 2024, 7.64 GW); 2025/26 at £20/kW/year (12 March 2025, 7.9 GW); 2026/27 auction ended 4 March 2026, procuring 7,192 MW across 195 units (price not verified). [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2, 9]
5. Refurbishing plant: the capex threshold for "refurbishing" three-year agreements was lowered to £65/kW for units prequalifying in 2025, indexed thereafter. New or substantially refurbishing combustion plant in the 2026 T-4 (for 2029/30) must commit to a credible decarbonisation plan (hydrogen-to-power or CCUS). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. The next auctions (T-1 for 2027/28 and T-4 for 2030/31) are to be held in early 2027, under parameters set in the Minister's letter to NESO of 14 July 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

8. 2025/26 BRA (report 30 July 2024): RTO $269.92; BGE $466.35 and Dominion $444.26 (constrained zones). Drivers PJM cited: a 3,243 MW load forecast increase; the installed reserve margin rising from 14.7% to 17.8%; retirements; and the move to marginal ELCC accreditation (FERC-approved Critical Issue Fast Path changes). Offered supply fell 13,252 MW. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
9. 2026/27 BRA (report 22 July 2025): every zone cleared at the cap of $329.17/MW-day UCAP. A temporary cap of $256.75/MW-day ICAP and floor of $138.25/MW-day ICAP was applied to the VRR curve for 2026/27 and 2027/28 (later extended to 2028/29). Total procured including FRR was 146,244 MW, 139 MW above the reliability requirement. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
10. 2027/28 BRA (report 17 December 2025): cleared at the cap of $333.44; 809.6 MW UCAP did not clear because it was offered above the cap. Total procured was 6,516.6 MW UCAP below the reliability requirement, at a 14.4% IRM against 20%. A shortfall of more than one percentage point for three consecutive BRAs triggers a Reliability Backstop Auction under the tariff. [confidence: high] [source: 14]
11. 2028/29 BRA (report 14 July 2026): cleared at the cap of $325.00 (cap/floor about $325/$175 per MW-day UCAP, filed in FERC docket ER25-1357). Total procured was 6,831.3 MW UCAP below the requirement: the second consecutive shortfall of more than 1 point. The load forecast rose by 1,374.5 MW, "largely due to additional Large Loads". New generation and uprates were only 524.7 MW UCAP. The RTO failed the three-pivotal-supplier test, so offers from existing generation were mitigated. PJM is working on Critical Issue Fast Path processes for large-load additions and reliability backstop procurement. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

### C. ERCOT ancillary services (current rules: ERCOT Nodal Protocols and annual AS Methodology; independent market monitor Potomac Economics)

12. Products (since June 2023): Regulation Up/Down (responds to load-frequency control signals every four seconds); RRS (responds within 10 minutes to restore frequency after contingencies; sub-types are primary frequency response, under-frequency relay load, and fast frequency response, mostly from batteries within 30 cycles); ECRS (restores frequency within 10 minutes of a significant deviation, recovers deployed regulation, and covers intra-hour net-load forecast uncertainty; online or offline units); NSRS (responds within 30 minutes). Costs are allocated to load-serving entities by real-time load ratio share. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
13. ECRS was introduced in June 2023. Because operators released it only after ten consecutive minutes of at least 40 MW under-generation, it created artificial scarcity. The IMM estimated excess real-time costs of about $12 billion in 2023, $720 million in 2024 and $90 million in 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
14. Real-time co-optimisation (RTC) went live on 5 December 2025. Energy and ancillary services are now procured together in real time. Day-ahead ancillary awards became financial positions settled against real-time prices, and virtual ancillary-service positions were introduced. The ORDC price adder (since 2014) was discontinued. ECRS duration fell from two hours to one hour (NPRR 1282). NSRS remained at four hours. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
15. 2025 outcomes: ancillary-service costs were $0.39/MWh of load (down 60% from 2024, the lowest since 2020). The ORDC adder added only $0.02/MWh. ESR capacity exceeded 17,000 MW and 31,500 MWh at end-2025, up almost 80%. Batteries provided 94% of Reg-Up, 86% of Reg-Down, 51% of RRS, 42% of ECRS and 24% of NSRS. Battery revenue per unit of capacity fell about 37%: ancillary revenue fell more than 37% while net energy-arbitrage revenue rose 57%. The IMM considers ERCOT's AS Methodology to procure more than double the reserves needed for a reasonable reliability standard, including about 2 GW with virtually no reliability value. [confidence: high] [source: 15]

## Financing and structure details

- GB: a multi-year T-4 agreement (new build) is a fixed £/kW/year stream with penalty exposure during stress events. Lenders treat it as contracted revenue but stress de-rating factors, penalties and the risk of termination for late delivery. The fall from £60 to £27.10 shows how sensitive new-build economics are to the auction price in a single year.
- PJM: BRA revenue is one delivery year at a time, set three years ahead. It is not a long-term contract, so lenders hedge or haircut it. The 2026/27–2028/29 cap and floor ($325–$333 and about $175–$177 per MW-day UCAP) bound the outcome for those years only.
- ERCOT: no capacity payment; ancillary revenue is short-term and was falling in 2025. Battery projects typically rely on tolls or floors (see facts/t-corporate-ppa-market.md, item 8, for tolling trends).

## What went wrong or right, and why

- PJM's price spike and shortfalls: PJM's own reports cite rising load forecasts (large loads), a higher reserve target, retirements, ELCC accreditation and slow new entry (new generation of 774 MW UCAP in 2027/28 and 524.7 MW UCAP in 2028/29). The temporary cap limited prices but, in PJM's words, "a price cap below the established VRR Curve can reduce the amount of investment." [source: 11, 14]
- ERCOT: the IMM blames ECRS deployment rules for the 2023 cost spike and credits new solar and storage, then RTC, for reducing it. [source: 15]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 11: Use the three markets to explain "missing money" and the three designs that fill it: a centralised long-term capacity auction (GB), a three-year-forward annual auction (PJM) and energy-only with scarcity pricing and ancillary services (ERCOT).
- Ch 20: Show capacity and ancillary revenue as layers in a merchant revenue stack. Contrast a GB multi-year agreement (debt-sizable) with PJM one-year prices and ERCOT ancillary prices, which fell as batteries saturated the market (battery revenue per kW down 37% in 2025). Teach haircutting: lenders should not extrapolate 2023 ECRS or 2025/26–2028/29 PJM capped prices.
- Ch 73: For storage, ancillary services were the first revenue source and saturated first. Use ERCOT's 17 GW of batteries and the ECRS duration change (two hours to one) to show how product rules change which battery durations earn revenue. Use GB T-4 battery awards and de-rating to discuss duration and capacity credit.
