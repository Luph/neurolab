# Duqm refinery (OQ8), Oman: a project-financed export refinery

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: OQ's 2024 annual report and financial statements, published 2025, recording the refinery's inauguration in February 2024 and continuing shareholder-loan support)

## Summary

The Duqm refinery, now operated by OQ8 (formerly Duqm Refinery and Petrochemical Industries Company LLC, DRPIC), is a greenfield 230,000 barrel-per-day export refinery in the Duqm Special Economic Zone on Oman's Arabian Sea coast. It is a 50/50 joint venture between Oman's state energy group OQ and Kuwait Petroleum Europe B.V., a subsidiary of Kuwait Petroleum Corporation. The project was split into three EPC packages (process units; utilities and offsites; crude pipeline, crude storage at Ras Markaz and product export facilities at Duqm port) and was financed with a multi-ECA project financing, including UK Export Finance (UKEF) insurance of approximately GBP 700 million, alongside KEXIM, K-SURE and Spain's CESCE. Construction ran late: OQ reported 89% progress at end-2021, 11% behind schedule, mainly because of COVID-19 constraints. The refinery was inaugurated in February 2024. OQ's statements show that the sponsors kept injecting subordinated shareholder loans through 2021–2024, and OQ recorded net losses from OQ8 in 2024. The headline total of the 2018 debt package is widely reported but was not verified from a primary source for this sheet (see "Do not state"). The case is useful for teaching an export refinery that is project-financed but sponsor-supported: national-oil-company sponsors, ECA cover tied to EPC contractors' nationalities, separately financed associated infrastructure, and sponsor support that continues after completion.

## Verified facts

1. OQ8 is a joint venture between OQ and Kuwait Petroleum Europe B.V. (KPE), based in Duqm. It was designed to process around 230,000 barrels of crude oil per day, with diesel, jet fuel, naphtha, LPG, sulphur and petcoke as its main products. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. OQ describes the venture as 50/50 with Kuwait Petroleum (Europe), a wholly owned subsidiary of Kuwait Petroleum Corporation, Kuwait's national oil company. OQ's financial statements record a 50% interest in OQ8, accounted for as a joint venture. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
3. UKEF's January 2018 Category A disclosure described the project as a greenfield 230,000 bpd refinery with on-site utilities at Duqm, oil storage at Ras Markaz, an approximately 80 km crude pipeline from Ras Markaz to Duqm and a product export terminal at the Port of Duqm. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
4. UKEF's May 2019 "Category A project supported" notice states that UKEF agreed to provide insurance for the design, construction and operation of the refinery. Construction was divided into three EPC packages: (1) refinery process units; (2) refinery utilities and offsite equipment; (3) crude oil pipeline, crude storage and Duqm port export facilities. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
5. The same notice names the other ECAs involved in the financing as K-EXIM and K-SURE (South Korea) and CESCE (Spain). It names the borrower and sponsor entity as DRPIC, the UK exporter as Petrofac International Limited, and the export credit agent bank as Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation Europe Ltd. It gives the principal value of UKEF support as approximately GBP 700 million. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
6. UKEF screened the project as Category A under the OECD Common Approaches in September 2017. It reviewed the project against the 2012 IFC Performance Standards and the World Bank Group EHS Guidelines, including the 2016 Petroleum Refining guideline. It listed associated facilities, among them the Duqm Integrated Water and Power Plant (supplying the refinery), a gas spur line and wider port and storage facilities. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. OQ's 2021 annual report put overall project progress at 89% at end-2021, 11% behind schedule, primarily because of constraints from the COVID-19 pandemic. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. OQ's FY2022 investor presentation reported OQ8 project progress of 96.6%. [confidence: medium] (the presentation's figure layout makes it harder to tie each percentage to a project) [source: 6]
9. A separately financed OQ group entity signed a facilities agreement on 8 April 2020 for up to RO 134.8 million (USD 350 million) to build crude handling facilities. The term is 10 years, interest is US LIBOR plus a margin, and repayment is in 14 semi-annual instalments plus a balloon. Repayment starts in April 2023 or 12 months after the refinery's commercial operation date, whichever is earlier. USD 319.5 million had been drawn at 31 December 2022. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
10. OQ (the parent) increased its investment in OQ8 through subordinated shareholder loans: RO 115.0 million in 2021, RO 73 million in 2022, RO 331 million in 2023 and RO 167 million in 2024. OQ's 2024 statements describe the 2024 loan as repayable at OQ8's discretion. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
11. OQ's 2024 annual report says the OQ8 refinery was inaugurated in February 2024. It also reports that OQ disbursed a further shareholder loan of USD 434 million to DRPIC (OQ8) in 2024, primarily for project-related expenditure, and that net losses from OQ8 weighed on group downstream results in 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
12. In 2024, OTTCO, the group crude terminal company, impaired its tank terminal assets. The facility had been built with more capacity than OQ8 required, and management could not secure new customers. Recoverable value was RO 157.0 million on a value-in-use basis at a 9.5% pre-tax WACC. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

## Financing and structure details

- Borrower: DRPIC/OQ8, a 50/50 joint venture of OQ and Kuwait Petroleum Europe. [source: 1, 2, 5]
- ECA participation (per UKEF): UKEF insurance of approximately GBP 700 million linked to Petrofac's EPC scope, with SMBC Europe as the ECA agent bank; KEXIM and K-SURE (Korea) and CESCE (Spain) also involved. The ECA mix follows the nationalities of the EPC contractors and suppliers. UKEF's cover was tied to a UK exporter. [source: 5]
- Associated infrastructure: crude handling and storage (Ras Markaz) and the crude terminal sit in other OQ group companies. They have their own financing (USD 350 million facility, April 2020) and repayment linked to the refinery's commercial operation date, which creates cross-project dependency. [source: 3]
- Sponsor support: there has been repeated subordinated shareholder lending from OQ (2021–2024). OQ's 2024 loan is repayable at OQ8's discretion, which makes it in effect quasi-equity. [source: 2, 3, 7]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Schedule: OQ itself attributed the 11% schedule slippage at end-2021 primarily to COVID-19 constraints. [source: 1]
- Sponsor dependence: OQ's statements show continuing subordinated shareholder loans and, in 2024, net losses at OQ8. The financing was non-recourse in form but relied in practice on deep-pocketed national oil company sponsors to absorb delay, cost and margin risk. This reading follows OQ's own disclosures. Lender-side analysis was not available.
- Associated-facility risk: OQ's impairment note says that OTTCO's terminal was oversized relative to OQ8's needs and could not attract third-party customers, a reminder that infrastructure built for one anchor project carries that project's volume risk. [source: 2]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 77 (Refining, petrochemicals and manufacturing finance): Use Duqm to show how an export refinery is financed. The ECA stack mirrors the EPC split (UK, Korean and Spanish exporters), and the national oil company sponsors supply crude-linked support and absorb overruns through subordinated loans. Contrast it with Northvolt (facts/northvolt.md), where sponsor support ran out. Discuss how refining margin risk differs from a tolling or contracted power project, why lenders lean on sponsor quality, and why associated facilities (crude pipeline, storage, terminal, water and power) need interface and timing protections.

## Do not state

- Do not state the total size of the 2018 debt package (widely reported as approximately USD 4.6 billion), the number of lenders, the Islamic tranche, tenors or pricing. Not verified from a primary source here.
- Do not state the final project cost, the date of first crude or commercial operation (only the February 2024 inauguration is verified), or Kuwait Petroleum's shareholder loans.
- Do not state that JBIC participated. No JBIC press release on Duqm was found in JBIC's 2017–2019 lists.
- Do not state OQ8's offtake or crude supply arrangements in detail (OQ reports mention crude supply through Ras Markaz, but contract terms were not seen).
- Dangote refinery (Nigeria): its financing (Afreximbank and Nigerian bank facilities, NNPC stake) was not researched for this sheet. Do not use it as a substitute case without separate verification.
- KEXIM and K-SURE sites were unreachable from this research environment. Their amounts are unverified.
