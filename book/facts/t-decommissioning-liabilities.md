# Decommissioning obligations and financial security: offshore wind, oil and gas, mining reclamation, nuclear

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: BOEM proposed rule on oil and gas financial assurance, 9 March 2026, comment period extended to May 2026; no final rule found by 3 October 2026)

## Summary

Every regime for long-lived energy and extractive assets answers the same three questions. Who must decommission? How much security must be posted? When must it be posted? The answers move risk between the owner, its lenders and the taxpayer. In US federal waters, BOEM's 2024 Renewable Energy Modernization Rule (effective 15 July 2024) lets offshore wind lessees post decommissioning financial assurance before installation rather than at plan approval, and lets them fund it incrementally on a BOEM-approved schedule. BOEM may require full funding before PPAs or renewable energy credits expire. For US offshore oil and gas, BOEM's April 2024 rule (effective 24 June 2024) codified the use of BSEE's probabilistic decommissioning estimates and was expected to require about USD 6.9 billion of new supplemental assurance. A March 2026 proposed rule would reverse much of this, cutting the burden by about USD 6.2 billion. In the UK, offshore renewables are governed by the Energy Act 2004 (sections 105ff, decommissioning programmes). The government guidance (March 2019) expects ring-fenced security in favour of the Secretary of State, either upfront or accrued mid-life, for example starting by year 10 and complete by year 15 for a 15-year CfD. Parent company guarantees are not normally accepted. UK oil and gas decommissioning is governed by Part IV of the Petroleum Act 1998, with "section 29 notices" that can reach parent companies. Security is usually provided through Decommissioning Security Agreements (DSAs) in cash, letters of credit or bonds from A-/A3-rated issuers, sized post-tax where a Decommissioning Relief Deed applies, plus a 10–30% risk factor. US surface coal mining bonds must be sufficient for the regulator to complete reclamation itself, with phased release (60% after backfilling and regrading). BLM hardrock guarantees must cover third-party reclamation cost, plus trust funds for long-term water treatment. US nuclear licensees must assure decommissioning funds by formula (USD 105 million for a large PWR in January 1986 dollars, escalated), using prepayment, external sinking funds or surety methods.

## Verified facts

### A. US offshore wind (BOEM, 30 CFR part 585)

1. The Renewable Energy Modernization Rule (BOEM and BSEE) was published on 15 May 2024 (89 FR 42602) and took effect on 15 July 2024. Among other changes it "tailors financial assurance requirements and instruments". [confidence: high] [source: 1]

2. Current 30 CFR 585.516(a) staging (rule text):
   - Before BOEM will execute a commercial lease or approve an assignment: a bond or other financial assurance equal to 12 months' rent. This replaced the previous USD 100,000 lease-specific bond.
   - Before installing facilities approved in a Site Assessment Plan (SAP): supplemental assurance based on the anticipated decommissioning costs of those facilities.
   - Before installing facilities approved in a Construction and Operations Plan (COP): supplemental assurance based on the anticipated decommissioning costs. "If you propose to incrementally fund your financial assurance instrument, BOEM must approve the schedule."
   The rule removed the assurance previously due at COP approval. BOEM explained that liability accrues with installation, not with approval. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

3. BOEM finalized changes allowing decommissioning trust accounts (585.529) to be incrementally funded under a BOEM-approved schedule. It reserved the right to change the amount and timing, and noted it may require an account to be fully funded before PPAs or offshore renewable energy credits expire. BOEM argued that low operating costs relative to revenue make funding over time from revenue reasonable. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

4. Under 585.527, BOEM may accept financial strength in lieu of security if the lessee has an investment-grade issuer credit rating from an SEC-recognized NRSRO (using the highest rating where they differ), or a BOEM-determined proxy credit rating based on audited financials. The supplemental amount under 585.517 covers 12 months' rent and other payments not already covered, past-due payments, other monetary obligations and the estimated cost of facility decommissioning. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

5. Release (585.534): BOEM generally holds lease-specific assurance until seven years after all operations, including decommissioning and site clearance, have ceased, or longer if litigation requires. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### B. US offshore oil and gas (BOEM, 30 CFR part 556)

6. "Risk Management and Financial Assurance for OCS Lease and Grant Obligations", final rule, 89 FR 31544, published 24 April 2024 and effective 24 June 2024 (corrected date). It streamlined the financial-health criteria, codified the use of BSEE's probabilistic decommissioning cost estimates for setting supplemental assurance demands, and removed restrictions on third-party guarantees and decommissioning accounts. BOEM estimated it would require about USD 6.9 billion of new supplemental financial assurance. It does not apply to renewables. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

7. On 9 March 2026 BOEM proposed amendments (91 FR 11212), citing E.O. 14154 (20 January 2025) and Secretarial Order 3418. The proposal would return to considering the financial strength of jointly liable predecessor lessees, revise the credit-rating threshold, revise the decommissioning estimate used for sizing, and revise the IBLA appeal-bond provision. BOEM estimated it would reduce the financial burden by about USD 6.2 billion. The comment period was extended by seven days (notice of 8 May 2026). No final rule was found in the Federal Register as of 3 October 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### C. UK offshore renewables (Energy Act 2004)

8. Decommissioning of offshore renewable energy installations (OREIs) in England and Wales falls under the Energy Act 2004. The Secretary of State serves a section 105 notice requiring a decommissioning programme, which may be served on multiple parties. Guidance is "Decommissioning of offshore renewable energy installations under the Energy Act 2004: guidance notes for industry (England and Wales)", March 2019 (BEIS, now DESNZ), which updated the 2011 guidance after a 2018 consultation. Scottish waters are handled by Marine Scotland (now Marine Directorate). [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]

9. Policy. The Secretary of State is the "decommissioner of last resort". Security must be ring-fenced for decommissioning, and the Secretary of State must be the sole beneficiary. Cash reserved in the owner's own accounts, even if segregated, is not acceptable. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

10. Acceptable security: upfront cash in an escrow or trust account or paid to BEIS; cash reserving into such an account; and standby letters of credit, bank guarantees or performance bonds. The issuer must be a UK branch of an OECD bank or a UK/EEA-authorised insurer rated at least A- (S&P) or A3 (Moody's). The instrument must be irrevocable and on demand, payable within 5 business days, denominated in GBP, and governed by English law (ISP98 or URDG 758). It must be renewed at least one month before expiry, with "pay or renew" for short terms (typically 1–3 years). [confidence: high] [source: 4]

11. Parent company guarantees are "not normally accepted" (except perhaps as very short-term or secondary security). Insurance is generally not expected to be acceptable. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

12. Timing. Upfront security is generally expected for pre-commercial projects. For large commercial projects with a predictable revenue stream (CfD or OFTO) and proven technology, a segregated fund accruing early or mid-life may be acceptable, but it must be fully accrued by the end of the subsidy period, and late accrual is not acceptable. The guidance's table sets: Renewables Obligation projects, accrual starting no later than year 10 and complete by year 20; 15-year CfD projects, starting no later than year 10 and complete by year 15; OFTOs with 20-year licences, starting no later than year 10 and complete by year 20. If mid-life accrual is not justified, security is required upfront of construction, or no later than 3 months after programme approval. Full security must be maintained if decommissioning is deferred, including through life extension or repowering. [confidence: high (2019 guidance; CfD terms have since lengthened to 20 years for some technologies in later rounds, which the 2019 table does not address)] [source: 4]

### D. UK offshore oil and gas (Petroleum Act 1998, Part IV)

13. OPRED (Offshore Petroleum Regulator for Environment and Decommissioning, now within DESNZ) administers Part IV of the Petroleum Act 1998. Owners must submit a decommissioning programme. The main guidance is the "Offshore Oil and Gas Decommissioning Guidance Notes" (November 2018). [confidence: high] [source: 6]

14. OPRED's financial assurance guidance ("Assessing the financial capability of offshore oil and gas companies to deliver decommissioning obligations", April 2019) describes two statutory tools. The first is section 29 notices, which can be given to a wide class of persons, including parent or associated companies (s.30(1)(e) for installations and s.30(2)(c) for pipelines). The second is the power under section 38(4) to require financial security, normally through a decommissioning security agreement (DSA). Sellers' section 29 notices are usually not withdrawn after a transfer, so former owners remain potentially liable. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

15. DSA minimum requirements where the Secretary of State is party. Security must be cash, irrevocable standby letters of credit from a prime bank, or on-demand performance bonds from prime banks or FSMA-regulated insurers. Issuers must be established in an EU or OECD country, have a UK office, and be rated A- or better (S&P/Fitch) or A3 or better (Moody's). The DSA is full-field. If a Decommissioning Relief Deed is in place and there is relevant tax history, security is sized post-tax. A risk factor adding "around 10–30%" is usual, and ongoing site clear-up security of 1–3% of total cost is required after main removal. Allowance may be made for post-tax future revenues where the field is producing. Instruments renew annually, two months before each period. Failure to renew triggers a draw into a trust fund. Cost and reserves estimates must be audited at least every 3 years by an OPRED-approved independent expert. The DSA must be independent of the JOA. A template DSA exists through Oil & Gas UK (now OEUK). [confidence: high] [source: 7]

16. Parent company guarantees are "not an acceptable form of decommissioning security" in DSAs, because they are not irrevocable and on demand and can be disputed. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

17. The Energy Act 2023 amended the Petroleum Act 1998 to enable a new fee-charging method for OPRED's decommissioning functions. This requires secondary legislation, which has been consulted on. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### E. Mining reclamation bonding (US)

18. Surface coal mining (SMCRA; 30 CFR 800.14). The bond amount is set by the regulatory authority, reflects the probable difficulty of reclamation, and must be "sufficient to assure the completion of the reclamation plan if the work has to be performed by the regulatory authority in the event of forfeiture". The minimum is USD 10,000 per permit. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

19. SMCRA phased bond release (30 CFR 800.40): 60% of the bond for the area at completion of Phase I (backfilling, regrading and drainage control). Phase II (after revegetation) releases a further amount, retaining enough to cover re-establishing vegetation. The balance follows at Phase III. Release applications require public notice (four successive weekly newspaper advertisements). [confidence: high for the 60% and procedure] [source: 9]

20. Hardrock mining on BLM land (43 CFR 3809.552). An individual financial guarantee must cover "the estimated cost as if BLM were to contract with a third party to reclaim your operations according to the reclamation plan", including treatment facilities, interim stabilization and infrastructure maintenance. BLM may require a trust fund or other funding mechanism for long-term water treatment and post-closure maintenance, for as long as needed. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

### F. Nuclear decommissioning funding (US NRC; UK cross-reference)

21. Under 10 CFR 50.75(c), power reactor licensees must show minimum decommissioning funds in January 1986 dollars: PWR at or above 3,400 MWt USD 105 million (between 1,200 and 3,400 MWt: USD 75 million + 0.0088P); BWR at or above 3,400 MWt USD 135 million (USD 104 million + 0.009P). These are escalated by a factor of at least 0.65L + 0.13E + 0.22B (labour and energy from BLS regional data; waste burial from NUREG-1307). The amounts exclude spent fuel management and non-radiological demolition. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

22. The acceptable methods under 50.75(e) are: prepayment into a segregated account outside the licensee's control; an external sinking fund, acceptable alone only for rate-regulated licensees or those with a non-bypassable charge; and surety, insurance or other guarantee methods (surety bond or letter of credit; if term-limited, automatically renewed unless 90 days' notice is given). Licensees may credit earnings up to a 2% annual real rate of return. Licensees report decommissioning funding status at least every 2 years. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

23. UK new nuclear decommissioning finance (the Funded Decommissioning Programme under the Energy Act 2008, and the decommissioning provisions of the Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act 2022) is covered in facts/hinkley-sizewell.md and facts/t-uk-cfd-rab.md. It is not repeated here. [confidence: per those sheets]

## Financing and structure details (parties, tranches, amounts, tenors, guarantees, where verified)

Structuring consequences (analysis derived from the verified rules):
- Decommissioning reserve accounts in project finance must satisfy the regulator first. UK OREI rules make the Secretary of State the sole beneficiary, so lenders cannot treat that cash as security. Model it as a ring-fenced reserve ranking ahead of distributions.
- Letter-of-credit security consumes bank capacity and fees, with issuer ratings of A-/A3 or better. Rating downgrades of the issuer can trigger a draw (UK OREI guidance).
- Accrual windows (e.g., years 10–15 under a 15-year CfD) coincide with the debt tail, so model the interaction with debt service coverage ratios and the cash sweep.
- In the US, incremental funding (BOEM 2024) shifts risk to later years, and investment-grade sponsors may avoid posting security altogether.

## What went wrong or right, and why (well-established analysis only, attributed to named sources)

- BOEM's stated rationale (2024) was that the previous rules under-protected taxpayers against lessee default, which the new USD 6.9 billion of assurance addresses. Its 2026 proposal argues the burden diverts capital from production (sources 2, 3). The swing shows decommissioning security as a policy variable, not a fixed rule.
- The UK OPRED guidance rejects PCGs because a guarantor in a distressed group is likely to fail at the same moment as the subsidiary (source 7). This is the correlation logic lenders also apply.
- BOEM's 2024 renewables rationale was that liability accrues with installation and that revenue-funded accrual reduces default risk (source 1). Critics in the comment record argued it shifts risk to consumers and taxpayers, and BOEM disagreed (source 1).

## Teaching angles by chapter (for each listed chapter: the transferable lesson and the angle to take, 2–4 sentences)

- **Chapter 14 (Risk taxonomy):** Define decommissioning risk as a long-dated, contingent liability whose bearer depends on law. Owners bear it first; in the UK, former owners and parents can be reached through section 29 notices; the state is last resort (UK OREI guidance; NRC; BOEM). Contrast cost-estimate risk with counterparty risk on the security provider.
- **Chapter 65 (End of life):** Compare the four regimes on who, how much and when. Model a UK offshore wind fund accruing from year 10 to year 15 alongside debt service. Contrast BOEM's incremental schedule and SMCRA's phased release (60% after regrading). Show why letter-of-credit costs, post-tax sizing (DSAs) and the 2% real-return credit (NRC) materially change the NPV of the obligation.

## Do not state

- Do not state that BOEM's 2026 oil and gas proposal has been finalized. No final rule was found as of 3 October 2026.
- Do not state BOEM's offshore wind decommissioning assurance must be fully funded by a specific operating year. The rule leaves the schedule to BOEM approval, with the PPA/OREC-expiry idea given only as an example.
- Do not present the UK OREI 2019 table as covering 20-year CfDs. It addresses 15-year CfDs. Check DESNZ for updates.
- Do not state SMCRA Phase II or III release percentages beyond what is quoted (the Phase II amount is "an additional amount" with a retention).
- Do not state current NRC formula values in today's dollars. They require the escalation calculation.
- Do not quote the Finance Act provision number for Decommissioning Relief Deeds. It was not verified here.
- Do not describe the Scottish regime (Marine Directorate) or other jurisdictions (e.g., Netherlands, Germany, Australia's offshore regime) as covered. They were not researched here.
