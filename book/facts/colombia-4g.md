# Colombia 4G roads program: local-currency and institutional finance

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: February 2022 financing of a Nariño 4G toll road; with brief reference to April 2024 5G financings and the current text of Law 1508 as updated to September 2026)

## Summary

Colombia's Fourth Generation (4G) road concession program was unveiled in 2012 and awarded from 2014. Under it, the Agencia Nacional de Infraestructura (ANI) tendered some 30 toll-road public-private partnerships (PPPs) worth roughly USD 14–15 billion of initial investment. The program as originally framed covered more than 7,000 km and an estimated USD 24–26 billion of road works. The design was built for a financing problem. Projects earned mostly in Colombian pesos, from tolls topped up by ANI payments, and were too big for the domestic bank market alone. The 2012 PPP law (Law 1508) tied any payment to the concessionaire to the availability and service levels of independently usable "functional units". The government created ANI and recapitalized the Financiera de Desarrollo Nacional (FDN) as a national infrastructure development bank, with the World Bank Group, IFC and CAF as advisers and investors. FDN provided subordinated liquidity facilities, peso mini-perm loans, a standardized project-bond structure and, later, senior debt. Financings evolved in waves. The first two closed in 2016 with dual-currency 144A bonds, including a UVR (inflation-linked peso) tranche for Pacífico 3 and Costera. Later deals used US-dollar bank tranches alongside peso bank debt. Pension-fund-backed local debt funds and DFIs and ECAs followed (US DFC, IDB Invest, CABEI). A 2021 bond carried a full US DFC guarantee and was rated above the sovereign. Law 1882 of 2018 fixed a key bankability gap by setting how lenders are repaid when a PPP contract is annulled. By April 2020, 18 of 29 projects had reached financial close.

## Verified facts

1. In 2011 the Government of Colombia asked the World Bank Group to support the 4G program. The WBG described the program as requiring an estimated USD 26 billion for 8,100 km of roads. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Milbank describes the program as unveiled in 2012 to inject approximately USD 24 billion into the construction or rehabilitation of over 7,000 km of roads under ANI, using a PPP structure. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. By March 2017, 32 projects had been awarded, for a total expected initial investment of USD 14.8 billion. IFC's transaction advice directly or indirectly assisted the preparation and award of these 32 projects. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. As of December 2017, ANI had achieved financial close for nine projects, representing USD 4.6 billion. About USD 1.7 billion came from international financiers and USD 2.9 billion from national banks. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Oxford Business Group reports that the first stage (2014) comprised 10 projects (1,630 km, about COP 15 trillion) and the second stage (2015) 9 projects (1,830 km). By mid-2019, 15 projects had closed financing with about COP 21 trillion committed. [confidence: medium] [source: 3]
6. As of April 2020, 18 of the 29 projects in the 4G program had reached financial close, with seven moving toward close on a delayed basis. As of February 2020, construction was under way on 22 of the 29 concessions. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
7. Law 1508 of 10 January 2012 sets the legal regime for PPPs in Colombia. Article 5, as modified by Law 1753 of 2015, makes the right to collect revenues, receive public funds or any other remuneration conditional on the availability of the infrastructure and on meeting service levels and quality standards in the project's functional units or stages. Paragraph 2 allows remuneration by functional unit only for fully structured projects whose functional units could have been contracted independently. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
8. Risk-sharing in the 4G concession template (Milbank's summary): ANI shares cost overruns for land acquisition, environmental permitting, utility relocation and force majeure. On early termination, ANI must compensate the concessionaire for capital expenditures and O&M expenses incurred, net of project revenues received and certain capped penalties. During operations, ANI makes availability payments and guarantees minimum (inflation-adjusted) toll revenues at predetermined intervals. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
9. FDN and the World Bank Group: the WBG helped the government establish FDN with an initial IFC investment of USD 70 million. The same WBG brief also states that IFC and CAF invested USD 78 million and USD 50 million respectively in FDN; the two IFC figures likely reflect different stages and are not reconciled in the source. MIGA provided a guarantee for a loan to FDN. Joint IBRD–IFC technical assistance helped FDN set up a standardized project-bond structure and guarantee, develop regulations, improve pricing benchmarks and train domestic institutional investors. [confidence: high for the roles; medium for the stake amounts] [source: 1]
10. IFC and FDN co-invested in the Colombia Infrastructure Collective Debt Vehicle, which channels institutional money such as pension funds into infrastructure. IFC invested USD 50 million in a local infrastructure debt fund. IFC and CAF were anchor investors in a local infrastructure fund that mobilized domestic institutions and invested in a 4G project. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. FDN introduced multiple peso-denominated mini-perm loans in July 2017. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
12. Pacífico 3 (Concesión Pacífico Tres), 22 February 2016: the first 4G debt financing of its kind. It comprised Series A notes of USD 260.4 million due 2035 at 8.250% and Series B UVR-indexed notes of COP 397 billion due 2035 at 7.000%, plus three loan tranches and an FDN liquidity facility. Goldman Sachs placed the bonds in New York. Infra PPP World reports the USD bond as the first international bond sale for the 4G program and Goldman as mandated lead arranger of an accompanying loan of about USD 439 million. Sponsors were MHC Ingeniería y Construcción de Obras Civiles, Construcciones El Cóndor and Constructora MECO (Costa Rica). [confidence: high for the bond terms; medium for the loan size] [source: 5, 6]
13. Financing waves (Milbank). Pacífico 3 and Costera (Cartagena–Barranquilla) were financed mainly through capital-market bonds. Conexión Norte then used mainly a USD tranche from international commercial banks. A later wave (Pasto–Rumichaca, Alto Magdalena, Ruta del Cacao) was financed by DFIs or ECAs, including US DFC (formerly OPIC), CABEI and IDB Invest. Bond, bank or ECA debt often sat alongside a COP tranche from local banks, which infrastructure debt funds later joined. Lenders frequently also provided interest-rate and currency hedges. Many 4G financings carried an FDN liquidity line, and in later deals FDN joined the commercial bank tranche as a senior lender. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
14. Crédit Agricole CIB reports that, as of December 2021, 5 of more than 15 4G financings had been done in the project-bond market. It also reports that 4G-related USD bond sizes were constrained because the projects rely substantially on local-currency revenues from tolls, complemented by ANI availability payments and revenue top-ups. 4G capital structures were "sized by reference to the currency denomination of the project's revenue streams." FDN provided external credit enhancement in the form of subordinated revolving liquidity facilities. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
15. Puerta de Hierro (Patrimonio Autónomo Montes de María / Sociedad Concesionaria Vial Montes de María), May 2021: COP 760 billion (about USD 210 million) of UVR-indexed 144A/Reg S senior secured notes at 4.100%, maturing May 2045, fully amortizing. The sponsor was Sacyr. The notes were backed by ANI availability payments and toll revenues and carried a full, irrevocable US DFC guarantee of principal, interest and make-whole, counter-guaranteed by Assured Guaranty. They were rated Aa1/AA (Moody's/S&P). CA-CIB calls it the first Colombian project bond with a full DFC guarantee, the first 4G social bond, and the only 4G bond rated above the sovereign. It refinanced a mini-perm. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
16. February 2022: a 4G toll road of about 83 km in Nariño, co-sponsored by Sacyr and Ecuador's Sudinco, closed approximately USD 906 million of financing. It comprised: about USD 262 million (COP-equivalent) of UVR-indexed social notes at 6.660% due 2041; an international dual-tranche term loan and letter-of-credit facility of about USD 305 million; a local term loan and LC facility of about USD 260 million (COP-equivalent); and a local revolving liquidity facility of about USD 79 million. Lenders included international banks, Siemens Financial Services, FDN, Bancolombia, Davivienda and the debt fund FCP Deuda Infraestructura II Sura–Credicorp Capital. Latham describes it as the largest-ever 4G financing, the first project-level LC facility in Colombia and the IDB Group's first investment in a local-currency project bond. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
17. Law 1882 of 15 January 2018, Article 20, replaced Article 32 of Law 1508. Where a court declares a PPP contract absolutely null, or the contract is terminated on a ground of absolute nullity, the liquidation must recognize the CPI-updated value of costs, investments and expenses incurred by the contractor, including interest, less remuneration received. Recognized items must have served the public interest, relate to the contract and be at market prices, validated by the supervisor or an independent expert. Payment comes first from balances in the project trust accounts. Any remainder is paid in up to five equal annual instalments, the first within 540 days of liquidation. The concessionaire may not receive, after creditors are paid, more than its shareholders' contributed capital net of dividends and capital reductions (CPI-updated). A concessionaire responsible for the nullity must pay the agreed penalty clause, or 5% of the contract value. The provision also applies to transport concessions signed before Law 1508. Parts of the article were later declared conditionally constitutional or struck down by the Constitutional Court. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
18. ANI's 5G program succeeded 4G. In April 2024 two 5G concessions (Troncal del Magdalena 1 and 2) closed peso-denominated financings of about COP 4.3 trillion combined. Lenders included Bancolombia, IFC, IDB Invest, CAF, J.P. Morgan, SMBC, MUFG and FDN. [confidence: medium; the amount is translated from a law-firm release] [source: 10]

## Financing and structure details

- Grantor/offtaker: ANI. Revenue: user tolls, ANI availability payments and, at set intervals, guaranteed minimum toll revenue top-ups (inflation-adjusted). [source: 2, 7]
- Payment trigger: remuneration by functional unit, conditional on availability and service levels (Law 1508, art. 5). [source: 4]
- Credit enhancement: FDN subordinated revolving liquidity facilities; FDN senior and mini-perm loans; a US DFC full guarantee in Puerta de Hierro (Aa1/AA). [source: 1, 2, 7]
- Currency matching: tranches sized to the currency of revenues. UVR-indexed peso bonds for inflation-linked toll and availability flows (Pacífico 3 Series B, Puerta de Hierro, Nariño 2022). USD tranches limited in size and often paired with hedges from lenders. [source: 2, 5, 7, 8]
- Institutional money: pension-fund-backed infrastructure debt funds (e.g., FCP Deuda Infraestructura II Sura–Credicorp Capital); the IFC/FDN Collective Debt Vehicle; IFC/CAF-anchored local funds. [source: 1, 8]
- Lender protection on contract failure: Law 1882 compensation formula, with priority to creditors over equity and payment from trust accounts first. [source: 9]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right: institutions before deals. The WBG credits the program's financing success to a reformed legal and institutional framework (PPP law, ANI, FDN) and to capacity building for officials and local investors, not to any single deal. [source: 1]
- Right: matching currency to revenue. CA-CIB notes that 4G capital structures were sized by the currency of revenues. This capped dollar bond sizes and pushed the market toward UVR and peso instruments, local debt funds and DFI local-currency products. [source: 7]
- Right: a development bank as a market maker. FDN's liquidity facilities, mini-perms and, later, senior participation filled gaps that commercial banks would not, and helped take projects to refinancing (Milbank; CA-CIB). [source: 2, 7]
- Difficulty: slower closings than planned. Only nine projects had closed by end-2017 and 18 of 29 by April 2020, with seven delayed. OBG links delays partly to the Odebrecht corruption scandal around the Ruta del Sol 2 concession. Law 1882's nullity regime was the legislative response to lenders' fear of being unpaid if a contract was annulled. [source: 2, 3, 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 34 (Blended, concessional and local-currency finance): Use 4G as the reference case for building a local-currency market around a pipeline. Cover the DFI-anchored national development bank (FDN), its subordinated liquidity facilities and mini-perms, the IFC/CAF-anchored pension debt funds, UVR-indexed project bonds and a sovereign-plus guarantee (DFC). The angle is to size each tranche to the currency of revenues and use concessional or DFI tools to lengthen tenor and widen the investor base, rather than to absorb the currency risk.
- Ch 79 (Toll roads, bridges and tunnels): Show how the concession template converts traffic and construction risk into something bankable. It does so through payment by functional unit, ANI cost-sharing for land, permits, utilities and force majeure, minimum toll-revenue guarantees, availability payments and a termination-payment formula. Pair this with Law 1882 to teach why a lender's recovery on annulment or termination is a core credit question, and why ramp-up and construction delays still occurred.

- That FDN's shareholders include Sumitomo Mitsui or give exact current shareholdings. These were not verified here.
- Specific details of an ANI "dollar compartment" or of USD-denominated vigencias futuras. This was not verified.

- Odebrecht bribe amounts or the specific Ruta del Sol 2 nullity decisions and dates. Not verified here. Note that Ruta del Sol 2 was a pre-4G concession and should not be called a 4G project.
