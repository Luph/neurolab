# FPSO lease-and-operate financing (SBM Offshore)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: ExxonMobil Guyana's purchase of FPSO ONE GUYANA completed 4 February 2026)

## Summary

Under a lease-and-operate model, an FPSO contractor such as SBM Offshore builds the vessel in a special purpose company (SPC). The SPC is often co-owned with partners such as Mitsubishi Corporation, NYK or Chinese leasing companies. It charters the vessel to the oil company and operates it for a day rate. The SPC funds construction with a project loan that is guaranteed by the sponsors until completion. After the FPSO is completed and accepted, the pre-completion guarantees fall away and the loan becomes non-recourse, serviced from charter hire. SBM's public releases show two variants. In Brazil (Petrobras), long charters of about 22.5 years support long post-completion tenors of 14–15 years, with ECA cover (NEXI, SACE, Sinosure) on part of the debt. In Guyana (ExxonMobil, Stabroek block), short leases of up to about two years are followed by the client buying the vessel. The project loan therefore has only a two-year post-completion tenor and is repaid from the purchase price. SBM also uses short SPC-level bridge loans, sells minority stakes in operating SPCs (for example to China Merchants Financial Leasing), and has used sale-and-leaseback refinancing.

## Verified facts

1. FPSO Liza (Liza Destiny; Guyana, Stabroek). On 19 December 2017 SBM completed a USD 720 million project financing with 12 banks. The loan becomes non-recourse once the FPSO is completed and pre-completion guarantees are released. Post-completion tenor is 10 years at LIBOR + 1.65%. The lease and operate contract runs 10 years with options for up to 10 more. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. FPSO Liza Unity (Guyana). On 16 October 2019 SBM completed a USD 1.14 billion project financing with nine banks, with a two-year post-completion tenor "in line with the duration of the charter" at LIBOR + 1.50%. The loan converts to non-recourse after completion and guarantee release. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. ExxonMobil Guyana completed its purchase of Liza Unity on 9 November 2023, a few months before the maximum lease term ended in February 2024. Total cash consideration was about USD 1.26 billion, used mainly to repay in full the USD 1.14 billion project financing. SBM continues to operate and maintain the unit until 2033. The FPSO had been on hire since February 2022. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. FPSO ONE GUYANA (Yellowtail). On 21 July 2022 SBM completed a USD 1.75 billion project financing with 15 banks, with a two-year post-completion tenor at SOFR + 2.2%. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
5. ExxonMobil Guyana's purchase of ONE GUYANA completed on 4 February 2026, ahead of the maximum lease term ending August 2027. Cash consideration was about USD 2.32 billion, used mainly to repay in full the USD 1.74 billion project financing. SBM operates and maintains the unit to 2035. The FPSO had been on hire since August 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
6. FPSO Jaguar (Whiptail, Guyana) was SBM's first project under a "sale and operate" model. SBM completed a USD 1.5 billion project financing with 16 financial institutions, announced 21 November 2024. The loan tenor is in line with the construction phase. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
7. FPSO Sepetiba (Mero, Brazil). SBM signed contracts with Petrobras on 11 December 2019 for a 22.5-year lease and operation. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
8. Sepetiba bridge loan: on 1 July 2020 SBM announced a USD 600 million SPC-level bridge loan from four banks. It had a six-month tenor plus a six-month extension option and sponsor guarantees released on repayment, and was to be repaid from the first drawdown of the project loan. The SPC was owned by SBM (64.5%), Mitsubishi Corporation (20%) and NYK (15.5%). [confidence: high] [source: 8]
9. Sepetiba project loan: on 16 September 2021 SBM completed USD 1.6 billion, which it called the largest project financing in its history. Thirteen banks lent, with ECA cover from NEXI and SACE, and Sinosure signed a letter of intent to join and replace part of the bank commitments. The loan has four tranches with a 4.3% weighted average cost of debt, a 14-year post-completion maturity on the ECA-covered tranches and 15 years on the uncovered tranches. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
10. On 24 October 2024 SBM completed the sale of a 13.5% interest in the Sepetiba SPCs to China Merchants Financial Leasing (Hong Kong) Holding, keeping 51% and the operatorship. The sale had been agreed in February 2022 to take effect after start of operations. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
11. FPSO Alexandre de Gusmão (Mero, Brazil). SBM signed a USD 1.615 billion project financing, announced 20 June 2023, with 12 banks and cover from three ECAs. It has four facilities, a weighted average cost of debt of about 6.6% and a 14-year post-completion maturity. The FPSO has a 22.5-year lease and operate contract with Petrobras, and the SPCs are 55% SBM and 45% partners. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
12. FPSO Cidade de Paraty (Brazil). In April 2025 SBM signed its first sale-and-leaseback refinancing: USD 400 million, non-recourse, 8-year tenor. Ownership transfers to four Chinese leasing companies, while SBM and partners (SBM 63.125%) continue to operate for the remaining 8.5 years of the charter. [confidence: high] [source: 12]

## Financing and structure details

- **Structure.** The borrower is a vessel SPC. The loan is drawn during construction and supported by sponsor pre-completion guarantees. It becomes non-recourse after completion and acceptance, and is then repaid from charter hire (Brazil) or the purchase price (Guyana). [source: 1, 2, 9]
- **Tenor follows the charter.** Brazil's 22.5-year charters support 14–15-year post-completion debt. Guyana's leases of up to about two years support two-year post-completion debt. [source: 4, 9, 11]
- **Pricing data points.** LIBOR + 1.50–1.65% (2017–2019, Guyana); SOFR + 2.2% (2022, Guyana); weighted average costs of 4.3% (2021) and about 6.6% (2023) in Brazil, all-in including base rates. [source: 1, 2, 4, 9, 11]
- **Capital recycling.** Minority stake sales and sale-and-leaseback after start of operations. [source: 10, 12]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right: tying debt tenor and recourse to the charter lets a contractor carry several USD 1–2 billion vessels while limiting parent exposure to the construction period. SBM's CFO linked the model to providing "financing solutions" for clients (SBM's own description). [source: 6, 12]
- Risk to teach (from the Jubilee case): if contractor-arranged financing fails, the field partners may have to buy the vessel. See upstream-field-pf.md. [source: upstream-field-pf.md]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 76.** Compare the two charter archetypes. In the long Petrobras charter, credit rests on charterer credit and day-rate cover over 14–15 years, with ECA support. In the short ExxonMobil lease plus purchase, the lender's real takeout is the purchase price. Walk through the completion-guarantee release and the bridge-to-project-loan sequence, and use the Cidade de Paraty sale-leaseback and CMFL stake sale to show refinancing and capital recycling.

## Do not state

- MODEC project financing details. MODEC releases were not retrieved for this sheet.
- Charter day rates, DSCR covenants, or the identity of the three ECAs on Alexandre de Gusmão. Not disclosed in the releases used.
- That the Guyana purchase prices equal construction cost. The releases give only cash consideration.
