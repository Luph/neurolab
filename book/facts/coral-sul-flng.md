# Coral Sul (Coral South) FLNG, Mozambique: project financing

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Coral North FLNG sail-away, January 2026, per Eni 2025 Form 20-F)

## Summary

Coral South is the first development in Area 4 of the Rovuma basin offshore Mozambique, and is operated by Eni through Mozambique Rovuma Venture. A floating LNG vessel, Coral Sul FLNG, liquefies gas from six subsea wells, with a capacity of about 3.4 million tonnes per year (mtpa). All the LNG is sold to BP under a long-term contract signed in October 2016. The Area 4 concessionaires took the final investment decision on 1 June 2017. **The project financing was signed in 2017 and closed in December 2017, not in 2020.** It was the first project financing anywhere for a floating liquefaction unit, provided by 15 international banks and guaranteed by five export credit agencies, and covered 60% of the FLNG investment. Eni's financial statements put the ECA and bank financing at EUR 4,164 million. The vessel is owned by Coral FLNG SA, a special purpose entity, and funds were channelled through Coral South FLNG DMCC (Dubai). The structure was full recourse to the partners during construction, through a debt service undertaking capped pro rata (Eni's 25% share up to EUR 1,425 million). Once the lenders validated performance tests, it was to become non-recourse. Repayment is "pay-when-paid" from LNG sales to the long-term buyer, and the gas reserves are excluded from the lenders' security. The vessel was moored in the first half of 2022 and shipped its first LNG cargo in November 2022. The partners took FID on a second unit, Coral North FLNG (3.6 mtpa), in October 2025.

## Verified facts

1. The FID for Coral was made on 1 June 2017. In June 2017 drilling, construction and installation contracts were signed, together with agreements with lenders and the Government of Mozambique on the regulatory framework and financing. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
2. Eni's 2017 interim report stated that FLNG construction "will be financed through Project Finance covering 60% of its entire cost" and that this was "the first Project Finance ever arranged in the world for a liquefaction floater". The agreement was subscribed by 15 international banks and guaranteed by 5 ECAs. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. The financing closed in December 2017. Eni's 2017 Form 20-F lists "December 2017 – Eni, together with its Area 4 Partners, closed the project financing of Coral South FLNG construction project", and Eni reported "financial close" in a June 2018 filing. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
4. Capacity is about 3.4 mtpa (Eni also gives 3.37 mtpa), fed by six subsea wells. The LNG is sold to BP under a 20-year contract with an option for a further 10 years. The BP agreement was signed in October 2016 as the first LNG sale agreement in Mozambique. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5, 7]
5. Eni's 2019 20-F describes the structure. Coral FLNG SA (Eni 25%) operates the vessel under a service arrangement on behalf of the Area 4 concessionaires. The financing is "carried out partly through funds provided by the venturers and partly by a project financing with Export Credit Agencies and commercial banks for a total amount of €4,164 million". The EPCIC contract value was EUR 4,673 million. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. During construction and commissioning the project financing was supported by a debt service undertaking (DSU), with Eni's liability capped at EUR 1,425 million, proportional to its 25% interest. Once the lenders validated the vessel's performance tests, the guarantee was to be released and the facility to "convert to non-recourse". Lenders would then be secured only by the cash flows from LNG sales, "excluding the gas reserves from the scope of the guarantee". [confidence: high] [source: 4]
7. Repayment is made under a "pay-when-paid" clause from the cash flows of LNG sales to the long-term buyer. After conversion, Eni and the concessionaires have no obligation to guarantee Coral FLNG SA's performance to the lenders. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
8. The concessionaires opened a credit facility under which each committed pro rata to fund the capex share and DSU share of ENH, Mozambique's state company. Eni's share was capped at EUR 123 million and EUR 158 million respectively. At end-2022 Eni reported EUR 167 million of guarantee attributable to ENH's share, assumed by Eni in favour of the lenders. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
9. Eni also issued a parent company guarantee to the EPCIC contractor consortium Technip–JGC–Samsung Heavy Industries (TJS) of up to EUR 1,168 million (25% of contract value). It also gave the Government of Mozambique an unlimited parent guarantee for petroleum activities, capped at EUR 1,335 million for third-party claims (EUR 1,405 million by 2022), which runs until decommissioning. The other concessionaires took over their shares of the latter. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
10. Area 4 interests: Eni holds a 25% indirect interest through a 35.71% stake in Mozambique Rovuma Venture (with ExxonMobil 35.71% and CNPC 28.57%, rounded in filings). ENH, Galp and Kogas held 10% each. [confidence: high for the period of the 2019 and 2022 filings] [source: 4, 5]
11. Coral Sul FLNG was moored in the first half of 2022 and started commissioning. Gas entered the plant in June 2022, and the first LNG cargo was shipped in November 2022, the first commercial production from Area 4. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
12. Eni's related-party tables show a guarantee on behalf of Coral South FLNG DMCC of EUR 1,449 million at end-2023 and EUR 1,541 million at end-2024. No such line appears for end-2025, which suggests (but does not state) that the DSU had been released. [confidence: medium (inference from tables)] [source: 6]
13. In October 2025 Eni and partners CNPC, ENH, Kogas and XRG took FID on Coral North FLNG (3.6 mtpa). The hull launched and sailed away in January 2026, and start-up is expected at the end of 2028, bringing Mozambique's total LNG capacity to about 7 mtpa. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

## Financing and structure details

- **Borrower and vehicle.** Funds were raised through Coral South FLNG DMCC (Dubai) for on-lending to the vessel owner Coral FLNG SA. Eni controlled DMCC temporarily and planned to sell down to its working interest. [source: 1]
- **Debt.** EUR 4,164 million from ECAs and commercial banks (15 banks, 5 ECAs), covering 60% of the investment. [source: 1, 4]
- **Credit support.** Pre-completion DSU, pro rata and several, with Eni capped at EUR 1,425 million. Post-completion it is non-recourse and secured on LNG sale cash flows only, with no reserves security. The partners fund ENH's share. [source: 4]
- **Not verified:** ECA names, tenor, pricing, and the USD amount.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right: it was the first project financing of an FLNG unit, structured around a single creditworthy buyer (BP) and the vessel's own cash flows. Completion risk on a new asset type was kept with the sponsors through the DSU, and full non-recourse waited for lender-validated performance tests. [source: 1, 4]
- Unlike the onshore Mozambique LNG (Area 1) project, Coral Sul was built by an EPCIC consortium (Technip, JGC and Samsung Heavy Industries) and then moored offshore. It reached first cargo in 2022, while the onshore project was under force majeure from 2021; see mozambique-lng.md for that contrast. [source: 5]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 76.** Teach Coral Sul as the template for financing a floating liquefaction asset. The key elements are: a single long-term offtaker; ECA-heavy debt matched to the shipbuilding and EPC nationality; sponsor debt service undertakings until lender-tested performance; and a pay-when-paid, cash-flow-only security package that deliberately excludes the reserves. Show how partners carry the state company's (ENH's) share, a recurring feature of host-NOC participation.

## Do not state

- That Coral Sul's financing was signed or closed in 2020. Eni's filings date it to 2017 (signing at FID in June, financial close in December).
- The commonly cited "USD 4.7 billion" debt figure. Eni's statements give EUR 4,164 million of financing and EUR 4,673 million of EPCIC contract value. The USD figure and its basis were not verified.
- The names of the five ECAs (often reported as including SACE, Bpifrance, K-SURE, KEXIM and Sinosure). Not verified in a primary source.
- Tenor, margin, or the exact date the DSU was released.
- Current Area 4 interests after the XRG and Galp-related changes. Not verified in detail.
