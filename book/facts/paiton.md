# Paiton I (Indonesia): USD-linked PPA meets the Asian crisis, renegotiation

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: the December 2004 sale of Edison Mission Energy's stake; core coverage runs to the February 2003 debt restructuring)

## Summary

Paiton I is a 1,230 MW coal-fired plant of two 615 MW units in East Java, owned by PT Paiton Energy. It was Indonesia's first and largest independent power project. Its power purchase agreement (PPA) with the state utility PLN was signed on 12 February 1994 for 30 years. PLN paid in rupiah, but the portion covering non-rupiah costs, including investor returns, was indexed to the rupiah/dollar rate at the time of the PPA. The tariff was front-loaded, higher in early years and stepping down. The Government of Indonesia supported PLN's obligations. The sponsors were Edison Mission Energy (EME), Mitsui, General Electric (GE) and PT Batu Hitam Perkasa. Financial close came in 1995 on a project cost of about USD 2.5 billion, with heavy support from the US Export-Import Bank (US Ex-Im), the Export-Import Bank of Japan (JEXIM, later the Japan Bank for International Cooperation, JBIC), Japan's Ministry of International Trade and Industry (MITI, later Nippon Export and Investment Insurance, NEXI) and the US Overseas Private Investment Corporation (OPIC). The 1997-98 Asian crisis collapsed the rupiah and power demand. When both units reached commercial operation in 1999, PLN dispatched the plant to zero, underpaid using an artificial exchange rate, and in October 1999 sued in Jakarta to annul the PPA, alleging corruption, collusion and nepotism. Interim agreements from 2000 kept cash flowing while a long-term deal was negotiated. A binding term sheet (December 2001) and a PPA amendment (effective 23 December 2002) cut prices, scheduled payment of arrears and extended the PPA from 2029 to 2040. The debt was restructured in February 2003, with US Ex-Im taking out construction-period commercial banks.

## Verified facts

1. Paiton I is a 1,230 MW coal-fired plant of two 615 MW units (Units 7 and 8 of the Paiton complex) in East Java, owned by PT Paiton Energy. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3, 5]
2. The PPA between PT Paiton Energy Company (seller) and Perusahaan Umum Listrik Negara (PLN, buyer) is dated 12 February 1994. UPI reported in September 1994 that it was a 30-year contract. EME's FY2002 10-K says the original expiry was 2029. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4, 5]
3. In September 1994 UPI reported the ownership as Mission Energy 32.5%, General Electric 20%, Mitsui & Co. 32.5% and PT Batu Hitam Perkasa 15%. In January 1996 EME bought an additional 7.5% from a GE subsidiary, taking it to 40%. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 5]
4. EME and its partners began construction of the approximately USD 2.5 billion project in April 1995. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
5. Payment terms (EME 10-K for FY1998): "Payments are in Indonesian Rupiah, with the portion of such payments intended to cover non-Rupiah project costs (including returns to investors) indexed to the Indonesian Rupiah/U.S. dollar exchange rate established at the time of the Power Purchase Agreement in February 1994." The tariff "is higher in the early years and steps down over time." PLN's obligations "are supported by the Government of Indonesia." [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. Original ECA and insurance support, per the 26 March 2003 joint release of US Ex-Im, JBIC, NEXI and OPIC: [confidence: high] [source: 3]
   - US Ex-Im gave a USD 540 million guarantee in 1995, covering political risk during construction and later reduced to USD 507 million.
   - JEXIM provided a USD 855 million export credit with 36 commercial banks, of which JEXIM lent USD 513 million and the banks USD 342 million.
   - OPIC gave a USD 200 million investment guarantee, funded by Certificates of Participation.
   - MITI/EID insured JPY 31,806 million, approximately USD 360 million, of Japanese commercial bank loans.
7. US Ex-Im's financing agreement provided that its guarantee would convert into a direct loan at completion, taking out the commercial lenders, subject to conditions. The Asian financial crisis delayed the conversion. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
8. After the crisis the rating agencies cut the project's senior debt from investment grade to speculative grade, because PLN might not honor the PPA. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
9. Paiton Energy notified PLN that Unit 7 had achieved commercial operation in May 1999 and Unit 8 in July 1999. With demand low, PLN dispatched the plant to zero through February 2000. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
10. PLN paid USD 2.5 million on the USD 7.8 million May 1999 capacity and fixed-cost invoice. It used an exchange rate of IDR 2,450 per USD, which the PPA did not provide for but which PLN applied to other independent power producers (IPPs). Invoices for June to September 1999 totaling USD 164.1 million were unpaid at the time of the Q3 1999 report. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
11. On 7 October 1999 PLN announced a lawsuit in the Central Jakarta District Court to annul the PPA. It alleged the contract resulted from "corruption, cronyism and nepotism" and was "one-sided and against the public interest." The PPA required arbitration in Stockholm, and Paiton Energy started arbitration immediately. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
12. On 15 October 1999 the project signed an interim agreement with its lenders that waived defaults and deferred principal, first to 31 July 2000. It was later extended to 31 December 2000 and then to 31 December 2001. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
13. On 20 January 2000 PLN withdrew its lawsuit and Paiton Energy withdrew its arbitration against PLN and the Government of Indonesia. An Interim Agreement signed on 21 February 2000 set dispatch and fixed and energy payments for 2000, and PLN paid all USD 115 million of fixed payments due under it. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 7]
14. At 31 December 2000 PLN had not paid USD 814 million of capacity and fixed operating charges invoiced under the original PPA. Under a "Phase I Agreement" for January to June 2001, with fixed payments of USD 108 million over six months, Paiton Energy agreed to seek to recoup no more than USD 590 million of those arrears in the long-term restructuring. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
15. In April 2001 the PLN labor union sued Paiton Energy in the Central Jakarta District Court to set aside the PPA. The court dismissed the suit on 16 April 2002 for lack of standing, and the union appealed. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
16. PLN and Paiton Energy signed a Binding Term Sheet on 14 December 2001 that set capacity and energy payments from January 2002 and a monthly "restructure settlement payment" for arrears and other claims. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 8]
17. A PPA amendment dated 28 June 2002 became effective on 23 December 2002. It changed capacity and energy prices, provided for payment over time of amounts unpaid before January 2002, and extended the PPA's expiry from 2029 to 2040. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 8]
18. Paiton Energy and all its lenders completed the debt restructuring in February 2003; US Ex-Im gives the date as 14 February 2003. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 5]
   - US Ex-Im made a USD 381 million direct loan that repaid the construction-period commercial banks.
   - JBIC provided USD 844 million, rescheduled and switched from fixed to floating rate.
   - NEXI kept insurance on USD 337.6 million of commercial bank financing.
   - OPIC's USD 200 million guarantee was reissued through new Certificates of Participation.
   - Bondholders and 38 commercial banks provided USD 307 million.
   - Amortization was extended to fit the lower tariff cash flow, and the first new principal payment was made on 18 February 2003.
19. As a condition of the US Ex-Im loan, all commercial disputes related to the project were settled. EME did not expect distributions to shareholders before 2006. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
20. On 16 December 2004 EME sold MEC International B.V., holding most of its international portfolio including the 40% Paiton interest, to a consortium of International Power plc (70%) and Mitsui (30%). The total consideration for the portfolio was approximately USD 2.0 billion. [confidence: medium] [source: 9]

## Financing and structure details

- **Owner.** PT Paiton Energy. Sponsors at 1994: Mission Energy 32.5%, Mitsui 32.5%, GE 20% and Batu Hitam Perkasa 15%. From January 1996: EME 40% and GE 12.5%. The 2003 release names EME, GE Capital and Mitsui as principal owners. [source: 2, 3]
- **Offtake.** A 30-year PPA with PLN, extended to 2040 in 2002. It is two-part (capacity and fixed operating costs payable once units are commercial, plus energy) and front-loaded. Payment is in rupiah but indexed to the dollar for non-rupiah costs. [source: 1, 2, 5]
- **Credit support.** Government of Indonesia support for PLN's obligations. The 10-K calls it "support," not a guarantee; see Do not state. [source: 1, 2]
- **1995 debt package.** About USD 2.5 billion project cost.
  - US Ex-Im guarantee of USD 540 million (later USD 507 million) on construction loans, convertible to a direct loan.
  - JEXIM USD 513 million plus USD 342 million from commercial banks.
  - MITI-insured Japanese bank loans of about USD 360 million.
  - OPIC USD 200 million guarantee via Certificates of Participation.
  - Plus bonds; see Do not state on the amount. [source: 3]
- **Dispute forum.** Stockholm arbitration under the PPA. [source: 6]
- **2003 restructured debt.** US Ex-Im USD 381 million direct loan; JBIC USD 844 million; NEXI-insured USD 337.6 million; OPIC USD 200 million; bonds and banks USD 307 million. Maturities were extended, and there were no shareholder distributions until about 2006. [source: 3, 5]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Currency mismatch.** EME's filings set out the mechanism plainly. The tariff's non-rupiah component was dollar-indexed, while PLN's revenues were in rupiah, and the growth and exchange rate assumptions "deteriorated significantly" after the project was contracted and financed. When the rupiah collapsed, the dollar-linked tariff became unaffordable to PLN. [source: 1, 2]
- **Demand.** Low post-crisis demand led PLN to dispatch the plant to zero. Capacity charges still fell due, which made the contract politically exposed. [source: 1, 6]
- **Legitimacy.** PLN's lawsuit framed the PPA as a product of "corruption, cronyism and nepotism." After Suharto fell, contracts signed under the old regime faced a legitimacy challenge separate from their economics. [source: 6]
- **What went right.** The parties kept negotiating instead of litigating to the end. They made a sequence of interim deals (lender waivers, a 2000 interim agreement, a 2001 phase agreement, a binding term sheet) that kept partial cash flowing. The sponsors accepted a haircut on arrears (no more than USD 590 million of USD 814 million) and lower prices in exchange for a longer PPA. The ECAs and OPIC worked together to restructure, according to their joint release. The plant stayed in service, and the debt was restructured without enforcement. [source: 1, 3, 5]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 3 (History).** Paiton marks the Asian crisis lessons. Dollar-indexed tariffs sold to local-currency utilities moved FX risk to the party least able to bear it, and the shock tested every ECA-backed IPP in the region at once. Contrast it with later Indonesian practice under PLN tenders, which is out of scope here and should be verified separately.
- **Ch 59 (Country, currency and payment risk).** Use Paiton as the worked example of indexation. Model a capacity charge partly indexed to IDR/USD at the 1994 rate and show PLN's rupiah bill when the rate multiplies. Show why "government support" short of a guarantee left lenders reliant on renegotiation, and how a utility can underpay by applying its own exchange rate (IDR 2,450 per USD).
- **Ch 69 (Thermal power).** Teach that a capacity payment guarantees revenue only while the buyer can pay, and that zero dispatch of a contracted base-load coal plant is a warning sign. Cover front-loaded tariff design and why extending the PPA tenor (2029 to 2040) is the standard way to cut the annual tariff while preserving the lenders' NPV.

## Do not state

- Specific original or renegotiated tariffs (for example "8.56 US cents/kWh for years 1-6" or "slightly under 5 cents" after 2002). Secondary sources conflict, and none was verified against the PPA or a filing.
- That the Government of Indonesia "guaranteed" the PPA. EME filings say PLN's payment obligations were "supported by" the government. Do not characterize the instrument further without the document.
- The exact amount of the 1996 Rule 144A bond issue (often cited as USD 180 million). It was not verified in a primary source.
- Corruption findings. Corruption was alleged by PLN in its 1999 suit and by the PLN labor union, but no verified judgment found it. Do not name individuals linked to Batu Hitam Perkasa.
- Peak rupiah depreciation figures or dates unless taken from a central bank series.
- That Paiton was "Indonesia's first IPP" without the qualifier. The 2003 ECA release says "first and largest independent power project," so use that wording with attribution.
- The NPV value of the 2002 renegotiation or the sponsors' returns. Not verified.
- Post-2004 ownership details (Paiton III expansion, later stake sales). Not verified for this sheet.
