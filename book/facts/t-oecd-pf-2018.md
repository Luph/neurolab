# OECD Arrangement project finance terms in force 2017–2018, and what the 2023 modernization changed

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: Arrangement text revised January 2026, OECD/LEGAL/5005; the 2017 consolidated text as reproduced in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/179, OJ L 37, February 9, 2018)

## Summary

Until the 2023 modernization, the OECD Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits gave project finance its own set of terms. These were longer tenors and freely sculpted repayment, available only to transactions that met defined eligibility criteria. The criteria were limited recourse, an independent project company, and repayment from project cash flows.

In the consolidated Arrangement published by the OECD on February 1, 2017, these terms sit in **Annex VII** ("Terms and conditions applicable to project finance transactions"). That is the text in force through 2017. The EU reproduced it in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/179. Annex X of that text is a different annex (market benchmarks for Category 0 countries). The "Annex X" label for the project finance annex belongs to other editions of the Arrangement and is not verified here.

**The 2017 terms:**
- Maximum repayment term of 14 years.
- Principal could be repaid unequally, but:
  - no more than 25 percent of principal in any six-month period;
  - first repayment within 24 months of the starting point, with at least 2 percent repaid by then;
  - interest at least annually;
  - weighted average life (WAL) no longer than 7.25 years.
- A 20 bp surcharge on CIRR for fixed-rate terms over 12 years.
- Ten-day prior notification.

**The 2018 switch.** A footnote limited the 14-year and 7.25-year WAL terms to final commitments issued by December 31, 2017. After that, unless Participants agreed otherwise, a 10-year cap and a 5.25-year WAL applied where official ECA support exceeded 35 percent of the syndication for a project in a high-income OECD country. US EXIM's competitiveness reports for 2019 and 2022 describe the project finance maximum as "10–14 years," which is consistent with that fallback applying.

**The 2023 modernization** (agreed March 31, 2023; effective July 2023) removed the separate project finance regime:
- The general maximum term became 15 years for all transactions (12 years for non-CCSU power plants; 22 years under the climate and nuclear understandings).
- Sculpted repayment moved into the main text (Article 13(e)): 30 percent per six months, first repayment within 24 months, WAL up to 65 percent of tenor or six years, whichever is longer.
- The project finance definition survives only for premium (market-benchmark) and buyer-risk purposes.

This sheet complements t-oecd-arrangement, which covers the current rules in full.

## Verified facts

**The 2017 consolidated text (in force through 2017 and, subject to the fallback footnote, into 2018)**
1. **Source of the text.** On February 1, 2017, the OECD published a consolidated revised Arrangement. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/179 of September 25, 2017 (OJ L 37, February 9, 2018) replaced Annex II of Regulation (EU) No 1233/2011 with that text. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Article 7 and scope.** Participants "may apply" the Annex VII terms to transactions meeting Annex VII Appendix 1. Article 7(b) extends this to exports covered by four sector understandings: nuclear, renewable energy/climate change mitigation and adaptation/water, railway infrastructure, and coal-fired generation. The Ship Sector Understanding disapplied Article 7 and Annex VII "until such provisions have been further reviewed." [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Maximum term.** Annex VII Article 2: "The maximum repayment term is 14 years," subject to the Coal-Fired Electricity Generation Sector Understanding for projects in its scope. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **Repayment conditions.** Annex VII Article 3 allows unequal principal instalments, and principal and interest less often than semi-annually, provided that:
   - (a) no single repayment, or series within a six-month period, exceeds 25 percent of principal;
   - (b) the first principal repayment is no later than 24 months after the starting point of credit, and at least 2 percent of principal has been repaid by then;
   - (c) interest is paid at least every 12 months, with the first payment no later than six months after the starting point;
   - (d) the WAL of the repayment period does not exceed seven and a quarter years;
   - (e) prior notification is given. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. **CIRR surcharge.** For fixed-rate official financing, the relevant CIRR applies up to 12 years. A 20 bp surcharge on CIRR applies for repayment terms over 12 years, in all currencies. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. **Notification.** Participants must notify all others at least ten calendar days before issuing any commitment. If any Participant asks for an explanation, the notifying Participant waits a further ten calendar days. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. **The sunset footnote.** Footnote 1 to Annex VII Chapter II provides that:
   - Articles 2 (14 years) and 3(d) (7.25-year WAL) apply to final commitments issued on or before December 31, 2017;
   - after that date they "shall be discontinued unless the Participants agree otherwise";
   - if discontinued, they are replaced as follows: the maximum term remains 14 years, except that where official ECA support is more than 35 percent of the syndication for a project in a high-income OECD country, the maximum is 10 years and the WAL limit is five and a quarter years. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. **Basic eligibility criteria (Appendix 1, Part I).** The transaction involves:
   - (a) financing of an economic unit where the lender looks to its cash flows and earnings for repayment and to its assets as collateral;
   - (b) export transactions with a legally and economically independent project company (for example an SPV) for investment projects generating their own revenues;
   - (c) appropriate risk-sharing among partners, including adequate equity;
   - (d) project cash flow sufficient during the entire repayment period to cover operating costs and debt service;
   - (e) priority deduction of operating costs and debt service from revenues;
   - (f) a non-sovereign buyer or borrower with no sovereign repayment guarantee (performance guarantees such as offtake arrangements excluded);
   - (g) asset-based security (assignments, pledges, proceeds accounts);
   - (h) limited or no recourse to sponsors after completion. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. **Additional criteria in high-income OECD countries (Appendix 1, Part II).**
   - The ECA participates in a syndication with private lenders that have no official support.
   - The ECA is a minority partner with pari passu status throughout.
   - Official support is less than 50 percent of the syndication.
   - Premium rates do not undercut private market financing and are commensurate with those charged by the private lenders in the syndicate. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
10. **Comparison: general terms in the same 2017 text.**
    - Maximum term: 5 years for Category I countries, extendable to 8.5 years with prior notification; 10 years for Category II countries; 12 years for non-nuclear power plants.
    - Standard repayment: equal semi-annual instalments, with the first within six months of the starting point.
    - Exceptional flexibility: 25 percent cap per six months, first principal within 12 months, and maximum WAL of 4.5 years (sovereign, Category I), 5.25 years (sovereign, Category II), 5 years (non-sovereign, Category I), 6 years (non-sovereign, Category II), or 6.25 years for non-nuclear power plants. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. **How the US ECA described the terms.** EXIM's 2019 Competitiveness Report (and again its report on calendar year 2022) states that general transactions qualify for 8.5–10 years, and lists exceptions:
    - project finance: 10–14 years;
    - renewable energy and nuclear power plants: 18 years;
    - commercial aircraft: 12 years;
    - rail infrastructure: 12–14 years. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]

**The 2023 modernization**
12. **Agreement and effective date.** Participants agreed the package on March 31, 2023, and it took effect in July 2023 (Mayer Brown: "effective as of 5 July 2023"). The revised CIRR rules apply to commitments from July 15, 2023. [confidence: high for agreement date; medium for the exact effective day] [source: 5, 6, 7]
13. **Current tenor rules (January 2026 text).**
    - Article 12: the term may not exceed the useful life of the goods or project; the general maximum is 15 years; power plants not eligible under the CCSU or NSU are capped at 12 years.
    - Prior notification is required above 10 years for credits of SDR 10 million or more. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
14. **Current flexible repayment (Article 13(e)).** Where justified by a mismatch between the obligor's or project's cash flows and the standard profile:
    - no repayment, or series within six months, may exceed 30 percent of principal;
    - the first principal repayment is due within 24 months of the starting point;
    - the WAL may not exceed 65 percent of the repayment term or six years, whichever is longer.

    Notification is required for credits of SDR 10 million or more, with a justification of the profile. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
15. **Project finance in the current text.** The term survives in the premium rules. In market-benchmark countries (Category 0, high-income OECD and high-income euro area), ECA pricing in syndicated asset-backed or project finance deals may be benchmarked to the private lenders in the same deal. At least 25 percent of the syndicated financing must be commercial loans or guarantees without official support, ranking pari passu. The text defines project finance as exports to a legally and economically independent project company, repaid from its cash flows and secured on its assets. The current annex list (Annexes I–XIII) contains no separate project finance annex. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
16. **Climate Change Sector Understanding (CCSU).** CCSU transactions over 15 years (other than Class B Type 1) get wider limits: 35 percent per six months, first repayment within 36 months, and WAL up to 70 percent of tenor or six years. The CCSU's review clause still lists, as an open item, "examining the possibility" of applying the Arrangement's project finance provisions to that understanding. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

**What changed, side by side**

| Feature | 2017 Annex VII (project finance) | 2026 text (any transaction) |
|---|---|---|
| Max term | 14 years (10 years if official support exceeds 35 percent in high-income OECD, after December 31, 2017 fallback) | 15 years general; 12 years non-CCSU/NSU power plants; up to 22 years CCSU/NSU |
| Max principal in any 6 months | 25 percent | 30 percent (35 percent for long CCSU deals) |
| First principal repayment | ≤ 24 months, at least 2 percent repaid by then | ≤ 24 months (36 months for long CCSU deals) |
| WAL cap | 7.25 years (5.25 years in fallback case) | greater of 65 percent of tenor or 6 years (70 percent for long CCSU deals) |
| Eligibility | Appendix 1 project finance criteria | Cash-flow mismatch justification; no project finance test |
| CIRR | +20 bp surcharge above 12 years | CIRR reformed in 2023 (see t-oecd-arrangement) |
| Notification | 10 days before commitment, all project finance deals | Above 10 years or flexible profile, for SDR 10 million or more |

## Financing and structure details

What the change means for an ECA tranche in a project financing:
- **Before 2023.** The sponsor had to show the transaction met the eight Appendix 1 criteria (plus the syndication tests in high-income OECD countries) to unlock 14 years and sculpting up to 25 percent per half-year, with WAL at most 7.25 years. Equal semi-annual principal instalments over 14 years (payments at 0.5, 1.0, ... 14.0 years after the starting point) have a WAL of exactly 7.25 years. So any back-ending of a 14-year profile had to be offset by front-loading elsewhere: the WAL cap, not the tenor, was the binding constraint on sculpting.
- **After 2023.** Any transaction, project finance or not, can get 15 years and sculpting with a cash-flow justification. On a 15-year term the WAL ceiling is 9.75 years (65 percent × 15), materially looser than 7.25 years. Power plants outside the CCSU are still capped at 12 years (WAL ceiling 7.8 years), so a gas-fired IPP gains less than a renewable or transmission project.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The project finance annex existed because equal semi-annual repayment over 8.5–10 years did not fit greenfield cash flows. The 2023 package generalized that flexibility. EXIM's 2023 report describes the aim as making Arrangement financing "flexible enough to better face challenges posed by the economic and financial needs of projects" and the competitive landscape from non-OECD members (source 5).
- The 2017 footnote shows Participants treated the 14-year/7.25-year terms as provisional and tied to a review. Its fallback targeted ECA-heavy deals in rich countries, where official support most risks crowding out commercial banks (source 1).

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 29 (The lenders):**
  - Use the before-and-after table to show how a rule change reshapes ECA term sheets.
  - Before 2023, "project finance" was a regulatory classification with eligibility tests. Since 2023 it matters mainly for pricing: the market-benchmark rule and the 25 percent commercial co-lending requirement.
- **Ch 36 (Sizing and sculpting debt):**
  - Have the reader test a sculpted profile against both regimes: 14 years with WAL of 7.25 years and 25 percent per half-year, versus 15 years with WAL of 65 percent and 30 percent per half-year.
  - Show which constraint binds for a contracted solar project versus a gas plant (12-year cap).
  - Stress that the binding ECA constraint is often the WAL test, not the headline tenor.

## Do not state

- Do not call the 2017–2018 project finance annex "Annex X" without checking the specific edition. In the February 2017 consolidated text (EU 2018/179), it is Annex VII, and Annex X is the Category 0 market-benchmark annex. The January 2018 OECD edition (TAD/PG(2018)1) could not be retrieved, so its annex numbering is unverified.
- Do not state whether Participants formally agreed in late 2017 or 2018 to extend the unconditioned 14-year/7.25-year terms. The decision document was not found. EXIM's "10–14 years" description is consistent with the 35 percent fallback, but it is not proof of which rule applied.
- Do not state the year the project finance terms were first adopted (often given as 1998 for a Project Finance Understanding, or 2005 for its incorporation). Not verified here.
- Do not say the 2023 reform "abolished" project finance support. Project finance remains eligible for the general flexible terms and has its own premium benchmarking rule.
- Do not give an exact percentage or basis-point effect of the 2023 premium changes (see t-oecd-arrangement).
