# Accounting standards for projects and sponsors: IFRIC 12, IFRS 16, IFRS 9, IFRS 10/11, ASC 853/842/815, IFRS 18

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: IFRS 9/IFRS 7 amendments for nature-dependent electricity contracts, effective 1 January 2026; IFRS 18 effective 1 January 2027)

## Summary

The accounting a project company and its sponsors must apply is set by a small group of standards.

**IFRIC 12 (Service Concession Arrangements)**, issued by the IFRS Interpretations Committee and effective from 1 January 2008, covers public-to-private concessions where the grantor controls the services, users and price and any significant residual interest. In such a concession the operator does not recognise the infrastructure as property, plant and equipment. Instead it recognises one of the following, depending on who bears demand risk:

- a financial asset, where it has an unconditional right to cash from the grantor;
- an intangible asset, where it has a right to charge users;
- both.

The US counterpart, **ASC 853**, was introduced by ASU 2014-05. It likewise bars lease and PP&E treatment, and ASU 2017-10 later made the grantor the customer for operation services.

**IFRS 16** (effective 2019) and **ASC 842** (public business entities, fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2018) put most leases on lessees' balance sheets. This matters for offtake contracts that may contain embedded leases.

**IFRS 9** (effective 2018) governs financial instruments:

- classification of financial assets;
- the expected-credit-loss model;
- hedge accounting (the US counterpart is ASC 815, with ASU 2017-12 targeted improvements).

December 2024 amendments, effective 2026, relieve renewable power purchase agreements (PPAs) from some own-use and hedge-accounting problems.

**IFRS 10 and IFRS 11** (effective 2013) decide whether a sponsor consolidates the project company, accounts for it as a joint venture under the equity method, or as a joint operation.

**IFRS 18** replaces IAS 1 from 1 January 2027. It brings defined "operating profit" subtotals and disclosure of management-defined performance measures, which will change how project-company and sponsor income statements look.

## Verified facts

### Service concessions

1. **IFRIC 12 dates.** IFRIC 12 applies for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2008, with earlier application permitted. The EU endorsed it by Commission Regulation (EC) No 254/2009 of 25 March 2009. EU companies had to apply it at the latest from the start of their first financial year beginning after that regulation entered into force. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **IFRIC 12 scope (para. 5).** IFRIC 12 applies to public-to-private service concession arrangements where both conditions hold:
   - (a) the grantor controls or regulates what services the operator must provide with the infrastructure, to whom, and at what price;
   - (b) the grantor controls, through ownership, beneficial entitlement or otherwise, any significant residual interest in the infrastructure at the end of the term.

   Whole-of-life assets are in scope if condition (a) is met. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **No PP&E (para. 11).** Infrastructure within the scope of IFRIC 12 is not recognised as the operator's property, plant and equipment, because the arrangement does not give the operator the right to control the use of the infrastructure. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
4. **Financial asset or intangible asset (paras. 15–18).**
   - The operator recognises a financial asset to the extent it has an unconditional contractual right to receive cash from, or at the direction of, the grantor. This includes a grantor guarantee of specified amounts or of any shortfall between user receipts and specified amounts.
   - The operator recognises an intangible asset to the extent it receives a right (licence) to charge users, which is not an unconditional right to cash because receipts depend on usage.
   - Where both apply, each component is accounted for separately (the "bifurcated" model).

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. **Borrowing costs (para. 22).** Under IAS 23, borrowing costs attributable to the arrangement are expensed as incurred unless the operator has a contractual right to receive an intangible asset. In that case they are capitalised during construction. Revenue for construction and operation services now follows IFRS 15, which replaced IAS 18 in the interpretation's cross-references. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
6. **ASC 853 (ASU 2014-05).** FASB issued ASU 2014-05 (Topic 853) in January 2014. It covers an operating entity's service concession arrangements with a public-sector grantor where:
   - the grantor controls or can modify or approve the services, the users and the price;
   - the grantor controls any residual interest at the end of the term.

   Such arrangements are not accounted for as leases under Topic 840, and the infrastructure is not recognised as the operator's PP&E. The ASU is effective for public business entities for annual periods beginning after 15 December 2014, applied on a modified retrospective basis. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
7. **ASU 2017-10.** Issued in May 2017, ASU 2017-10 clarifies that for arrangements within Topic 853 the grantor, not third-party users such as toll-road drivers, is the customer of the operation services in all cases. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

### Leases

8. **IFRS 16.** IFRS 16 was issued in January 2016, effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2019. It replaced IAS 17, IFRIC 4, SIC-15 and SIC-27. Lessees recognise a right-of-use asset and a lease liability for all leases longer than 12 months unless the asset is of low value. Lessor accounting is substantially carried forward from IAS 17. A contract is a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period in exchange for consideration. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
9. **ASC 842 (ASU 2016-02).** ASU 2016-02 was issued in February 2016. Lessees recognise a right-of-use asset and a lease liability for both finance and operating leases. The two differ in presentation:
   - finance leases show interest and amortisation separately;
   - operating leases show a single, generally straight-line lease cost, with cash payments in operating activities.

   It is effective for public business entities (and certain others) for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2018. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### Financial instruments and hedging

10. **IFRS 9.** The completed version was issued in July 2014, effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2018. It sets three measurement categories for financial assets:
    - amortised cost (held to collect, with cash flows that are solely payments of principal and interest);
    - fair value through other comprehensive income (held to collect and sell);
    - fair value through profit or loss (everything else).

    It replaced incurred-loss impairment with an expected-credit-loss model. It includes a hedge accounting chapter, and entities may choose to continue applying IAS 39 hedge accounting. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
11. **Nature-dependent electricity contracts.** On 18 December 2024 the IASB issued "Contracts Referencing Nature-dependent Electricity", amending IFRS 9 and IFRS 7. The amendments:
    - clarify the own-use requirements for PPAs whose output depends on nature, such as wind and solar;
    - allow such contracts to be designated as hedging instruments, with a variable nominal volume of forecast electricity transactions as the hedged item;
    - add disclosures.

    They are effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026, with earlier application permitted. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 7]
12. **ASC 815 (ASU 2017-12).** "Targeted Improvements to Accounting for Hedging Activities" was issued in August 2017. It:
    - allows hedging of contractually specified components of non-financial risk and of contractually specified interest rates;
    - stops separate measurement and reporting of hedge ineffectiveness;
    - eases documentation and effectiveness testing, including qualitative assessments after inception.

    It is effective for public business entities for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2018, and for other entities for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2019. [confidence: high] [source: 9]

### Consolidation and joint arrangements

13. **IFRS 10 and IFRS 11 dates.** Both were issued in May 2011 and apply for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2013. The EU endorsement (Commission Regulation (EU) No 1254/2012 of 11 December 2012) required EU companies to apply IFRS 10, IFRS 11, IFRS 12 and the amended IAS 27 and IAS 28 at the latest from financial years starting on or after 1 January 2014. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
14. **IFRS 10 control.** IFRS 10 establishes control as the basis for consolidation and sets out how to assess whether an investor controls an investee. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
15. **IFRS 11.** Joint control is "the contractually agreed sharing of control of an arrangement, which exists only when decisions about the relevant activities require the unanimous consent of the parties sharing control". IFRS 11 replaced IAS 31. Joint arrangements are classified as joint operations or joint ventures. [confidence: high] [source: 12]

### Presentation and Pillar Two

16. **IFRS 18.** "Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements" was issued in April 2024 and replaces IAS 1. It is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with earlier application permitted. It requires:
    - defined subtotals, including "operating profit" and "profit before financing and income taxes";
    - disclosure of management-defined performance measures;
    - enhanced aggregation and disaggregation principles.

    Some IAS 1 requirements move to IAS 8 and IFRS 7. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
17. **IAS 12 and Pillar Two.** May 2023 amendments to IAS 12 introduced a temporary exception from recognising and disclosing deferred taxes related to Pillar Two income taxes, with targeted disclosures (see t-pillar-two). [confidence: high] [source: 14]

## Financing and structure details

Not a transaction. Points of structure that follow from the verified rules:

- **Availability versus demand risk.**
  - An availability-payment PPP, where the grantor pays specified amounts, tends toward the IFRIC 12 financial-asset model: the receivable is measured under IFRS 9 and interest-like income is recognised.
  - A user-paid toll concession tends toward the intangible model: construction revenue and an amortising intangible are recognised, and borrowing costs are capitalised during construction (para. 22).
  - Minimum-revenue guarantees can produce a split ("bifurcated") model.

  [source: 1]
- **Offtake contracts.** A long-term offtake contract that gives the buyer the right to control the use of an identified plant can be a lease under IFRS 16 or ASC 842. That changes the project company's revenue presentation, and for lessees it brings the obligation on balance sheet. [source: 5, 6]
- **Consolidation drives the sponsor's numbers.** Whether a sponsor consolidates the project company under IFRS 10, or equity-accounts it as a joint venture under IFRS 11, determines whether non-recourse project debt appears on the sponsor's consolidated balance sheet. [source: 11, 12]
- **Hedging.** Interest rate swaps on project debt and power price hedges or virtual PPAs depend on hedge accounting under IFRS 9 or ASC 815 to avoid profit-and-loss volatility. The 2024 IFRS amendments target renewable PPAs specifically. [source: 7, 8, 9]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Why IFRIC 12 and ASC 853 refuse PP&E treatment.** Both standard-setters reason that the operator acts as a service provider and does not control the use of public infrastructure. FASB noted that before ASU 2014-05 US GAAP had no specific guidance, so preparers were confused about whether concessions were leases. [source: 1, 3]
- **Why the PPA amendments were needed.** The IASB states that the amendments enable companies to better reflect nature-dependent electricity contracts, whose volumes vary with weather and other uncontrollable factors, in their financial statements. Before them, many such contracts struggled with the own-use exemption and with hedge designation. [source: 8]
- **Why ASU 2017-10 was issued.** FASB says the absence of guidance on identifying the customer of operation services led to diversity in practice, which the update eliminated. [source: 4]

## Teaching angles by chapter

**Chapter 7 (Accounting for the project company).** Use IFRIC 12 to show the novice that a project company's balance sheet may show no power plant or road at all, only a receivable or an intangible. Explain that accounting profit and debt-service cash flow therefore diverge. Tie capitalised interest during construction to IAS 23 and IFRIC 12 para. 22: borrowing costs are capitalised under the intangible model and expensed under the financial-asset model.

**Chapter 66 (Accounting for projects and sponsors).** Work through the decisions a sponsor faces:

- the consolidation test under IFRS 10, and joint venture versus joint operation under IFRS 11;
- whether an offtake contract contains a lease;
- whether a receivable passes the solely-payments-of-principal-and-interest test and how expected credit losses are set;
- hedge designation for swaps and PPAs, including the 2026 PPA relief;
- how IFRS 18's operating-profit subtotal will reframe reported results from 2027.

Contrast IFRS with ASC 853, 842 and 815, and show how accounting goals (deconsolidation, avoiding P&L volatility) shape ownership splits and hedging structures. Tell readers where the current text is found: the IFRS Foundation's issued standards and the FASB Codification.
