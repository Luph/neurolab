# Infrastructure fund terms: management fees, carried interest, hurdle rates, fund life, open-ended vs closed-ended, continuation funds

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest items covered: Lazard Interim 2026 Secondary Market Report; HICL Infrastructure PLC Annual Report 2026, effective fee change of July 1, 2026; Blackstone Infrastructure Strategies L.P. and KKR Infrastructure Conglomerate LLC Forms 10-K for 2025, filed March 2026; Brookfield Asset Management Ltd. Form 10-K for 2025)

## Summary

Infrastructure equity reaches investors through three main wrappers, each with a typical fee model.

**1. Closed-end limited partnerships.** This remains the dominant wrapper for institutional investors (Andonov et al., 2021). Brookfield's 2025 10-K describes its closed-end funds as typically committed for 10 years with two one-year extension options. For managers' own funds, fee disclosures in US filings describe:
- management fees on committed or invested capital;
- carried interest of 20 percent of net profits after return of contributed capital and a preferred return of "typically 8% per annum" (Brookfield).

A 2024 SEC registration statement for a fund investing in third-party infrastructure funds (CION Grosvenor) says those funds "generally charge a management fee of 1.00% to 2.00% based on committed capital, and approximately 15% to 20% of net profits as a carried interest."

**2. Open-ended or perpetual vehicles.** These are aimed at core and core-plus assets and, increasingly, at private wealth. The two large US evergreen vehicles show the semi-liquid template:
- **KKR Infrastructure Conglomerate (K-INFRA):** management fee of 1.25 percent of NAV a year, and a performance participation of 12.5 percent of total return over a 5 percent hurdle, with a high-water mark and 100 percent catch-up. Repurchases are capped at 5 percent of NAV per quarter, with a 5 percent early-repurchase fee within 24 months.
- **Blackstone Infrastructure Strategies (BXINFRA):** management fee of 1.25 percent of NAV (lower for larger series from March 2026) and a 12.5 percent / 5 percent hurdle performance participation.

**3. Listed vehicles.** UK infrastructure investment trusts pay external managers on NAV or market capitalization. HICL moved to a fee based 100 percent on market capitalization from July 1, 2026.

**Continuation funds (GP-led secondaries).** These let a manager hold assets beyond a closed-end fund's life by selling them to a new vehicle backed by secondary buyers. The overall secondary market reached an estimated USD 233 billion in 2025, half of it GP-led. About 73 percent of infrastructure and real asset GP-led deals priced above 90 percent of NAV (Lazard).

## Verified facts

**Closed-end funds**
1. **Fund life (Brookfield).** Brookfield Asset Management's long-term private funds "are generally closed-end and have a long duration, typically committed for 10 years with 2 one-year extension options." Brookfield Corporation's 2025 annual report describes base management fees "on capital that is typically committed for 10 years with two one-year extension options." [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. **Carry and preferred return (Brookfield).** Brookfield's general partners are "generally entitled to a carried interest that allocates to it 20% of the net profits realized by the limited partners ... subject to the return of contributed capital and a preferred return of typically 8% per annum." Carry is typically paid towards the end of fund life and may be subject to clawback. Management fees are charged "typically on committed capital or invested capital, depending on the nature of the fund." [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Third-party fund fees (CION Grosvenor N-2/A, November 2024).** The infrastructure funds it intends to invest in "generally charge a management fee of 1.00% to 2.00% based on committed capital, and approximately 15% to 20% of net profits as a carried interest allocation." Elsewhere the filing says sponsor managers' carried interest is "generally equal to 20% of the net profits ... typically subject to a preferred return." [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. **Investor base and fund size (Andonov et al., 2021).** Institutional investors "gain exposure to infrastructure predominantly through finite-horizon closed private funds." Fund sizes for funds raised 2002–2018 were:
   - closed infrastructure funds (Preqin): mean USD 1.35 billion, median USD 0.72 billion, similar to buyout funds and larger than real estate and VC funds;
   - Burgiss sample: mean USD 1.58 billion, median USD 0.82 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
5. **Fund life and performance (Andonov et al., 2021).** Infrastructure fund performance "similarly depends on quick deal exits." They argue the finite fund horizon conflicts with the long-lived, stable cash flows investors seek. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

**Open-ended and evergreen vehicles (US non-traded, SEC-reporting)**
6. **K-INFRA management fee.** KKR Infrastructure Conglomerate LLC (10-K for 2025) pays its manager:
   - 1.25 percent a year of month-end NAV for Class D, I and S shares;
   - 1.00 percent for a 60-month period after June 1, 2023 for Class U shares (and certain other classes named in the filing). [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. **K-INFRA performance participation.** KKR receives 12.5 percent of Total Return attributable to investor shares, subject to a 5.0 percent Hurdle Amount and a High Water Mark, with a 100 percent catch-up, measured and paid annually and accrued monthly. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
8. **K-INFRA liquidity terms.**
   - Repurchases are limited to no more than 5.0 percent of aggregate NAV per calendar quarter.
   - Shares repurchased within 24 months of issue pay a 5.0 percent Early Repurchase Fee.
   - The board may repurchase fewer shares, or none. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
9. **BXINFRA fees.** Blackstone Infrastructure Strategies L.P. (10-K for 2025):
   - management fee of 1.25 percent of NAV a year, plus an administration fee of 0.10 percent;
   - from March 6, 2026, the management fee is 1.25 percent, 1.05 percent and 0.95 percent for Class I-Series I, II and III units respectively;
   - performance participation of 12.5 percent of total return subject to a 5 percent annual hurdle and a high-water mark with 100 percent catch-up, measured annually, paid quarterly and accrued monthly. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
10. **Fee offsets and expense caps.** BXINFRA's management fee is reduced by 100 percent of the unitholder's pro rata share of break-up, transaction, monitoring and similar fees. KKR agreed an expense limitation capping K-INFRA's annual "Specified Expenses" (which exclude the management fee) at 0.60 percent of net assets through December 31, 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]
11. **Perpetual strategies (Brookfield).** Brookfield distinguishes closed-end long-term funds (value-add, opportunistic) from "perpetual strategies," which include core and core-plus funds and its listed affiliates. Brookfield Infrastructure Partners (BIP) pays incentive distributions to Brookfield equal to 18 percent and 33 percent of increases in limited partner distributions above the first and second hurdles. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

**Listed infrastructure investment companies (UK)**
12. **HICL fee basis.**
    - At the start of the financial year to March 31, 2026, HICL's board agreed with InfraRed to change the management fee basis to an equal blend of NAV and market capitalization.
    - From July 1, 2026, the fee is calculated entirely by reference to market capitalization, capped at what the historic gross-asset-value (GAV) basis would have produced.
    - Applied to the year to March 31, 2026, the new basis would have cut the fee by 11 percent against the blended arrangement and by 24 percent against the GAV basis.
    - The management agreement's notice period falls from three years to two from July 1, 2027. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

**Continuation funds and GP-led secondaries**
13. **Market size.** Lazard estimates:
    - 2025: global secondary volume of USD 233 billion (USD 152 billion in 2024), of which GP-led USD 116 billion and LP-led USD 117 billion;
    - first half of 2026: USD 124 billion (GP-led USD 61 billion), with trailing-12-month volume of about USD 260 billion by June 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9]
14. **Infrastructure GP-leds.** In Lazard's 2025 survey:
    - about 73 percent of infrastructure and real asset GP-led transactions were priced above 90 percent of NAV;
    - underwriting for these deals focuses on "establishing yield with downside protection for the secondary buyer";
    - the average secondary-buyer target for this segment appears to be 1.9x net MOIC and an 18.7 percent net IRR (read from a three-panel chart; medium confidence).

    Lazard's interim 2026 report highlights "continuation fund-to-continuation fund exits" emerging as a tool for trophy assets. [confidence: high for pricing share; medium for the return target] [source: 8, 9]
15. **Valuation and conflict concerns.** An EDHEC analyst, quoted in *Private Debt Investor* (July 1, 2026) about private credit continuation vehicles, said "valuation-related conflicts of interest are apparent." The comment concerned credit continuation vehicles, not infrastructure, but it illustrates the general governance debate on GP-led deals. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

## Financing and structure details

Indicative fee templates, with market and date as stated:

| Wrapper | Management fee | Performance fee | Hurdle | Life / liquidity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Closed-end third-party infrastructure funds (US-registered fund-of-funds view, 2024) | 1.00–2.00% on committed capital | ~15–20% carry | Preferred return, typically present | Finite life | CION Grosvenor N-2/A |
| Brookfield closed-end private funds (2025) | On committed or invested capital | 20% carry | Typically 8% p.a., after return of capital | 10 years + 2 × 1-year extensions | BAM 10-K |
| US evergreen infrastructure (K-INFRA, BXINFRA, 2025–26) | 1.25% of NAV (0.95–1.05% for larger series at BXINFRA) | 12.5% of total return | 5% annual, high-water mark, 100% catch-up | Quarterly repurchases capped (K-INFRA 5% of NAV per quarter) | 10-Ks |
| UK listed trust (HICL, from July 1, 2026) | On market capitalization, capped at GAV-basis level | None stated | n/a | Daily listed liquidity | HICL AR 2026 |

Mechanics to teach:
- A fee on committed capital during the investment period, stepping down to invested capital afterwards, is a common closed-end design. Brookfield's "committed or invested" language is consistent with this, but the step-down is not verified fund by fund.
- A European (whole-fund) waterfall returns capital plus the preferred return before carry. The 100 percent catch-up then gives the manager its full percentage of total profit.
- Evergreen vehicles crystallize performance fees annually against a hurdle and high-water mark, rather than at exit.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Horizon mismatch.** Andonov et al. argue that infrastructure exposure through finite-life funds produces private-equity-like cash flow volatility and dependence on exits, which is at odds with investors' aim of long-term stable returns. Continuation funds and perpetual vehicles are the market's responses (sources 4, 8).
- **Fee pressure.** HICL's move to a market-cap fee followed a period of discounts to NAV. Infrastructure trusts traded at about 10–20 percent discounts after 2022 (EDHEC, cross-reference t-infra-equity-returns). This is a direct case of fee terms shifting to align managers with shareholders' share-price experience (source 7).
- **Lower-fee evergreen template.** The 12.5 percent / 5 percent hurdle structure in K-INFRA and BXINFRA reflects a lower-return, cash-yielding core and core-plus strategy, compared with 20 percent / 8 percent closed-end terms (sources 1, 5, 6).

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 47 (Bidding, acquisitions and funds):**
  - Build a worked waterfall comparing a closed-end 1.5 percent fee with 20 percent carry over an 8 percent preferred return, against an evergreen 1.25 percent fee with 12.5 percent over a 5 percent hurdle. Use gross returns from t-infra-equity-returns to show the gross-to-net gap in each.
  - Explain the 10+1+1 fund life, and why it pushes managers to sell or roll assets into continuation vehicles. Cover the conflict issues (both sides of the trade) and the pricing evidence (about 73 percent of infrastructure GP-leds above 90 percent of NAV).
  - Use the HICL fee change to show listed-vehicle governance in action.

## Do not state

- Do not state a single "market standard" closed-end infrastructure fee (for example "1.5 and 15 over 8"). The verified evidence is the 1–2 percent and 15–20 percent ranges from one 2024 filing, plus Brookfield's 20 percent / 8 percent. Core and core-plus closed-end terms below these were not verified.
- Do not quote ILPA or Preqin averages for infrastructure fee levels. Not retrieved.
- Do not give 3i Infrastructure, INPP or other listed trusts' fee or performance-fee terms. Not retrieved.
- Do not name specific infrastructure continuation vehicles, or their sizes or buyers. None was verified in a primary source here.
- Do not say BXINFRA's or K-INFRA's repurchase terms guarantee liquidity. The filings reserve discretion to limit or suspend repurchases.
- Do not quote Brookfield's management fee rates for its infrastructure flagship funds. Not disclosed in the passages read.
