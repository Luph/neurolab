# Indicative construction-contract terms: LDs, caps, bonds, retention, and major equipment lead times (2019–2026)

## Summary

Public documents that give exact percentages are rare, because filed EPC contracts redact the LD rates, caps and security amounts. Several reliable anchors do exist:

- **World Bank standard Plant contract (October 2017 edition).** It sets a template payment profile: 10% advance against an advance payment security, 80% on delivery or monthly progress, then 5% at completion and 5% at operational acceptance. Delay LDs are a percentage rate of the contract price up to a stated maximum. The contractor's aggregate liability defaults to the total contract price where no multiplier is specified.
- **US LNG EPCs.** Bechtel's Rio Grande LNG contracts require a standby letter of credit of 10% of the contract price, stepping down to 5% at substantial completion, plus a parent guarantee. Venture Global expects total LDs, construction plus equipment performance, of up to 10% of aggregate construction cost per project, alongside an uncapped obligation to make good on performance.
- **Gas turbine supply.** GE's equipment supply agreement for a 1,875 MW Ohio CCGT (2019) capped delay LDs at 25% and performance LDs at 15% of contract price, with a cumulative cap of 35%.
- **Bonding norms.** Siemens Energy described bank guarantees for advance payments, performance and warranty as "usually between 5% and 25% of the order value". Its 2023 need for a government-backed EUR 12 billion guarantee facility shows that OEM bonding capacity can itself become a constraint.
- **Equipment lead times.** Gas turbine order books are measured in years of output. GE Vernova had 116 GW of gas equipment backlog plus slot reservations at mid-2026 against annual output of about 20 GW. An Israeli CCGT that ordered GE Vernova equipment in February 2026 expects completion during 2030.

## Verified facts

### A. Standard-form anchors (multilateral procurement)

1. World Bank Standard Procurement Document, Request for Bids, Plant: Design, Supply and Installation (October 2017). The Terms of Payment template:
   - **Imported plant:** 10% advance against an "irrevocable advance payment security", reducible pro rata as plant is delivered; 80% on delivery to the carrier (CIP); 5% on the Completion Certificate; 5% on the Operational Acceptance Certificate.
   - **Locally supplied plant and installation services:** the same 10/80/5/5 profile. For installation services the 80% is paid monthly on measured value.
   - **Design services:** 10% advance and 90% on design acceptance. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The same document has the following liability and LD provisions:
   - **Delay LDs:** payable "as a percentage rate of the Contract Price or the relevant part thereof", capped at a "Maximum" stated in the particular conditions as a percentage of the contract price. Once the maximum is reached, the employer may consider termination.
   - **Aggregate liability:** the contractor's aggregate liability "shall not exceed the amount resulting from the application of the multiplier specified in the PCC, to the Contract Price or, if a multiplier is not so specified, the total Contract Price". The cap does not apply to the cost of repairing or replacing defective equipment or to patent indemnities.
   - **Consequential loss:** indirect and consequential losses are excluded. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. The same document's functional-guarantee template sets minimum acceptance levels at 95% of guaranteed production capacity and/or 105% of guaranteed consumption. It caps performance LDs at a percentage of the contract price left blank for the employer to fill in. The performance security may be reduced to 10% of the value of components covered by an extended defects liability. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### B. US LNG lump-sum turnkey EPCs (2024–2025)

4. Rio Grande LNG Train 4 and Train 5 (NextDecade; contractor Bechtel Energy Inc.; Texas), from NextDecade's 8-K summaries.
   - **Prices:**
     - Train 4 EPC signed 5 August 2024 at USD 4.327 billion. Its price was refreshed on 7 June 2025 to USD 4.768 billion, about 10.2% higher (computed).
     - Train 5 EPC signed 12 June 2025 at USD 4.320 billion.
     - Both prices include euro-denominated equipment, and Bechtel takes currency risk after full notice to proceed.
   - **Delay and performance LDs:**
     - Delay LDs are payable for each day substantial completion is late, up to a maximum set in the agreement.
     - Performance LDs are payable if performance guarantees are missed but minimum acceptance criteria are met.
   - **Liability limits:**
     - Bechtel's "maximum aggregate liability ... (including its liability for liquidated damages)" is set in the agreement.
     - Neither party is liable for consequential damages or lost profit, other than agreed LDs and third-party indemnities.
   - **Force majeure:** Bechtel's right to a price increase for force majeure delay is capped at USD 25 million in aggregate.
   - **Security:**
     - Bechtel must provide a standby letter of credit equal to 10% of the contract price, reducing to 5% on substantial completion and payment of any LDs or bonus, and expiring at the end of the warranty period.
     - Bechtel must also provide a parent company guarantee from Bechtel Global Energy, Inc.
     - An early production bonus is payable for early completion. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
5. The filed Train 4 EPC text redacts the LD rates, the aggregate cap, the force majeure price cap amount and the letter of credit percentages ("[***]"). The aggregate-cap clause carves out third-party indemnities and certain other obligations, including delivery of legal title. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. Venture Global (2024 10-K):
   - Its EPC contractors "increasingly have a limited work scope". Venture Global contracts directly with equipment vendors such as Baker Hughes, and the EPC contractor integrates that equipment and guarantees full operation of the facility.
   - "The EPC contractor has an uncapped make-good obligation to deliver a facility capable of passing certain performance tests". It also pays LDs "subject to a specified cap and sub-limits for certain milestones" for construction or performance-testing delay.
   - Aggregate LDs under the EPC contracts and the Baker Hughes equipment purchase orders "are expected to be up to 10% of the aggregate construction cost for each project". [confidence: high] [source: 5]

### C. Power equipment supply and EPC (US and Israel)

7. Guernsey Power Station (Guernsey County, Ohio; approximately 1,875 MW CCGT; COD 1 May 2023):
   - **Equipment supply agreement:** signed with GE on 31 January 2019 for three power blocks (gas turbine, HRSG, steam turbine) at approximately USD 381.364 million. It was assigned to the EPC contractor, Gemma Power Systems, which "stands in front of GE"; the owner pays invoices into escrow.
   - **LD caps:** GE was subject to LDs of "25% of contract price for delay, 15% of contract price for performance, cumulatively capped at 35% of the contract price".
   - **Delivery delay LDs:** beyond 60 days, USD 90,000 a day for Major Components and USD 20,000 a day for Minor Components, with all delivery-delay LDs capped at USD 270,000 a day.
   - **Owner support:** the owners also signed an Equity Contribution Agreement and Guarantee to fund completion. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
8. Hadera Expansion (OPC Energy; Israel; approximately 850 MW CCGT):
   - **Order and EPC:** main equipment ordered from GE Vernova in February 2026. A lump-sum turnkey EPC was signed on 2 June 2026 with a joint entity of an international EPC contractor and Solel Boneh Infrastructure, and the equipment supply agreement was reassigned to it.
   - **Schedule:** notice to proceed was issued on 30 June 2026. Completion is scheduled "for the course of 2030".
   - **LDs and bonus:** delay compensation and an early-delivery bonus apply, both "subject to ... capped amounts and limitations of liability".
   - **Security:** the contractor gives bank and company guarantees "at certain rates".
   - **Cost and financing:** EPC plus equipment consideration is approximately USD 1 billion (NIS 3 billion), about USD 1,180 per kW (computed). Senior debt from Bank Leumi is approximately USD 1.7 billion, expected at approximately 80% of construction cost. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

### D. Bonding capacity and guarantee norms (OEM view)

9. Siemens Energy's Q4 FY2023 press release (15 November 2023) states: "guarantees for advanced payments, performance or warranty bonds are a standard instrument of the industry. These guarantees are issued by banks for a fee and the guarantee is usually between 5% and 25% of the order value." Citing an ICC study of April 2022, it gave an industry average guarantee default rate of 0.2%. [confidence: high for what Siemens Energy stated; the ICC figure is as cited by Siemens Energy] [source: 8]
10. In the same release, Siemens Energy disclosed that the German government would counter-guarantee EUR 7.5 billion of a EUR 12 billion guarantee facility, for a standard market fee:
    - EUR 11 billion provided by a bank consortium;
    - EUR 1 billion from a further consortium led by Deutsche Bank;
    - Siemens AG providing access to a first-loss amount of up to EUR 1 billion, covered by share collateral and payment deferrals.
    The facility was needed to support order growth after the Siemens Gamesa losses. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

### E. Major equipment lead times, 2024–2026

11. GE Vernova, Q2 2026 results (22 July 2026):
    - Gas Power equipment backlog plus slot reservation agreements (SRAs) grew from 100 GW to 116 GW in the quarter. GE Vernova expects at least 125 GW under contract by year-end 2026.
    - In the quarter it signed 18 GW of SRAs and 2 GW of orders and converted 10 GW of SRAs to orders, taking backlog from 44 to 53 GW and SRAs from 56 to 63 GW.
    - It plans annual gas turbine output of 20 GW from Q3 2026, 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
12. GE Vernova's filings show that customers pay deposits to hold manufacturing slots. Contract liabilities grew by USD 11.8 billion in the first half of 2026, mainly from "higher down payments on orders and slot reservation agreements at Power". GE Vernova's FY2025 10-K warns that "some counterparties to slot reservation agreements may not place orders equal to the value of their reservation amount or at all". [confidence: high] [source: 10, 11]
13. Siemens Energy, Q3 FY2026 analyst presentation (5 August 2026): gas turbine "firm customer commitments (69 GW backlog / 26 GW SRAs)"; 15 GW booked (12 GW converted from SRAs) and 6 GW shipped. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
14. Arithmetic on the GE Vernova figures (computed, not stated by the company): 125 GW under contract against 20 GW of annual output is about six years of production at the 2026 run-rate. The rate falls as output rises to 24 GW (2028) and 30 GW (2030). Order-to-COD at Hadera Expansion is about four years (February 2026 to 2030). The IEA (April 2026) cites Wood Mackenzie that gas turbine deliveries could take about five years and gives average transformer lead times of two to three years; see facts/t-frontier-data.md. [confidence: medium (derived indicator)] [source: 7, 9; t-frontier-data.md]

### F. Liquidated damages in practice

15. At Ludington (Michigan), the owners and Toshiba America Energy Systems stipulated USD 11 million of delay LDs for late performance. The jury separately awarded USD 383 million of damages for breach (December 2025). Delay LDs therefore covered only a small part of the owners' loss where defective work, not delay, was the main harm. [confidence: high] [source: 13]

## Market norms: indicative ranges and where they come from

| Term | Indicative level (market, period) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Advance payment | 10% against an advance payment security (MDB-funded plant, 2017 template) | [1] |
| Retention / holdback | Effectively 10% of each line, released 5% at completion and 5% at operational acceptance (MDB template) | [1] |
| Performance security | 10% of contract price by letter of credit, stepping to 5% at substantial completion, through warranty (US LNG LSTK, 2024–2025); bank guarantees "usually between 5% and 25% of the order value" across advance payment, performance and warranty (OEM statement, 2023) | [2], [3], [8] |
| Delay LD cap / total LD cap | Total LDs up to 10% of construction cost (US LNG, owner-procured equipment model, 2024); OEM supply agreement 25% delay + 15% performance, 35% cumulative (US CCGT, 2019) | [5], [6] |
| Overall liability cap | 100% of contract price by default in MDB template; carve-outs for defective-equipment repair and IP indemnities; private LNG EPC caps redacted | [1], [4] |
| Minimum acceptance criteria | 95% of guaranteed capacity / 105% of guaranteed consumption (MDB template) | [1] |
| Parent support | Parent company guarantee from contractor group (US LNG; Ludington) | [2], [13] |
| Gas turbine lead time | About 4–6 years from order to COD in 2025–2026 (GE Vernova backlog; Hadera; IEA) | [7], [9], t-frontier-data |
| Transformer lead time | 2–3 years on average (IEA, April 2026) | t-frontier-data |

Drivers that move these terms:
- the contractor's balance sheet and bonding capacity (Siemens Energy 2023);
- the EPC structure: a full wrap against owner-procured equipment, where caps shift to equipment suppliers (Venture Global);
- equipment scarcity, which in 2024–2026 strengthened OEMs' hands on deposits, slots and LD caps;
- inflation and price-refresh mechanisms before notice to proceed (Rio Grande Train 4 rose about 10% between signing and refresh);
- lenders' requirements that delay LDs cover debt service during the delay;
- governing law on penalties (see t-contract-law.md).

## Financing and structure details

- Rio Grande Train 4/5: before full notice to proceed, the owner must show Bechtel it has funds or firm lender commitments for the contract price. This is a reverse credit-support term that protects the contractor. [2], [3]
- Guernsey: the equipment supply agreement was assigned to the EPC contractor, which "stands in front of GE". LDs owed by GE are paid to the EPC contractor, which "may or may not have corresponding obligations" to the owner. This is the wrap and interface question in a single sentence. [6]
- Hadera Expansion: equipment was ordered before the EPC and assigned into it once the EPC was signed, which preserved the manufacturing slot. [7]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Redaction limits public benchmarks.** Securities filings summarise EPC structures but redact the numbers ([***]). Treat any single "market" LD rate from commentary with caution. [4]
- **Bonding capacity is a real constraint.** Siemens Energy's 2023 facility, with state counter-guarantees and a first-loss layer from its former parent, shows that large OEMs' ability to post 5–25% guarantees can bind when losses hit the balance sheet. [8]
- **Owner procurement trades wrap for control.** Venture Global accepts a narrower EPC scope and direct vendor exposure, with total LDs of up to 10% of construction cost, in exchange for control over equipment and schedule. [5]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 22 (The EPC contract):**
  - Calibrate Case P's delay LDs against debt service and the LD cap against a 10% LC; then compare with the World Bank default overall cap of 100% of contract price and the 10/80/5/5 payment profile.
  - Use Rio Grande to show how security steps down at substantial completion and why lenders want a parent guarantee as well as the LC.
  - Use Ludington to show that delay LDs do not cover defective performance.
- **Ch 23 (Construction structures beyond the single EPC):**
  - Contrast a full wrap with Venture Global's owner-procured equipment model and the Guernsey/Hadera assignment of equipment supply contracts into the EPC.
  - Discuss OEM credit and bonding capacity (Siemens Energy 2023).
  - Explain how slot reservation deposits and 4–6 year turbine lead times push developers to commit equipment money before financial close, and how lenders treat that pre-FID spend.

## Do not state

- Typical "wrap premium" percentages for an EPC contractor taking on split-package interface risk. No primary source was retrieved.
- Typical EPCM fee levels (for example "8–12% of direct costs"). Mining feasibility studies on EDGAR describe EPCM as a budgeted indirect but did not give a citable percentage in the documents read.
- Any specific delay LD rate (for example "0.5% per week" or "0.1% per day" as a norm), except the 0.1% per day in Triple Point (a software contract) and the GE delivery figures above. The Rio Grande and Cheniere rates are redacted.
- Market-wide performance bond percentages beyond the Siemens Energy statement and the 10%/5% Rio Grande LC.
- Precise current transformer lead times in weeks, or prices, from Wood Mackenzie or NREL. The NREL source could not be reached, and the IEA's two-to-three-year average is the verified figure.
- That GE Vernova's or Siemens Energy's turbines are "sold out" to a particular year. The companies give GW of backlog and output plans, not sold-out dates.
