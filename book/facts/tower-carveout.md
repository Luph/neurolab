# Africa tower carve-outs: IHS Towers' sale-and-leasebacks with MTN (2013–2022), the MTN South Africa deal and its financing, and MTN's 2026 agreement to buy IHS

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: IHS shareholders approved the MTN merger at the EGM of 4 August 2026; IHS completed the sale of its Latin American towers to Macquarie Asset Management, 7 August 2026; IHS 6-K on a note redemption notice, 9 September 2026)

Cross-references: facts/t-digital-norms.md (tower ABS and lease norms). Cellnex and Vantage Towers were not researched for this sheet.

## Summary

A tower carve-out separates a mobile operator's passive infrastructure (masts, shelters, power) from its network. The operator sells the towers to a specialist tower company and leases space back under a long master lease agreement (MLA). The tower company then earns more by adding tenants (colocation). IHS Towers built Africa's largest independent tower business mostly through such deals with MTN. It bought MTN's towers in Côte d'Ivoire and Cameroon (2013), Zambia and Rwanda (2014) and Nigeria (10,966 towers in 2014–2015, including 2,116 from 9mobile). Its last big one, closed on 31 May 2022, was 5,691 towers in South Africa from MTN, with a managed-services agreement covering about 7,100 more MTN sites. Gross cash consideration was USD 421.2 million. It was funded partly by a ZAR 3.47 billion facility from South African banks (3-month JIBAR + 2.75%, maturing May 2029), with 30% to be owned by a Black economic empowerment (B-BBEE) consortium. IHS listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2021 (18 million shares at USD 21). MTN, its anchor tenant and a large shareholder, then signed a merger agreement on 17 February 2026 to take IHS private at USD 8.50 a share, an enterprise value of about USD 6.2 billion. Shareholders approved it on 4 August 2026, and it is not yet closed. The arc of sale-and-leaseback, IPO and buyback by the original seller is the teaching point. It also shows the carve-out risks a lender must underwrite: one tenant dominating revenue, FX-linked lease escalators in volatile currencies, and the operator's incentive to buy back its infrastructure when the price is right.

## Verified facts

1. IHS milestones (from its 20-F):
   - 2001: founded in Nigeria as a tower builder;
   - 2009: began owning towers and colocation;
   - 2013: acquired MTN Côte d'Ivoire's 911 towers and MTN Cameroon's 818 towers, and completed managed-lease (MLL) agreements for Orange Côte d'Ivoire's 1,191 and Orange Cameroon's 819 towers;
   - 2014: acquired MTN's 719 towers in Zambia and 550 in Rwanda;
   - 2014–2015: acquired 10,966 towers in Nigeria (4,154 from MTN Nigeria and 2,116 from 9mobile in 2014, and 4,696 more from MTN Nigeria in 2015);
   - 2015: acquired Airtel Zambia's 949 and Airtel Rwanda's 200 towers;
   - 2016: acquired HTN Towers (1,211 towers in Nigeria), described as the first tower-company-to-tower-company deal in Africa. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. IHS completed its IPO in 2021, issuing 18,000,000 ordinary shares at USD 21 for net proceeds of USD 357.7 million. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. MTN South Africa deal: on 31 May 2022 IHS Towers South Africa Proprietary Limited acquired 5,691 towers from MTN South Africa. The deal included an agreement to provide managed services, including to about 7,100 additional MTN South Africa sites, and added 7,017 tenants. IHS was to own 70%, with 30% to be owned by a B-BBEE consortium (a shareholding agreement closed in January 2025). It also agreed to achieve and maintain B-BBEE contributor levels set by the Competition Commission of South Africa. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
4. Acquisition accounting (provisional, FY2022): gross and net cash consideration USD 421.2 million; towers and equipment USD 251.7 million; customer-related intangible USD 128.0 million; network-related intangible USD 67.8 million; right-of-use assets and lease liabilities each USD 211.3 million (ground leases); deferred tax USD (52.9) million; provisions USD (34.4) million; goodwill USD 61.0 million. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Financing: IHS SA entered into a ZAR 3,470.0 million (about USD 204.3 million) facility agreement under South African law, originally dated 26 May 2022. Funds were "partly applied toward the payment of consideration" for the MTN SA acquisition, and the undrawn portion was available for 24 months for capex and general purposes. Interest is 3-month JIBAR + 2.75%. Covenants include net debt to EBITDA and interest cover. The facility terminates in May 2029. At 31 December 2022, ZAR 3,400.0 million had been drawn. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. MLAs with MTN group companies (per the FY2025 20-F) expire in December 2032 (Nigeria), March 2033 (Cameroon), April 2033 (Côte d'Ivoire), March 2034 (Zambia) and April 2034 (South Africa). In the FY2022 20-F, the South Africa MLA expiry was April 2032 and the Nigeria MLAs expired in December 2024 and 2029, so the MLAs were extended in the meantime. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
7. Lease economics (FY2025): MLAs generally contain inflation-linked escalators. Some contracts are linked to the US dollar (with FX resets), and those in Côte d'Ivoire and Cameroon to the euro (CFA franc peg). In 2025, 32% of revenue from continuing operations was linked to the US dollar, 15% to the euro and 23% to the cost of power through indexation and pass-through clauses. Key customers' contracted revenue was USD 11.1 billion. Deferred tax recognition relies on MLAs with an average remaining term of 12 years and CPI-linked escalators. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
8. Portfolio pruning: IHS sold its 70% interest in IHS Kuwait (December 2024) and its Rwanda operations (October 2025). In February 2026 it agreed to sell its Latin American tower operations to Macquarie Asset Management, completed on 7 August 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4]
9. MTN merger:
   - on 17 February 2026 IHS signed an Agreement and Plan of Merger with MTN Group Limited and Mobile Telephone Networks (Netherlands) B.V. A merger sub will merge into IHS under Cayman Islands law, and IHS will become privately held;
   - price: USD 8.50 per ordinary share in cash, an enterprise value of about USD 6.2 billion (cash consideration about USD 2.2 billion, or USD 3.0 billion on a 100% basis, plus about USD 3.2 billion of net borrowings, lease liabilities and noncontrolling interests, less cash);
   - termination fees: USD 104.29 million payable by IHS in specified circumstances, and USD 148.98 million payable by MTN;
   - conditions include two-thirds shareholder approval, antitrust approvals, IHS holding cash of USD 998.1 million (subject to adjustment) towards the consideration, operating cash of at least USD 355 million, and gross debt under specified caps;
   - outside date: 17 November 2026, with automatic extensions. MTN and Wendel signed support agreements. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
10. Shareholders approved all merger proposals at an EGM on 4 August 2026. As of the 6-K of 9 September 2026, IHS was still filing as a public company and the merger had not closed. On 25 August 2026 IHS announced a sale of an additional USD 200 million of its 7.875% Senior Notes due 2030 and a conditional redemption notice. [confidence: high for approval and filings; medium for "not closed", inferred from continued filings] [source: 3, 5]

## Financing and structure details

- Carve-out mechanics: the seller (MTN) receives cash and signs a long MLA as anchor tenant. The buyer (IHS) books towers, customer intangibles and ground-lease right-of-use assets, funds the price with local-currency bank debt plus group equity, and grows by colocation. [source: 1]
- Local-currency acquisition debt: the ZAR facility (JIBAR + 2.75%, about 7-year tenor) matches the rand-denominated MTN SA lease revenue, which avoids the USD/ZAR mismatch on the acquisition. [source: 1]
- Group capital structure: IHS raises US dollar bonds at holding companies guaranteed by Nigerian and Mauritian subsidiaries (for example 2026, 2027, 2028, 2030 and 2031 notes) and has used a 2024 dual-tranche term loan with a ZAR tranche of ZAR 3,246.0 million. Lenders rely on USD-linked lease escalators to offset naira and other currency depreciation. [source: 2]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Concentration: MTN group companies are anchor tenants under MLAs in each of IHS's remaining African markets listed above (per-market revenue shares were not verified). The MTN merger turns a landlord-tenant relationship back into common ownership, which raises the question of what independent colocation economics are worth to an anchor tenant. [source: 2]
- FX: IHS's 20-F stresses inflation and FX resets as protection. The fact that 32% of revenue is USD-linked shows that a tower company can partly pass currency risk to tenants, though the tenant still bears it. [source: 2]
- Exit: the merger price is USD 8.50 against the USD 21 IPO price in 2021 (a share-price comparison, not a return calculation). [source: 1, 2]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 82 (Telecoms, fiber, towers and data centers):** Walk through the MTN South Africa deal as a carve-out template: purchase price allocation (tower assets against customer intangibles against ground leases), local-currency acquisition debt sized to the anchor MLA, BEE ownership conditions, and a managed-services add-on. Then use the 2026 MTN take-private to discuss how anchor-tenant concentration, MLA renewal (the MLA expiries moved out by about two years between the 2022 and 2025 filings) and FX-reset clauses drive tower valuations and lender comfort in emerging markets. Contrast with the US tower ABS structures in t-digital-norms.md.
