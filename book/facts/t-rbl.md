# Reserve-based lending (RBL)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest filing used: Kosmos Energy Form 10-K for 2025, filed 2 March 2026; Talos Energy amended and restated credit agreement dated 20 January 2026)

## Summary

A reserve-based loan is a secured revolving facility whose availability, the "borrowing base", is set by the lenders' own valuation of the borrower's oil and gas reserves and is reset at regular "redeterminations". Two documentary families exist. In the US onshore and Gulf of America market, the borrowing base is redetermined at least twice a year from a reserve report prepared at the agent bank's price deck (its forward curve), primarily on proved reserves. Increases need every lender's consent, while decreases need only the required lenders (two-thirds by commitment in the example below). A shortfall (a "borrowing base deficiency") must be cured within short fixed periods. In the international (London-law, Africa, North Sea and Asia) market, the borrowing base is set from a cash flow model agreed between the borrower and technical and modelling banks. It is the net present value of forecast field cash flows, haircut through cover ratios such as the field life cover ratio (FLCR) and loan life cover ratio (LLCR). It can also credit probable reserves and fields still in development. The public filings of Talos Energy (US) and Kosmos Energy (Ghana and Equatorial Guinea) show both models. Kosmos's 2009–2011 Jubilee facilities show how an international RBL can finance a field development before first oil (see upstream-field-pf.md).

## Verified facts

US-style RBL (Talos Energy, Gulf of America)
1. On 20 January 2026 Talos Production Inc. entered into an Amended and Restated Credit Agreement with a bank syndicate, with JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. as administrative agent. The initial borrowing base and total commitments were each USD 700 million, with a USD 250 million letter of credit sublimit. It replaced the credit agreement dated 10 May 2018. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. Maturity is the earlier of 20 January 2030 and the 91st day before the earliest stated maturity of Talos's junior lien notes due February 2029, unless those are refinanced or repaid first (a "springing maturity"). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. The agreement defines "Bank Price Deck" as the administrative agent's forward curve for oil, natural gas and other hydrocarbons, furnished to the borrower from time to time. Each reserve report must use the then-current bank price deck. The agent must provide an updated price deck within three business days of a request. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. Reserve reports are prepared as of 31 December and 30 June. The 31 December report must be prepared by approved independent petroleum engineers. The 30 June report may, at the borrower's election, be prepared in-house. Redetermined borrowing bases take effect on or about 30 April and 31 October each year. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
5. Between scheduled redeterminations, the borrower and the agent (at its discretion or at the direction of the required lenders) may each call one interim redetermination. The borrower may request further interim redeterminations after acquiring proved reserves with a PV-10 above 10% of the borrowing base then in effect. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. Approval thresholds: a proposed borrowing base that would increase the borrowing base must be approved by the "Borrowing Base Required Lenders", defined as non-defaulting lenders holding 100% of commitments. A proposal that would decrease or maintain it needs only the "Required Lenders", defined as at least 66⅔%. Lenders have 15 days to respond, silence is deemed approval, and failing approval the agent polls for the highest amount acceptable to the requisite group. Each lender acts "in good faith in accordance with [its] usual and customary oil and gas lending criteria as they exist at the particular time". [confidence: high] [source: 2]
7. Automatic adjustments: if hedges the lenders relied on are terminated or offset with a borrowing base value above 5% of the borrowing base (7.5% when aggregated with asset dispositions), the required lenders may reduce the borrowing base by that value. Dispositions of borrowing base properties are treated similarly. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
8. The borrower may elect, within three business days of a new borrowing base notice, a lower borrowing base until the next scheduled redetermination. A borrower typically does this to reduce commitment fees or covenant pressure. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
9. Talos's 2025 10-K describes how a deficiency can be cured. The borrower may (i) repay within 30 days, (ii) mortgage additional properties acceptable to the banks within 30 days, (iii) pay in four equal monthly instalments starting within 30 days, or (iv) combine these. It must elect within 10 days. [confidence: high; describes the facility as summarised in the 10-K] [source: 1]
10. Talos states that reductions in the borrowing base can follow from lower reserve estimates, "downward adjustments to the lender's internal price deck" and ongoing production. The facility's margin rises with borrowing base utilisation. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. Under the facility in place through 2025, Talos's borrowing base and commitments were reduced to USD 700 million by a "Borrowing Base Redetermination Agreement and Twelfth Amendment" dated 4 August 2025. That facility was secured by mortgages covering at least 85% of the company's oil and gas assets. It also carried a total debt to EBITDAX covenant and a minimum current ratio of 1.0x. Undrawn commitments count as current assets in that ratio. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

International-style RBL (Kosmos Energy, Ghana and Equatorial Guinea)
12. Kosmos's "Facility" is a revolving credit and letter of credit facility. The borrowing base is determined every March and September. It is based on "the sum of the net present values of net cash flows and relevant capital expenditures reduced by certain percentages" plus value attributed to reserves and/or resources in the Jubilee and TEN fields (Ghana) and Ceiba/Okume (Equatorial Guinea). [confidence: high] [source: 3]
13. In the September 2025 redetermination the lenders approved a borrowing base equal to the full facility size of USD 1.35 billion. At 31 December 2025 USD 1.2 billion was drawn. In February 2026 Kosmos prepaid USD 100 million from a Nordic bond. Final maturity is 31 December 2029. From 1 April 2027 outstanding borrowings are constrained by an amortisation schedule. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
14. Kosmos's covenants: FLCR at least 1.50x; LLCR at least 1.10x through 30 September 2027 and 1.30x thereafter; interest cover at least 2.25x; debt cover (net debt/EBITDAX) at most 3.50x. Lenders relaxed the debt cover test to 4.0x and 4.25x for September 2025 and March 2026 (July 2025 amendment), then to 4.5x and 4.25x for March and September 2026 (February 2026 amendment). [confidence: high] [source: 3]
15. Kosmos defines FLCR as the NPV of forecast net cash flow through depletion, plus the NPV of certain forecast capex, divided by loans outstanding. LLCR uses the same numerator but only through the facility's final maturity. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
16. Kosmos's margin is 4.00% to 5.50% over term SOFR, stepping up with time since signing. Commitment fees are 30% of the applicable margin on available commitments and 20% on unavailable commitments. If debt cover exceeds 2.50x, Kosmos must hold restricted cash for the next six months' interest; lenders waived this for 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
17. In 2011 Kosmos's then-new USD 2.0 billion facility had technical and modelling banks: Société Générale (lead) and Crédit Agricole CIB (co-technical and modelling), with HSBC as co-technical bank and BNP Paribas as facility and security agent. Margin was 3.25%–4.75% over LIBOR; covenants were FLCR at least 1.30x and LLCR at least 1.10x. The borrowing base was set each 15 June and 15 December. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

## Financing and structure details

- **Collateral and structure.** In the US, lenders take mortgages over a high share of properties (85% for Talos), with guarantees from the parent and subsidiaries. The facility ranks first, and second-lien notes sit behind it on the same collateral (Talos's 9.000% and 9.375% notes). Internationally, lenders take security over the borrowing subsidiaries and their field interests. Unsecured senior notes rank effectively junior and receive subordinated guarantees from the RBL obligors (Kosmos). [source: 1, 3]
- **US versus international, as shown by these filings:** see the table below.

| Feature | US RBL (Talos) | International RBL (Kosmos) |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation basis | Proved reserves at the bank price deck, via engineer reports | Agreed cash flow model, NPV with cover-ratio haircuts; can credit resources and development capex |
| Redetermination timing | Semi-annual (April and October), plus interim redeterminations | Semi-annual (March and September) |
| Who sets the number | Agent proposal, then lender vote (100% to increase, 66⅔% to decrease) | Agreed with technical and modelling banks |
| Covenants | Corporate (leverage, current ratio) | FLCR, LLCR, interest cover, debt cover |
| Tenor | About 4 years revolving, with a springing maturity | Revolving with a scheduled amortisation phase |

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Talos itself identifies the procyclical mechanism: lower prices feed the price deck, cut the borrowing base and shrink liquidity just when cash flow falls. Lenders also re-underwrite each cycle, so availability depends on bank appetite as well as on the reserves. [source: 1]
- The Kosmos 2025–2026 amendments show the international route to flexibility: negotiated covenant resets rather than a forced deficiency cure. They were linked, according to Kosmos, to lower oil prices and start-up costs at Greater Tortue Ahmeyim. [source: 3]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 75.** Teach RBL as asset-based revolving project-style credit. Walk through the calculation (NPV at the bank deck, haircut, then cover ratios), the vote mechanics and the cure options, using the Talos clauses. Contrast US proved-reserve RBLs with international model-based RBLs that can fund development (Kosmos/Jubilee). Then show a stress case: a price deck cut that turns availability into a deficiency.
