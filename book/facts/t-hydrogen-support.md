# Hydrogen support schemes: UK HAR1 results and contracts, EU Hydrogen Bank auctions, US 45V final rules (January 2025) and the 2025 law change

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: European Commission update on the IF25 Hydrogen Auction, page dated 19 June 2026, and draft terms for a fourth (IF26) auction published 9 July 2026; IF24 final grant awards, 20 January 2026; UK HAR1 contract signature announcement, 23 July 2025; One Big Beautiful Bill Act, Public Law 119-21, 4 July 2025)

Cross-references: facts/neom-hydrogen.md (NEOM financing and Air Products' 2026 retreat), facts/t-frontier-data.md section E (IEA hydrogen FID data and Stegra), facts/t-us-tax-credits.md (OBBBA overview, transferability, FEOC rules; this sheet does not repeat them), facts/t-uk-cfd-rab.md (the CfD model that HAR1 borrows from).

## Summary

Low-carbon hydrogen costs more to make than the grey hydrogen or natural gas it replaces, so every serious programme pays the producer a subsidy linked to output. The three big schemes do it differently, and the differences are the lesson. The UK's Hydrogen Allocation Round 1 (HAR1) uses a negotiated, CfD-style Low Carbon Hydrogen Agreement (LCHA). It runs for 15 years, pays the gap between a strike price and a reference price, and puts a gas-price-linked floor under the producer's sales price. HAR1 selected 11 electrolytic projects (125 MW) in December 2023 at a volume-weighted average strike price of GBP 241/MWh (GBP 175/MWh in 2012 prices). Ten had signed by July 2025, after a delay of more than a year from the planned Q1 2024 award. The EU's Hydrogen Bank instead runs pay-as-bid auctions for a fixed premium per kilogram, paid for up to 10 years on certified output and funded from EU ETS revenues. The pilot (IF23) cleared at EUR 0.37–0.48/kg. The second round (IF24) showed heavy attrition: 15 projects were selected in May 2025, but only six signed grant agreements (up to EUR 270.6 million) by January 2026. The third (IF25) selected nine, later 13, projects in 2026 at EUR 0.44–3.49/kg across three topics. The US took a tax route. Section 45V pays up to USD 3.00/kg (before inflation adjustment) for 10 years, tiered by lifecycle emissions. Treasury's final rules of January 2025 kept the "three pillars" for grid power (incrementality, temporal matching, deliverability), with hourly matching from 1 January 2030. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) of 4 July 2025 then cut the construction-start deadline from 1 January 2033 to 1 January 2028.

## Verified facts

### A. United Kingdom: HAR1 and the Low Carbon Hydrogen Agreement (where to find the current rule: gov.uk "Hydrogen production business model" and "Hydrogen Allocation Rounds" collections; signed contracts on the Low Carbon Contracts Company (LCCC) website)

1. HAR1 was launched in July 2022 and offered joint Hydrogen Production Business Model (HPBM) revenue support and Net Zero Hydrogen Fund (NZHF) capex support. NZHF grant intensity was capped at 20% of capex. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. Funnel: 20 projects were shortlisted; 17 (262 MW) were invited to negotiations; 15 (243 MW) submitted best and final offers and 2 withdrew; 11 (125 MW) were successful (announced 14 December 2023). The planned timetable had "Award of Low Carbon Hydrogen Agreement – Q1 2024". [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
3. The 11 projects were Barrow Green Hydrogen (Carlton Power, 21.0 MW), Bradford Low Carbon Hydrogen (Hygen, 24.5 MW), Cromarty Hydrogen (ScottishPower and Storegga, 10.6 MW), Green Hydrogen 3 (HYRO, 10.6 MW), HyBont (Marubeni Europower, 5.2 MW), HyMarnham (JG Pears and GeoPura, 9.3 MW), Langage Green Hydrogen (Carlton Power, 7.0 MW), Tees Green Hydrogen (EDF Renewables Hydrogen, 5.2 MW), Trafford Green Hydrogen (Carlton Power, 10.5 MW), West Wales Hydrogen (MorGen Energy and Trafigura, 14.2 MW) and Whitelee Green Hydrogen (ScottishPower, 7.1 MW). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. The weighted average strike price was GBP 241/MWh (GBP 175/MWh in 2012 prices). It was weighted by expected lifetime contract volumes, and the government noted that the subsidy varies with the natural gas reference price. HAR1 was expected to provide over GBP 2 billion of HPBM revenue support (paid once projects operate) and over GBP 90 million of NZHF capex grants. The government cited GBP 413 million of private capital to be invested in 2024–2026. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
5. On 23 July 2025 the government announced that 10 HAR1 projects had signed LCHAs with LCCC, which it described as the UK's first Low Carbon Hydrogen Agreements. It said it was working with the developer of the final (eleventh) project so that it could sign "as soon as possible". The 10 signatories did not include HyBont. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
6. LCHA term: the contract runs from signature to the 15th anniversary of the earlier of the Start Date and the end of a 12-month Target Commissioning Window. If the plant is late, the 15-year term starts to erode, although payments begin only from the Start Date. The contract also ends early if cumulative volumes reach the LCHA Sales Cap. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
7. LCHA payment mechanics (HAR1, December 2024 terms):
   - when the strike price exceeds the reference price for qualifying volumes, LCCC pays the producer the difference times the qualifying volumes; when the reference price exceeds the strike price, the producer pays back;
   - the reference price for qualifying volumes is the higher of the producer's achieved sales price and a floor price;
   - the floor price is the lower of the strike price and the Gas Reference Price (the monthly average of month-ahead natural gas settlement prices, floored at zero). For feedstock users the alternative floor is the lower of the strike price and 1.2 times the Gas Reference Price;
   - a Price Discovery Incentive pays the producer a share of any amount by which its achieved price exceeds the floor;
   - the strike price is negotiated project by project and indexed to CPI. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
8. HAR2: applications closed in spring 2024. On 7 April 2025 the government shortlisted 27 electrolytic projects across England, Scotland and Wales for due diligence and cost assessment. HAR1 guidance had said HAR2 aimed to support up to 875 MW. No HAR2 contract awards were found on gov.uk as of this sheet's date. [confidence: high for the shortlist; medium for the absence of awards] [source: 5, 1]
9. The June 2025 Spending Review confirmed an extra GBP 500 million for the first hydrogen transport and storage network, according to the July 2025 announcement. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### B. European Union: European Hydrogen Bank auctions (where to find the current rule: European Commission DG CLIMA Innovation Fund "Competitive bidding" and "Calls for proposals" pages; CINEA runs the auctions)

10. Design: Innovation Fund auctions are the domestic pillar of the European Hydrogen Bank. Bidders bid a fixed premium in EUR per kg. Bids are ranked from lowest to highest and awarded until the budget is used up (pay-as-bid). The premium is paid for up to 10 years on certified and verified production only, with no pre-financing. Funding comes from EU ETS allowance auction revenues. Under "Auctions-as-a-Service", EEA states can fund ranked projects on their territory from national budgets. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
11. IF23 (pilot, RFNBO hydrogen):
    - opened 23 November 2023 and closed 8 February 2024, with 132 bids from 17 countries (119 eligible);
    - bids ranged from EUR 0.37/kg to the EUR 4.50/kg ceiling;
    - seven projects were selected for nearly EUR 720 million. One withdrew to avoid missing the entry-into-operation deadline. Six signed grant agreements by 7 October 2024 for about EUR 694 million over 10 years, with bids of EUR 0.37–0.48/kg and grants of EUR 8–245 million;
    - the six winners were eNRG Lahti (Finland, 90 MWe, EUR 0.37), Grey2Green-II (Petrogal, Portugal, 200 MWe, EUR 0.39), HYSENCIA (Spain, 35 MWe, EUR 0.48), SKIGA (Norway, 117 MWe, EUR 0.48), Catalina (Renato PtX, Spain, 500 MWe, EUR 0.48) and MP2X (Madoquapower 2X, Portugal, 500 MWe, EUR 0.48);
    - projects must start production within 5 years of signing. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
12. IF24:
    - opened 3 December 2024 with a EUR 1.2 billion budget, including EUR 200 million for projects with maritime off-takers, and drew 61 bids from 11 countries;
    - on 20 May 2025 the Commission selected 15 projects requesting EUR 992 million. The 12 general-topic projects bid EUR 0.20–0.60/kg and the three maritime projects EUR 0.45–1.88/kg;
    - by 16 September 2025 only eight of the 15 were proceeding, and 10 reserve-list projects had been invited;
    - by 20 January 2026 six projects (two maritime) had signed grant agreements for up to EUR 270.6 million in total, with bids of EUR 0.33–1.88/kg and grants of EUR 1.8–135.5 million for 5–200 MWe plants. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
13. IF25:
    - launched December 2025 with EUR 1.3 billion across three topics: RFNBO (EUR 600 million); RFNBO and/or electrolytic low-carbon hydrogen (EUR 400 million; the first time low-carbon hydrogen was eligible); and supply to maritime or aviation off-takers (EUR 300 million);
    - results published 7 May 2026: nine projects in seven countries, EUR 1.09 billion, almost 1.1 GW of electrolysis;
    - in June 2026 four reserve projects (EUR 147.73 million, 77 MW) were added, bringing the total to 13 and covering more than 95% of the budget;
    - selected bids ranged from EUR 0.44/kg (Cloudberry, Finland, 500 MWe, low-carbon topic) and EUR 0.57/kg (RFNBO general) to EUR 3.48–3.49/kg (two 12–12.5 MWe Norwegian maritime projects);
    - grant signature is expected September–November 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
14. A fourth hydrogen auction (IF26) is planned for December 2026 with a EUR 500 million budget. Draft terms were consulted on from 9 July to 24 August 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### C. United States: section 45V (where to find the current rule: 26 U.S.C. §45V as amended by Public Law 119-21; Treas. Reg. §§1.45V-1 to -6, T.D. published at 90 FR 2224, 10 January 2025)

15. Credit amount: for each kg of qualified clean hydrogen produced during the 10 years after the facility is placed in service, the credit is an applicable percentage of USD 0.60, inflation-adjusted. The percentage depends on lifecycle emissions (kg CO2e per kg H2):
    - 4.0 to 2.5: 20%;
    - under 2.5 to 1.5: 25%;
    - under 1.5 to 0.45: 33.4%;
    - under 0.45: 100%.

    The amount is multiplied by five if the prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements are met, giving a maximum of USD 3.00/kg before inflation adjustment. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
16. The final regulations were published in the Federal Register on 10 January 2025 (90 FR 2224) and took effect that day. They cover lifecycle emissions under the 45VH2-GREET model, provisional emissions rates, verification, modified facilities, the use of energy attribute certificates (EACs) for grid power, and the election to take the §48 investment credit instead. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
17. Grid electricity is counted as clean only through qualifying EACs that meet the three pillars:
    - incrementality: generally, generation that began commercial operation no more than 36 months before the hydrogen facility was placed in service, with alternative pathways;
    - temporal matching: annual matching is allowed until the end of 2029, and hourly matching applies to electricity generated on or after 1 January 2030 (the 2023 proposal had required hourly matching from 2028);
    - deliverability: the generation must be in the same region.

    The final rules also deem electricity in states with a qualifying GHG cap and clean-electricity standard to meet incrementality; California and Washington qualified at publication. They add pathways for at-risk nuclear plants and carbon-capture retrofits. [confidence: high for hourly-from-2030, the 36-month window and the qualifying states; medium for the precise scope of the nuclear and CCS pathways] [source: 10]
18. The 2025 law change: Section 70511 of Public Law 119-21 (enacted 4 July 2025) amended §45V(c)(3)(C) by replacing "January 1, 2033" with "January 1, 2028". A facility must now begin construction before 1 January 2028 to qualify. The rest of the credit (amounts, tiers, 10-year period) was left in place. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

### D. Drivers that move the numbers

19. Support levels reflect: the delivered electricity price and load factor (power is the largest electrolytic cost); electrolyser capex and FX; the counterfactual fuel price (the UK floor is tied to gas, so the subsidy falls when gas rises); offtaker credit and volume risk; and allocation design. Pay-as-bid auctions with no pre-financing produced very low bids, followed by attrition (IF24: 15 selected, six signed). Negotiated contracts produced higher prices but bankable, project-specific terms. [confidence: medium (analytical synthesis of the cited facts)] [source: 2, 4, 7, 8]

## Financing and structure details

- UK LCHA: a private-law contract with LCCC, the government-owned counterparty that also runs CfDs. It is a two-way difference payment against a strike price indexed to CPI for 15 years, with a gas-linked floor that protects the producer if it sells cheaply. A sales cap limits total subsidised volume. The 20% NZHF capex grant was available alongside. Lenders gain a creditworthy counterparty and indexation. They still carry volume and offtaker risk, because only qualifying sales to eligible offtakers earn the difference. [source: 2, 4]
- EU Hydrogen Bank: a fixed premium per kg (not a CfD), so the producer keeps merchant price risk on the hydrogen itself. Payments are output-only for up to 10 years, so construction and ramp-up must be financed elsewhere. Commercial operation must start within five years of signing. [source: 6, 7]
- US 45V: a federal tax credit for 10 years, monetised through transferability or elective pay (see facts/t-us-tax-credits.md). Eligibility depends on emissions accounting each year, so EAC procurement and the GREET model version are financing risks. [source: 10]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- UK HAR1: the government's own announcements show the timetable slipped. Award was planned for Q1 2024, and 10 of 11 contracts were reported signed only in July 2025. [source: 1, 3]
- EU auctions: the Commission's pages record that selected projects drop out. One of seven IF23 winners withdrew over the operation deadline, and only six of 15 initially selected IF24 projects (plus reserve-list substitutes) reached signature. Low premiums from auctions without pre-financing, followed by withdrawals, are a pattern the reader should test against later rounds. [source: 7, 8]
- US: the final rules relaxed the start of hourly matching to 2030 but kept the three pillars. Congress then cut the construction window by five years, which shortens the time to reach FID for projects relying on the credit. [source: 10, 11]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 83 (Hydrogen and derivatives, CCS and sustainable fuels):** Lay the three schemes side by side as revenue models: a two-way CfD with a gas-linked floor (UK), a one-way fixed premium per kg won at auction (EU), and a tiered tax credit (US). Ask which risks each leaves with the project: price, volume, emissions accounting, timing. Use the IF24 attrition figures and the HAR1 delay to show that headline awards are not financial closes. Have students work out what GBP 241/MWh means per kg (an MWh of HHV hydrogen is roughly 25 kg; compute it rather than quoting a figure).
- **Chapter 88 (The frontier):** Use hydrogen as the case for "policy as the revenue line". Every support scheme changed within three years: the hourly-matching date moved, the US construction deadline was cut by five years, and the EU added low-carbon and maritime topics. A lender must underwrite rule change, not just a rule. Pair with the IEA FID data in t-frontier-data.md.

## Do not state

- That HAR1 contracts were awarded in Q1 2024 (that was the plan), or that all 11 HAR1 projects have signed. As at the July 2025 announcement, 10 had signed. The status of the eleventh (HyBont) after that date was not verified.
- Any HAR2 winners, strike prices or capacity awarded. Only the April 2025 shortlist of 27 was verified.
- The exact date the IF23 results were published. The Commission page's timeline shows "30/11/2024", which conflicts with the grant signature date of 7 October 2024 on the same page. It is commonly reported as April 2024, but that was not verified here.
- A national "Auctions-as-a-Service" total for IF24 (the Commission page shows a figure that appears garbled).
- Strike prices for individual HAR1 projects (not published in the sources read).
- That 45V is "repealed". It was shortened (construction before 1 January 2028), not repealed.
- Precise details of the 45V nuclear and CCS EAC pathways beyond the outline above. Read the regulation text before stating them.
