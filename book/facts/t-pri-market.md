# Political risk insurance (PRI) market: MIGA, ECA PRI, private PRI (Lloyd's/Berne Union data), cover types, waiting periods, claims history

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest data: Berne Union State of the Industry Report 2025, published 24 June 2026, covering calendar 2025; MIGA Operational Policies 2023 as posted May 2024; MIGA website accessed 2026-10-03)

## Summary

Political risk insurance (PRI), which the Berne Union calls investment insurance, protects cross-border equity and debt against losses from government action or political events. The Berne Union lists the covered perils as:
- inconvertibility and non-transfer of currency;
- political violence;
- confiscation, expropriation or nationalization;
- trade embargo, selective discrimination, forced abandonment and forced divestiture;
- non-repossession of assets;
- breach of contract or default on an arbitration award;
- typically, sovereign default where applicable.

There are three groups of providers:
- **public:** national export credit agencies (ECAs) and investment insurers;
- **multilateral:** MIGA (World Bank Group), ICIEC, ATI, the Inter-Arab Investment Guarantee Corporation (Dhaman), and the regional development banks' guarantee products;
- **private:** insurers and Lloyd's syndicates based mainly in London, Bermuda and New York, backed by reinsurers such as Munich Re, Hannover Re, Swiss Re and Berkshire Hathaway/General Re.

Berne Union members wrote USD 34.5 billion of new PRI cover in 2025, down 18.4 percent. ECAs wrote USD 21.9 billion, private insurers USD 7.5 billion and multilaterals USD 5.1 billion. PRI is small next to the roughly USD 3.7 trillion of trade the members supported overall. PRI claims are rare and lumpy: USD 26 million in 2020, USD 681 million in 2024 and USD 2.8 billion in 2025. The 2025 figure was driven by one USD 2.6 billion Russia-related expropriation claim. Waiting periods are written into each contract. For example, MIGA's Operational Policies deem a total expropriation loss after the investor has been unable to exercise a covered right, or the project has ceased operations, for 365 consecutive days, "or such other period as the contract of guarantee may provide". MIGA paid its first claim, USD 15 million for a power project in Indonesia, at the end of fiscal 2000. For MIGA's institutional details, products and guarantee volumes, see t-dfis.md. For lender insurance generally, see t-insurance-market.md.

## Verified facts

**Definitions and perils (Berne Union, current)**

1. The Berne Union defines PRI as "Insurance or Guarantee covering a cross-border investment against political risks." In its claims data, "Political" claims cover:
   - inconvertibility/non-transfer;
   - political violence;
   - confiscation/expropriation/nationalization;
   - trade embargo;
   - selective discrimination;
   - forced abandonment;
   - forced divestiture;
   - non-repossession of an asset;
   - breach of contract or arbitration award default;
   - "typically" sovereign default where applicable. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The Berne Union describes investment insurance as protecting cross-border equity and debt against political risks including expropriation, political violence, currency inconvertibility, embargo, forced abandonment or breach of contract. It names MIGA, ICIEC, ATI and Dhaman as multilateral providers, and Lloyd's syndicates and the specialist divisions of global insurers as the private market. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

**Market size and composition (Berne Union members, calendar years)**

3. New PRI cover by Berne Union members (USD billion; Table 1):

   | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 45 | 39 | 34 | 42 | 41 | 42 | 35 |

   Total trade supported across all product lines was USD 3,710 billion in 2025. The Berne Union website separately says members provide "around USD 2.5 trillion" a year of payment-risk protection. The two figures are measured differently. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
4. In 2025:
   - PRI new cover fell 18.4 percent to USD 34.5 billion, "extending a period of subdued demand";
   - ECA new cover fell 27.3 percent to USD 21.9 billion;
   - private insurer new cover fell 10.4 percent to USD 7.5 billion, despite more members;
   - multilateral cover rose 33.5 percent to USD 5.1 billion, all of the growth coming from one member, mostly by covering commercial banks against expropriation of mandatory reserves held at national banks;
   - one ECA discontinued its PRI product and another saw its PRI exposure run off completely. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. 2025 new PRI cover by sector and region:
   - **Sectors:** manufacturing USD 11.4 billion (down 11.1 percent); natural resources USD 8.0 billion; energy USD 2.0 billion and infrastructure USD 2.2 billion. Energy and infrastructure each "roughly halved" to the lowest level since the sector split began. Renewable energy was USD 2.4 billion, flat at USD 2.4–2.7 billion for four years.
   - **Regions:** East APAC was the largest recipient at USD 12.7 billion; LATAM and Caribbean USD 7.6 billion. Russia and CIS was the only region to grow, up 40 percent to USD 1.5 billion, led by Uzbekistan at USD 888 million. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. The Berne Union notes that private insurers continue to add PRI capacity "even as overall PRI business remains subdued despite a heightened perception of political risk". [confidence: high] [source: 3]
7. The Berne Union was founded in 1934. In 2013 it had 86 members, including Prague Club members; the Prague Club was started in 1993 with EBRD funding. In June 2026 it announced Africa Specialty Risks, Canopius and Chaucer as new members. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 3]

**Private market capacity and structure (historical benchmark, 2010–2013)**

8. MIGA (WIPR 2013) describes private PRI providers as based mainly in London, Bermuda and the United States, chiefly New York. Brokers play an important role, and reinsurance is "an underlying factor driving both pricing and capacity". Only a small number of Lloyd's syndicates offer investment insurance. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

   [confidence: high for the table] [source: 4]
10. MIGA (2013) reported about 40 PRI underwriters in the private market. New entrants included ANV (Lloyd's, which began PRI underwriting in March 2013) and Brit. Chubb exited PRI in 2010 and QBE in 2011, and their run-offs paid all obligations. Market participants said risk appetite remained skewed to medium tenors, and that multilaterals and ECAs dominated the longest tenors. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
11. MIGA (2009) records how private PRI products evolved. Since September 11, political violence policies cover the full spectrum from terrorism to civil war. Many insurers offer non-honoring or non-payment cover for sovereign guarantors and borrowers without requiring the investor to obtain an arbitral award, which breach-of-contract cover requires. [confidence: high] [source: 5]

**Cover types (MIGA as the reference product set; current website and Operational Policies)**

12. **Currency inconvertibility and transfer restriction.**
    - Covers losses from an investor's inability to legally convert local currency into hard currency (USD, EUR or JPY) or to transfer it out of the host country, where this results from government action or failure to act. The local-currency amounts covered include capital, interest, principal, profits and royalties.
    - Currency depreciation is not covered.
    - Coverage may extend to "passive" as well as active restrictions.
    - The freezing of assets is not covered under this heading; it falls under expropriation.
    - The insured must apply for conversion through legal channels and pursue administrative remedies. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
13. **Expropriation.**
    - Covers outright nationalization, confiscation and "creeping" expropriation.
    - Partial expropriation is covered on a limited basis.
    - Compensation for total expropriation of equity is based on net book value; for loans, it is outstanding principal and accrued interest, paid on assignment of the investor's interest to MIGA.
    - Non-discriminatory, bona fide general regulation, including taxes, tariffs, price controls, and environmental and labour laws, is excluded unless it is discriminatory or designed to be confiscatory.
    - Legislative omissions and decisions of independent courts or arbitral tribunals are not covered measures. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
14. **War and civil disturbance.**
    - Covers loss of, damage to, or destruction of tangible assets, or total business interruption, caused by politically motivated war, revolution, insurrection, coups, sabotage or terrorism.
    - Temporary business interruption cover is available on request.
    - Asset losses are compensated at the lesser of replacement cost or repair cost, or at book value if the assets are not replaced. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
15. **Breach of contract and non-honoring.**
    - Breach of contract requires the investor first to invoke the contract's dispute mechanism.
    - Non-honoring of sovereign financial obligations does not.

    See t-dfis.md, items 21–24. [confidence: high] [source: t-dfis.md]
16. **Waiting periods (MIGA Operational Policies 2023).**
    - For expropriation, a total loss "may be deemed to have occurred" if the guarantee holder has been unable to exercise a fundamental covered right for 365 consecutive days, or the project has ceased operations for that period, "or such other period as the contract of guarantee may provide".
    - The same 365-day default applies to non-equity direct investments where measures prevent the project enterprise from paying the guarantee holder. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
17. **Why long waiting periods exist.** MIGA (WIPR 2011) observes that "most PRI contracts covering expropriation have embedded long waiting periods" that give negotiated settlements time to mature. This matters especially in democracies, where measures taken by legislatures or sub-national bodies are often settled once the executive engages. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
18. **Tenor (MIGA Operational Policies 2023).** The guarantee period must be more than one year and not more than 15 years. In special circumstances it may be extended to up to 20 years. MIGA's website FAQ gives a three-year minimum; see t-dfis.md item 25. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
19. **ECA product grading.** Under the OECD Arrangement, ECA insurance without cover for interest during the claims waiting period is graded "below-standard" for minimum premium purposes. See t-oecd-arrangement.md item 23. [confidence: high] [source: t-oecd-arrangement.md]

**Claims history**

20. **MIGA's first claim.** MIGA paid its first claim at the end of fiscal 2000 (the year to June 2000). The USD 15 million claim was for a MIGA-guaranteed power project in Indonesia. In fiscal 2001 Indonesia agreed to repay the claim, and MIGA reopened cover in Indonesia case by case. Before then, MIGA had resolved all potential claims through its good offices. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
21. **MIGA pre-claims.** MIGA (2013) reported that since its founding "just over 25 years ago", over 100 guaranteed projects had experienced difficulties that could have led to a claim ("pre-claim" events), 32 of them in the preceding seven years. The three main factors in recent breach-of-contract pre-claims were financing difficulties, inconsistent public policy and tariff disputes, especially in power. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
22. **Berne Union investment claims, 1996–2008.** Members paid claims of around USD 795 million, with recoveries averaging around 45 percent. Lloyd's syndicates were reported to have paid about USD 608 million of expropriation claims over the same period. [confidence: high, as reported by MIGA] [source: 5]
23. **Berne Union investment and political risk claims, 2010–2012.**
    - 2010: USD 221 million, about ten times 2009, giving an aggregate loss ratio of 20.5 percent.
    - 2011: USD 179 million, about half (USD 91 million) for political violence, mainly in MENA.
    - 2012: USD 125 million, of which political violence was USD 43 million (about 34 percent).

    MIGA attributes the 2010 high to the global financial crisis. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 8]
24. **Berne Union PRI claims paid (USD million; Table 7):**

    | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | 92 | 26 | 182 | 412 | 152 | 681 | 2,801 |

    Total claims across all product lines were USD 11,107 million in 2025, the highest on record. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
25. **2025 claims drivers.** A single USD 2.6 billion PRI claim (expropriation or indirect expropriation) "single-handedly pushed claims higher than 2024 levels". The Berne Union links the jump in investment insurance claims to "the aforementioned Russia-related expropriation claim". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
26. **Claims behaviour.** MIGA (2009) wrote that "historically, there have been very few claims each year", forming a small share of members' maximum liability. It also noted that the Argentine crisis "raised questions about the value of PRI, but also demonstrated that the industry paid claims". [confidence: high] [source: 5]

## Financing and structure details

- **Who buys PRI in project finance.** Equity investors (for their equity and shareholder loans), commercial lenders (often for loan principal and interest, through MIGA or ECA cover), and hedge providers. For worked examples see azura-edo.md (MIGA cover for lenders, hedges and equity), oyu-tolgoi.md (a USD 1 billion MIGA guarantee for commercial lenders), nam-theun-2.md and bujagali.md (breach-of-contract cover).
- **Co-insurance and reinsurance.** Private insurers use co-insurance and reinsurance with ECAs and multilaterals to extend tenors and reach harder markets. MIGA also reinsures and is reinsured. [source: 4, 6]
- **Capacity limits.** Benchmark private per-risk capacity (2013) fell steeply with tenor: about USD 1.7 billion at three years against USD 0.44 billion at 15 years. That is why long project-finance debt usually needs a multilateral or ECA at the long end. [source: 4]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Right: claims are paid and often recovered.** Berne Union recoveries averaged around 45 percent in 1996–2008, and MIGA recovered its first claim from Indonesia. The "deterrence" or halo effect of multilateral cover is part of the product (MIGA, 2006; see t-political-risk-theory.md). [source: 5, 9]
- **Wrong or limiting: the gaps in cover.** Currency depreciation is never covered, only convertibility and transfer. General regulation, including taxes, is excluded from expropriation cover. Breach-of-contract cover may require an arbitral award, which takes years. Long waiting periods delay recovery. Lenders must therefore pair PRI with structural protections such as offshore accounts, liquidity facilities and indexation. [source: 6, 7, 8]
- **Market cyclicality.** Large single events dominate PRI claims: 2025's single expropriation claim was 93 percent of PRI claims that year. New private capacity can be "fickle" after unexpected losses (MIGA 2013, reporting market participants). [source: 1, 4]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 27 (insurance).** Place PRI next to CAR, DSU and operational covers as the lender's protection against the sovereign. Teach the perils list (Berne Union definition), what each cover pays (net book value for equity, principal and interest for loans) and the exclusions, above all depreciation and bona fide regulation. Explain why lenders require assignment of PRI proceeds just as they do for property insurance.
- **Chapter 60 (political risk and its protection).** Use the market data to show who supplies what. ECAs write most PRI by volume. Private insurers are agile but tenor-constrained. Multilaterals cover the longest tenors and bring the halo effect. Use the claims record (rare, lumpy, often recovered) and the 365-day expropriation waiting period to discuss how far PRI really protects a project's cash flow, compared with a balance sheet after the event.

## Do not state

- Do not state a standard waiting period for transfer restriction (often quoted as 180 days) or for political violence as a MIGA or market rule. It was not verified here; it is set in each contract.
- Do not state current private-market capacity per risk. The latest verified figures are from July 2013. More recent broker reports (for example WTW, Gallagher or Marsh) were not checked.
- Do not repeat the "USD 1.7 trillion" private capacity figure in the WIPR 2013 text. It is inconsistent with that report's own table (USD 1,742 million at three-year tenor) and appears to be an error for USD 1.7 billion.
- Do not identify the investor, project or country behind the 2025 USD 2.6 billion expropriation claim, or the provider that paid it. The Berne Union only calls it "Russia-related".
- Do not name the Indonesian power project behind MIGA's first claim. The annual report does not name it here.
- Do not give a total for MIGA claims paid since inception. It was not verified.
- Do not treat Berne Union figures as the whole market. They exclude non-member private insurers and much of Lloyd's.
