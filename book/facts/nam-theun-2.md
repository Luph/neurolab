# Nam Theun 2 Hydropower Project (Lao PDR)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: October 2024, EDF–Lao government MoU on a nearby pumped-storage study; latest independent evaluation evidence: World Bank IEG and ADB IED ex post evaluations, December 2020)

## Summary

Nam Theun 2 (NT2) is a 1,070 MW trans-basin diversion hydropower plant on the Nakai Plateau in central Lao PDR, built under a 25-year build-own-operate-transfer concession by Nam Theun 2 Power Company (NTPC), a project company owned by Électricité de France (EDF) interests, Thailand's EGCO and the Lao government. About 95 percent of its output is exported to Thailand under a take-or-pay power purchase agreement (PPA) with the Electricity Generating Authority of Thailand (EGAT), paid half in US dollars and half in Thai baht. Financial close was reached in June 2005 with 26 financiers, including the World Bank Group, the Asian Development Bank (ADB), the European Investment Bank (EIB), Agence Française de Développement (AFD), export credit agencies (ECAs), and international and Thai commercial banks; dollar lenders would not lend without multilateral political risk cover, which came from an IDA partial risk guarantee, MIGA guarantees and an ADB political risk guarantee. The plant reached commercial operation in April 2010 with a 4.5-month delay and a cost overrun well inside its USD 200 million contingency, has produced above contracted volumes, and has never triggered the guarantees. The environmental and social (E&S) program, which resettled about 6,300 people and set aside a large biodiversity offset, was far more ambitious than the safeguards required; the independent evaluators judged resettlement and income restoration largely achieved after a two-year extension, but found the watershed offset poorly protected, downstream livelihoods only partly restored, and the revenue-earmarking arrangements of doubtful additionality.

## Verified facts

1. NT2 has a listed installed capacity of 1,070 MW, of which 995 MW is dedicated to export to Thailand and 75 MW to domestic supply in Lao PDR. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2, 3]
2. The PPAs contract annual export of 5,354 GWh to EGAT and supply of 200 GWh to Électricité du Laos (EDL). The 2005 MIGA brief describes EGAT capacity of up to 995 MW and 5,636 GWh per year at an agreed tariff on a take-or-pay basis; the later evaluations use 5,354 GWh as the contracted EGAT export volume. [confidence: high for 5,354/200 GWh as stated by IEG and ADB; medium for the reason for the difference from 5,636 GWh] [source: 1, 2, 3]
3. The facility comprises a dam on the Nam Theun river, a reservoir of approximately 450 km² on the Nakai Plateau, a powerhouse, approximately 138 km of 500 kV transmission line to the Thai border and approximately 70 km of 115 kV line to the Lao grid. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
4. NTPC was incorporated in 2002 under Lao law; the concession agreement with the Lao government was signed in 2002; the EGAT PPA was signed on 17 November 2003 and runs 25 years from the commercial operation date (COD). The concession term also runs 25 years from COD under the EGAT PPA, after which the plant transfers to the government. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3, 6, 9]
5. The project was structured as a limited-recourse public–private partnership on a BOOT basis. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
6. At PPA signing (2003) and financial close the shareholders were EDF International (35 percent), Italian-Thai Development (15 percent), EGCO (25 percent) and the Lao government's vehicle (25 percent; MIGA's 2005 brief names this vehicle Nam Theun 2 Power Investment Company, investing on behalf of EDL; it is now Lao Holding State Enterprise, LHSE). NTPC's current published shareholding is EDF Nam Theun Holding 40 percent, EGCO 35 percent and LHSE 25 percent. LHSE was established in February 2005 to hold the government's NT2 shares. [confidence: high for both structures; the date and terms of Italian-Thai Development's exit are not verified] [source: 3, 6, 9]
7. Appraisal base cost was approximately USD 1.25 billion, plus USD 200 million of contingent financing committed by lenders for overruns (USD 1.45 billion total including contingencies). [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2, 3]
8. The base cost was funded approximately 28 percent equity (about USD 350 million) and 72 percent debt; the contingent USD 200 million was to be funded 50:50 by equity and debt, pro rata. The IEG reports total debt raised at appraisal for base costs of USD 950 million; ADB describes loan facilities of about USD 900 million; MIGA (2005) gives USD 1,000 million of debt including contingencies. [confidence: high for the 72/28 split and USD 350 million equity; medium for any single debt total, since sources differ by basis] [source: 1, 2, 3]
9. The Lao government's equity contribution was USD 87 million, about 22.8 percent of total equity (per the ADB evaluation's financial analysis). ADB lent the government USD 20 million (sovereign loan from ordinary capital resources) toward that equity, and the World Bank provided a USD 20 million IDA grant. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
10. ADB also provided a non-sovereign loan to NTPC of up to USD 50 million without government guarantee, and a political risk guarantee of up to USD 50 million to support commercial debt. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
11. World Bank Group support comprised the USD 20 million IDA grant (Nam Theun 2 Social and Environment Project), an IDA guarantee of up to USD 50 million (Guarantee B-0080-LA) backstopping government obligations to private lenders, and MIGA guarantees (MIGA Guarantee 5139) of up to USD 200 million covering equity and debt in Lao PDR and Thailand. MIGA's 2005 issuance was USD 91 million, of which USD 86 million covered a non-shareholder loan by Fortis Bank (for itself and as agent for commercial banks) against expropriation, breach of contract, war and civil disturbance, and transfer restriction and inconvertibility in both Lao PDR and Thailand; the remainder covered EDF International against transfer restriction in Lao PDR. [confidence: high for the instruments and the USD 91/86 million figures; medium on how the "up to USD 200 million" ceiling relates to the USD 91 million issued, which the sources do not reconcile] [source: 1, 3]
12. MIGA's breach-of-contract cover responded to selected Lao government obligations under the concession agreement (termination payments to NTPC and lenders), obligations under the direct agreement with commercial lenders (recognition of lenders' rights, including subrogation), and lenders' rights on termination of the EGAT PPA and EGAT's obligations under its acknowledgement and consent and subordination agreement. Payment was conditioned on a final arbitral award that the lenders could not enforce within a specified waiting period. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
13. Debt guarantees from IDA, ADB and MIGA covered approximately USD 186 million of the debt financing; MIGA described multilateral political risk guarantees as a prerequisite for the dollar lenders' participation. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
14. The financing group comprised 26 financial institutions: four multilateral development banks (World Bank Group, ADB, EIB, Nordic Investment Bank), three ECAs, three bilateral agencies, nine international commercial banks and seven Thai commercial banks lending in baht. The nine dollar banks were ANZ, BNP Paribas, Bank of Tokyo-Mitsubishi, Fortis Bank, Crédit Agricole Indosuez, ING, KBC, Société Générale and Standard Chartered. ADB names ECAs from France, Norway, Sweden and Thailand among the financiers. [confidence: high for counts and named banks; medium for the identity of each ECA, which secondary sources give as COFACE, EKN and GIEK] [source: 1, 2, 4]
15. EIB signed a loan of EUR 45 million in 2005. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
16. The EGAT and EDL PPAs require payment for at least 95 percent of the energy declared and made available, whether or not taken (take-or-pay). Tariffs for primary and secondary energy are denominated and paid 50 percent in US dollars and 50 percent in Thai baht; the excess-energy tariff is entirely in baht. EDL takes only primary energy, at a different rate, also paid 50:50. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
17. ADB's appraisal team reduced foreign exchange risk by matching the debt currency profile to the tariff currency split, mitigated offtake and hydrology risk through take-or-pay terms with EGAT, and introduced a revenue stabilization mechanism for hydrological risk. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
18. The head construction contract between NTPC and EDF was a turnkey, price-capped EPC contract; the IEG notes it passed most overrun costs to the contractors. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
19. The World Bank Board approved its support on 31 March 2005; the World Bank operations became effective 10 June 2005 and closed as planned on 31 December 2017. Financial close was reached in June 2005. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
20. During construction the downstream channel collapsed in water-absorbent karst limestone; the developer drilled down several hundred meters to a granite formation to keep the original route. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
21. COD was April 2010, a delay of 4.5 months. The physical infrastructure overrun was under 2 percent of original estimates (IEG); ADB reports total capital cost at loan closure of USD 1,304.8 million, a 4.4 percent overrun, with USD 57 million drawn from the USD 200 million contingent financing, about 60 percent of it from equity. IEG reports actual total project cost of USD 1,308 million. [confidence: high; the different overrun percentages reflect different bases] [source: 2, 3]
22. From 2011 to 2018 average annual output was 5,912 GWh (IEG); ADB cites average output of 5,961 GWh. Both exceed contracted volumes. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
23. EGAT accounts for about 95 percent of sales and, according to NTPC as reported by IEG, has paid promptly; NTPC told evaluators it had not foreseen any risk that would invoke the guarantees. [confidence: high as a report of NTPC's statement] [source: 3]
24. IEG cites a levelized cost of NT2 supply to EGAT of 2.7 US cents/kWh against an average Thai system generation cost of 5.7 US cents/kWh (from Bucknall et al. 2014). [confidence: high as cited] [source: 3]
25. Resettlement: about 6,300 people (6,289 actual) from about 1,300 houses in 17 villages on the Nakai Plateau were relocated. The first wave of 600 families was moved to temporary housing in 2006, two years earlier than planned and before permanent housing and cleared plots were ready, contrary to the concession agreement; some families waited up to a year for permanent housing. Some project land was acquired before resettlement action plans were complete. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
26. Downstream project-affected people were estimated at 70,000 at appraisal; ex post estimates are about 100,000 (NTPC) to 115,000 (ADB). IEG reports that as many as half may not have restored livelihoods to pre-project levels, and found no documentation that the issue was resolved. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
27. The E&S Panel of Experts in April–May 2015 recommended a two-year extension of the resettlement implementation period (RIP) to end-2017; the RIP was closed in July 2018 (ADB) after the Panel concurred that income targets were met. EDF reported in September 2018 that 97 percent of resettlers had achieved their income targets. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3, 7]
28. Appraisal E&S cost estimates were exceeded: IEG reports component 2 (E&S impacts) at USD 48.8 million estimated versus USD 78.6 million actual; ADB reports E&S spending (excluding the watershed) of USD 70.4 million by COD against USD 38.4 million estimated, mainly from higher resettlement costs. [confidence: high; figures differ by scope] [source: 2, 3]
29. The Nakai-Nam Theun National Protected Area watershed (approximately 4,000 km²) was designated as a biodiversity offset; IEG judged its protection the project's most significant shortcoming, with ineffective institutions, limited capacity, inadequate monitoring and unrealistic targets. The reservoir emits more greenhouse gas than expected because of uncleared biomass, though IEG still finds a net climate benefit. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 7]
30. Revenue management: the government did not establish the agreed NT2 Revenue Account; a functional equivalent tagging NT2 revenues in the budget was developed only by 2015. Net government revenue was about USD 25 million a year in early operations (against a target of USD 30 million less government debt service), and more than USD 180 million in total by end-2017 (ADB: USD 180.4 million), under 1 percent of total government revenue. Dividends since 2013/14 have partly been retained in LHSE rather than transferred to the budget. IEG could not verify additionality of spending. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
31. Both evaluations rated the project positively overall: IEG rated outcome satisfactory; ADB's evaluation rated it successful for both sovereign and non-sovereign operations. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
32. In October 2024 EDF signed a memorandum of understanding with the Lao government to study a pumped-storage hydropower project near NT2 of up to 2,000 MW. [confidence: medium] [source: 10]

## Financing and structure details

- Project company: Nam Theun 2 Power Company Ltd (NTPC), Lao law; BOOT concession, 25 years from COD. [source: 1, 3]
- Base cost approximately USD 1.25 billion; USD 200 million contingent financing (50:50 equity and debt). Actual cost about USD 1.305–1.308 billion. [source: 1, 2, 3]
- Equity: approximately USD 350 million (28 percent of base cost). Lao government share USD 87 million, financed in part by a USD 20 million ADB sovereign loan and the USD 20 million IDA grant. [source: 2, 3]
- Debt: approximately USD 900–1,000 million depending on basis, from 26 institutions: MDBs (ADB USD 50 million non-sovereign loan; EIB EUR 45 million; Nordic Investment Bank; AFD), ECAs (France, Norway, Sweden, Thailand per ADB), bilateral agencies, nine international banks lending US dollars, seven Thai banks lending baht. Individual tranche sizes and tenors are not verified. [source: 1, 2, 4, 5]
- Political risk cover on dollar debt: IDA partial risk guarantee (up to USD 50 million; some summaries say USD 42 million, see Do not state), ADB political risk guarantee (up to USD 50 million), MIGA (USD 91 million issued in 2005; up to USD 200 million per IEG data sheet), plus ECA cover. About USD 186 million of debt was covered by IDA, ADB and MIGA guarantees. [source: 1, 2, 3]
- Revenue: two 25-year PPAs (EGAT, EDL) with 95 percent take-or-pay; primary and secondary energy 50 percent USD, 50 percent THB; excess energy in THB. Currency mix of debt matched to tariff currencies. [source: 2]
- Construction: turnkey, price-capped head construction contract with EDF. [source: 1]
- Monitoring: an independent Panel of Environmental and Social Experts (reports to the government), an International Advisory Group (advises World Bank management), a lenders' technical adviser, and Bank supervision. [source: 3, 4]

## What went wrong or right, and why

The IEG (2020) attributes the construction success to the choice of an experienced developer, an independent review of cost reasonableness at appraisal, a fixed-price PPA that penalized delay, and a head construction contract that pushed most overruns to the contractor; it contrasts NT2's 4.5-month delay and sub-contingency overrun with its finding that World Bank Group–supported hydropower projects on average ran more than 30 percent over budgeted contingencies and 14 months late. [source: 3]

The IEG's focus groups of financiers reported that they would not have participated without the World Bank Group and other international financial institutions, because Lao PDR had no recent hydropower financings (none since Theun-Hinboun and Houay Ho in 1996), little experience with private investment, and limited E&S oversight capacity; IDA, MIGA and ADB guarantees backstopped the government's commitments where little private insurance was available. [source: 3]

ADB's evaluation credits the appraisal team's risk mitigation: least-cost probabilistic analysis confirming competitiveness in the Thai market, currency matching between debt and tariff, EGAT take-or-pay, and a revenue stabilization mechanism for hydrology. [source: 2]

On E&S, the IEG concludes that the design went beyond institutional capability: resettlement timing was driven by the construction and impoundment schedule rather than readiness of sites; prescriptive livelihood "pillars" in the concession agreement prevented adaptive management; targets for the biodiversity offset were unrealistic; and the joint working group that resolved multiparty issues was set up only late. The IEG recommends distinguishing stretch targets from safeguard compliance and agreeing a multiparty coordination mechanism up front. [source: 3]

On revenue management, the IEG concludes that the government's commitment to its overall development program, not earmarking of NT2 revenues in a fungible budget, drove poverty reduction, and that the earmarking could not be shown to be additional. [source: 3]

Civil society critics, notably International Rivers, have argued that downstream impacts on the Xe Bang Fai were understated and that compensation was inadequate; these are advocacy positions and should be presented as such. [source: 8]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 50 (Environmental and social risk and standards): NT2 shows E&S as a schedule and credit item, not a side workstream. Teach the gap between a well-written resettlement plan and an impoundment-driven timetable (600 families moved early to temporary housing), the cost of E&S running at roughly 1.6 to 1.8 times appraisal estimates, and the IEG lesson that a concession agreement should separate safeguard compliance (measurable, enforceable) from stretch development targets. Use the Panel of Experts and RIP extension as a walkthrough of how lenders' independent monitors gate a project's E&S close-out.
- Chapter 72 (Hydropower and geothermal): NT2 is the model of an export hydro IPP in a weak host state selling to a creditworthy neighbor's utility. Teach the 95 percent take-or-pay design with primary, secondary and excess energy tiers as the device that converts hydrology risk into a bankable revenue line, the 50:50 USD/THB tariff matched to a USD/THB debt mix, and the turnkey price-capped EPC plus contingent financing as the construction-risk package; then use the karst-geology episode to show why geotechnical risk is the residual hydro risk lenders size contingency for.
- Cross-reference for Chapter 60 (political risk): MIGA's breach-of-contract cover conditioned on an unenforceable arbitral award is a clean worked example of how PRI responds to sovereign contract breach, and of the "multilateral halo." Use only facts 11 to 13.

## Do not state

- Do not give a single definitive debt figure: sources give USD 900 million (ADB), USD 950 million (IEG, base cost) and USD 1,000 million including contingencies (MIGA). State the basis if a figure is used.
- Do not state the IDA guarantee as USD 42 million without qualification; one PPIAF-hosted case summary states USD 42 million, while IEG's data sheet states "up to USD 50 million" and MIGA's 2005 brief says USD 50 million. Use "up to USD 50 million."
- Do not reconcile MIGA's "up to USD 200 million" (IEG data sheet) with the USD 91 million issued in 2005 unless a source is found.
- Dam height: IEG says 39 m; MIGA (2005) and EIB say 48 m. Do not state a dam height.
- Installed capacity is variously given as 1,070, 1,075 and 1,080 MW; use 1,070 MW (IEG, ADB, EIB).
- Reservoir area appears as 450 km² (IEG, MIGA, EIB) and 490 km² (EDF, NTPC). Say "approximately 450 km² at full supply" or avoid.
- Do not state individual loan tenors, margins, ECA cover amounts per agency, Thai baht tranche amounts, or tariff levels in cents/kWh other than the IEG-cited levelized 2.7 cents/kWh.
- Do not state when or on what terms Italian-Thai Development exited and EDF and EGCO increased their stakes.
- Do not state the claim that NT2 was "the world's largest private-sector hydro financing" as fact; it is MIGA's 2005 characterization.
- Do not quote figures for downstream affected people beyond the cited ranges, or treat NGO estimates (for example 150,000 attributed to Environmental Defense) as verified.
- Do not state that the guarantees have been called; per NTPC (2019, via IEG) none had been.
- Do not present the pumped-storage project as committed; it is at feasibility-study stage per the 2024 MoU.
