# Odebrecht 2016 DOJ plea and the Latin American infrastructure corruption fallout (Peru, Colombia Ruta del Sol II)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: ICSID docket entries of September 2026 in the Peru pipeline arbitrations)

## Summary

On 21 December 2016 the US Department of Justice (DOJ) announced that Odebrecht S.A., Brazil's largest construction group, and its petrochemical affiliate Braskem had pleaded guilty. The resolution was coordinated with Brazilian and Swiss authorities. The companies admitted paying about USD 788 million in bribes in 12 countries to win public contracts, and agreed to combined global penalties of at least USD 3.5 billion, then a record. Odebrecht ran a dedicated bribery unit, the "Division of Structured Operations", with its own shadow budget and layered offshore payment channels. Besides Brazil, the countries named were Angola, Argentina, Colombia, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Guatemala, Mexico, Mozambique, Panama, Peru and Venezuela. The DOJ papers sparked investigations across Latin America. Political fallout was greatest in Peru:
- former president Alejandro Toledo was charged over the Interoceánica Sur highway and later convicted;
- President Pedro Pablo Kuczynski resigned in March 2018;
- former president Alan García killed himself during his arrest in April 2019.

Peru also ended the Gasoducto Sur Peruano concession, held by an Odebrecht–Enagás consortium, in January 2017 after the project failed to reach financial close. That led to treaty arbitrations still running in 2026. In Colombia, the bribes centred on the Ruta del Sol Sector 2 highway concession. Officials and a senator were prosecuted, the contract unwound, and Odebrecht's local partner Corficolombiana (Grupo Aval) resolved US FCPA charges in 2023. For project finance, the case is the defining example of integrity risk in a sponsor: corruption by one consortium member can void contracts, strand lenders and freeze a country's PPP pipeline.

## Verified facts

**The US/Brazil/Swiss resolution (December 2016)**

1. In December 2016, in a case brought by the DOJ together with Brazil and Switzerland, Odebrecht and Braskem admitted bribery of USD 788 million and agreed to "a record-breaking fine of at least $3.5bn". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Odebrecht admitted paying "more than $788m in bribes to win construction contracts across 12 countries". [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. The Congressional Research Service (CRS) states that the bribes were paid "to secure public contracts worth more than $3.3 billion". According to the DOJ, bribes went to officials, or to support elections and parties, in Angola, Argentina, Colombia, the Dominican Republic, Ecuador, Guatemala, Mexico, Mozambique, Panama, Peru and Venezuela, as well as Brazil. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. The US criminal case is *United States v. Odebrecht S.A.*, Cr. No. 16-643 (E.D.N.Y.). CRS cites it as "filed April 17, 2017". That date is presumably a later docket entry, such as sentencing, not the plea date. [confidence: high for docket number; medium for the meaning of the date] [source: 3]
5. Odebrecht's bribery department was called the Division of Structured Operations. It managed its own shadow budget and paid bribes through a complex, multi-layered network of shell companies and offshore finance. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. Odebrecht's share of the penalties is commonly given as USD 2.6 billion, and the bribery as running from 2001 to 2016. [confidence: medium; tertiary source, consistent with the at-least-USD-3.5-billion combined figure] [source: 4]

**Peru**

7. The DOJ documents published on 21 December 2016 stated that Odebrecht paid approximately USD 29 million in bribes in Peru, earning more than USD 143 million in benefits. [confidence: medium; DOJ figures as reported, the primary document not retrieved] [source: 5]
8. In February 2017 a Peruvian judge ordered 18 months' preventive detention for former president Alejandro Toledo (2001–2006) and issued national and international arrest warrants. Prosecutors alleged he received USD 20 million in bribes from Odebrecht for the contract to build the transoceanic highway (Interoceánica Sur) linking Brazil to the Peruvian coast. Odebrecht's former Peru head, Jorge Barata, gave testimony implicating him. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
9. Barata later testified that the Interoceánica Sur bribe to Toledo was USD 31 million, not USD 20 million. Toledo was later convicted and sentenced to 20 years' imprisonment, in 2024, for influence peddling, collusion and money laundering. [confidence: medium] [source: 5]
10. In December 2017 President Pedro Pablo Kuczynski appeared before Congress in impeachment proceedings over payments of USD 782,000 from Odebrecht to his company, Westfield Capital, made while he was finance minister, as reported by El Comercio. He resigned in March 2018. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3, 7]
11. CRS (May 2019) reported several further consequences:
    - four former Peruvian presidents were linked to the Odebrecht scandal or other corruption charges;
    - Ollanta Humala and Nadine Heredia were under investigation for money laundering and corruption;
    - Peru was seeking Toledo's extradition from the United States;
    - Alan García shot himself during his arrest on Odebrecht-related charges in April 2019 and died;
    - Keiko Fujimori was ordered into pretrial detention in October 2018 over alleged laundering of Odebrecht campaign contributions. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
12. **Gasoducto Sur Peruano (GSP).**
    - The pipeline concession was awarded on 30 June 2014 to the Gasoducto Sur Peruano consortium of Odebrecht and Enagás.
    - The losing bidder was a consortium of GDF Suez, Sempra, Techint and TGI.
    - On 23 January 2017 the concession was terminated because the consortium failed to secure financing.
    - A penalty of USD 262 million was collected in full by 28 January 2017.
    - Graña y Montero was also a consortium member. [confidence: medium; secondary source] [source: 5]
13. **Enagás v. Peru.** *Enagás S.A. and Enagás Internacional S.L.U. v. Republic of Peru* (ICSID Case No. ARB/18/26), under the Spain–Peru BIT (1994), concerns the natural gas pipeline project. Key docket events:
    - registered 24 July 2018;
    - award rendered 20 December 2024, with a dissenting opinion by arbitrator Claus von Wobeser;
    - rectification decision 23 May 2025;
    - Peru's annulment application registered 2 June 2025;
    - annulment pending, with costs submissions filed on 14 September 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
14. **Odebrecht Latinvest v. Peru.** *Odebrecht Latinvest S.à r.l. v. Republic of Peru* (ICSID Case No. ARB/20/4), under the Peru–Belgium–Luxembourg BIT (2005), concerns an oil and gas enterprise. It was registered on 4 February 2020. It is pending, with Procedural Order No. 11 issued on 1 September 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

**Colombia: Ruta del Sol Sector 2**

15. Ruta del Sol Sector 2 is a dual-carriageway concession of about 528 km between Puerto Salgar (Cundinamarca) and San Roque (Cesar).
    - **Concessionaire:** Concesionaria Ruta del Sol S.A.S.
    - **Shareholders:** Constructora Norberto Odebrecht S.A., Odebrecht Participações e Investimentos S.A., Estudios y Proyectos del Sol S.A.S. (Episol, a Corficolombiana company) and CSS Constructores S.A.
    - **Contract:** Concession Contract 001 of 2010, signed with INCO (the predecessor of ANI) on 14 January 2010.
    - **Investment:** about COP 3.6 trillion in 2011 pesos, approximately USD 1.98 billion. [confidence: medium; secondary source] [source: 9]
16. Corficolombiana, a Grupo Aval subsidiary, owned 33 percent of the Ruta del Sol II concession. [confidence: medium] [source: 10]
17. The DOJ document reported, as summarised in Spanish-language secondary sources, that between about 2009 and 2014 Odebrecht paid about USD 11 million to secure public works contracts in Colombia, obtaining benefits of more than USD 50 million. [confidence: medium] [source: 10]
18. CRS (May 2019) states that Odebrecht executives admitted providing USD 32.5 million in bribes "to facilitate the building of Colombia's Ruta del Sol highway and other infrastructure projects". It adds that a Colombian senator who accepted bribes cooperated with prosecutors. This figure differs from item 17; see Do not state. [confidence: high as a CRS statement] [source: 3]
19. Two prosecutions in Colombia illustrate the scheme:
    - Former vice-minister of transport Gabriel García Morales was charged with receiving USD 6.5 million to favour Odebrecht in the award of Ruta del Sol Sector 2.
    - Former senator Otto Bula was arrested on 14 January 2017 over a bribe linked to adding the Ocaña–Gamarra road to the Ruta del Sol concession.

    Former congressman Bernardo Elías was convicted of bribery offences. [confidence: medium] [source: 10]
20. In August 2023 Grupo Aval (through Corficolombiana) accepted responsibility before US authorities for FCPA violations related to bribes for Ruta del Sol II and agreed to pay USD 80 million to resolve the matter. [confidence: medium; the DOJ and SEC primary releases were not retrieved] [source: 10]
21. After the scandal, Colombia legislated for what happens when a PPP contract is annulled. Law 1882 of 15 January 2018, Article 20, replaced Article 32 of Law 1508. On a declaration of absolute nullity, the liquidation recognises the CPI-updated value of costs, investments and expenses incurred by the contractor, including interest, less remuneration received. See colombia-4g.md item 17. [confidence: high per that sheet] [source: colombia-4g.md]

**Other countries (CRS, May 2019)**

22. Other national fallout reported by CRS:
    - **Panama:** court documents contend Odebrecht paid more than USD 59 million in bribes in 2010–2014; Odebrecht agreed in August 2017 to pay the government USD 220 million.
    - **Dominican Republic:** 14 people were indicted in May 2017 over USD 92 million of bribes.
    - **Ecuador:** Vice President Jorge Glas was convicted in late 2017 of taking bribes "exceeding $13 million".
    - **Venezuela:** bribes of more than USD 98 million were reportedly paid. [confidence: high as CRS statements] [source: 3]

**Odebrecht corporate aftermath**

23. Marcelo Odebrecht, the group's CEO, was sentenced in Brazil in March 2016 to 19 years and 4 months for paying more than USD 30 million in bribes to Petrobras executives. The group filed for Chapter 15 recognition in the United States on 26 August 2019, following a Brazilian judicial recovery filing about two months earlier. It renamed itself Novonor in December 2020. [confidence: medium] [source: 4]

## Financing and structure details

- **Ruta del Sol II** was a road concession with Odebrecht as majority sponsor and a Colombian financial group, Corficolombiana/Episol, as minority partner. Secondary sources say the financing was arranged by Corficolombiana. Lender identities and amounts were not verified. [source: 9]
- **GSP**: the concession ended because financing could not be closed. The common explanation is that lenders would not lend once Odebrecht's admitted corruption raised the risk of contract voidance and sponsor collapse; this is not verified here (see Do not state). The termination triggered collection of a USD 262 million guarantee, and the sponsors later sued under investment treaties. [source: 5, 8]
- **Penalty structure.** Combined US/Brazil/Swiss penalties of at least USD 3.5 billion. The split between countries and between Odebrecht and Braskem was not verified beyond the Odebrecht share of about USD 2.6 billion. [source: 1, 4]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Wrong: systematic, institutionalised bribery.** A dedicated division with an off-book budget and offshore layering defeated ordinary controls (BBC). Corruption was used to win the award itself (Ruta del Sol II, Interoceánica) and to obtain later additions (the Ocaña–Gamarra extension). [source: 1, 10]
- **Contagion to projects and partners.** Projects in which Odebrecht was a sponsor lost financing (GSP) or had their contracts unwound (Ruta del Sol II). Local partners faced their own enforcement (Corficolombiana). Countries slowed their PPP programs: colombia-4g.md notes delayed closings linked partly to the Ruta del Sol II scandal. [source: 5, 10; colombia-4g.md]
- **Right, in part: cross-border cooperation.** The coordinated US, Brazil and Switzerland resolution, and US support to Latin American prosecutors (CRS), exposed the scheme across many jurisdictions. Colombia's Law 1882 later clarified how annulled PPP contracts are liquidated, a lender-relevant reform. [source: 3; colombia-4g.md]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 49 (legal, insurance, model, tax and integrity diligence).** Use Odebrecht to show why integrity due diligence covers co-sponsors and EPC contractors, not just the borrower. Key points: sanctions and debarment screening; review of how the award was won (was the tender clean?); representations, undertakings and events of default on anti-corruption; and the consequences if a contract is annulled for corruption, including whether lenders get any termination compensation (Colombia Law 1882 as the example). Ask what red flags in an Odebrecht-led consortium a lender's KYC process should have caught before 2016.
- **Chapter 60 (political risk and its protection).** Contrast corruption-driven loss with classic political risk. When a contract is voided because the sponsor bribed officials, PRI and treaty protection may not respond: illegality defences and exclusions apply. Yet the GSP sponsors are still pursuing treaty claims against Peru (Enagás award 2024; annulment pending). Discuss FCPA and UK Bribery Act exposure (see t-anticorruption-sanctions.md), and the host-country political shock: presidents falling and PPP pipelines frozen.

## Do not state

- Do not state per-country bribe amounts as DOJ figures without the primary DOJ plea documents. The Colombia figure conflicts: Spanish-language secondary sources report the DOJ as "about USD 11 million" (2009–2014), while CRS reports USD 32.5 million for Ruta del Sol and other projects. Peru's USD 29 million (benefits above USD 143 million) is also from secondary reporting.
- Do not give the split of the USD 3.5 billion between the US, Brazil and Switzerland, or Braskem's separate penalty. Neither was verified.
- Do not give the amount or reasoning of the 20 December 2024 Enagás award. Only its existence and dates were verified.
- Do not state the date or amount of any Colombian arbitral or court decision annulling or liquidating the Ruta del Sol II contract. Secondary sources mention an arbitral award, but its details were not verified.
- Do not state the exact sentence length or date of Toledo's 2024 conviction beyond "about 20 years" and "2024" (secondary source).
- Do not state that lenders refused to fund GSP *because of* the plea as an established fact. It is the common explanation, but the verified source says only that financing was not secured.
- Do not describe the 2023 Aval matter as a DOJ plea or a deferred prosecution agreement, or split the USD 80 million between the DOJ and the SEC. Primary releases were not retrieved.
- Do not attribute quotations to DOJ officials. The DOJ press release (justice.gov) could not be accessed.
