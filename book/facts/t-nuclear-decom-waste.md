# Nuclear decommissioning and waste funding (US NDT funds, UK Funded Decommissioning Programme, waste transfer pricing)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest documents covered: NRC decommissioning backgrounder and financial-assurance page reviewed September 2026; Sizewell C Funded Decommissioning Programme documents published 22 July 2025, last updated 19 November 2025)

## Summary

Nuclear plants carry liabilities that arise decades after the revenue. Those liabilities are decommissioning (radiological clean-up and site release), spent fuel management, and disposal of higher-activity waste. Two models dominate.

**United States.** The NRC requires each power reactor licensee to provide decommissioning funding assurance before operation. Licensees report to the NRC every two years (more often near or after shutdown). The minimum amount comes from a formula in 10 CFR 50.75(c), in January 1986 dollars, escalated for labour, energy and waste burial. Accepted methods:
- prepayment;
- an external sinking fund (allowed only where costs are recovered through rate regulation or non-bypassable charges);
- surety, insurance or a parent guarantee.

Section 468A of the Internal Revenue Code lets the taxpayer deduct contributions to a qualified Nuclear Decommissioning Reserve Fund, and the fund's income is taxed at 20 percent. Spent fuel is a separate federal responsibility under the Nuclear Waste Policy Act of 1982:
- Utilities paid a 0.1 cent/kWh fee into the Nuclear Waste Fund until it was set to zero in 2014 after a court ruling.
- DOE's failure to take fuel from 1998 has produced damages paid from the taxpayer-funded Judgment Fund.

**United Kingdom.** For new nuclear, the Energy Act 2008 requires a Funded Decommissioning Programme (FDP) approved by the Secretary of State. It consists of a Decommissioning and Waste Management Plan (DWMP) and a Funding Arrangements Plan (FAP) that builds a segregated fund over the plant's life. Government will take title to spent fuel and intermediate-level waste (ILW) under Waste Transfer Contracts at a price set under the 2011 Waste Transfer Pricing Methodology. That price includes a risk premium and is subject to a cap fixed in the contract. Hinkley Point C's FDP was published in September 2016 and Sizewell C's in July 2025. The two are designed differently, because Sizewell C is funded under the RAB model while Hinkley Point C has a CfD.

## Verified facts

**United States: decommissioning funding (NRC; current rule in 10 CFR 50.75)**

1. Before operation, a licensee must set up or obtain a financial mechanism (for example a trust fund or a parent company guarantee) to ensure enough funds for decommissioning. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Reporting runs every two years, annually within five years of planned shutdown, and annually after shutdown. The report must estimate the minimum amount using the 10 CFR 50.75(c) formulas. A site-specific estimate may be used if it is greater than the generic amount. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
3. The 10 CFR 50.75(c) minimum amounts, in January 1986 dollars:

    | Reactor type | Thermal power (P) | Minimum amount (USD millions) |
    |---|---|---|
    | PWR | 3,400 MWt or more | 105 |
    | PWR | 1,200 to 3,400 MWt (use 1,200 if below) | 75 + 0.0088P |
    | BWR | 3,400 MWt or more | 135 |
    | BWR | 1,200 to 3,400 MWt (use 1,200 if below) | 104 + 0.009P |

    The amounts are escalated by a factor of at least 0.65L + 0.13E + 0.22B, where L, E and B are labour, energy and waste-burial escalators. They exclude spent fuel removal and disposal and non-radiological demolition. Worked example: a 3,000 MWt PWR gives 75 + 0.0088 × 3,000 = USD 101.4 million in 1986 dollars before escalation. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. NRC guidance on cost and method:
    - **Cost range:** the decommissioning backgrounder says costs "generally range from $300 million to $400 million". The financial-assurance page gives an NRC estimate of USD 280–612 million.
    - **Sinking funds:** approximately 70 percent of licensees, generally rate-regulated utilities or indirectly regulated generators, may accumulate funds over the operating life, so they need not hold full funding today.
    - **Other licensees:** must use prepayment or a surety, insurance or guarantee.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
5. Further 50.75 rules:
    - **Investment returns:** licensees using a site-specific estimate with a defined safe-storage period may credit projected earnings at up to a 2 percent annual real rate through the decommissioning period.
    - **Prohibited investments:** trust funds may not invest in the securities of the licensee, its parent or affiliates, or other owners or operators of nuclear plants. Index or mutual fund exposure to such entities is limited to 10 percent of assets.

    [confidence: high] [source: 3]
6. Decommissioning must be completed within 60 years of the plant ceasing operations. Licensees may choose DECON (prompt dismantling) or SAFSTOR (deferred dismantling), or a mix. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. **Tax.** Under 26 U.S.C. §468A a taxpayer may elect to deduct payments to a Nuclear Decommissioning Reserve Fund. The fund's gross income is taxed at 20 percent, and the fund may accept only deductible payments. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

**United States: spent fuel and the Nuclear Waste Fund**

8. The Nuclear Waste Policy Act of 1982 required DOE to begin removing spent fuel from reactor sites by 1998 under standard contracts with utilities, funded by a 0.1 cent/kWh fee to the Nuclear Waste Fund. After a federal appeals court decision, utility contributions were set to zero in 2014. Per WNA:
    - utilities paid USD 24.6 billion (nominal) between 1983 and 2025;
    - the Fund stood at USD 51.4 billion at the end of FY2025.

    [confidence: medium (WNA secondary; the statutory fee is well established)] [source: 5]
9. GAO notes:
    - the US has over 90,000 metric tons of commercial spent fuel, growing by about 2,000 metric tons a year;
    - no permanent repository exists;
    - the government has paid billions of dollars in damages to utilities and may have to pay tens of billions more.

    WNA reports these settlements come from the taxpayer-funded Judgment Fund, not the Nuclear Waste Fund. [confidence: high for GAO; medium for WNA detail] [source: 5, 6]

**United Kingdom: Funded Decommissioning Programme (Energy Act 2008)**

10. On 8 December 2011 the government laid before Parliament:
    - statutory FDP Guidance for prospective operators of new nuclear power stations;
    - the government response to the December 2010 consultation;
    - a Waste Transfer Pricing Methodology for disposal of higher-activity waste from new nuclear stations.

    The Methodology sets out how the government will price the transfer of title to, and liability for, ILW and spent fuel to the government, for disposal in the Geological Disposal Facility (GDF). [confidence: high] [source: 7]
11. The Hinkley Point C FDP was published on 29 September 2016. It comprises:
    - a Funding Arrangements Plan;
    - a Decommissioning and Waste Management Plan;
    - Waste Transfer Contracts for spent fuel and for ILW;
    - a Section 46 Agreement, a Deed of Undertaking and a Standstill Agreement;
    - Nuclear Liabilities Financing Assurance Board (NLFAB) advice.

    NLFAB advised on the HPC plan and was then disbanded. It was reconstituted in April 2022 to advise on Sizewell C. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9]
12. **Waste transfer pricing principles**, as recited in the HPC spent fuel Waste Transfer Contract:
    - The government aims for disposal "without cost to the taxpayer" and to give operators certainty over the maximum price.
    - The price is set above expected costs and includes a risk premium.
    - Setting the price is deferred for a period so it can rest on actual cost data, with the operator making prudent provision meanwhile on an Expected Waste Transfer Price.
    - A Default Methodology applies if no GDF site has been selected by the end of the deferral period.
    - A lump sum covers government waste management between transfer and disposal.

    [confidence: high] [source: 10]
13. **HPC figures:**

    | Waste stream | Expected Waste Transfer Price | Cap | Risk Fee |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Spent fuel | GBP 585,475/tU (September 2012 money) | GBP 1,159,250/tU (December 2012 money) | GBP 3,020/tU |
    | ILW | GBP 23,034/m³ (September 2012 money) | GBP 65,850/m³ (December 2012 money) | GBP 170/m³ |

    The spent fuel figures are per tonne of uranium of packaged spent fuel; the ILW figures are per cubic metre of packaged ILW. On these figures the spent fuel cap is approximately twice the expected price, and the ILW cap approximately 2.9 times. The money-value dates differ slightly, so the ratios are approximate. [confidence: high for figures; ratios computed] [source: 10, 11]
14. **Sizewell C FDP** (published 22 July 2025, last updated 19 November 2025). Per NLFAB's advice to the Secretary of State:
    - **Contributions:** the operator pays regular contributions under the FAP to an independent special-purpose "FDP Implementation Company", which holds the segregated Fund Assets. Contributions are recalibrated at five-yearly reviews.
    - **Funding periods:** a Primary Funding Period of years 1–55 after first criticality, targeting "fully funded" at year 55, and a Secondary Funding Period of years 56–60.
    - **After closure:** in the Decommissioning Period there are no further operator contributions, so a shortfall would fall on public funds.
    - **Security:** before first criticality the operator grants security over its assets and shares to the FDP Implementation Company.

    [confidence: high] [source: 9, 12]
15. **NLFAB on the differences between Sizewell C and HPC:**
    - **Funding period:** 55 years for SZC against 37 years for HPC in the ordinary course.
    - **Investment control:** for SZC the independent FDP Implementation Company controls investment strategy; for HPC the operator does, because HPC investors bear more of the fund risk.
    - **Risk and inflation:** SZC can take more investment risk for longer. Its FAP adds an explicit allowance for decommissioning-cost inflation above CPI.
    - **Late-life checks:** SZC adds Funding Outcomes Reports at 10 years and 2 years before, and at the end of, the Primary Funding Period.
    - **Revenue backing:** under the RAB, an "FDP Allowance Building Block" in allowed revenue funds the contributions, so they are "effectively underwritten by consumers on a pass-through basis".
    - **Early closure:** a "Partial Revocation" regime lets any FDP shortfall be recovered through allowed revenue for up to 10 years after closure.

    [confidence: high] [source: 12]
16. NLFAB records that Sizewell C financing has two phases. In the first, the National Wealth Fund is the majority debt provider. The FAP interacts with that financing through security and intercreditor arrangements. [confidence: high] [source: 12]

## Financing and structure details

- **US merchant (non-rate-regulated) owners** cannot use an unfunded sinking fund. They must prepay or provide a surety or parent guarantee, which is a capital cost in acquisitions and financings. In utility rate base, decommissioning is collected from customers over the operating life. [2, 3]
- **UK FDP and lender interaction:** the FDP fund is held by a separate company. NLFAB's advice on both HPC and SZC addresses security and intercreditor arrangements with project lenders, including the enforcement waterfall. Waste transfer caps give lenders and investors a maximum disposal liability, at the price of a risk premium paid to government. [10, 11, 12]
- **Rough NDT sizing:** apply the 50.75 formula, then escalate, then compare with the site-specific estimate. The reserve need depends heavily on the assumed real return (NRC allows up to 2 percent real credit in some cases) and on whether DECON or SAFSTOR is chosen. [3]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **US spent fuel (GAO):** with no repository, fuel accumulates at reactor sites, and federal damages for DOE's breach of the standard contracts keep growing. That shifts a cost that utilities' fees were meant to cover onto taxpayers through the Judgment Fund. [5, 6]
- **UK design choice (NLFAB):**
    - HPC puts fund risk on equity, with a shorter funding period and operator control of investments.
    - SZC moves more risk to consumers through the RAB, with a longer funding period, independent investment control and partial-revocation protection.

    NLFAB judged that overall shortfall risk at end of generation is not impaired relative to HPC. It noted that the Section 46 Agreement restricts how far the Secretary of State can later restore prudence. [12]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 74 (Nuclear power):** Use HPC and SZC to show how a back-end liability that runs for a century is ring-fenced: a segregated fund, government take-over of waste at a capped price, and statutory approval. Contrast who bears fund-performance risk under a CfD (equity) and under a RAB (consumers). Ask the reader why fixing a waste transfer cap at FID, at about twice the expected price, makes the project financeable, and what it costs.
- **Ch 65 (End-of-life):** Use 10 CFR 50.75 to teach formula-based minimum reserves against site-specific estimates. Cover permitted funding methods (prepayment, sinking fund, surety or guarantee) and the tax treatment of qualified funds. Work the 3,000 MWt PWR example (USD 101.4 million in 1986 dollars) and discuss escalation and real-return assumptions. Contrast these funded obligations with an unfunded government promise (the US Nuclear Waste Fund and Judgment Fund damages).
