# Reserves and resources reporting codes: SPE-PRMS 2018, JORC 2012, NI 43-101, CRIRSCO Template, SEC S-K 1300 (and SEC oil and gas rules)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: CSA proposal of 12 June 2025 to repeal and replace NI 43-101, still not adopted according to the OSC instrument listing reviewed on 3 October 2026; CRIRSCO International Reporting Template update of June 2024)

## Summary

Lenders size reserve-based loans and mine financings on reserve estimates, so they need to know which code produced the numbers and what each category means.

Petroleum is classified under the Society of Petroleum Engineers' Petroleum Resources Management System (SPE-PRMS). The current version was approved by the SPE Board in June 2018 and is sponsored by seven societies. PRMS is project-based. It classifies resources by chance of commerciality (Reserves, Contingent Resources, Prospective Resources) and categorizes them by uncertainty (1P/2P/3P, with P90/P50/P10 thresholds when probabilistic methods are used).

Solid minerals are reported under national codes aligned with the CRIRSCO International Reporting Template, last updated in June 2024. These codes include:
- JORC 2012 (Australasia), effective 20 December 2012 and mandatory from 1 December 2013;
- Canada's NI 43-101, which relies on the CIM Definition Standards;
- the US SEC's Regulation S-K subpart 1300 (S-K 1300), adopted 31 October 2018, effective 25 February 2019, and mandatory for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2021.

All of these codes require a named, experienced professional to take responsibility for the estimate: a Competent Person (JORC), a Qualified Person (NI 43-101, S-K 1300) or a Qualified Reserves Evaluator (PRMS). They also convert resources into reserves only through "modifying factors", and allow Inferred resources into economic studies only with heavy restrictions.

For US-listed oil and gas reporters, the SEC uses its own definitions. These rest on "reasonable certainty" and a 12-month average first-day-of-month price.

## Verified facts

SPE-PRMS (petroleum)

1. The SPE Oil and Gas Reserves Committee completed the PRMS revision, and the SPE Board approved it in June 2018. The process included a 90-day public comment period. It also required approval by six co-sponsoring societies: the World Petroleum Council, the American Association of Petroleum Geologists, the Society of Petroleum Evaluation Engineers, the Society of Exploration Geophysicists, the European Association of Geoscientists and Engineers, and the Society of Petrophysicists and Well Log Analysts. SPE publishes errata for 2018 and 2022. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The PRMS 2018 key-changes document lists the foundation principles carried over from 2007:
- The system is project-based.
- Classification is based on the project's chance of commerciality.
- Categorization is based on uncertainty in recoverable quantities.
- The base case uses the evaluator's forecast of future conditions.
- Deterministic and/or probabilistic methods may be used.
- The system applies to both conventional and unconventional resources.
- Quantities are sales quantities.
- Net resources follow contractual entitlement.

[confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. PRMS defines Reserves as quantities "anticipated to be commercially recoverable by application of development projects to known accumulations from a given date forward under defined conditions". To count as Reserves, quantities must meet four criteria: discovered, recoverable, commercial and remaining. Contingent Resources are potentially recoverable from known accumulations but not yet commercial because of one or more contingencies. Prospective Resources are potentially recoverable from undiscovered accumulations and carry both a chance of geologic discovery and a chance of development. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. Uncertainty categories: when probabilistic methods are used, there should be at least a 90% probability (P90) that actual recovery equals or exceeds the Proved (1P) estimate; at least 50% (P50) for Proved plus Probable (2P); and at least 10% (P10) for Proved plus Probable plus Possible (3P). Under deterministic methods, "reasonable certainty" expresses a high degree of confidence for Proved. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
5. Changes introduced in 2018:
- New reference labels for incremental Contingent Resources (C1, C2, C3) and cumulative Prospective Resources (1U, 2U, 3U).
- Undeveloped Reserves projects "should" begin development within five years of initial classification, with exceptions to be clearly justified. This "5-year rule" applies to the whole Reserves class, not just Proved.
- Stand-alone Possible Reserves are not allowed, with limited exceptions.
- Abandonment, decommissioning and restoration costs must be included in the economics of new investments.
- Reserves require Established Technology.
- Technically Recoverable Resources was added as a defined term.
- Qualifications for a Qualified Reserves Evaluator and a Qualified Reserves Auditor were added.
- A project is commercial when the entity has shown a firm intention to proceed, typically because its return is at or above its weighted average cost of capital or hurdle rate, and all other contingencies are satisfied.

[confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. In May 2023 the SPE Oil and Gas Reserves Committee approved a PRMS English–Chinese version (V1.0). SPE hosts the PRMS 2018 text in this bilingual format. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

SEC oil and gas reporting (Regulation S-X Rule 4-10)

7. SEC definition: Proved oil and gas reserves are quantities that "can be estimated with reasonable certainty to be economically producible—from a given date forward, from known reservoirs, and under existing economic conditions, operating methods, and government regulations". This applies until the contracts providing the right to operate expire, unless evidence indicates that renewal is reasonably certain. When probabilistic methods are used, the SEC rules give the same thresholds as PRMS: at least 50% probability for proved plus probable and at least 10% for proved plus probable plus possible. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
8. SEC price rule: the economic producibility test uses the average price over the 12 months before the end of the reporting period. This is calculated as the unweighted arithmetic average of the first-day-of-the-month price for each month, unless contracts define prices, and excludes escalations based on future conditions. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
9. SEC five-year rule: undrilled locations can be classified as undeveloped reserves only if a development plan has been adopted that schedules them to be drilled within five years, unless specific circumstances justify a longer time. The SEC's "Modernization of Oil and Gas Reporting" rule containing these provisions took effect on 1 January 2010. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]

CRIRSCO International Reporting Template (solid minerals)

10. CRIRSCO (Committee for Mineral Reserves International Reporting Standards) publishes the International Reporting Template, latest update June 2024 (approved for release 27 June 2024). This release adopted Standard Definition changes agreed at the October 2023 CRIRSCO AGM in Rio de Janeiro: the defined term "Mineral" was removed and "Life of Mine Plan" was added. CRIRSCO states that a full review of the Template was not carried out. The Template is advisory, and national codes take precedence. It is reviewed on a five-to-ten-year cycle. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
11. The Template has sixteen Standard Definitions: Public Reports, Competent Person, Modifying Factors, Exploration Targets, Exploration Results, Mineral Resources (Inferred, Indicated, Measured), Mineral Reserves (Probable, Proved), Life of Mine Plan, Scoping Study, Pre-Feasibility Study and Feasibility Study. CRIRSCO members use definitions "identical to, or not materially different from" these. Its overriding principles are Transparency, Materiality and Competence. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
12. CRIRSCO lists national reporting organizations including JORC (Australasia), CIM (Canada), PERC (Europe), SAMREC (South Africa), SME (USA), Chile's certification code, CBRR (Brazil), NACRI (India), KCMI (Indonesia), PMRC (Philippines), KAZRC (Kazakhstan), OERN (Russia) and UMREK (Turkey), among others. CRIRSCO formed an alliance with the International Council on Mining and Metals in 2007. [confidence: medium (from website summary)] [source: 7]

JORC Code 2012 (Australasia)

13. The JORC Code 2012 Edition took effect on 20 December 2012 and became mandatory from 1 December 2013. It is produced by the Australasian Joint Ore Reserves Committee (established 1971). The committee's parent bodies are the AusIMM, the AIG and the Minerals Council of Australia, with ASX, FINSIA, accounting and other representatives. The Code has been incorporated in the ASX listing rules since 1989 and the NZX listing rules since 1992. JORC released a draft new JORC Code for public consultation on 1 August 2024, and the consultation closed on 31 October 2024. At the date of this sheet, the JORC website still describes the 2012 edition as current. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9]
14. JORC Competent Person: a minerals industry professional who is a Member or Fellow of the AusIMM, the AIG or a Recognised Professional Organisation, with a minimum of five years' relevant experience in the style of mineralisation or type of deposit and in the activity being undertaken. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
15. JORC Table 1 is a checklist of assessment and reporting criteria. When Exploration Results, Mineral Resources or Ore Reserves are first reported, or materially changed, a summary of the relevant Table 1 sections must be included on an "if not, why not" basis. JORC uses "Ore Reserve" where the CRIRSCO Template and other codes use "Mineral Reserve". [confidence: high] [source: 9]

NI 43-101 (Canada)

16. National Instrument 43-101 Standards of Disclosure for Mineral Projects is the Canadian Securities Administrators' (CSA) rule for mineral project disclosure. The OSC-hosted unofficial consolidation, current to 9 May 2016, defines an "acceptable foreign code" as the JORC Code, PERC Code, SAMREC Code, SEC Industry Guide 7, the Certification Code (Chile), or any other generally accepted foreign code consistent with the CIM-based definitions. SEC Industry Guide 7 has since been rescinded (see item 20). [confidence: high] [source: 10]
17. NI 43-101 "qualified person": an engineer or geoscientist with a relevant university degree or equivalent, at least five years' relevant experience, experience relevant to the project and the technical report, and good standing with a professional association. Foreign associations must meet extra membership tests. The instrument also requires an independent qualified person in specified circumstances. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
18. On 12 June 2025 the CSA published a Notice and Request for Comment proposing to repeal and replace NI 43-101. As of 3 October 2026, the OSC's instrument page lists no final replacement rule after that proposal, so the existing NI 43-101 (with amendments) remains the rule in force. [confidence: medium (absence of adoption inferred from the OSC listing)] [source: 11]

SEC Regulation S-K subpart 1300 (US mining)

19. The SEC adopted "Modernization of Property Disclosures for Mining Registrants" (Release Nos. 33-10570 and 34-84509), dated 31 October 2018 and published in the Federal Register on 26 December 2018. It became effective on 25 February 2019. Registrants had to comply for their first fiscal year beginning on or after 1 January 2021, after a two-year transition, and voluntary early compliance was allowed. The rule rescinded Industry Guide 7 and moved mining disclosure into a new subpart 1300 of Regulation S-K, aligning it more closely with CRIRSCO-based codes. [confidence: high] [source: 12, 13]
20. S-K 1300 definitions:
- A mineral reserve is "an estimate of tonnage and grade or quality of indicated and measured mineral resources that, in the opinion of the qualified person, can be the basis of an economically viable project".
- A proven reserve can only come from a measured resource.
- A probable reserve comes from an indicated resource and, in some cases, a measured resource.
- An inferred resource may not be converted to a reserve.
- An initial assessment is required to disclose mineral resources but "cannot be used as the basis for disclosure of mineral reserves".

[confidence: high] [source: 13]
21. S-K 1300 qualified person: a mineral industry professional with at least five years' relevant experience in the type of mineralization, the deposit and the specific activity, who is an eligible member or licensee in good standing of a recognized professional organization. The rule sets criteria for which organizations qualify. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
22. S-K 1300 cost-estimate accuracy (Item 1302, Table 1). An initial assessment with cash-flow analysis needs operating and capital cost accuracy of about ±50%, with contingency of no more than 25%. A pre-feasibility study needs about ±25% accuracy and contingency of no more than 15%. A feasibility study needs about ±15% accuracy and contingency of no more than 10%. A feasibility study "may serve as the basis for a final decision by a proponent or financial institution to proceed with, or finance, the development of the project." [confidence: high] [source: 13, 14]
23. S-K 1300 price rule: the qualified person must use a price that gives a reasonable basis for the prospects of economic extraction, disclose it and explain the choice "with particularity", including the time frame used. A contractual price may be used if reasonable and disclosed. The price must be current as of the end of the most recently completed fiscal year. [confidence: high] [source: 14]

## Financing and structure details

- Reserve-based lending typically sizes the borrowing base on a PRMS-compliant or SEC-compliant reserves report from an independent evaluator. PRMS 2018 added Qualified Reserves Evaluator and Auditor qualifications that a lender can require in its engagement terms. [source: 2]
- In mine finance, S-K 1300 states that a feasibility study can be the basis for a financial institution's decision to finance. Its ±15% accuracy and ≤10% contingency benchmarks give lenders an objective test of study maturity. [source: 13, 14]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- In its adopting release the SEC explains that it replaced Industry Guide 7 to give investors a more complete picture of mining properties and to align US disclosure "more closely" with global practice and CRIRSCO-based codes. Industry Guide 7 had not allowed mineral resources to be disclosed. [source: 12]
- The Template's three principles of Transparency, Materiality and Competence explain why reported numbers must be traceable to a named professional who can be disciplined by a professional body. [source: 6]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 12 (how resource assets work): Teach the two axes of PRMS: classification by chance of commerciality and categorization by uncertainty (1P/2P/3P, P90/P50/P10). Then teach the mineral pathway: exploration results → inferred, indicated and measured resources → probable and proved reserves via modifying factors. The shared idea across both systems is "discovered + recoverable + commercial + remaining".
- Ch 48 (technical, resource and market diligence): Teach the lender's checklist. Which code was used? Who is the CP/QP/QRE, and are they independent? What price deck was used (the SEC 12-month first-day-of-month average for oil and gas versus the QP-selected price under S-K 1300)? What study stage is it, and does it meet the S-K 1300 accuracy and contingency bands? Have any inferred resources crept into the economics?
- Ch 75 (upstream and midstream, reserve-based lending): Size the borrowing base on 1P (PDP-heavy) or 2P, depending on the market. Show how the PRMS five-year rule and the SEC five-year rule both limit undeveloped reserves, and how the SEC's historical price average differs from a bank price deck.
- Ch 78 (mining): Map JORC, NI 43-101 and S-K 1300 to the same CRIRSCO categories. Note that NI 43-101 is under proposed replacement (June 2025) and JORC is under revision (2024 draft), so writers must cite the edition in force on the deal date.

## Do not state

- That a new JORC Code or a replacement NI 43-101 is in force. Neither was verified as adopted by 3 October 2026.
- Exact NI 43-101 technical-report triggers (for example, the "100 percent or greater change" test and the specified-exchange independence rule) without checking the current consolidated text. The OSC consolidation reviewed was current to 2016.
- CRIRSCO's founding year (often given as 1994) or the full member list. Neither was verified from a primary document in this session.
- Canada's oil and gas rule (NI 51-101 and COGEH) details. These were not researched in this session.
- That PRMS is legally binding. It is a voluntary industry standard unless a regulator or contract adopts it.
- That SEC S-K 1300 requires a particular price methodology (for example a "3-year trailing average"). It requires a reasonable, disclosed and justified price instead.
