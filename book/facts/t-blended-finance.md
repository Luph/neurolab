# Blended finance: market data, structures (first-loss, guarantees, concessional tranches) and climate funds (GCF, CIF)

As of: 2026-10-03. Latest data points: GCF portfolio dashboard as of 7 July 2026; IFC blended finance concessionality data updated April 2026; CIF portfolio data as of 31 December 2025; Convergence market data for calendar 2024 (State of Blended Finance 2025, published 21 May 2025).

## Summary

Blended finance uses concessional public or philanthropic money to draw commercial capital into developing-economy deals. Convergence, the industry's main data source, recorded 123 deals reaching financial close in 2024, totalling USD 18.3 billion. That was below the record USD 23.1 billion of 2023 but above USD 14.3 billion in 2022. Its database holds 1,350 deals worth USD 249.2 billion. Deals are getting larger: the median rose from USD 38 million (2020–2023) to USD 65 million (2024), and the median project deal reached USD 100 million. A few "whale" deals above USD 1 billion drive totals. Climate accounted for 49% of 2024 deals and over 62% of financing.

The main blending archetype remains concessional debt or equity, delivered mostly as senior debt (49% of concessional investments in 2022–2024) and subordinated debt (18%). Guarantees and risk insurance are rising: they appeared in 34% of 2024 transactions. In climate deals from 2018 to 2023, the concessional sub-instruments were:
- senior debt: 41%;
- partial guarantees: 28%;
- subordinated debt: 18%;
- first-loss equity: 10%;
- first-loss debt: 5%.

IFC, the largest single implementer, has committed USD 6 billion of donor funds to 653 projects since 2010, alongside USD 34.9 billion of additional financing. Its average concessionality is only 4.7% of total project cost: 4.0% in infrastructure, 6.8% for guarantees and 10.9% for local-currency support. The two biggest multilateral climate funds work differently:
- **Green Climate Fund (GCF):** USD 20.4 billion committed to 358 projects as of July 2026. Its instruments are grants, loans, equity, results-based payments and guarantees, and 35% of its funding goes to the private sector.
- **Climate Investment Funds (CIF):** a fund of almost USD 14 billion that works only through six MDBs. It has approved USD 9.4 billion for 471 projects with USD 98.6 billion of expected co-financing, and was the first multilateral climate fund to list bonds, through its Capital Markets Mechanism.

Convergence's later editions (Climate Edition 2025, State of Blended Finance 2026) were not retrieved, so 2024 is the latest full-year figure here and may since have been revised upward.

## Verified facts

### A. Market size and trend (global, EMDEs; source: Convergence Market Data; current data at convergence.finance)

1. 2024: **123 blended finance deals reached close, totalling USD 18.3 billion**. Annual volumes rose by an average of USD 1.7 billion a year, from USD 11.5 billion in 2020 to USD 18.3 billion in 2024. Annual aggregate financing [confidence: high] [source: 1]:
   - 2020: USD 11.5 billion;
   - 2021: USD 17.7 billion;
   - 2022: USD 14.3 billion;
   - 2023: USD 23.1 billion;
   - 2024: USD 18.3 billion.
2. **Data restatement caveat.** Convergence warns that first-reported figures rise later as deals are disclosed. Its Spring 2024 report first put 2023 at 99 deals and USD 15 billion, later revised to 127 deals and USD 23.1 billion in the Climate Edition. It expected 2024 figures to rise likewise. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Cumulative market.** 1,350 deals totalling USD 249.2 billion; more than 8,571 financial commitments by more than 2,197 investors. The historical median investment is USD 10 million, excluding guarantees and insurance. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **Deal size.** The median deal size was USD 38 million in 2020–2023 and USD 65 million in 2024. Deals above USD 1 billion averaged five a year in 2020–2022 (about USD 2.6 billion a year), then seven deals and USD 9.3 billion in 2023. Three deals launched in 2024 had closed USD 4.75 billion. Whale deals were 2.5% of activity over three years but about 27% of financing. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. **Climate share.** Climate-focused deals were 49% of 2024 blended deals and over 62% of total financing. [confidence: high] [source: 1] For 2023, the Climate Edition reported climate blended finance of USD 18.3 billion, up from USD 8 billion in 2022, and six climate deals of USD 1 billion or more. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. **2023 whale deals named by Convergence** [confidence: high] [source: 2]:
   - Águas do Rio Bloco 4: USD 1.6 billion;
   - Águas do Rio Bloco 1: USD 1.5 billion;
   - São Paulo Metropolitan Train Lines 8 and 9: USD 1.5 billion;

   - SDG Loan Fund: USD 1.11 billion.

### B. Instruments and archetypes (Convergence, 2022–2024 unless stated)

7. **Concessional debt/equity** is the most common archetype. Concessional capital went in as senior debt (49% of concessional investments), subordinated debt (18%) and senior equity (9%). Development agencies and multi-donor funds provided 65% of it, and DFIs/MDBs 19%. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. **Guarantees and risk insurance** featured in **34% of 2024 transactions**, almost all as guarantees (insurance under 1%). Development agencies and multi-donor funds provided 60% of concessional guarantees over three years. 36% were loan-portfolio guarantees to financial institutions and 33% were credit guarantees, mainly for individual projects and bonds. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. **Climate concessional sub-instruments, 2018–2023** (share of climate blended transactions) [confidence: high] [source: 2]:
   - senior debt: 41%;
   - partial guarantee: 28%;
   - subordinated debt: 18%;
   - first-loss equity: 10%;
   - senior equity: 6%;
   - junior equity: 5%;
   - first-loss debt: 5%.
   
   Guarantees and risk insurance appeared in 27% of mitigation deals, 21% of cross-cutting deals and 6% of adaptation deals.
10. **Projects.** Median project deal size rose from USD 22 million (2022) to USD 70 million (2023) to USD 100 million (2024). In 2024 projects mobilised USD 4.6 billion (25% of financing) while being only 17% of deals. Projects were 47% renewable energy, 21% transport and 16% water/sanitation (2022–2024). In projects, private investors were mainly de-risked by concessional senior debt (36%) and investment-stage grants (24%). IFC invested in only four blended projects in 2024, against 12 in 2022 and 11 in 2023. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. **Funds.** Funds were 9% of 2024 deals but mobilised the most of any vehicle type (USD 5.1 billion). Institutional investors made 50% of the private commitments to funds. Brookfield's Catalytic Transition Fund announced a USD 2.4 billion initial close. It was anchored by USD 1 billion of catalytic capital from ALTÉRRA and attracted CDPQ, GIC, Prudential Financial and Temasek. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
12. **Layered fund example.** The SDG Loan Fund (USD 1.11 billion private debt fund, AllianzGI and FMO) used first-loss equity from FMO and a second-loss guarantee from the MacArthur Foundation. It achieved a 1:9 mobilisation ratio, with the senior capital entirely from institutional investors. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
13. **Project-level guarantee example.** EBRD lent USD 45 million to Sarimay Solar (100 MW PV, Uzbekistan). USD 7 million of the senior tranche was backed by an unfunded concessional guarantee from the European Fund for Sustainable Development Plus (EFSD+). [confidence: high] [source: 1]

## Financing and structure details

How the layers stack in a blended project (generic, from the instruments verified above):
- **Senior concessional debt:** below-market rate or longer tenor, pari passu with commercial lenders. This is the most common instrument (facts 7, 9, 10).
- **Subordinated or first-loss debt or equity:** absorbs losses ahead of commercial lenders. Examples are FMO's first-loss equity in the SDG Loan Fund and the 10% first-loss equity share in climate deals (facts 9, 12).
- **Second-loss guarantee:** sits behind first loss, for example MacArthur in the SDG Loan Fund (fact 12).
- **Partial credit or risk guarantee on a senior tranche:** for example the USD 7 million EFSD+ cover on Sarimay Solar's USD 45 million EBRD loan (fact 13). See also World Bank and MIGA guarantees in t-dfis.md.
- **Local-currency subsidy:** the most concession-intensive IFC product, at 10.9% of project cost (fact 15). See t-local-currency.md.
- **Investment-stage and design-stage grants and TA:** 24% of de-risking in projects (fact 10).

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Mobilisation remains the weak point.** Convergence notes that in many cases concessional capital mobilises MDB and DFI money on commercial terms rather than private money. It also cites the lack of donor private-sector mobilisation strategies, low local investor participation, poor transparency and an underdeveloped ecosystem as the reasons blended finance has not scaled. [source: 1]
- **Guarantees are gaining because they are capital-efficient.** Convergence's Climate Edition cites research that well-structured credit guarantee facilities can mobilise 6 to 25 times more financing than traditional debt. That is the rationale behind the rise in guarantee use. [source: 2]
- **Minimum concessionality is the discipline.** IFC's average concessionality of 4.7% of project cost shows that most blended deals need only a thin layer of subsidy to rebalance risk and return. The DFI principles exist to stop subsidy crowding out commercial money. [source: 4]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 34 (Blended, concessional and local-currency finance):**
  - Open with the market: about USD 18–23 billion a year, about 120–130 deals, small next to infrastructure needs, with heavy concentration in whale deals. Teach the restatement caveat when citing Convergence numbers.
  - Then teach the stack: first-loss, second-loss, partial guarantee and concessional senior. Use the SDG Loan Fund (first-loss equity plus second-loss guarantee, 1:9 mobilisation) and Sarimay Solar (a USD 7 million guarantee on a USD 45 million loan) as the two worked examples.
  - Use IFC's 4.7% average concessionality, and 10.9% for local currency, to show students how to size the minimum concession.
  - For Case P's DFI concessional element, model a small concessional tranche of a few percent of project cost and show its effect on blended pricing and DSCR.
  - Contrast GCF (accredited entities, many instruments, 35% private) with CIF (MDB-only channel, co-financing ratio of about 10x, bond issuance).
