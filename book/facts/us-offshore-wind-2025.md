# US offshore wind policy and project disruptions, 2025–2026 (stop-work orders, lease actions, Empire Wind, Revolution Wind)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: Interior–Invenergy lease-termination settlement, June 2026; Vineyard Wind–GE Vernova settlement, 16 September 2026)

## Summary

From January 2025 the US federal government moved from promoting offshore wind to actively obstructing it. Main federal actions:

- **20 January 2025:** a presidential memorandum on wind energy. The Department of the Interior (DOI) says it paused new approvals of leases, permits, rights-of-way and loans while it reviewed offshore wind.
- **16 April 2025:** BOEM ordered Equinor's Empire Wind 1, then under construction and project-financed, to halt all activity on the Outer Continental Shelf. The order was lifted on 19 May 2025 after state–federal negotiation.
- **July–August 2025:** BOEM rescinded all designated Wind Energy Areas (30 July), dropped the five-year offshore wind lease-sale schedule (5 August), and launched a review of offshore wind rules, including financial assurance and decommissioning (7 August).
- **August 2025:** a stop-work order hit Ørsted's Revolution Wind, then more than 80 percent complete. A federal judge allowed work to resume on 22 September 2025.
- **22 December 2025:** Interior "paused" the leases of all five large projects under construction, citing classified Department of War reports on national security risks, including radar clutter. The five were Vineyard Wind 1, Revolution Wind, CVOW-Commercial, Sunrise Wind and Empire Wind 1.

Developers sued, and federal courts granted relief:
- Empire Wind obtained a preliminary injunction in the District of Columbia on 15 January 2026.
- Vineyard Wind obtained a stay of the order in the District of Massachusetts on 27 January 2026.

Revolution Wind delivered first power in March 2026. In 2026 Interior moved to buy back undeveloped leases:
- **TotalEnergies (March 2026):** about USD 928 million in lease payments reimbursed against TotalEnergies' investment in US LNG and oil and gas, with a pledge to develop no new US offshore wind.
- **Invenergy (June 2026):** four leases totalling USD 765 million terminated, with the money redirected to gas and geothermal.

BOEM's functions are being merged with BSEE into a new Marine Minerals Administration (announced April 2026).

For project finance, the episode turned "permitted and under construction" into a live political-risk category for the US. Lenders' main exposures were:
- equity-commitment guarantees that convert to debt repayment in a "full stop" (Empire);
- supplier termination fees;
- dependence on courts for relief.

## Verified facts

1. **Presidential memorandum and approvals pause.** Per DOI (7 August 2025), the President signed a memorandum on wind energy on 20 January 2025. The Department "has paused new approvals for offshore wind projects—including leases, permits, rights-of-way and loans" in compliance with it, while reviewing offshore wind projects. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Rule changes.** On 30 July 2025 BOEM rescinded all designated Wind Energy Areas on the Outer Continental Shelf. On 5 August 2025 it eliminated the requirement to publish a five-year offshore wind lease-sale schedule. On 7 August 2025 DOI launched a review of offshore wind regulations (30 CFR parts 585, 586 and 285), including the Renewable Energy Modernization Rule, financial assurance requirements and decommissioning cost estimates. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Empire Wind 1, first stop-work order.**
    - On 16 April 2025 Empire Offshore Wind LLC received a BOEM notice ordering it to halt all activities on the OCS until BOEM completed its review.
    - At 31 March 2025 the project's gross book value was around USD 2.5 billion, including South Brooklyn Marine Terminal.
    - Around USD 1.5 billion had been drawn under the project-finance term loan.
    - Equinor US Holdings had guaranteed the equity commitment. In a "full stop" scenario, the USD 1.5 billion would be repaid to lenders from the equity commitment, and Empire would face supplier termination fees.

    [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. On 19 May 2025 (announced 20 May CEST) BOEM lifted the Empire Wind stop-work order. The project was then more than 30 percent complete and aimed for commercial operation in 2027. Equinor said it would reassess project economics in the second quarter. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
5. **Empire Wind financing (cross-reference facts/t-market-norms.md).** Financial close was reached at the end of December 2024 on a project financing package of over USD 3 billion. Expected total capital investment was approximately USD 5 billion including the effect of expected ITCs. Offtake is a 25-year NYSERDA purchase and sale agreement at a strike price of USD 155.00/MWh for 810 MW. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. Empire Wind's lender group, per Equinor:
    - **Agents and roles:** Crédit Agricole CIB administrative agent; DNB collateral agent; Natixis and NatWest documentation banks; BNP Paribas green loan coordinator; Société Générale financial adviser and Milbank legal adviser to Equinor.
    - **Coordinating lead arrangers (15):** Société Générale, DNB, Commerzbank, BNP Paribas, Standard Chartered, Helaba, J.P. Morgan, Crédit Agricole CIB, NatWest, Barclays, Desjardins, Lloyds, Natixis, BBVA and AIB.
    - **Joint lead arrangers:** SEB, Bank of China, CaixaBank and OCBC.

    [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. **Revolution Wind stop-work order.** On 23 August 2025 the governors of Connecticut and Rhode Island responded to a federal order stopping construction of Revolution Wind, which they described as "more than 80% complete". On 22 September 2025 a federal judge issued an order allowing construction to resume. [confidence: high for the dates and the CT/RI characterisation; BOEM's order text not reviewed] [source: 6, 7]
8. **Lease pause of 22 December 2025.** DOI paused, "effective immediately", the leases of all large-scale offshore wind projects under construction, citing national security risks identified in classified Department of War reports and radar "clutter". The paused leases were:

    | Project | Lease |
    |---|---|
    | Vineyard Wind 1 | OCS-A 0501 |
    | Revolution Wind | OCS-A 0486 |
    | CVOW-Commercial | OCS-A 0483 |
    | Sunrise Wind | OCS-A 0487 |
    | Empire Wind 1 | OCS-A 0512 |

    [confidence: high] [source: 8]
9. **Empire Wind status at the December 2025 pause:**
    - more than 60 percent complete;
    - gross book value around USD 3.1 billion at 30 September 2025;
    - around USD 2.8 billion drawn under the project-finance term loan at 30 November 2025.

    Empire sued in the US District Court for the District of Columbia on 2 January 2026. Equinor then said it had invested over USD 4 billion, of which USD 2.7 billion had been drawn under the project financing. On 15 January 2026 the court granted a preliminary injunction allowing construction to resume while the suit continues. [confidence: high; note the USD 2.8 billion and USD 2.7 billion figures come from different Equinor releases] [source: 9, 10, 11]
10. **Vineyard Wind challenge.** Vineyard Wind sought a TRO and preliminary injunction in the District of Massachusetts on 15 January 2026. On 27 January 2026 the court stayed BOEM's suspension order, allowing full activities to resume. [confidence: high] [source: 12] (Details in facts/vineyard-wind-2024.md.)
11. **Revolution Wind first power.** Connecticut announced on 14 March 2026 that Revolution Wind (704 MW) had begun delivering first power to the New England grid, with full commercial operation expected later in 2026. Connecticut buys 304 MW and Rhode Island 400 MW. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
12. **TotalEnergies settlement** (DOI release, 23 March 2026). TotalEnergies committed to invest USD 928 million in 2026 in Rio Grande LNG Trains 1–4 and in US oil and shale gas. After that investment, the US will terminate and reimburse two leases:
    - OCS-A 0545 (Carolina Long Bay), paid USD 133,333,333;
    - OCS-A 0538 (New York Bight, Attentive Energy), paid USD 795,000,000.

    TotalEnergies pledged not to develop new US offshore wind. [confidence: high] [source: 14]
13. **Invenergy settlement** (DOI, June 2026). Invenergy affiliates will voluntarily terminate four offshore wind leases in the New York Bight, Central California and the Gulf of Maine, totalling USD 765 million, and redirect that amount to gas-fired plants in five states and Western geothermal. DOI described the agreements as "partial reimbursement" for the leases. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
14. **Agency restructuring.** On 3 April 2026 DOI announced a phased plan to establish the Marine Minerals Administration, combining BOEM and BSEE functions. [confidence: high] [source: 16]

## Financing and structure details

- **Empire Wind 1:** a non-recourse term loan with a large bank club (see facts 5–6). The sponsor's equity commitment was backed by an Equinor US Holdings guarantee. In a full stop, that guarantee would repay drawn debt (USD 1.5 billion at March 2025). This is the clearest disclosed example of how construction-phase political risk falls on the sponsor through equity-commitment guarantees. [2, 4, 5]
- **Lease buy-backs:** reimbursement of lease bonus payments against investment in other US energy assets is a new form of political-risk exit for leaseholders without construction. [14, 15]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Federal rationale (DOI):** national security risks from radar clutter, identified in classified reports, and an energy policy favouring "reliable" sources. [8]
- **Developers' position (Equinor):** Empire had coordinated on national security reviews with federal agencies, including the Department of War, since 2017. It called the order unlawful. Courts granted preliminary relief in each case reviewed. [9, 10, 11]
- **What protected lenders:** sponsor equity-commitment guarantees, and courts' willingness to grant interim relief. What hurt them: schedule slippage and the threat of a full stop with supplier termination costs. [2, 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 71 (Offshore wind):** Use 2025–2026 to teach regulatory and political risk after permitting. Even a fully permitted, project-financed asset can be ordered to stop. Walk through Equinor's disclosure: drawn debt, the equity-commitment guarantee and termination fees in a full stop. Ask what covenants, equity support and political-risk mitigants lenders would demand for the next US deal. Contrast with Ocean Wind (2023), where the risk was economic rather than political (see facts/ocean-wind.md).
