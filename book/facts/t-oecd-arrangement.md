# OECD Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits (2023 modernisation), sector understandings, ECA cover types, content rules, premia

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: the January 2026 revision of the Arrangement text, OECD/LEGAL/5005, reflecting changes agreed during 2025)

## Summary

The Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits is a non-binding "Gentlemen's Agreement" among eleven Participants: Australia, Canada, the European Union, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, Norway, Switzerland, Türkiye, the United Kingdom and the United States. It is administered with OECD Secretariat support but is not an OECD Act. It was adopted on 22 February 1978 and in force from 1 April 1978. It covers official export credit support with repayment terms of two years or more. It caps the generosity of that support so that exporters compete on price and quality, not on subsidised finance. To do that it sets five things:
- a minimum 15% down payment;
- a maximum of 85% official support on the export contract value;
- local-cost limits;
- maximum repayment terms and repayment profiles;
- minimum fixed interest rates (CIRR) and minimum premium rates (MPRs) for credit risk.

The participants agreed a modernisation package on 31 March 2023 and applied it from July 2023 (the revised CIRR rules apply to transactions committed from 15 July 2023). It made the following changes:
- The general maximum repayment term became 15 years, up from 8.5 years (high-income OECD buyers) and 10 years (all others).
- The renamed Climate Change Sector Understanding (CCSU) now allows up to 22 years, up from 18, for a much wider set of green sectors.
- Minimum premium rates were reduced for longer tenors.
- CIRR construction was reformed.
- Repayment-profile flexibility was increased.

Four sector understandings now sit in annexes: Climate Change (CCSU), Nuclear (NSU), Civil Aircraft (ASU) and Ships (SSU). The main text also prohibits official export credits for new coal-fired power plants that lack carbon capture.

## Verified facts

Unless stated otherwise, rules below are those of the Arrangement text as revised in January 2026 (OECD/LEGAL/5005). They were read in the official French version published in that document and are paraphrased in English here. The current text is found on the OECD Legal Instruments site and the OECD export credits pages.

**Status and scope**
1. The Arrangement was adopted on 22 February 1978 and came into force on 1 April 1978, building on a 1976 export credit "Consensus" among a smaller group. It is a "Gentlemen's Agreement" and not an OECD Act under Article 5 of the OECD Convention. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The current Participants are Australia, Canada, the European Union, Japan, Korea, New Zealand, Norway, Switzerland, Türkiye, the United Kingdom and the United States. Other countries may join by invitation. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
3. The Arrangement applies to official support for exports of goods and/or services, including leases, with a repayment term of two years or more. It does not apply to military equipment or agricultural commodities. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. Official support may take three forms: pure cover (export credit guarantee or insurance), official financing support (direct credit/financing, refinancing, or interest-rate support), or any combination of these. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. The most recent revision, in January 2026, made three changes agreed during 2025. It removed a deadline on the CIRR margin (Annex XII footnote 3). It also revised two parts of the Aircraft Sector Understanding: the rules on maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) contracts, and the obligor-rating process for de minimis transactions. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

**Financial terms (Chapter II)**
6. **Down payment.** Buyers must pay at least 15% of the export contract value by the starting point of credit. Official support for the down payment is limited to insurance or guarantee against the usual pre-credit (manufacturing) risks. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. **Maximum official support.** Official support may not exceed 85% of the export contract value. That value includes third-country goods and services but excludes local costs. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. **Local costs.** Official support for local costs may not exceed 40% of the export contract value for Category I countries (high-income OECD countries, classified on World Bank GNI per capita) or 50% for Category II countries. It may not be on terms more favourable than the related export. Where local-cost support exceeds 15% of the export contract value, prior notification is required. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. The 40%/50% local-cost caps date from a 2021 amendment, which raised them from 30% for all countries. They apply to all sectors except aircraft and ships. [confidence: medium, from secondary reporting; the current 40/50 caps are confirmed in the text] [source: 1, 5]
10. **Maximum repayment term (main text).** The repayment term may not exceed the useful life of the goods or project. The general maximum is 15 years. Power plants not eligible under the CCSU or NSU are capped at 12 years. Prior notification is required for repayment terms over 10 years where the credit is SDR 10 million or more. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. **Standard repayment profile.** Principal is normally repaid in equal, regular instalments (or equal instalments of principal plus interest, for example for leases), at least annually. The first principal instalment falls no later than one year after the starting point of credit. Interest is paid at least six-monthly, and interest after the starting point may not be capitalised. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
12. **Flexible (sculpted) profiles under the main text.** Where justified by a mismatch with the obligor's or project's cash flows, two limits apply:
    - no single principal repayment, or series of repayments within a six-month period, may exceed 30% of principal;
    - the first principal repayment must fall within 24 months of the starting point, and the weighted average life of the repayment period may not exceed 65% of the repayment term or six years, whichever is longer.

    Prior notification is required for credits of SDR 10 million or more. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
13. **Project finance definition.** For premium purposes the Arrangement treats a transaction as project finance where the exports go to a legally and economically independent project company. The lender must look to the project's cash flows and revenues as the source of repayment and to its assets as collateral. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
14. **Validity of terms.** Financial terms may not be fixed for more than six months before final commitment. CIRR validity has its own rules. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
15. **Loss-avoidance and matching.** Terms may be relaxed after contract signature solely to avoid or minimise losses. A Participant may also "match" terms offered by another Participant or a non-Participant, following the notification procedures. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

**CIRR (minimum fixed rates)**
16. A Participant giving fixed-rate official financing must charge at least the Commercial Interest Reference Rate (CIRR). The CIRR is a base rate plus a margin and may not be below 15 basis points. Base rates are calculated monthly, effective on the 15th, from government bond yields. The bond maturity is set by formula: drawdown period + 0.5 × repayment period + 0.5 × repayment frequency (in years), rounded and floored at three years. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
17. The CIRR margin formula is 0.5 × (three-month average of the five-year government-bond/swap spread) + 80 bp, bounded between 80 and 120 bp. A text footnote records that, following LIBOR cessation, Participants applied a temporary 100 bp margin for all currencies "until another solution is found". The January 2026 revision removed the deadline attached to that footnote. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
18. A CIRR may be held for up to 12 months before the financial contract date. A holding premium is added, rising from 20 bp (up to six months) to 44 bp (12 months). A commitment fee at or above market practice is charged on direct credits. Voluntary prepayment after the financial contract date requires the borrower to compensate the lender's breakage costs. The revised CIRR provisions apply to transactions committed from 15 July 2023, and the Participants must complete a full review of the CIRR provisions by 15 July 2027. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

**Premia (credit-risk pricing)**
19. Participants must charge premia on top of interest. These premia must be risk-based, converge, and be adequate to cover long-term operating costs and losses. They may not go below the Minimum Premium Rate (MPR). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
20. The MPR depends on six factors:
    - the country risk category;
    - the risk horizon (half the drawdown period plus the full repayment period);
    - the buyer risk category;
    - the percentage of cover and the quality of the product;
    - any country-risk mitigation techniques (offshore future-flow structures with escrow, or local-currency financing);
    - any buyer-risk credit enhancements.

    MPRs are expressed as an upfront percentage of principal. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
21. Countries are classified into eight country risk categories (0-7) using a quantitative Country Risk Assessment Model plus qualitative assessment. Classifications are reviewed at least annually and published by the Secretariat. No MPR is set for Category 0. Category 7 countries are in principle subject to premia above the Category 7 MPR. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
22. **Market-benchmark rule.** Transactions with obligors or guarantors in Category 0, high-income OECD or high-income euro-area countries, or with qualifying multilateral or regional institutions, are priced case by case against market benchmarks so that ECA pricing does not undercut private markets. For syndicated asset-backed or project finance packages, ECA pricing may be benchmarked to the private lenders in the same deal. At least 25% of the financing must be commercial loans or guarantees without bilateral or multilateral official support, ranking pari passu. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
23. **Buyer risk categories and product quality.** Buyer risk categories run from SOV/CC0 through CC1 upward, with "better than sovereign" SOV+ priced 10% below CC0. Project finance transactions may be classified on a transaction basis, that is after credit enhancements, but then receive no further discount for those enhancements. Cover products are graded below-standard, standard or above-standard, and MPRs differ accordingly:
    - below-standard: insurance without cover for interest during the claims waiting period, or with it but for an appropriate surcharge;
    - standard: insurance with waiting-period interest cover at no surcharge, and direct credit;
    - above-standard: guarantees. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

**2023 modernisation**
24. The Participants agreed the modernisation in principle on 31 March 2023, and the European Commission announced it on 3 April 2023. The EU had launched the initiative in June 2019, and the Participants agreed to proceed in 2020. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
25. The modernisation raised general maximum repayment terms from 8.5 years (high-income OECD countries) and 10 years (all others) to up to 15 years. It raised the maximum for climate-friendly/green and nuclear transactions from 18 to 22 years. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3, 4]
26. CCSU eligibility was broadened to seven areas:
    - environmentally sustainable energy production;
    - CO2 capture, storage and transport;
    - transmission, distribution and storage of energy;
    - clean hydrogen and ammonia;
    - low-emissions manufacturing;
    - zero- and low-emission transport;
    - clean energy minerals and ores.

    The sector understanding was renamed the Sector Understanding on Export Credits for Climate Change. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
27. The modernisation reduced minimum premium rates for longer repayment periods and added repayment flexibility. It reformed the CIRR so that it reflects the drawdown period and the repayment profile, not only the repayment term. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]

**Sector understandings and prohibitions**
28. There are four sector understandings, in Annexes I to IV:
    - Climate Change (CCSU), Annex I;
    - Nuclear (NSU), Annex II;
    - Civil Aircraft (ASU), Annex III;
    - Ships (SSU), Annex IV.

    The ASU and SSU have different Participants from the general Arrangement. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
29. **CCSU terms.** Maximum repayment terms for mitigation sectors are set sector by sector in CCSU Appendix I. Most listed sectors carry 22 years, with some sub-types at 18 years. Adaptation projects meeting Appendix II criteria, and water supply and wastewater projects, may receive up to 22 years. The NSU likewise provides up to 22 years. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
30. **CCSU repayment flexibility.** Transactions with up to 15 years, and Class B Type 1 transactions, follow the main-text flexibility rules: 30% per six months, first repayment within 24 months, and weighted average life at most 65% of tenor or six years. All other CCSU transactions get wider limits:
    - no more than 35% of principal in any six-month period;
    - first repayment within 36 months;
    - weighted average life of up to 70% of the tenor or six years. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
31. **CCSU notification and scope.** CCSU support above SDR 10 million requires prior notification at least 10 calendar days before commitment, with a description showing how eligibility criteria are met. For Class A renewable plants, CCSU terms do not extend to the buyer's off-site items (roads, site preparation, power lines and so on). The exceptions are the initial interconnection line, and substations and transmission lines of at least 60 kV, which may receive terms no better than the plant's. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
32. **Coal.** Participants may not give official export credits or tied aid for new coal-fired power plants, or new units at existing plants. Equipment for existing coal plants is allowed only if it reduces pollution or CO2 without extending plant life or raising capacity. Plants with operational carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS), and retrofits adding it, are excepted. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

**National content rules (outside the Arrangement)**
33. The Arrangement caps support relative to contract value. Each ECA sets its own national content rules.
    - **US EXIM** (medium- and long-term): generally supports the lesser of 85% of the value of eligible goods and services in the US export contract, or 100% of the US content. For congressionally defined "transformational export" sectors it may give full OECD-compliant financing if US content is at least 51%, but content of People's Republic of China origin is ineligible. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
    - **UKEF:** buyer credit and direct lending require at least 20% UK content in the contract value. Buyer credit cover is up to 85% of contract value. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

## Financing and structure details

How the rules fit a typical ECA-covered project financing:
- The exporter's contract is the base. The buyer (here the project company) pays at least 15% down, typically funded from equity or commercial debt. The ECA covers up to 85% of the contract value.
- Local costs can be supported up to 40% or 50% of the contract value, on terms no better than the export.
- Cover is delivered in one of three forms:
  - pure cover: a guarantee or insurance to commercial lenders;
  - direct lending by the ECA, at CIRR if fixed-rate;
  - refinancing or interest-rate support.
- For project finance, the Arrangement's own profile flexibility is what allows sculpted repayment:
  - main text: 30% per six months, first repayment within 24 months, WAL up to 65% of tenor or 6 years;
  - CCSU long-tenor deals: 35% per six months, 36 months, 70%.
- Premium is paid upfront or as a margin. It is set no lower than the MPR for the host country's risk category, the project's transaction-based buyer category, the cover percentage and the product quality. In high-income OECD markets, pricing is benchmarked to the commercial banks in the same deal, where at least 25% must be uncovered pari passu commercial debt.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The European Commission framed the 2023 package as an EU initiative to align export credits with climate objectives. It describes the main effect as longer terms and lower minimum premia for green projects (source 2). TXF reported the reaction of the Dutch ECA, which called the package a major breakthrough (source 4).
- One academic assessment of the new Arrangement asks whether it was a "breakthrough or bad compromise". It is published in Global Policy (Wiley, DOI 10.1111/1758-5899.13255). Its arguments were not reviewed for this sheet; cite it only after reading it.
- A structural limitation is that the Arrangement binds only its eleven Participants. Non-participants, notably China, are not bound. The Arrangement recognises this through information-sharing with non-Participants and through matching of non-Participant terms (Articles 4 and 17) (source 1).

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 29 (The lenders):**
  - Teach ECA debt as the product of a rulebook: the Arrangement sets the outer boundary of every ECA term sheet (85/15, local costs, tenor, profile, CIRR, MPR), and inside it each ECA adds its own content and national-interest rules. Have the reader build the ECA tranche of Case P from the rule: contract value → 85% eligible → local costs → tenor test (15 years general, 22 years CCSU) → sculpting limits → premium driver checklist.
  - Show why a renewable or transmission project gets longer, more sculpted ECA debt than a gas plant (12-year cap for non-CCSU power plants).
  - Show why ECA pricing in high-income OECD markets must track the commercial banks in the same deal.
  - Stress the as-of date. The text is revised often, so the reader should check the current version on the OECD site.

## Do not state

- Do not quote specific current MPR percentages for any country category or tenor. The figures depend on a formula in Annex VI and the current country classification, which were not extracted here.
- Do not state that 2023 modernisation "doubled" or "halved" anything, or give a percentage reduction in premia. Sources say only that MPRs were reduced for longer tenors.
- Do not state the exact day the modernised text entered into force beyond "July 2023". Secondary sources differ: Mayer Brown says 5 July 2023, while the CIRR annex applies from 15 July 2023.
- Do not state current ECA market volumes (for example TXF or Berne Union totals). They were not verified.
- Do not describe a separate "Project Finance Sector Understanding" or "Rail Sector Understanding" as current. The current text lists only the CCSU, NSU, ASU and SSU, and project finance flexibility now sits in the main text (Article 13(e)).
- Do not state UKEF Direct Lending Facility limits. Product-brochure figures may be outdated.
- Do not claim the Arrangement is legally binding international law. It is a gentlemen's agreement (EU law does give it effect for EU member states, but that was not verified here).
