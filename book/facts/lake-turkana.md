# Lake Turkana Wind Power: transmission delay, deemed energy

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: LTWP's reported milestones through March 2022; core coverage 2005 to the July 2019 inauguration and the 2019 penalty payment)

## Summary

Lake Turkana Wind Power (LTWP) is a 310 MW wind farm of 365 Vestas V52 turbines (850 kW each) near Loiyangalani in Marsabit County, northern Kenya. It sells all its output to Kenya Power (KPLC) at a fixed price under a 20-year power purchase agreement (PPA). Costing approximately EUR 620–630 million, it was described as the largest private investment in Kenya's history. Financial close came in March 2014, with the African Development Bank (AfDB) as mandated lead arranger. Construction began in October 2014. All turbines were erected by March 2017, and LTWP reports that its 310 MW was ready for full commercial operation in July 2017. The plant could not deliver that power, however. The government's state transmission company, KETRACO, was responsible for the roughly 428–438 km, 400 kV line from Loiyangalani to Suswa, financed by Spain. The line's construction started only in November 2015, and its Spanish contractor, Isolux Corsán, then collapsed into insolvency. The line was energized in September 2018, and LTWP declared full commercial operation in March 2019. Because the risk of a late grid connection sat with the Kenyan side, Kenya became liable for payments for energy the plant could not deliver. These were reported as a penalty of about EUR 127 million. The government negotiated in 2017 to spread part of it through a consumer surcharge, and in 2019 a last-minute budget amendment authorized a KES 1.16 billion payment that the press said averted a sovereign-default scenario. The case is a textbook example of interface risk between a privately financed plant and publicly built transmission. It shows how deemed-energy provisions move that risk onto the offtaker and the state, and what it costs when it materializes.

## Financing and structure details

- Sponsors at financial close (Power Technology): KP&P Africa, Aldwych International, IFU, Vestas, Finnfund and Norfund. Google's planned 12.5% acquisition from Vestas was reported in 2017 (fact 10). [source: 3, 4]
- Debt of approximately EUR 475 million (Business Daily), with the AfDB as MLA (EUR 115 million) and a 13-lender group. [source: 1, 3, 5]
- Offtake: 20-year fixed-price PPA with KPLC. KPLC's payment obligations were backed by a government letter of support and, from 2017, a consumer-funded contingency fund (fact 9). [source: 1, 4, 5]
- Interface risk: the plant was privately financed, but the evacuation line was a separate public project, built by KETRACO and financed by Spain. The PPA put the cost of late grid availability on the Kenyan side, as the penalty and surcharge arrangements show. [source: 4, 5]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- What went wrong: the plant and its only route to market were built on separate timetables, with separate financiers and separate contractors. The public line started about a year after the plant and lost its EPC contractor to insolvency. The plant was complete in 2017 but could not be fully dispatched until late 2018. [source: 2, 5]
- What went right for lenders: the contracts allocated the transmission-delay risk to Kenya. As a result, the project company was paid for undelivered energy (reported as a penalty), and the sovereign treated paying it as a matter of creditworthiness. LTWP director Rizwan Fazal told Business Daily, in substance, that honouring contracts was essential to attracting foreign investment. [source: 4, 5]
- The cost of that allocation fell on Kenyan consumers and taxpayers through the surcharge and budget payments. This became a political issue and shows the fiscal contingent liabilities that sovereign-backed IPP risk transfers create. [source: 4, 5]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 18 (PPAs and tolling): Use LTWP to teach deemed energy: the offtaker pays for energy the plant could have delivered but for grid unavailability. Show what must be specified: how the energy is measured (wind data and availability), the start date (the scheduled connection date), any cap or deferral, and the payment source. Contrast the 2017 renegotiation, which spread payments over six years through a surcharge, with the strict contractual entitlement.
- Ch 25 (inputs and access: grid connection): The interconnection was the binding constraint, not the wind. Teach grid-connection diligence: who builds, who finances, the contractor's credit, the critical-path linkage, and the remedies. Show that interface risk does not disappear when it is allocated by contract. It moves to the party least able to control it, here the state, which then pays with public money.
- Ch 70 (onshore wind and solar): A remote, world-class resource (here the Turkana corridor winds) is only bankable with firm evacuation. Lenders should require evacuation completion tests, deemed-energy cover and government support. Model a delay case of 12–24 months: the plant was ready in July 2017 and energized in September 2018.

## Do not state

- The precise split and timing of the EUR 127 million penalty. The 2017 report describes a EUR 638,889 a month surcharge over six years (which works out to about EUR 46 million). The 2019 report calls EUR 46 million a lump sum and has the remaining EUR 81 million paid over six years. Present the total as reported and avoid asserting the mechanism.
- That the PPA term is literally "deemed energy" with a specific formula, or that an AfDB partial risk guarantee of a specific amount backed the line-delay payments. Both are widely reported, but neither was verified in primary documents for this sheet.
- The PPA tariff. Business Daily reported "8.5 US cents" per unit, while other sources give a euro-denominated tariff. Unverified.
- That the World Bank withdrew because of the transmission risk, or any specific reason for a World Bank exit. Not verified.
- Detailed equity percentages today, or the dates of Vestas's, Google's and Finnfund's exits. Google's deal was reported as cancelled, but this was not verified in a primary source.
- Post-2019 curtailment or "unabsorbed energy" payments by KPLC. Reported in the Kenyan press but not verified here.
- Land-rights litigation outcomes. Not covered in this sheet.
