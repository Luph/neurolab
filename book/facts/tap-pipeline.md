# Trans Adriatic Pipeline (TAP) project financing (2018–2019)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: TAP five-year operations release, 15 November 2025; first-level capacity expansion available from 1 January 2026)

## Summary

TAP is the roughly 877–878 km final section of the Southern Gas Corridor. It carries gas from the Shah Deniz field in Azerbaijan, receiving it from the Trans Anatolian Pipeline (TANAP) at Kipoi on the Turkish–Greek border, and runs across northern Greece, Albania and the Adriatic Sea to southern Italy. TAP AG, based in Baar, Switzerland, reached financial close on its project financing in December 2018. It announced the deal on 11 January 2019 as EUR 3.9 billion, which it called the largest project finance for a European infrastructure project in 2018. The third-party senior debt was EUR 3,765 million with a door-to-door tenor of 16.5 years. It combined a EUR 700 million EIB direct facility (backed by an EU guarantee under the European Fund for Strategic Investments), an EBRD A/B loan (EUR 500 million plus EUR 500 million), ECA-covered facilities from Bpifrance, Euler Hermes and SACE, and an uncovered commercial term loan. Seventeen commercial banks took part. Until financial close the shareholders had funded all costs. The commercial basis was long-term ship-or-pay capacity contracts with Shah Deniz II shippers, made possible by an EU exemption from third-party access, tariff regulation and ownership unbundling (Commission decision of 16 May 2013). Commercial operations began on 15 November 2020.

## Verified facts

1. TAP completed financial close in December 2018 and announced it on 11 January 2019. TAP described the financing as EUR 3.9 billion and "the largest project finance agreed for a European infrastructure project in 2018". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Third-party senior debt raised was EUR 3,765 million, with a door-to-door tenor of 16.5 years. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. Tranches: EIB direct facility EUR 700 million (with an EFSI guarantee from the European Union); EBRD A-loan EUR 500 million; EBRD B-loan EUR 500 million funded by commercial banks; ECA facilities with comprehensive cover from Bpifrance (EUR 450 million), Euler Hermes (EUR 280 million) and SACE (EUR 700 million); and a commercial term loan of EUR 635 million with no ECA or multilateral involvement. The tranches sum to EUR 3,765 million. [confidence: high; arithmetic checked] [source: 1]
4. Seventeen commercial banks participated alongside the EBRD and EIB. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Shareholders had funded all costs before financial close. At that time they were BP 20%, SOCAR 20%, Snam 20%, Fluxys 19%, Enagás 16% and Axpo 5%. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. Advisers: Société Générale (financial adviser to TAP), Allen & Overy (TAP legal) and Clifford Chance (lenders' legal). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. TAP's managing director stated that TAP had voluntarily committed to comply with the environmental and social standards required by the international financial institutions. An EIB vice-president described the EIB as an anchor lender, and the project as the largest energy project then being built in Europe. [confidence: high as reported in TAP's release] [source: 1]
8. The current shareholding is bp, SOCAR, Snam, Fluxys and Enagás at 20% each. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
9. TAP began commercial operations on 15 November 2020. By November 2025 it had transported more than 52 bcm to Europe, and its company page later reported more than 60 bcm delivered, including 50 bcm to Italy. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
10. The first level of capacity expansion was triggered after the binding phase of the 2021 market test, announced 30 January 2023, adding 1.2 bcm per year. The Kipoi compressor works were completed in 2025, giving additional long-term capacity from 1 January 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 5]
11. The European Commission decided on 16 May 2013 to exempt TAP from third-party access, tariff regulation and ownership unbundling under Directive 2009/73/EC. TAP had applied in 2011 so that it could sign long-term ship-or-pay transportation agreements with Shah Deniz II shippers. [confidence: medium; via secondary encyclopedia citing the Commission decision, which was not opened directly] [source: 5]
12. Construction started on 16 May 2016. Shah Deniz was chosen over Nabucco West as the export route in June 2013. [confidence: medium; secondary source] [source: 5]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right: the DFIs and ECAs gave long tenor (16.5 years) and anchored a large commercial bank group. The EFSI guarantee let the EIB take a larger exposure. Long-term ship-or-pay contracts under a regulatory exemption gave revenue certainty that a regulated-tariff TSO would not have had at the outset. [source: 1, 5]
- Note: the IFI environmental and social standards came with the money. TAP's statement links its E&S compliance to the lenders' requirements. [source: 1]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 75 (midstream).** Use TAP as the archetype of a merchant-exempt, contracted gas pipeline financing. Revenue came from long-term ship-or-pay contracts enabled by an EU third-party access exemption. Debt came from a layered DFI/ECA/commercial structure in which every tranche can be traced and summed. Show how the B-loan structure spreads EBRD's preferred-creditor umbrella over commercial banks, and how ECA cover links to equipment and contractor nationality.

## Do not state

- TAP's total capex (figures around EUR 4.5 billion circulate). Not verified.
- That the EIB lent EUR 1.5 billion. That figure is sometimes cited for an earlier EIB board approval, but it is unverified here. TAP's financial close release shows EUR 700 million from the EIB. The EIB and EBRD project pages were not retrievable (404).
- Pricing, gearing or covenant levels.
- Exact pipeline length beyond "approximately 877–878 km". TAP has used both figures.
