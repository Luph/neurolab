# Jubilee field, Ghana: financing a deepwater field development (2009–2011)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Ghana licence extensions to 2040 approved December 2025, per Kosmos 2025 10-K)

## Summary

The Jubilee field was discovered offshore Ghana in 2007 and straddles the West Cape Three Points (WCTP) and Deepwater Tano (DT) blocks. It was unitised on 13 July 2009 and reached first oil on 28 November 2010, through a converted-VLCC FPSO (the Kwame Nkrumah) built and leased by MODEC. This sheet uses Jubilee because its financing is documented in public SEC filings. The field was not financed through a single field-level SPV loan. Each partner financed its own share. Kosmos Energy, a private equity-backed company with no production at the time, used senior and junior reserve-based commercial debt facilities. These were secured on its Ghana subsidiary, carried borrowing base tests set on a forecast cash flow model, and included IFC participation. The facilities totalled USD 1.25 billion by August 2010, and in March 2011 Kosmos refinanced them with a USD 2.0 billion facility. The FPSO was a second financing layer. The unit partners advanced funds to MODEC under an Advance Payments Agreement while MODEC sought long-term bank financing. That financing was suspended in 2011 during an anti-corruption investigation, and in December 2011 the Jubilee Unit bought the FPSO from MODEC for USD 754.5 million. Jubilee is therefore a good case for showing how "project financing" of upstream fields is usually done: through partner-level, model-based RBLs plus lease or vessel finance, not through one non-recourse field loan. The single-SPV structure is more typical of midstream and LNG projects.

## Verified facts

1. Kosmos entered into the Unitization and Unit Operating Agreement (UUOA) on 13 July 2009 with Ghana National Petroleum Corporation (GNPC) and the other WCTP and DT block partners. Kosmos was Technical Operator for development, and a Tullow affiliate was Unit Operator. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Initial unit interests per the May 2011 prospectus: Tullow 34.7047%, Kosmos 23.4913%, Anadarko 23.4913%, GNPC 13.75%. The interests were subject to redetermination. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. First oil was on 28 November 2010, 42 months after discovery, which Kosmos described as a record for a West African deepwater development at that water depth. The FPSO's design capacity was 120,000 bopd, with 1.6 million barrels of storage. [confidence: high as company statement] [source: 1]
4. Kosmos had received three rounds of equity commitments totalling USD 1.05 billion from Warburg Pincus and Blackstone (with management) before its IPO. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. In 2009 Kosmos secured commercial debt facilities "from a number of financial institutions, including the IFC" of up to USD 900 million to fund its share of Jubilee Phase 1. USD 825 million was committed at 31 December 2009. Kosmos Energy Holdings granted a charge over the shares of its Ghana subsidiary (KEO) to the lenders. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. On 23 August 2010 the facilities were increased to USD 1.25 billion. This comprised a USD 950 million senior facility, a USD 200 million junior facility and USD 100 million from IFC (USD 50 million senior, USD 50 million junior). Standard Chartered was Global Coordinator, Senior Facility Agent and co-technical and modelling bank. BNP Paribas was security trustee, junior agent and hedging coordinator. Société Générale was lead technical and modelling bank. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. The senior facilities matured on 15 December 2015 and the junior on 15 June 2016. The borrowing base was set each 15 June and 15 December from a forecast agreed with the technical and modelling banks. It was based in part on the NPV of net cash flows and capex, "reduced by certain percentages". At 31 December 2010 drawings were about USD 1.05 billion, and borrowing base constraints limited availability. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. In March 2011 Kosmos replaced these with a USD 2.0 billion facility (accordion to USD 3.0 billion) maturing 29 March 2018. Margin was 3.25%–4.75% over LIBOR, with covenants of FLCR at least 1.30x and LLCR at least 1.10x. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. The FPSO was financed through an Advance Payments Agreement. Tullow signed it as Unit Operator, and Kosmos, Tullow and Anadarko lent funds to MODEC for construction. MODEC was to repay by the earlier of 15 September 2011 and its first long-term drawdown. The agreement gave the unit partners an option to buy the FPSO at a discount to market value. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
10. The May 2011 prospectus disclosed that MODEC had reported possible violations of the US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, and that MODEC's long-term bank funding had been suspended pending investigation. The Jubilee partners and IFC were working with MODEC on the matter. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. In December 2011 Tullow, as Unit Operator and on behalf of the Jubilee Unit, acquired the FPSO from MODEC for USD 754.5 million (USD 202.6 million net to Kosmos). Kosmos paid USD 99.8 million in cash and applied its USD 102.8 million note receivable under the Advance Payments Agreement. MODEC continued under an operations and maintenance agreement. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
12. Kosmos's 2025 10-K reports that in December 2025 the Ghana partnership received government approval to extend the WCTP and DT petroleum agreements to 2040. From July 2036 GNPC's share increases by an additional 10%. Kosmos's paying interest on Jubilee development is 43.05%. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
13. Kosmos's current RBL still values Jubilee and TEN reserves and resources in its borrowing base. Kosmos's 2026 covenant set is in t-rbl.md. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

## Financing and structure details

- **Borrower level.** Each partner financed its share separately. Kosmos's financing was a sponsor-level RBL, secured on shares of the Ghana subsidiary, with senior and junior tranches and an IFC tranche. Availability was governed by borrowing base redeterminations. [source: 1]
- **Pre-completion financing.** The RBL funded development capex before production, because the borrowing base credited the NPV of forecast cash flows net of remaining capex. This is a defining feature of the international RBL (see t-rbl.md). [source: 1]
- **Vessel layer.** The contractor FPSO was funded first by partner advances, with a lease to the unit. When the contractor's financing failed, the partners bought the vessel. [source: 1, 2]
- **Not verified:** Tullow's and Anadarko's own financing terms, IFC's separate investment amounts in Tullow, the total Phase 1 capex, and individual commercial bank names beyond the roles listed.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right: the model-based RBL let a company with no production fund its share of a deepwater development before first oil. A two-tier senior and junior structure plus IFC participation broadened the lender pool. Kosmos's own account credits phased development with the fast timeline. [source: 1]
- Risk realised: dependence on contractor-arranged vessel finance. When MODEC's long-term financing was suspended, the partners had to step in and buy the FPSO, converting a lease into owned capex. [source: 1, 2]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 75.** Use Jubilee to show what "project-financed upstream" usually means. Partner-level RBLs are sized on a forecast cash flow model (NPV haircuts, FLCR and LLCR) and refinanced as the field derisks, from USD 1.25 billion pre-first-oil to USD 2.0 billion post-first-oil. Unitisation and redetermination of interests are credit issues because the borrowing base depends on the unit share. The FPSO purchase is a worked example of contractor-finance risk; cross-refer to fpso-financing.md.

## Do not state

- Total Jubilee Phase 1 development cost. Figures around USD 3 billion or more circulate, but none was verified in a primary source here.
- That Jubilee was financed by a single non-recourse field-level project loan. The filings show partner-level facilities.
- The amount of IFC's total investment across Jubilee partners, or IFC project numbers. IFC's disclosure pages were not retrieved.
- Details or outcome of the MODEC investigation beyond the prospectus disclosure.
- The later Jubilee turret and FPSO problems and related insurance claims. These were not researched for this sheet.
- That Johan Sverdrup or Mad Dog were project financed. No public field-level project financing was found, and both are generally understood to be corporate-funded; this was not verified here.
