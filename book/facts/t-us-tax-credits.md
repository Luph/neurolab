# US tax equity, IRA credits, transferability, 2025 changes (One Big Beautiful Bill Act), FEOC rules

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: the 4 July 2026 beginning-of-construction deadline for wind and solar has passed; Notice 2026-15 interim prohibited-foreign-entity guidance, 2026; Crux mid-year market report, 3 September 2026)

## Summary

US renewable projects have long been financed partly with federal tax credits that developers cannot use themselves, because they lack taxable income. Until 2022 the answer was "tax equity": a large taxpayer, typically a bank, invests in a project partnership (usually a "partnership flip") in exchange for most of the credits and tax losses. The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 (IRA, Public Law 117-169, 16 August 2022) changed the market in two ways. It created technology-neutral credits for zero-emission electricity: a production credit (§45Y) and an investment credit (§48E) for facilities placed in service after 31 December 2024. And it made most credits transferable for cash to unrelated buyers (§6418), with direct "elective pay" (§6417) for tax-exempt and government entities. A transfer market of about USD 30 billion in 2024 grew alongside "hybrid" tax equity, in which the partnership sells some credits. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, Public Law 119-21, 4 July 2025) kept transferability and the credits for storage, geothermal, hydro and nuclear. But it ends §45Y/§48E for wind and solar facilities placed in service after 31 December 2027, unless construction began by 4 July 2026. It also added "prohibited foreign entity" (PFE) rules (popularly "FEOC" rules), which deny credits to Chinese-linked owners and to projects whose costs rely too heavily on PFE-made components. Treasury tightened beginning-of-construction rules for wind and solar in Notice 2025-42, and issued interim PFE material-assistance safe harbours in Notice 2026-15. All rules here are US federal, as at 3 October 2026. They are found in the Internal Revenue Code (26 U.S.C.) as amended by Public Law 119-21, and in IRS notices and regulations.

## Verified facts

**IRA architecture (before OBBBA amendments)**

1. Sections 45Y (Clean Electricity Production Credit) and 48E (Clean Electricity Investment Credit) were added by IRA §§13701-13702 (Public Law 117-169, enacted 16 August 2022). They apply to electricity generating facilities placed in service after 31 December 2024 with a greenhouse gas emissions rate not greater than zero. Final regulations under §§45Y and 48E were published on 15 January 2025 (90 FR 4006). Revenue Procedure 2025-14 lists wind and solar as having an emissions rate not greater than zero. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. Rates under §48E: [confidence: high] [source: 3]
   - a base credit of 6% of qualified investment;
   - up to five times the base (30%) for facilities meeting prevailing-wage and registered-apprenticeship requirements;
   - a 10-percentage-point bonus for meeting domestic content requirements (steel, iron and manufactured products);
   - a 10-percentage-point bonus for location in an energy community.

   §45Y uses the same five-times prevailing-wage-and-apprenticeship multiplier.
3. Section 6418 (transferability) lets an eligible taxpayer sell all or part of eligible credits to an unrelated buyer, on these terms: [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
   - the buyer must pay in cash;
   - the payment is not included in the seller's gross income and is not deductible by the buyer;
   - a transferee cannot transfer the credit again;
   - an excessive credit transfer increases the buyer's tax by the excess plus 20% of it (subject to a reasonable-cause exception);
   - the taxpayer must pre-register with the IRS.

   The eligible credits are §§30C, 45, 45Q, 45U, 45V, 45X, 45Y, 45Z, 48, 48C and 48E. Final regulations on transfer and elective pay were issued in 2024.
4. Section 6417 (elective pay) lets tax-exempt organisations, governmental entities, rural electric cooperatives, Alaska Native corporations and territorial governments receive certain credits as a refundable payment. The IRS treats the elective payment amount as a tax payment. [confidence: high] [source: 5]

**OBBBA (Public Law 119-21, 139 Stat. 72, enacted 4 July 2025)**

5. Section 70512(a) amends §45Y(d), and section 70513(a) amends §48E(e). Neither credit applies to an "applicable facility" (one using wind or solar energy) placed in service after 31 December 2027. For §48E, this covers qualified property placed in service by the taxpayer after that date that is part of an applicable facility. Energy storage at a wind or solar facility is excepted. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
6. Under §§70512(l)(4) and 70513(g)(5), the wind and solar termination applies only to facilities whose construction begins after 4 July 2026, which is 12 months after enactment. Facilities that began construction by then keep their credits under the normal continuity rules, which in practice allow a placed-in-service date up to the end of the fourth calendar year after construction began. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 6]
7. OBBBA sets the §45Y/§48E "applicable year" at calendar year 2032. That fixes the start of the existing phase-down for non-wind/solar zero-emission technologies such as storage, geothermal, hydro and nuclear: full credit for facilities beginning construction through 2033, then 75% (2034), 50% (2035) and zero thereafter, under the unchanged IRA phase-out schedule in §45Y(d)(2). [confidence: high that the applicable year is 2032; medium for the year-by-year percentages, which come from the pre-existing statute and were not re-read for this sheet] [source: 6]
8. Executive Order 14315 of 7 July 2025, "Ending Market Distorting Subsidies for Unreliable, Foreign-Controlled Energy Sources," directed Treasury to enforce the wind and solar termination strictly. That included restricting broad safe harbours unless a substantial portion of a facility has been built. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
9. Notice 2025-42 was issued on 15 August 2025. For the 4 July 2026 deadline, it makes the Physical Work Test the sole method of establishing beginning of construction for wind and solar facilities, and removes the Five Percent Safe Harbor. The exception is "low output solar facilities" with maximum net output not greater than 1.5 MW (AC), which may still use the Five Percent Safe Harbor. The Continuity Safe Harbor is met if the facility is placed in service by the end of the fourth calendar year after construction began. The notice applies to facilities whose construction did not begin before 2 September 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 7]
10. OBBBA ended several other credits: [confidence: high] [source: 6, 8]
    - the clean vehicle credits §§30D, 25E and 45W, for vehicles acquired after 30 September 2025;
    - the residential credits §§25D and 25C, after 31 December 2025;
    - §45V clean hydrogen, now limited to facilities beginning construction before 1 January 2028 (previously 2033);
    - §45X, which no longer applies to wind energy components produced and sold after 31 December 2027, and has a phase-out added for applicable critical minerals from 2031 to 2033.
11. OBBBA restored 100% bonus depreciation (§168(k)) for qualified property acquired after 19 January 2025. Section 70509 amended the §168(e)(3)(B)(vi) "energy property" cost-recovery class for property whose construction begins after 31 December 2024. [confidence: high for the bonus depreciation rule; medium for the precise effect of §70509 on specific technologies] [source: 6]

**Prohibited foreign entity ("FEOC") rules**

12. New §7701(a)(51) defines a "prohibited foreign entity" (PFE) as a "specified foreign entity" or a "foreign-influenced entity." Specified foreign entities include: [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
    - foreign entities of concern under §9901(8) of the FY2021 NDAA;
    - Chinese military companies listed under §1260H of the FY2021 NDAA;
    - entities on certain other lists;
    - "foreign-controlled entities," which include agencies and instrumentalities of covered-nation governments.

    An entity is foreign-influenced if, during the taxable year, any one of these is true:
    - a specified foreign entity can appoint a covered officer;
    - a single specified foreign entity owns at least 25%;
    - specified foreign entities together own at least 40%;
    - at least 15% of its debt is held by specified foreign entities.

    It is also foreign-influenced if, in the previous year, it made payments to a specified foreign entity under an arrangement giving that entity "effective control" over a qualified facility, energy storage technology or eligible component (for example through licensing).
13. Under §45Y(g)(13) and the parallel §48E provision, no §45Y/§48E credit is allowed to a taxpayer that is a specified foreign entity or a foreign-influenced entity. These rules apply to taxable years beginning after 4 July 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
14. "Material assistance": a facility or energy storage technology whose construction begins after 31 December 2025 is not a qualified facility if its material assistance cost ratio (MACR) is below the statutory threshold. MACR is broadly the share of direct costs of manufactured products not attributable to PFEs. The thresholds, by year construction begins: [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]

    | Construction begins | Qualified facilities | Energy storage |
    |---|---|---|
    | 2026 | 40% | 55% |
    | 2027 | 45% | 60% |
    | 2028 | 50% | 65% |
    | 2029 | 55% | 70% |
    | After 2029 | 60% | 75% |

    The §45X thresholds for components, by year of sale:

    | Component | Thresholds |
    |---|---|
    | Solar components | 50% (2026) rising to 85% (after 2029) |
    | Wind components | 85% (2026), 90% (2027) |
    | Inverters | 50% rising to 70% |
    | Battery components | 60% rising to 85% |
    | Applicable critical minerals | 0% through 2029, rising to 50% after 2032 |
15. Section 50(a)(4) adds a recapture rule. If a "specified taxpayer" makes an "applicable payment," meaning an effective-control payment to a specified foreign entity, within 10 years after placing §48E property in service, 100% of the §48E credit is recaptured. Specified taxpayers are those allowed a §48E credit for a taxable year beginning more than two years after enactment. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
16. Section 6418(g)(5) prohibits transferring §§45Q, 45U, 45X, 45Y, 45Z and 48E credits to a specified foreign entity. Transferability itself is otherwise preserved. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 6]
17. Notice 2026-15 gives interim guidance and safe harbours for calculating MACR under §§45X, 45Y and 48E, using cost-percentage tables building on the domestic-content safe harbour tables of Notices 2023-38, 2024-41 and 2025-08. It also covers other PFE matters ahead of proposed regulations. For PFE purposes, beginning of construction is determined under Notices 2013-29 and 2018-59 as in effect on 1 January 2025, not under Notice 2025-42. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

**Market data (indicative; US market; source and period stated)**

18. Crux estimates USD 30 billion of tax credit transfers in 2024: USD 24 billion of 2024-vintage credits plus USD 6 billion of forward sales. Average 2024 prices were about 92.5 cents per dollar of credit for ITCs (92.0 in 2023) and about 95.0 cents for PTCs (94.0 in 2023). Other 2024 features: [confidence: high, as Crux's estimates] [source: 9]
    - more than 90% of PTC deals were direct transfers, while 57% of ITC deals were sold out of tax equity partnerships (hybrids);
    - more than 75% of ITC deals carried full or partial tax insurance;
    - more than 80% of PTC deals relied on a seller-parent indemnity.
19. At Norton Rose Fulbright's "Cost of Capital: 2026 Outlook" (published 29 January 2026), Rubiao Song of J.P. Morgan estimated about USD 35 billion of 2025 tax equity and tax credit sale volume in solar, wind and batteries, plus USD 10-15 billion in other credits (45X, 45U, 45Z). That is USD 45-50 billion in total, about 10% up on 2024. Other points from the same panel: [confidence: medium (practitioner estimates)] [source: 10]
    - Jack Cargas of Bank of America said traditional tax equity with no intended credit sale was as much as 30% of the tax equity market in 2024, and less in 2025;
    - Song said tax equity is about 45% of the capital stack in ITC deals, and that nearly 80% of projects J.P. Morgan evaluates choose the ITC over the PTC.
20. Practitioners at Infocast New York (reported by Norton Rose Fulbright, 31 July 2026) described partnership flips as still predominant for portfolio solar tax equity. They described hybrid flip-plus-transfer deals as considerably more work, and FEOC compliance for batteries as an active concern. [confidence: medium] [source: 11]

Drivers of transfer prices and tax equity terms:

- credit type (PTC or ITC) and recapture exposure;
- buyer credit quality and indemnity or insurance;
- deal size;
- vintage (spot or forward);
- the supply-demand balance of tax capacity;
- policy risk (OBBBA deadlines, PFE compliance);
- interest rates.

## Financing and structure details

- **Partnership flip (traditional tax equity):** the sponsor and the tax equity investor form a partnership that owns the project. The investor receives most of the tax credits and depreciation plus some cash until it reaches a target after-tax return (the "flip"). Allocations then shift mostly to the sponsor, who usually holds a purchase option. Senior or back-levered debt sits at the sponsor level and is structurally subordinated to tax equity. Practitioners report flips as still common for portfolio solar in 2026. [source: 10, 11]
- **Hybrid tax equity:** a flip partnership in which the partnership sells some credits under §6418, typically ITCs. The investor provides capital closer to its own tax appetite and the transfer buyer pays cash for the rest. In 2024, 57% of ITC transfer deals came out of such partnerships. [source: 9, 10]
- **Direct transfer:** the project owner sells credits for cash, usually with a seller-parent indemnity (PTC deals) or tax insurance (ITC deals). Bridge loans against expected transfer proceeds are common, but no volume data was verified. [source: 9]
- **Elective pay:** for tax-exempt owners (municipal utilities, cooperatives), credits arrive as refundable payments. [source: 5]
- **PFE diligence in documents:** buyers and tax equity investors now need ownership and debt holdings tested against the 25%/40%/15% foreign-influence tests, effective-control payments (licensing) reviewed, and MACR calculations against the year's threshold. They also need representations, indemnities and insurance covering the 10-year §48E effective-control recapture. [source: 1, 6, 10]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Transferability widened the pool of tax capacity beyond the traditional bank tax equity investors. Corporate buyers entered, and pricing in the low-to-mid 90s cents (Crux) shows modest discounts against the cost of tax equity. [source: 9]
- OBBBA turned the clean-energy pipeline into a race for a deadline. Notice 2025-42 removed the cheap Five Percent Safe Harbor for most wind and solar, forcing real physical work before 4 July 2026. Practitioners reported developers buying equipment and starting construction early to protect portfolios. [source: 2, 11]
- The PFE rules move compliance risk from the tax code into the supply chain. Banks have stressed that they "never want to invest into an indemnity claim" (Cargas), and were awaiting Treasury guidance in early 2026. [source: 10]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 32 (Equity):** Teach tax equity as a separate layer of the equity stack: why it exists (the tax-capacity mismatch), how a partnership flip allocates benefits, and why it ranks ahead of sponsor back-leverage. Then show how §6418 made hybrids and direct transfers possible. Use the Crux price points (ITC about 92.5 cents, PTC about 95.0 cents in 2024) to compute the effective cost of monetising credits. Flag all figures as US, 2024-2026 and policy-dependent.
- **Chapter 67 (Tax structuring):** Use OBBBA as the case study in how tax law drives structure: placed-in-service and beginning-of-construction cliffs; the Physical Work Test against the Five Percent Safe Harbor; PFE ownership, debt and effective-control tests as structuring constraints on holding companies and licensing; the 10-year recapture; and the ban on transfers to specified foreign entities. Tell the reader where the rules are (26 U.S.C. §§45Y, 48E, 6418, 7701(a)(51)-(52); IRS Notices 2025-42 and 2026-15; forthcoming proposed regulations).
- **Chapter 70 (Onshore wind and solar):** The US wind and solar credit regime now has an end date: in service by 31 December 2027, unless construction began by 4 July 2026, with continuity up to four calendar years. Model the revenue gap a project faces without the credit; practitioners cited PPA price increases of around 40% being needed for solar after the ITC ends (a single CFO's estimate). Storage keeps §48E eligibility, but faces a 55%+ MACR threshold.

## Do not state

- Precise partnership flip parameters (for example a 99%/1% pre-flip and 5%/95% post-flip split, target flip yields, or Rev. Proc. 2007-65 safe-harbour specifics). These are common market descriptions but were not verified for this sheet; verify before stating.
- The number of active tax equity investors (often cited as about two dozen). Not verified.
- The inflation-adjusted §45Y/§45 PTC rate in cents per kWh for 2025 or 2026. Not verified; cite the IRS annual inflation-adjustment notice.
- The exact release date of Notice 2026-15. Not verified; it was issued in 2026.
- That OBBBA repealed transferability or elective pay. It did not; it added a ban on transfers to specified foreign entities.
- That wind and solar credits ended on 4 July 2026. That was the beginning-of-construction deadline; facilities that began construction by then can still qualify if placed in service within the continuity rules.
- That the 1.5 MW exception applies to wind. It applies only to low-output solar.
- The precise MACRS class consequences of OBBBA §70509 for specific technologies. Verify against the statute and current IRS guidance.
- Transfer-market volume for 2025 or 1H2026 from Crux's restricted report. Not publicly verified. Use the Norton Rose Fulbright panel estimate (USD 45-50 billion of 2025 tax equity plus credit sales), attributed.
- That FEOC/PFE thresholds apply to projects that began construction in 2025. Material assistance applies to construction beginning after 31 December 2025.
