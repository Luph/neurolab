# Sabine Pass LNG: fee-based SPAs making liquefaction financeable

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: substantial completion of Train 6 on 4 February 2022; 2020 cargo-cancellation data from Cheniere's Q3 2020 10-Q)

## Summary

Sabine Pass in Cameron Parish, Louisiana, was built by Cheniere as an LNG import (regasification) terminal. Its capacity was largely reserved under 20-year terminal use agreements (TUAs) with Total and Chevron, each paying about USD 125 million a year whether or not it used the terminal. When US shale gas turned the country from importer to potential exporter, Cheniere Energy Partners (CQP) added liquefaction trains next to the existing tanks and berths through its subsidiary Sabine Pass Liquefaction, LLC (SPL). The commercial breakthrough was the sale and purchase agreement (SPA) design. Between October 2011 and January 2012 SPL signed four 20-year SPAs, with BG, Gas Natural Fenosa, GAIL and KOGAS. Each buyer pays a price of about 115% of Henry Hub plus a fixed fee per MMBtu (USD 2.25 to 3.00). A buyer may cancel or suspend cargoes but must still pay the fixed fee. The fixed fees, about USD 2.3 billion a year for Trains 1-4 from investment-grade buyers, work like a capacity payment in a tolling contract. They carried the project's debt: USD 3.6 billion of bank debt for Trains 1-2 closed on 31 July 2012, followed by USD 5.9 billion for Trains 1-4 in May 2013 and then large bond issues. The first cargo sailed on 24 February 2016, the first LNG export from the US lower 48 states. Six trains of about 30 mtpa in total were complete by February 2022. In 2020, when COVID-19 collapsed global prices, buyers cancelled many US cargoes but kept paying fixed fees. Cheniere recognized USD 932 million of revenue from cancelled cargoes in the first nine months of 2020, which was the structure working as designed.

## Verified facts

1. The Sabine Pass LNG terminal in Cameron Parish, Louisiana, had regasification capacity of about 4.0 Bcf/d and storage of about 16.9 Bcfe (end-2012). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Total Gas & Power North America reserved about 1.0 Bcf/d of regasification capacity. It pays capacity fees of about USD 125 million a year for 20 years from 1 April 2009, with Total S.A. guaranteeing up to USD 2.5 billion. Chevron U.S.A. reserved about 1.0 Bcf/d on the same terms from 1 July 2009, with Chevron Corporation guaranteeing up to 80% of the fees. The remaining about 2.0 Bcf/d was reserved by an affiliate (from July 2012, SPL) at about USD 250 million a year. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. SPL signed its first SPA, with BG Gulf Coast LNG, LLC, announced 26 October 2011, for 3.5 mtpa. It was later amended to add volumes. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 1]
4. The Gas Natural Fenosa SPA (3.5 mtpa) was reported in November 2011. The GAIL (India) SPA was announced 11 December 2011 and the KOGAS SPA 30 January 2012, each about 3.5 mtpa for 20 years. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4, 5]
5. SPA pricing: the buyer pays a fixed fee per MMBtu (part of which is indexed annually to inflation) plus a variable fee of about 115% of Henry Hub. Buyers lift free on board (FOB) at Sabine Pass. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
6. The contract terms as of February 2013 were as follows. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
    - BG: 182,500,000 MMBtu/yr at USD 2.25/MMBtu from Train 1, plus 36.5m, 34.0m and 33.5m MMBtu/yr at USD 3.00 from Trains 2, 3 and 4. Expected fixed-fee cash flow is USD 723 million/yr, guaranteed by BG Energy Holdings (A2/A).
    - Gas Natural Fenosa: 182,500,000 MMBtu/yr at USD 2.49 from Train 2, USD 454 million/yr, guaranteed by Gas Natural SDG (Baa2/BBB).
    - KOGAS: 182,500,000 MMBtu/yr at USD 3.00 from Train 3, USD 548 million/yr (A/A1).
    - GAIL: 182,500,000 MMBtu/yr at USD 3.00 from Train 4, USD 548 million/yr (Baa2/BBB-).
    - Total: 104,750,000 MMBtu/yr at USD 3.00 from Train 5, USD 314 million/yr, guaranteed by Total S.A. (Aa1/AA).
7. SPL's later 2014 filing describes a further SPA with Centrica: 91,250,000 MMBtu/yr at USD 3.00 from Train 5, about USD 274 million/yr. It gives aggregate fixed fees of about USD 2.3 billion a year for Trains 1-4 and USD 2.9 billion with Train 5. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
8. SPA customers "may elect to cancel or suspend deliveries of LNG cargoes", with advance notice, and must still pay the fixed fee on volumes not delivered. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 9]
9. Cheniere Marketing (a Cheniere affiliate) agreed to buy, at its option, excess LNG from Trains 1-4 of up to 104,000,000 MMBtu/yr. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
10. Bechtel Oil, Gas and Chemicals was lump-sum turnkey (LSTK) engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) contractor, using ConocoPhillips Optimized Cascade technology. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
    - The Trains 1-2 EPC contract was signed November 2011, priced at about USD 3.97 billion after change orders to end-2012. Notice to proceed was given in August 2012.
    - The Trains 3-4 EPC contract was signed December 2012 at USD 3.77 billion.
11. FERC authorized Trains 1-4 in April 2012. On 20 May 2011, DOE Order No. 2961 was its first long-term conditional authorization to export LNG to non-free-trade-agreement countries; it was finalized in August 2012 (Order 2961-A), for about 16 mtpa (803 Bcf/yr). [confidence: high] [source: 1, 7]
12. On 31 July 2012 SPL closed a USD 3.6 billion credit facility for Trains 1-2. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
    - Lenders were 11 joint lead arrangers plus 10 other banks, 21 in total, with Société Générale as sole financial advisor.
    - Pricing was LIBOR + 350 bp in construction and LIBOR + 375 bp in operation, with at least 75% of debt hedged.
    - Maturity was the earlier of 31 July 2019 or two years after completion.
    - CQP's chairman called it the largest non-recourse debt financing in the US in the past decade.
13. Total project costs for Trains 1-2 were given as about USD 5.6 billion, funded by about USD 2.0 billion of equity and USD 3.6 billion of debt. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 10]
    - Blackstone affiliates bought Class B units in CQP. CQP's 2012 letter says USD 1.5 billion was raised from Blackstone.
    - Cheniere Energy, Inc. bought 22.2 million Class B units for USD 333 million.
14. On 28-29 May 2013 SPL closed credit facilities of USD 5.9 billion for Trains 1-4 and took final investment decision (FID) on Trains 3-4. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
    - A Term Loan A of USD 4.4 billion came from 27 banks, at LIBOR + 300 bp in construction and LIBOR + 325 bp in operation, maturing 28 May 2020.
    - USD 1.5 billion of Korean facilities came from KEXIM and K-sure, at LIBOR + 300 bp direct and LIBOR + 230 bp covered.
15. SPL then turned to the bond market, issuing senior secured notes. By 30 June 2014 SPL had about USD 6.5 billion of debt outstanding. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
    - USD 1.0 billion of 6.25% notes due 2022 (November 2013).
    - USD 0.5 billion of 5.625% notes due 2023 and USD 2.0 billion of 5.75% notes due 2024 (May 2014).
16. The first cargo was loaded on the Asia Vision and sailed on 24 February 2016. It was the first LNG exported from the US lower 48 states, bound for Brazil. [confidence: high] [source: 12, 13]
17. Train 1 reached substantial completion on 27 May 2016 (announced 31 May 2016). Train 6 reached it on 4 February 2022, bringing capacity to about 30 mtpa. Cheniere and Bechtel said every train was completed ahead of its guaranteed completion date and within budget. [confidence: high] [source: 14, 15]
18. In the nine months to 30 September 2020, Cheniere's LNG revenues included USD 932 million from cargoes that customers said they would not take (USD 171 million in Q3 2020). This was recognized as revenue because the fixed fee was still payable. [confidence: high] [source: 9]

## Financing and structure details

- **Borrower:** Sabine Pass Liquefaction, LLC, wholly owned by Cheniere Energy Partners, L.P. (an MLP controlled by Cheniere Energy, Inc.). SPL's revenue base was its third-party SPAs. It reserved regasification-terminal capacity from its sister company Sabine Pass LNG under a TUA guaranteed by CQP. [source: 1]
- **Trains 1-2 (2012):** about USD 5.6 billion of cost, funded by about USD 3.6 billion of bank debt (about 64%) and about USD 2.0 billion of equity. [source: 8, 10]
- **Trains 1-4 (2013):** USD 5.9 billion of facilities (USD 4.4 billion commercial Term Loan A; USD 1.5 billion KEXIM/K-sure). The Korean ECA participation tracks the KOGAS offtake. [source: 11]
- **Refinancing path:** the bank loans were short-dated, maturing in about 7 years or tied to completion. They were progressively replaced by long-dated senior secured bonds once construction risk was underway. [source: 6]
- **Key credit features:** fixed-fee take-or-pay economics with cancellation rights; investment-grade buyers or parental guarantees; a fixed-price LSTK EPC with an experienced contractor and proven process; existing brownfield site infrastructure (tanks, berths); interest-rate hedging of at least 75%. [source: 1, 8]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Right: the price formula separated commodity risk from infrastructure risk.** The buyer takes Henry Hub gas price and destination market risk; the project earns a fixed fee. Cheniere's own disclosure shows lenders could size debt to fixed fees from rated counterparties. The 2020 experience showed buyers paying fixed fees on cancelled cargoes. Cheniere's 10-Q explained that the fixed fee "is applicable regardless of a cancellation or suspension" (Cheniere Q3 2020 10-Q). [source: 9]
- **Right: brownfield advantage.** The import terminal's storage, berths and site reduced the scope and cost of the liquefaction build. CQP's filings describe the trains as built "adjacent to the existing regasification facilities." [source: 1]
- **Right: delivery.** All six trains were completed ahead of guaranteed dates and within budget, according to Cheniere and Bechtel. [source: 15]
- **Fragility at the start.** The sponsor was a development-stage company with a weak balance sheet; the project rested on contracts, not sponsor credit. The bank tenor in 2012 was short (maturity no later than July 2019), implying refinancing risk. It was managed through bond take-outs in 2013-14. [source: 6, 8]
- **Market view (verify wording before quoting).** Oxford Institute for Energy Studies analysis of US LNG contracts describes the US model's fixed liquefaction fee as typically treated by buyers as a sunk cost. Cargo decisions then turn on whether destination prices cover variable costs (about 115% of Henry Hub plus shipping and regasification). [source: 16]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 21 (Revenue contracts beyond power).** Sabine Pass is the reference case for the LNG tolling-style SPA. Dissect the price into a fixed fee (a capacity payment) and a variable Henry Hub-linked component (a pass-through), and show that the cancellation right is economically a put option the buyer pays for. Contrast with oil-indexed Asian LNG SPAs and with a pure tolling agreement where the buyer supplies its own gas. A worked example can reproduce BG's USD 723 million fixed-fee figure from the contract quantities.
- **Ch 76 (LNG liquefaction, regasification and FPSOs).** Use Sabine Pass for how a merchant-minded developer made liquefaction bankable: reach a contracted threshold (about 16 mtpa, 89% of nominal Trains 1-4 capacity) before FID, use LSTK EPC with Bechtel, and stage trains so each financing tranche matches signed SPAs. Then trace the debt life cycle from bank construction loans to ECA (Korean) participation to project bonds. Close with 2020 as a stress test the structure passed.

## Do not state

- That the first SPA "was" 5.5 mtpa from signing. BG's original October 2011 SPA was 3.5 mtpa; additional volumes came by amendment.
- Precise reconciliation of the USD 2.0 billion equity for Trains 1-2. Sources give Blackstone at USD 1.5 billion (CQP 2012 letter) and Cheniere at USD 333 million; other press describes Blackstone's commitment as USD 2 billion. State "about USD 2.0 billion of equity, mostly from Blackstone" unless the definitive unit purchase agreement is checked.
- Secondary-press figures for 2020 cancellations, for example "USD 513 million" or "more than 100 cargoes". Use Cheniere's 10-Q figure (USD 932 million for nine months, Cheniere consolidated, including Corpus Christi) and do not attribute it to Sabine Pass alone.
- Any current credit rating, outstanding debt balance, or recontracting/expansion status (for example the Sabine Pass Stage 5 expansion) without a fresh check.
- That 115% of Henry Hub is "exactly" fuel cost. It is a contractual multiplier; the 15% approximates fuel and shrinkage and is not a cost pass-through.
