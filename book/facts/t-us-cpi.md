# US CPI-U index levels 2015–2026 and USD LIBOR / SOFR-family rate levels at key dates 2016–2026

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest CPI-U data: August 2026, released 11 September 2026; latest rate data: 2 October 2026)

## Summary

The US Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), US city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted (BLS series CUUR0000SA0, base 1982–84 = 100), is the index most often written into US tariff, toll and availability-payment escalation clauses. It rose from 233.707 in January 2015 to 334.980 in August 2026, an increase of about 43%. The 2021–2022 surge is the period to teach: the annual average rose 4.7% in 2021 and 8.0% in 2022, against 1.2–2.4% a year in 2016–2020. BLS did not publish an October 2025 index. Data collection lapsed during the 2025 federal funding lapse, and the BLS table shows the month as unavailable. Any contract formula that references "October 2025 CPI-U" therefore needs a fallback, which makes it a live example for drafting indexation clauses. BLS also publishes semiannual averages (series CUUS0000SA0, "HALF1/HALF2"), which suit semi-annual payment models. On rates, USD LIBOR ended (panel settings after 30 June 2023; synthetic 1-, 3- and 6-month settings after 30 September 2024; see facts/t-reference-rates.md). Historical 6-month USD LIBOR levels are no longer freely downloadable from official sources, because ICE withdrew the data from FRED. This sheet therefore gives verified period-end values drawn from SEC-filed fund reports where available and marks gaps. For SOFR-era dates, it uses the New York Fed's official SOFR and 180-day SOFR Average as public proxies for a 6-month term rate. CME Term SOFR is licensed data and is not reproduced here.

17. Backward-looking versus forward-looking. A 180-day SOFR Average on date D is compounded over the previous 180 days, so it lags. 6-month LIBOR and 6-month CME Term SOFR were forward-looking rates fixed at the start of the period. In a rising-rate period they differ sharply. On 30 Dec 2022 overnight SOFR was 4.30% but the 180-day average was only 2.89%. Use SOFR Averages only as a historical in-arrears proxy, never as a stand-in for a forward term fixing. [confidence: high, mechanics] [source: 8, cross-reference facts/t-reference-rates.md]

18. CME Term SOFR (1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month) is published by CME Group Benchmark Administration under licence. Historical values were not retrieved for this sheet. If a model needs 6-month Term SOFR at dates from 2021 onward, label the values approximate. For reference, US Treasury 6-month constant-maturity par yields (official, bond-equivalent basis, not a bank term rate) were: 30 Dec 2016 0.62%; 29 Dec 2017 1.53%; 31 Dec 2018 2.56%; 31 Dec 2019 1.60%; 31 Dec 2020 0.09%; 31 Dec 2021 0.19%; 30 Dec 2022 4.76%; 30 Jun 2023 5.47%; 29 Dec 2023 5.26%; 31 Dec 2024 4.24%; 31 Dec 2025 3.59%; 30 Jun 2026 4.01%; 2 Oct 2026 4.27%. [confidence: high for the Treasury data; medium for its use as a Term SOFR proxy] [source: 9]

19. What moves the numbers. CPI-U moves with energy prices (the main source of month-to-month volatility), shelter (the largest weight and slow-moving) and goods prices (the 2021–22 supply shocks). SOFR tracks the Federal Reserve's target range, with spikes at quarter-ends and year-ends driven by repo-market and reserve conditions (e.g., 17 Sep 2019). Term rates (LIBOR then, Term SOFR now) also embed expectations of future policy moves. LIBOR also carried a bank-credit premium, which is why the fallback spread adjustment exists. [confidence: high] [source: 8, cross-reference facts/t-reference-rates.md]

## Financing and structure details (parties, tranches, amounts, tenors, guarantees, where verified)

Not applicable (data sheet). For modelling:
- Indexation drafting: specify the series ID (CUUR0000SA0, NSA), the base month, the lag (e.g., the index for the month two months before the adjustment date), the rounding, and a fallback for an unpublished month. October 2025 is the real-world test case. Common fallbacks are the last published index, interpolation between adjacent months, or a successor index. Which fallback a contract uses is a drafting choice and has no statutory default.
- Semiannual payment models can use the BLS HALF1/HALF2 averages directly.
- For historical floating-rate interest in a USD case before mid-2023, use 6-month LIBOR where verified (item 12), and mark other dates approximate. After the transition, use Term SOFR plus the 0.42826% spread adjustment for legacy loans, or Term SOFR plus a new margin for new loans.

## What went wrong or right, and why (well-established analysis only, attributed to named sources)

- The 2021–22 inflation surge (CPI-U annual average +4.7% and +8.0%; June 2022 year-on-year +9.06% computed) tested every partial-indexation clause. Projects whose revenue indexation lagged or was capped while their costs were fully exposed lost margin (computed from source 1; for case-specific effects see facts/covid-transport.md and the PPP sheets).
- The missing October 2025 index is a reminder that even official indices can fail to publish. Fallback language matters (BLS, source 2).
- LIBOR data withdrawal: once LIBOR ceased, freely available official history disappeared from FRED, so historic model inputs now need licensed data or filed documents (this research).

## Teaching angles by chapter (for each listed chapter: the transferable lesson and the angle to take, 2–4 sentences)

- **Chapter 5 (Money and time):** Use the CPI-U table to show real versus nominal. Deflate a 2015 tariff of USD 100 to 2026 dollars: the August 2026 index divided by the January 2015 index gives 1.433. Then show partial indexation, for example 60% indexed, compounding against full-cost inflation through 2021–22. Use the October 2025 gap to teach why an indexation clause needs a fallback and a defined lag.
- **Chapter 6 (Debt and interest rates):** Plot the verified 3-month and 6-month LIBOR points against SOFR to show the LIBOR–SOFR credit and term gap and why the 0.42826% 6-month spread adjustment exists. Contrast the backward-looking 180-day SOFR Average with a forward-looking term fixing using 30 December 2022 (4.30% overnight against 2.89% for the average).
- **Models:** Put CPI and rate paths on a dated input sheet with the source, series ID and an "approximate" flag per cell. Never mix NSA and SA series, and never present a computed 2025 annual average as official.

## Do not state

- Do not state an "official 2025 annual average CPI-U" (BLS method and value not confirmed here; 321.943 is only the 11-month mean).
- Do not state an October 2025 CPI-U value. None exists.
- Do not give exact 6-month USD LIBOR fixings for 2016, 2017, end-2018, end-2021, 2022 or 30 June 2023 as verified. If needed, give approximate values clearly flagged, e.g., "about 1.3% at end-2016, about 1.8% at end-2017, about 2.9% at end-2018, about 0.3% at end-2021, about 5.1% at end-2022, about 5.8% at mid-2023". These are recollection, not verified in this research.
- Do not quote CME Term SOFR historical values as verified. They are licensed and were not retrieved.
- Do not use the 180-day SOFR Average as if it were 6-month Term SOFR.
- Do not cite FRED's LIBOR series as currently available.
