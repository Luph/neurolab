# NEOM Green Hydrogen financing (2023)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: Air Products' fiscal third-quarter 2026 results and finalized Yara marketing agreement, 30 July 2026; trade reports of construction completion and commissioning, August 2026)

## Summary

NEOM Green Hydrogen Company (NGHC) is owned in equal thirds by ACWA Power, Air Products and NEOM Company. It is building a green hydrogen and ammonia plant at Oxagon in the NEOM region of north-west Saudi Arabia, designed to turn up to about 4 GW of solar and wind power into up to 600 tonnes a day of hydrogen, delivered as up to about 1.2 million tonnes a year of green ammonia. The project was announced on 7 July 2020 at USD 5 billion. ACWA Power announced the signing of the financing agreements in early 2023, giving a total investment cost of USD 8.5 billion, USD 5,852 million of senior debt and USD 475 million of mezzanine debt. That debt included USD 1.5 billion from the National Development Fund on behalf of the National Infrastructure Fund and USD 1.25 billion in riyal-denominated financing from the Saudi Industrial Development Fund. NGHC announced financial close on 22 May 2023 at a total investment value of USD 8.4 billion, with approximately USD 6.1 billion of non-recourse financing from 23 banks and financial institutions. The facilities included a tranche covered by the German export credit agency (Euler Hermes) and conventional and Islamic bank tranches, and S&P Global certified the package against the Green Loan Principles. What made the deal bankable was the offtake: Air Products, a sponsor, holds an exclusive 30-year take-if-tendered agreement for all of the ammonia, and it is also the USD 6.7 billion EPC contractor and system integrator. Air Products therefore wears three hats (sponsor, offtaker and contractor). Because of this it consolidates NGHC as a variable interest entity, while the project debt stays non-recourse to it. Debt was drawn over construction, reaching USD 5.4 billion by 31 March 2026. Meanwhile Air Products retreated from its own downstream hydrogen plans. In July 2026 it finalized an agreement under which Yara markets the NEOM ammonia that Air Products does not use, on a commission basis. Trade reports in August 2026 said construction was complete and commissioning under way, with first ammonia expected in 2027 after an end-2026 target set at financial close.

## Verified facts

1. On 7 July 2020 Air Products, ACWA Power and NEOM signed an agreement for a USD 5 billion green hydrogen-based ammonia production facility at NEOM, to be owned equally by the three partners. As announced, it would integrate over 4 GW of solar, wind and storage, produce 650 tonnes a day of hydrogen by electrolysis using thyssenkrupp technology, make nitrogen using Air Products air separation, and produce 1.2 million tonnes a year of green ammonia using Haldor Topsoe technology. Onstream was planned for 2025, with Air Products as exclusive offtaker of the ammonia. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. On 13 December 2021 thyssenkrupp (thyssenkrupp Uhde Chlorine Engineers, now thyssenkrupp nucera) announced a contract from Air Products for engineering, procurement and fabrication of an electrolysis plant of over 2 GW for NEOM, based on its 20 MW alkaline water electrolysis module. Production was then scheduled to start in 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. ACWA Power announced to the Saudi Exchange (Tadawul) the signing of the NGHC project-financing agreements, giving a total investment cost of USD 8.5 billion funded by long-term debt and equity. The debt comprised USD 5,852 million of senior debt and USD 475 million of mezzanine debt facilities, both arranged on a non-recourse project-finance basis. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
4. Within that financing, USD 1,500 million came from the National Development Fund (NDF) on behalf of the National Infrastructure Fund (NIF), and USD 1,250 million was Saudi riyal-denominated financing from the Saudi Industrial Development Fund (SIDF). The balance came from a consortium that included First Abu Dhabi Bank, HSBC, Standard Chartered, MUFG, BNP Paribas and 15 other financial institutions. ACWA Power holds 33.3 percent of NGHC. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
5. Arab News reported the financing signing on 1 March 2023 at USD 8.5 billion. [confidence: medium; exact Tadawul filing date not independently confirmed] [source: 4]
6. On 22 May 2023 NGHC announced financial close at a total investment value of USD 8.4 billion. The plant was financed with USD 6.1 billion of non-recourse financing from 23 local, regional and international banks and investment firms. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]
7. At financial close NGHC concluded the EPC agreement with Air Products as nominated contractor and system integrator for the entire facility, at a value of USD 6.7 billion. Air Products said it had awarded all major subcontracts, completed land preparation, and that construction was well under way. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]
8. S&P Global, acting as second-party opinion provider, certified the non-recourse financing as aligned with the Green Loan Principles. NGHC described it as one of the largest project financings under the green loan framework. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
9. At financial close NGHC said the plant would integrate up to 4 GW of solar and wind and produce up to 600 tonnes a day of carbon-free hydrogen, in the form of green ammonia, by the end of 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
10. Allen & Overy advised the ECAs and lenders. It describes 23 financial institutions and tranches that include a Euler Hermes (German ECA) covered tranche, an SIDF tranche, an NIF tranche, conventional bank facilities and Islamic bank facilities. It also describes the offtake as the world's first project-financed large-scale green ammonia offtake contract. Allen & Overy gives the closing date as 26 May 2023. [confidence: high for tranche types; note the four-day date difference from NGHC's 22 May announcement] [source: 7]
11. Air Products describes its arrangement as "a long-term take-if-tendered agreement" under which it is the exclusive offtaker of the green ammonia. NGHC's financial-close release describes it as an exclusive 30-year offtake of all the ammonia produced. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 8]
12. Air Products consolidates NGHC as a variable interest entity in its Middle East and India segment, although it holds only one-third of the votes. Its reasons are that substantially all of NGHC's activities involve or are conducted on behalf of Air Products, and that Air Products can unilaterally direct key design and construction decisions. NGHC's assets can only settle NGHC's obligations, and NGHC's creditors have no recourse to Air Products' general credit. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
13. Air Products states that the approximately USD 6.1 billion project financing was expected to fund about 73 percent of the project and is drawn over the construction period. NGHC also secured additional credit facilities of approximately USD 500 million, mainly for working capital, which are likewise non-recourse to Air Products. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
14. NGHC's total principal borrowings were USD 4.9 billion at 30 September 2025 and USD 5.4 billion at 31 March 2026. Approximately USD 415 million drawn in the six months to 31 March 2026 came mainly under a 2.00 percent stated-rate Saudi riyal facility. Short-term borrowings of USD 46.5 million at 31 March 2026 were under a variable-rate Saudi riyal facility at 5.02 percent. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
15. In May 2023 NGHC entered floating-to-fixed interest-rate swaps to hedge its variable-rate construction-period debt. In fiscal 2024 Air Products stopped cash-flow hedge accounting for some of those swaps "due to changes in the anticipated drawdown timeline for the hedged borrowings". Fair-value gains and losses then went through the income statement until 1 January 2026, by which date all swaps had been re-designated as hedges once borrowings had become commensurate with swap notionals. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
16. NGHC balances as consolidated by Air Products at 31 March 2026: total assets USD 7,896.0 million, including plant and equipment, net, of USD 7,282.1 million; total liabilities USD 5,295.6 million, including long-term debt of USD 5,080.2 million; noncontrolling interests USD 1,640.2 million. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
17. Air Products reported that its spending on the NEOM project declined in fiscal 2026 "as the project nears completion". [confidence: high] [source: 8]
18. The Ammonia Energy Association reported at financial close that the cost had risen from the original USD 5 billion because of inflation, higher borrowing rates, expanded services and front-loaded land-lease costs. It also reported that Larsen & Toubro was contracted for the renewable-energy infrastructure and that Air Products planned ammonia import and cracking terminals (including at Rotterdam and Immingham). [confidence: medium; trade association report] [source: 9]
19. On 30 June 2026 Air Products announced that it would not proceed with its Louisiana Clean Energy Complex and would discontinue a liquid hydrogen facility at Casa Grande, Arizona, and other smaller clean-energy distribution projects. It expected pre-tax charges of no more than USD 2.9 billion (approximately USD 2.2 billion after tax). In the same release Air Products said it and Yara were finalizing a marketing and distribution agreement for NEOM ammonia. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
20. On 30 July 2026 Air Products reported that it had finalized the marketing and distribution agreement with Yara. Under it, Yara commercializes, on a commission basis, the NEOM ammonia (up to 1.2 million tonnes a year) that Air Products does not sell as renewable hydrogen in Europe, through Yara's existing global supply chain. The companies had announced advanced negotiations in December 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 11, 12]
21. Reporting on the Yara agreement in August 2026, a fertilizer trade publication said plant construction was about 90 percent complete and first ammonia was expected in 2027. Air Products anticipated a long commissioning process and no gain or loss from the project in 2027, and had not taken a final investment decision on any of its planned north-west European cracking facilities. Another trade outlet, citing Hydrogen Insight, reported in late August 2026 that construction had finished and commissioning had begun, with ACWA Power targeting commercial operation in 2027. [confidence: medium; trade press, partly conflicting on completion] [source: 13, 14]

## Financing and structure details

- Borrower: NEOM Green Hydrogen Company, owned one-third each by ACWA Power, Air Products and NEOM Company. [source: 5]
- Total investment: USD 8.5 billion (ACWA Power, at signing) and USD 8.4 billion (NGHC, at financial close). [source: 3, 5]
- Debt at signing: USD 5,852 million senior plus USD 475 million mezzanine, totalling USD 6,327 million, all non-recourse. At close NGHC described approximately USD 6.1 billion of non-recourse financing, and Air Products describes approximately USD 6.1 billion of project financing (about 73 percent of project cost) plus approximately USD 500 million of separate working-capital facilities. The figures likely differ because of how working-capital and other facilities are counted, which is not confirmed. [source: 3, 5, 8]
- Lender groups: NDF/NIF USD 1,500 million; SIDF USD 1,250 million (SAR-denominated); Euler Hermes covered tranche; conventional commercial bank tranches; Islamic tranches; 23 institutions in total. Named consortium banks include First Abu Dhabi Bank, HSBC, Standard Chartered, MUFG and BNP Paribas. [source: 3, 4, 7]
- Pricing and hedging: at least one Saudi riyal facility carries a 2.00 percent stated rate, which is far below market and points to concessional public-fund funding. The working-capital riyal facility floats (5.02 percent at 31 March 2026). Floating-rate commercial debt was swapped to fixed in May 2023. [source: 8]
- Revenue: an exclusive 30-year take-if-tendered ammonia offtake with Air Products. [source: 5, 8]
- Construction: a USD 6.7 billion EPC with Air Products as nominated contractor and system integrator, with thyssenkrupp electrolysers, Topsoe ammonia technology and Air Products air separation as announced in 2020. [source: 1, 2, 5]
- Green label: S&P Global second-party opinion against the Green Loan Principles. [source: 5]
- Advisers: Allen & Overy for the ECAs and lenders. [source: 7]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right, in financing terms: a creditworthy, investment-grade industrial sponsor took full volume risk in a long-term take-if-tendered offtake. Together with concessional Saudi state-fund debt (NDF/NIF and SIDF, together USD 2.75 billion) and ECA cover, this made a first-of-a-kind, merchant-commodity green ammonia plant financeable at about 73 percent debt (Air Products). The structure sends demand risk for green ammonia to the offtaker, not to the lenders. [source: 3, 7, 8]
- Cost growth before close: from USD 5 billion (2020) to USD 8.4–8.5 billion (2023). The Ammonia Energy Association attributes this to inflation, higher borrowing rates, expanded scope and front-loaded land-lease costs. [source: 1, 3, 5, 9]
- Schedule: onstream was 2025 in 2020 and end-2026 at financial close, and first ammonia was expected in 2027 by mid-2026. Air Products' own filings point to changes in the drawdown timeline as early as fiscal 2024. [source: 1, 5, 8, 13]
- Market risk moved to the offtaker: as green hydrogen demand failed to materialize, Air Products cut its downstream plans (the Louisiana exit, no FID on European cracking) and set up Yara to market surplus ammonia on commission. The lenders' structure was intact in the sources reviewed, but the offtaker's economics were under strain. [source: 10, 11, 13]
- Accounting consequence: the sponsor-offtaker-EPC role led Air Products to consolidate USD 5.3 billion of non-recourse NGHC liabilities on its balance sheet, an outcome a project finance sponsor should anticipate when it controls key decisions. [source: 8]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 83 (Hydrogen and derivatives, CCS and sustainable fuels): NEOM is the model case for how a first-of-a-kind green molecule project was made bankable. There was no market price to underwrite, so lenders relied on a 30-year take-if-tendered offtake from an investment-grade industrial sponsor, plus concessional state funding, ECA cover and Islamic tranches. Teach the "who holds the market risk" trace: lenders are protected only so long as Air Products performs, so the credit analysis turns on the offtaker, not the plant. Use Air Products' 2026 retreat (the Louisiana exit, Yara commission marketing) to show that risk transferred to an offtaker does not disappear. Also cover the concentration of roles (sponsor, offtaker, EPC contractor) and its consequences: interface risk in one party's hands, conflicts in contract enforcement, and consolidation of non-recourse debt under US GAAP. Construction-period interest hedging and drawdown slippage (swap de-designation) make a concrete modeling exercise.
