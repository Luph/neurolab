## Verified facts

No case-specific facts are verified. The framework facts below are carried from t-solvency, where they are sourced, to define what a qualifying deal must show. They are cross-references, not new research.

1. **Qualifying infrastructure investments.** Introduced by Regulation (EU) 2016/467, effective 2 April 2016. Qualifying project equity takes a 30% shock rather than 49%, and qualifying project debt takes reduced spread-risk factors. [confidence: high] [source: t-solvency facts 3–5]
2. **What the documents must evidence** (t-solvency fact 6):
   - cash flows that meet all financial obligations under sustained stresses;
   - predictable revenues, meaning all but an immaterial part are availability-based, rate-of-return regulated, take-or-pay, or of regulated, fixed or low-demand-risk price and volume;
   - for rated debt, credit quality step 0–3;
   - for unrated debt, seniority, EEA/OECD location and contractual investor protections.

   [confidence: high] [source: t-solvency]
3. **Insurer duties.** The insurer must perform and document due diligence, monitoring, stress testing, construction-phase procedures and asset-liability matching (Article 261a). [confidence: high] [source: t-solvency fact 7]
4. **UK route.** Since 30 June 2024 the UK matching adjustment admits "highly predictable" assets, capped at 10% of a firm's MA benefit. Since PS17/25 (23 October 2025) the Investment Accelerator lets firms hold self-assessed assets for up to 24 months before formal approval. [confidence: high] [source: t-solvency facts 19–20]

- Show the capital effect with t-solvency's verified factors: the 49% to 30% equity shock, and a 20-year CQS 3 bond's spread factor moving from 30% to 20%.
- Do not attach those mechanics to a named real deal unless a sourced case is added to this sheet.

## Do not state

- Any named bond, loan, issuer or investor as "Solvency II qualifying", "structured for Solvency II" or "matching adjustment eligible" unless a primary source (offering circular, rating-agency release, issuer or investor announcement) is cited. None was verified for this sheet.
- That a deal's investment-grade rating alone makes it qualifying. The criteria also cover revenue predictability, stress resilience, investor protections and insurer duties (t-solvency).
- That insurers' holdings of a given project bond were booked as qualifying infrastructure. Classification is the insurer's own assessment and is rarely disclosed deal by deal.
- Any market-wide volume of qualifying infrastructure holdings (also a "Do not state" item in t-solvency).

**Suggested completion route (for a later session with search):** look for a dated primary source in which an issuer, arranger, rating agency or insurer states that a project bond or loan was designed or assessed to meet the Article 164a criteria or MA eligibility. Candidates are rating-agency press releases on EEA availability-PPP or regulated-network project bonds issued after April 2016, and UK insurer announcements of MA-eligible infrastructure private placements after June 2024. Record issuer, date, size, rating and the stated reason, and verify each against a second source.
