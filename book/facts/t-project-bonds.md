# Project bond market: 144A/Reg S bonds, US private placements, EU Project Bond Credit Enhancement, typical terms

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Venture Global Calcasieu Pass USD 750 million 144A/Reg S notes, 23 April 2026; NextDecade 2025 Form 10-K filed 2 March 2026)

## Summary

Project bonds reach institutional investors through three main US-law channels and one EU policy channel. Rule 144A lets an issuer's initial purchasers resell unregistered notes to "qualified institutional buyers" (QIBs), broadly institutions that own and invest at least USD 100 million in unaffiliated issuers' securities. Regulation S covers the offshore tranche sold to non-US persons, with a 40-day distribution compliance period for most debt. The US private placement (USPP) is a privately negotiated note purchase agreement with a small group of insurers and other buy-and-hold investors. It can fund in instalments, which is how project issuers manage negative carry during construction. Two recent US LNG filings show the mechanics. In April 2026 Venture Global Calcasieu Pass issued USD 750 million of 6.000% senior secured 144A/Reg S notes due 2036 to prepay a bank term loan. They carry a make-whole call until six months before maturity and a par call after it. NextDecade's Rio Grande LNG Train 5 signed a note purchase agreement at FID in October 2025 for USD 500 million of 6.56% senior secured notes due 2050. The notes are issued at par in instalments through October 2026 and amortise over 20 years from September 2031, pari passu with the bank facility. In Europe, the EIB and European Commission's Project Bond Initiative (pilot phase to 2016) offered Project Bond Credit Enhancement (PBCE). This is a subordinated tranche, funded or unfunded (letter of credit), of up to 20% of the senior bonds or about EUR 200 million. It was designed to lift typical low-BBB project ratings to a level institutional investors would buy, backed by EUR 230 million of EU budget first-loss money. The EIB still offers project bond credit enhancement on the same 20% / about EUR 200 million sizing. This research could not retrieve a primary copy of the PBI's mid-term or ex-post evaluation, nor any reliable aggregate figures for project bond or USPP market volumes. Those are listed under "Do not state".

## Verified facts

### A. US offering exemptions (jurisdiction: United States; current rule text at eCFR, 17 CFR 230.144A and 230.901–905)

1. **Rule 144A QIB definition.** A qualified institutional buyer includes listed entity types (for example insurance companies and registered investment companies) "acting for its own account or the accounts of other qualified institutional buyers, that in the aggregate owns and invests on a discretionary basis at least $100 million in securities of issuers that are not affiliated with the entity." Status: current eCFR text as of 1 September 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Rule 144A fungibility condition.** Securities sold under Rule 144A must not, when issued, be of the same class as securities listed on a US national securities exchange or quoted in a US automated inter-dealer quotation system. This is why 144A project notes are a separate, unlisted class. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Regulation S.** An offer or sale is deemed outside the US if it is an "offshore transaction" with no "directed selling efforts" in the US, plus category-specific conditions. For Category 2 and Category 3 debt, sales before the end of a **40-day distribution compliance period** may not be made to, or for the account of, a US person (other than a distributor). Category 3 debt must also be represented on issue by a temporary global security. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. **Combined 144A/Reg S use in a project bond.** Venture Global Calcasieu Pass, LLC (VGCP) stated that its April 2026 notes "were offered in the United States and sold to persons reasonably believed to be qualified institutional buyers pursuant to Rule 144A … and outside the United States to non-U.S. persons pursuant to Regulation S." [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### B. 144A/Reg S project bond example: Venture Global Calcasieu Pass (Louisiana LNG), April 2026

5. On 23 April 2026, VGCP issued **USD 750 million of 6.000% senior secured notes due 1 May 2036**, with interest paid semi-annually from 1 November 2026. Proceeds, plus cash on hand and hedge termination proceeds, prepaid in full VGCP's existing term loan facility. The trustee is The Bank of New York Mellon Trust Company, N.A. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
6. **Security and ranking.** The notes are guaranteed by TransCameron Pipeline, LLC. They share collateral equally with VGCP's working capital facility and the existing senior secured notes issued on 5 August 2021, 22 November 2021 and 13 January 2023. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
7. **Covenants.** The indenture restricts restricted payments, additional debt, liens and asset sales. It also restricts amending "certain material project agreements or certain qualifying SPAs", hedging, and the accounts the issuer may maintain. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
8. **Call structure (make-whole plus par call).** Before 1 November 2035 (six months before maturity) VGCP may redeem at 100% plus a "make-whole" plus accrued interest. On or after that date it may redeem at par plus accrued interest. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### C. US private placement and delayed-draw notes: Rio Grande LNG (Texas), 2023–2026

9. **Phase 1 (FID 12 July 2023).** At 31 December 2025, Rio Grande LNG Phase 1 debt included these senior secured notes and loans [confidence: high] [source: 4]:
   - 6.67% notes due 2033 (USD 700 million);
   - 6.85% notes due 2047 (USD 190 million);
   - 6.58% notes due 2047 (USD 1,115 million);
   - 6.72% senior secured loans due 2033 (USD 356 million);
   - 7.11% senior secured loans due 2047 (USD 251 million).

   All rank pari passu with, and share collateral with, the bank construction facility. That facility is USD 8,448 million (CD Credit Agreement, SOFR + 2.25%, commitment fee 0.68%, maturing 12 July 2030), plus a USD 800 million TotalEnergies-supported facility. t-market-norms.md fact 19 already records the 2023 USD 700 million 6.67% private placement at par.
10. **Train 5 delayed-draw private placement (FID 16 October 2025).** On FID, Train 5 LLC entered a note purchase agreement for **USD 500 million of 6.56% senior secured notes due 2050** [confidence: high] [source: 4]:
    - USD 150 million was issued at par in December 2025, and the rest is to be issued at par in instalments through October 2026;
    - principal amortises over 20 years from September 2031, with final maturity in September 2050;
    - the notes rank pari passu with, and share collateral with, the Train 5 bank credit agreement (about USD 3.6 billion, SOFR + 2.00% with rating-based step-downs, maturing seven years after FID).
11. **Make-whole in sponsor-level loans.** At NextDecade's holding level, the Series A loans under the amended corporate credit agreement (8.0%, maturing 17 November 2030) carry a make-whole premium if prepaid before 17 November 2028. The Series B loans (13.5%, maturing 16 October 2030) carry a make-whole before 30 June 2028 and a declining prepayment penalty after. This shows the same call-protection tools reaching up the capital structure. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

### D. EU Project Bond Initiative and Project Bond Credit Enhancement (EU; current product terms at eib.org, "Credit enhancement for project finance")

12. **Rationale (EIB guide, 21 December 2012).** The EIB said:
    - Higher regulatory capital had constrained long-tenor bank lending.
    - Project bond issuance had become difficult after 2007/08 because the monoline insurers had become much less active.
    - Bond investors were hesitant to invest in the "low BBB range", partly because of their own regulation (for example Solvency II). That is the rating a traditionally structured project finance deal would typically obtain without further credit enhancement.

    [confidence: high] [source: 5]
13. **Pilot phase.** The pilot phase of the Project Bond Initiative was to run until 2016. It targeted trans-European transport and energy networks and broadband. It was funded by redeploying **EUR 230 million** of unused EU budget resources: EUR 200 million for transport, EUR 10 million for energy and EUR 20 million for broadband. The EIB estimated that this money, acting as a first-loss piece, could let it provide about **EUR 750 million of PBCE**, supporting projects worth more than **EUR 4 billion**. These were ex-ante estimates, not outcomes. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
14. **PBCE design.** PBCE is a subordinated instrument sized at the lower of **EUR 200 million or 20% of the nominal amount of the credit-enhanced senior bonds**. It comes in two forms [confidence: high] [source: 5]:
    - **Funded PBCE:** a subordinated loan from the outset, which acts as a first-loss piece during construction and operation.
    - **Unfunded PBCE:** a letter of credit drawable for construction cash shortfalls or senior debt service shortfalls. Once drawn, it becomes a mezzanine instrument similar to funded PBCE.

    It is designed to raise the rating of the senior bonds, "not to extend the EIB's AAA credit rating to the project".
15. **Current EIB product (2026).** The EIB offers "credit enhancement for project finance" to project SPVs, including PPPs and concessions. The forms are subordinated financing, funded or unfunded guarantees and contingent credit lines. Funded structures are subordinated bond or loan tranches sculpted to the senior debt profile. Unfunded structures are irrevocable, unconditional, revolving first-demand guarantees, in the form of a letter of credit or equivalent. Rating agency coverage is required for Project Bond Credit Enhancement. The maximum size is "around €200 million or 20% of the nominal of credit-enhanced senior bonds". [confidence: high] [source: 6]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **The monoline gap.** The EIB's own diagnosis is that the collapse of monoline wraps after 2007/08 removed the credit enhancement that had made European project bonds saleable. Unenhanced projects tend to rate low-BBB, which many insurers and pension funds would not buy. PBCE was designed to replace the wrap with a subordinated, partial (20%) tranche instead of a full guarantee. [source: 5]
- **Bank-to-bond takeout.** VGCP used a 144A/Reg S bond to prepay an operating-phase bank term loan. The pattern (banks through construction, bonds once risk has fallen) matches the Sabine Pass history in sabine-pass.md. [source: 3]
- **Negative carry managed by delayed issuance.** Rio Grande Train 5 ties note funding to instalments through October 2026, matching construction draws. The project then avoids paying a fixed coupon on cash it cannot yet spend. This is the USPP market's main structural advantage over a single-settlement 144A issue for greenfield construction. [source: 4]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 30 (Project bonds and ratings):**
  - Use VGCP 2036 as the worked 144A/Reg S term sheet: QIB-only US sale, Reg S offshore tranche, senior secured indenture with project-agreement covenants, and make-whole to six months before maturity then par.
  - Contrast it with Rio Grande Train 5's delayed-draw private placement, explaining negative carry and why instalment funding solves it.
  - Close with PBCE as a lesson in rating uplift: a 20% subordinated piece is sized to move a low-BBB senior bond up a notch or more, without wrapping it in AAA.
- **Ch 63 (Refinancing, repricing and secondary sales):**
  - VGCP shows a bond refinancing of an operating-phase bank loan, including hedge termination proceeds as a source of funds.
  - Teach the make-whole as the cost of early bond refinancing (versus a prepayable loan), and the par call window as the refinancing opportunity.
  - Use NextDecade's holdco loans (make-whole to 2028, then declining penalties) to show call protection at holdco level when Case P considers refinancing and partial sale.
