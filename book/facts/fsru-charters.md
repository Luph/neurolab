# FSRU charter structures and financing (Excelerate, Höegh)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest filing used: Excelerate Energy Form 10-K for 2025, filed 27 February 2026)

## Summary

A floating storage and regasification unit (FSRU) is usually provided to a host-country buyer (a state gas company, transmission operator or utility) under a long-term time charter, or under a lease and maintenance agreement. The buyer pays a fixed daily hire. Charters run from about 5 to 20 years and often include extension options, early termination rights and purchase options. The owner finances the vessel with asset-level loans, sale-and-leasebacks or corporate facilities, and the debt is sized on contracted hire. Excelerate Energy's filings show this model in operation. At end-2025 it controlled or operated 11 floating regasification terminals, held about USD 3.3 billion of minimum contracted terminal-service cash flows with a 5.8-year weighted average remaining term, and used sale-and-leasebacks (Experience in 2016; Exquisite with a Nakilat joint venture in 2018) and limited-recourse project loans (Moheshkhali, Bangladesh, 2017). The project loans required a debt service reserve and an "off-hire reserve" to cover drydock periods. The Höegh LNG Partners filings show charter design: a 20-year Lampung charter with extension options and a purchase option, and a 20-year Höegh Grace charter in Colombia with mutual break options at years 10 and 15. They also show the dispute risk. In August 2021 PGN LNG served notice of arbitration seeking to annul or terminate the Lampung agreement.

## Verified facts

Excelerate Energy (US-listed)
1. At 31 December 2025 Excelerate controlled or operated 11 floating regasification terminals, with one more under construction at HD Hyundai Heavy Industries (170,000 m³, delivery expected in Q2 2026). It owned nine. It chartered Experience under a finance lease from a third party, Exquisite under a finance lease from the Nakilat joint venture (Excelerate 45%), and Hoegh Gallant under an operating lease. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Terminal services are provided "for a predetermined period of time at a specified rate per day, which is typically fixed". Rates are "typically based on the economic return requirements" for the investment, and the business is "substantially supported by long-term, take-or-pay agreements". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. At 31 December 2025 minimum contracted terminal-services cash flows were about USD 3,315.2 million, with a 5.8-year weighted average remaining term. Contracts covered Argentina, Brazil, Bangladesh, Finland, Germany, Iraq, Pakistan and the UAE. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. Excelerate was lessor under time charters on eight terminals accounted for as operating leases. Minimum future fixed revenues were USD 2,246.3 million (operating) and USD 667.6 million (sales-type, undiscounted). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Experience financing: in December 2016 a sale-leaseback provided USD 247.5 million. Because Excelerate must repurchase the vessel at the end, it is accounted for as a failed sale-leaseback, that is, a financing. It is now priced at three-month SOFR + 3.4%, with quarterly principal of USD 3.1 million, maturing December 2033. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. Exquisite financing: in June 2018 a sale-leaseback with the Nakilat JV provided USD 220.0 million through a 15-year lease at 7.73%. It has symmetrical put and call options at the end of the term or after optional five-year extensions, and is treated as a financing. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. Moheshkhali LNG (MLNG), Bangladesh: in June 2017 Excelerate signed two bank loans of up to USD 32.8 million (now six-month SOFR + 2.85%) and up to USD 92.8 million (now three-month SOFR + 4.76%), both maturing October 2029. They are "associated with limited recourse project financing facilities". They require a six-month debt service reserve, a monthly-funded off-hire reserve for drydock periods, stand-alone insurance (property, liability, business interruption), and a quarterly DSCR of at least 1.10x. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. The Experience sale-leaseback also requires the borrower partnership (EELP) to maintain minimum equity of USD 500 million, a maximum debt-to-equity ratio of 3.5:1 and minimum liquidity of USD 20 million. It requires a three-month debt service reserve and a terminal value of at least 110% of the amount outstanding (collateral maintenance), plus marine insurance. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. Excelerate exercised a purchase option on FSRU Sequoia in March 2023 for USD 265.0 million. The vessel had previously been on bareboat charter to Excelerate. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
10. Excelerate's risk factors say that if a regasification contract terminates, it "may be unable to re-deploy" the terminal at attractive rates. It states that operating its fleet as an integrated set reduces redeployment risk. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

Höegh LNG Partners (filings to 2022)
11. PGN FSRU Lampung (Indonesia): a 20-year time charter with PGN LNG from acceptance on 30 October 2014. PGN LNG has options to extend by 5 or 10 years, then a further 5. PGN LNG also has an option to buy at specified prices from June 2018. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
12. On 2 August 2021 PGN LNG served a notice of arbitration seeking to declare the Lampung lease and maintenance agreement null and void, and/or to terminate it, and/or to seek damages. Höegh warned that early termination could cause an impairment of the financing lease receivable. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
13. Höegh Grace (Colombia), chartered to SPEC (Sociedad Portuaria El Cayao): the initial term is 20 years, the non-cancellable period 10 years, and each party may cancel after 10 and 15 years without penalty. SPEC holds purchase options in years 10, 15 and 20. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
14. The Lampung facility required a minimum DSCR of 1.10x over the prior nine months, tested quarterly, plus partnership-level equity, working capital and liquidity covenants. The Lampung facility, the USD 385 million facility and an USD 85 million sponsor revolver were cross-defaulted. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
15. Höegh's charters allow the charterer to terminate for extended off-hire, specified owner defaults, and in some cases without cause, for force majeure or war. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

## Financing and structure details

- **Revenue.** Fixed day-rate hire (capacity payment), sometimes with LNG supply bundled on top (Excelerate's LNG, gas and power segment). [source: 1]
- **Debt forms.** Asset-level sale-leasebacks with repurchase obligations, which are accounted for as financings. Limited-recourse project loans with DSRA, off-hire reserve and a 1.10x DSCR. Loan-to-value collateral tests. [source: 1, 2]
- **Key contract levers.** Tenor, break options, purchase options, off-hire termination rights. [source: 2]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Risk realised.** Charterer disputes and early termination (Lampung) can undercut a 20-year contract. Lenders therefore look beyond tenor to termination payments, purchase-option pricing and redeployability. [source: 2]
- **Mitigant.** FSRUs are mobile. Excelerate presents fleet commonality as reducing redeployment risk, but also discloses that redeployment rates are uncertain. [source: 1]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 76.** Contrast FSRU finance with onshore regasification. The asset can be redeployed, so lenders take residual value and recharter risk instead of pure host-country completion risk. Use the Moheshkhali covenants (6-month DSRA, off-hire reserve, 1.10x DSCR) as a template term sheet, the Höegh Grace break options to show how a 20-year charter can be a 10-year credit, and Lampung as a dispute case.
