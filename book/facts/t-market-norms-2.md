# Indicative project finance terms outside the US, 2022–2026: Gulf IPPs and IWPs, European renewables and offshore wind, PPPs, emerging-market power, Latin America, Africa, mining, and rating-agency evidence (supplement to t-market-norms)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: IFC Summary of Investment Information for the Jordan IPP 7 CCGT, disclosed 17 September 2026; latest rating action covered: Morningstar DBRS downgrade of Plenary Health Care Partnerships Humber LP, 2 October 2026)

## Summary

This sheet fills the gaps listed under "Do not state" in t-market-norms.md. It relies only on documents fetched and read in this research: company filings with the US SEC (Atlantica, Eldorado Gold, Teck, Aya Gold & Silver, Osisko Development, Sigma Lithium, KEPCO, Fitch's own Form NRSRO), ACWA Power's 2025 integrated annual report, Northland Power's Q2 2026 report, JBIC press releases, IFC project disclosures, Morningstar DBRS rating press releases, law-firm deal notes, the UK National Audit Office and a peer-reviewed review of South Africa's REIPPPP. The evidence is uneven. It is strong for Canadian availability PPP coverage ratios (DBRS publishes projected minimum DSCRs of about 1.20–1.44x for A-range and BBB(high) deals), for European and Latin American renewables loan margins and tenors from Atlantica's filing (Spanish solar refinanced in 2022–2023 at EURIBOR + 1.50% stepping to 1.70–1.80%, maturing 2035–2037; Uruguayan wind at SOFR + 2.25–3.0% over 16 years; lock-up DSCRs of 1.10–1.25x), for DFI-led emerging-market gearing (IFC disclosures showing about 62–80% debt in Uzbek, Egyptian, Ethiopian and Mozambican power projects with 25-year PPAs) and for mining (bank and EU-backed debt for Skouries repaid by 2032; EBRD at SOFR + 5%; private credit at SOFR + 7.5–9.5%). It is weak for Gulf pricing (sponsors disclose debt amounts and maturity years but almost never margins or DSCRs), for Latin American toll-road pricing, and for Australian mini-perms. It is absent for the S&P, Moody's and Fitch DSCR-to-rating tables: their criteria documents could not be retrieved, so no threshold from them is stated here. Throughout, single-deal figures are evidence of what one deal did, not market averages.

## Verified facts

### A. Rating-agency evidence (what could and could not be verified)

1. Fitch Ratings' Form NRSRO Exhibit 2 (filed with the SEC on 30 March 2026) states that in infrastructure and project finance the quantitative metrics Fitch considers are the DSCR; leverage (net debt to CFADS or net debt to EBITDA); the project life coverage ratio (NPV of CFADS over the remaining project life divided by senior and pari passu debt outstanding); the loan life coverage ratio; and, in some sectors, interest cover, maximum annual debt service and net debt to regulated asset base. It also says Fitch assesses completion risk, operation and revenue risk, infrastructure renewal risk and debt structure (refinance risk, covenants, hedging, liquidity and reserves). The exhibit gives no numerical DSCR-to-rating thresholds. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
2. Morningstar DBRS, upgrading Mountain View Partners GP (a Canadian availability-based PPP) to "A" on 5 May 2026, described the project's projected minimum DSCR of 1.20x as standard for availability-based PPP projects rated in the "A" range. The project's actual DSCR for 2025 was 1.21x. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. Projected or model minimum DSCRs that DBRS cites for rated Canadian availability PPPs in 2026 releases (rating in brackets): Plenary Justice Okanagan LP, minimum and average 1.21x [A (low)], 27 January 2026; Plenary Health Hamilton LP, minimum 1.22x [A], 16 January 2026; Capital City Link GP, minimum 1.27x [A (low)], 18 September 2026; InPower BC GP (John Hart hydro replacement, 19.75-year project agreement with BC Hydro), minimum 1.26x [BBB (high)], 14 August 2026; Accès Recherche Montréal LP (33.3-year research-centre PPP), minimum 1.37x in operations [A], 22 September 2026; Women's College Partnership, minimum 1.44x [A], 1 May 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 1–7]
4. DBRS published an updated "Global Methodology for Rating Public-Private Partnerships" on 11 August 2026, superseding the 10 March 2026 version, with no rating changes expected. The methodology document itself sits behind a login and was not read. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
5. The DBRS releases show that operating availability PPPs typically run close to their model DSCR (for example, Plenary Justice Okanagan 1.25x actual against 1.21x projected; Capital City Link 1.31–1.32x actual against 1.27x projected minimum). They also show that ratings move on non-ratio events: DBRS downgraded Plenary Health Care Partnerships Humber LP to BBB from BBB (high) on 2 October 2026 because the hospital's lawsuits claim up to CAD 2.5 billion and CAD 1.5 billion against a design-build contract value of CAD 974.8 million. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 5, 10]

Shuaibah II water project debt was refinanced in 2021 by USD 166.2 million of 3.7% notes amortising semi-annually to 27 May 2044. Rabigh 3 IWP refinanced SAR 1,766.25 million of floating-rate senior debt in 2025 with SAR 1,237.5 million of amortising bonds (SAR 862.5 million fixed rate and SAR 375 million floating rate) plus a SAR 375 million floating-rate commercial facility; final maturities run from September 2045 to December 2046. Noor Ouarzazate I–III (Morocco) debt matures in 2038–2040. Margins and DSCR covenants are not disclosed. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

They include Hassyan IWP (UAE, March 2024), Taiba 1 and Qassim 1 CCGTs (July 2024), the PIF 4 solar projects (September 2024), Rumah 1 and Nairyah 1 CCGTs (August 2025), the Hajr expansion CCGT (September 2025) and the Saudi NREP round projects (November 2025). [confidence: high] [source: 16]

[confidence: high] [source: 14]

### D. PPP availability deals (Europe, Canada; Australia not verified)

17. UK PFI and PF2 (National Audit Office, 18 January 2018): PFI deals were financed about 90% senior debt and 10% equity, with contract lengths of 25–30 years. PF2 was launched at 75:25 but in practice reverted to 90:10. The projected post-tax return to debt and equity investors on the six PF2 deals then signed was 4.5–5%, about double the government's borrowing cost when they were agreed. Refinancing gain-share was at least 50% to the public sector under PFI. PF2 was abolished for new deals in October 2018 (see Ch 57), so these are historical UK norms. [confidence: high] [source: 42]
18. Canadian availability P3 coverage evidence is in item 3. Concession lengths in those releases run from about 19.75 years (John Hart) to 33–34 years (hospital and research-centre projects). [confidence: high] [source: 1–7]
19. Australian PPP and mini-perm terms: no primary or reliable source could be fetched in this research (Infrastructure Australia and federal infrastructure department pages timed out or were blocked, and state treasury and audit-office search pages are script-rendered). See "Do not state". [confidence: n/a]

20. Syrdarya CCGT (Uzbekistan; ACWA Power; BOOT with a 25-year PPA with NEGU; gas supplied under a tolling mechanism). Lenders were IFC (A loan up to USD 70 million plus swaps), JBIC and ECA-covered banks. It was signed on 16 March 2023. IFC describes the plant as 1,600 MW; ACWA lists 1,500 MW. [confidence: high] [source: 17, 11]

Monterrey (Mexico, 142 MW gas engines) had a USD 155.7 million loan maturing September 2027 at SOFR + 2.75%, stepping up 0.25% in September 2023 and again in September 2026, 85% hedged, also with a 1.20x distribution test. [confidence: high] [source: 30]
22. Cap des Biches (Senegal, 86 MW HFO IPP; ContourGlobal). IFC disclosed on 6 January 2026 a proposed debt package of approximately EUR 105 million to convert the plant to gas and refinance existing debt. The IFC A loan is up to EUR 40 million, indicated at a 10-year tenor (the figures are bracketed as indicative in the disclosure). [confidence: medium, indicative figures] [source: 19]

### H. Mining project finance

36. Skouries (Greece, Eldorado Gold). The project financing was signed on 5 April 2023:
   - a EUR 480.4 million commercial loan at 6-month EURIBOR plus a fixed margin (margin not disclosed), 70% hedged;
   - EUR 200 million from the Greek Recovery and Resilience Fund at fixed rates of 3.04% (EUR 100 million) and 4.06% (EUR 100 million);
   - a EUR 60 million contingent cost-overrun facility;
   - a EUR 30 million VAT revolver.
   The term facility is non-recourse to the parent and secured on Skouries and the operating Hellas Gold assets. Eldorado's equity commitment is backstopped by a letter of credit (EUR 206.8 million at 31 December 2025). Lenders required gold and copper swaps, gold collars, interest-rate swaps and FX forwards. After a deferral option was exercised in January 2025, repayment runs in 13 semi-annual instalments from 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2032. Covenants include debt service and leverage ratios (levels not disclosed). [confidence: high] [source: 31]
37. Quebrada Blanca Phase 2 (Chile, Teck). The USD 2.5 billion limited-recourse facility bears Term SOFR plus margins that vary over time (not disclosed). It is repaid in 17 equal semi-annual instalments of USD 147 million from 15 June 2023, so the final instalment falls in June 2031 (computed). Teck and SMM/SC guaranteed it severally until final completion. Breach of the completion longstop (June 2025) could have triggered acceleration. Completion was achieved in March 2025 and the guarantees were released. USD 1.6 billion was outstanding at 31 December 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 32, 33]
38. Aya Gold & Silver (Morocco): the EBRD project finance facility for the Zgounder expansion (USD 100 million, comprising a USD 92 million EBRD tranche plus a USD 8 million Climate Investment Funds tranche) and a further EBRD facility for Boumadine accrue interest at SOFR + 5% (9.15% at 31 December 2025). The CIF tranche fell to 1.00% all-in from 1 July 2024 after ESG and operational milestones were met. Scheduled repayments run to about 2030. [confidence: high] [source: 34]
39. Cariboo Gold (Canada, Osisko Development). On 21 July 2025 the company signed a USD 450 million senior secured project loan with funds advised by Appian (private credit). It matures after 8 years (21 July 2033). The initial draw prices at 3-month SOFR + 0.10% + 9.50% with a 2.00% SOFR floor and a PIK option; after further draws the margin steps down to 7.50%, payable in cash. The lender also received warrants and upfront and standby fees, and holds a limited-recourse parent guarantee with share and asset security. [confidence: high] [source: 35]
40. Sigma Lithium (Brazil) received a binding BNDES commitment on 27 August 2024 for a BRL 487 million, 16-year development loan at 7.45% per year in reais, with an 18-month grace period and 174-month amortisation, secured by a bank letter of credit rather than project assets. This is development-bank corporate-style lending, not limited-recourse project finance. [confidence: high] [source: 36]

### I. Drivers that move the numbers (as evidenced above)

41. Offtake length sets tenor. Gulf IWP and solar debt amortises to within a few years of 25-year contracts (Rabigh 3 to 2045–2046; Sakaka to 2044). Mining debt, without a long offtake contract, amortises over about 6–8 years after completion (Skouries 2026–2032; QB2 2023–2031). [source: 11, 31, 32]
42. Revenue quality sets coverage. Availability PPPs rated "A" sit at about 1.20–1.27x projected minimum DSCR. Regulated or contracted renewables lock up at 1.10–1.25x. A bond on Spanish solar thermal exposed to regulatory change carries a 1.65x test (Solaben 1 & 6). [source: 1–7, 30]
43. Lender type sets price. EBRD mining debt at SOFR + 5% contrasts with private credit at SOFR + 7.5–9.5% plus warrants (Cariboo), EU recovery funds at 3.04–4.06% fixed (Skouries), and Spanish bank refinancings at EURIBOR + 1.50–1.80%. [source: 30, 31, 34, 35]
44. DFIs and ECAs anchor frontier and first-of-kind deals, often with blended concessional tranches and IFC swaps covering the whole debt (UZ Solar 3, CEN, Aysha). Japanese and Korean ECAs anchor Gulf IPPs (Facility E, Saudi wind). [source: 13–15, 22–24]
45. Completion support is the mining norm. Several sponsor guarantees run until a completion test (QB2), LC-backstopped equity and cost-overrun facilities are used (Skouries), and mandatory commodity hedging is common. [source: 31, 32]
46. Margin step-ups over the life of the loan (Melowind, Monterrey, Solacor, Solnova) push sponsors to refinance. Lenders use them to price tail risk. [source: 30]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Litigation can move a PPP rating faster than ratios do: DBRS downgraded the Humber River hospital PPP although its operating cover was not the trigger. DBRS cited the size of the claims relative to the design-build contract value and its 100% liability cap. [source: 10]
- Completion tests matter in mining: Teck's filings show the QB2 lenders could have accelerated on a missed June 2025 final-completion longstop. Teck reported completion in March 2025, which released the sponsor guarantees. [source: 32, 33]
- Schedule slippage is priced into mining structures: Eldorado used a deferral option to push the start of Skouries repayments to December 2026, and the package carried a dedicated EUR 60 million overrun facility. [source: 31]
- Refinancing to extend tenor and cut margin is routine in operating renewables: Atlantica extended Spanish solar maturities by roughly 5–7 years in 2022–2023. ACWA Power refinanced Sakaka (2024) and Rabigh 3 (2025, into amortising bonds). [source: 11, 30]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 29 (Lenders): Contrast lender types on price for the same sector: EBRD mining debt at SOFR + 5%, Appian private credit at SOFR + 7.5–9.5% with PIK and warrants, and EU recovery-fund money at 3–4% fixed in Skouries. Use JBIC and KEXIM in Facility E and Saudi wind to show ECA anchoring of Gulf IPPs.
- Ch 30 (Bonds and ratings): Fitch's own SEC filing lists the metrics agencies use (DSCR, LLCR, PLCR, leverage). DBRS's 2026 releases show what "A" looks like for availability PPPs (about 1.20–1.27x projected minimum). State plainly that the S&P, Moody's and Fitch threshold tables are in criteria documents readers must consult directly.
- Ch 34 (Blended and concessional): CEN, UZ Solar 3 and Aysha show concessional tranches, IFC full-notional swaps and sovereign implementation agreements making 20-year debt possible in frontier markets.
- Ch 35 (Cover ratios): Distribution lock-ups of 1.10–1.25x on contracted assets versus 1.65x on a bond exposed to Spanish regulatory change show coverage pricing revenue risk.
- Ch 36 (Sizing and sculpting): Compare tail lengths. Gulf IWP debt runs to within a few years of 25-year contracts, while mining debt amortises over about 6–8 years after completion. Use QB2's 17 equal instalments as an example of non-sculpted mining amortisation.
- Ch 37 (Reserves, sweeps, covenants, hedging): Skouries is a full mining package: overrun facility, LC-backstopped equity, commodity swaps and collars, rate swaps and FX forwards. Atlantica's 75–99% hedge ratios illustrate hedge profiles.
- Ch 38 (Pricing): Margin step-ups (Melowind 2.25% to 3.0%; Solnova 1.50% to 1.80%; Monterrey +0.25% at years 3 and 6) are worked examples of ratchets that reward refinancing.
- Ch 57–58 (PPPs): NAO's 90:10 PFI structure and PF2's reversion from 75:25 to 90:10 show the gearing-versus-value-for-money tension. Canadian P3 DSCRs give current benchmarks.
- Ch 63 (Refinancing): ACWA's Rabigh 3 refinancing from bank debt into amortising bonds, and Atlantica's Spanish maturity extensions.
- Ch 69 (Thermal): Syrdarya (about 80% debt, 25-year tolling PPA), Jordan IPP 7, Facility E and ACT (SOFR + 3.75%, 1.20x) give 2013–2026 contracted thermal anchors. Cap des Biches shows a fuel-conversion refinancing.
- Ch 70 (Onshore wind and solar): REIPPPP's 70–80% gearing, Egyptian and Ethiopian wind at about 75–80%, and Uruguayan wind margins form a ladder against the US anchors in t-market-norms.md.
- Ch 71 (Offshore wind): Baltic Power's 25-year CfD and Hai Long's 30-year CPPA against UK 15-year CfD projects (Dogger Bank, Inch Cape, East Anglia THREE). Ask how contract length should change tenor and gearing.
- Ch 78 (Mining): Skouries, QB2, Aya and Cariboo together show completion support, short amortisation, commodity hedging and lender-type pricing. Sigma's BNDES loan shows a non-project-finance alternative.
- Ch 79 (Toll roads): Colombian 4G/5G concessions financed with COP-linked DFI loans and Brazilian upfront concession fees funded by bridge loans. Pricing is not public, which is itself a lesson.
- Ch 81 (Social infrastructure): Use the DBRS hospital, justice and research-centre releases for DSCR, lifecycle and litigation risk.

## Do not state

- Any S&P, Moody's or Fitch DSCR-to-rating threshold table or "indicative DSCR for BBB" figure. S&P's criteria site returned "Security Controls Triggered", Moody's returned 403 and Fitch's research pages are script-only. Fitch's SEC-filed methodology summary contains no thresholds. Cite the criteria by name and tell readers to consult the current version.
- That 1.20x is a rating-agency rule for all PPPs: it is DBRS's description of what is standard for availability-based PPPs in the "A" range, in one 2026 release.
- Australian mini-perm tenors (for example "5–7 years"), refinancing margins or Australian PPP gearing: no source could be retrieved.
- Gulf/Saudi loan margins, DSCR sizing levels, DSRA sizes or "typical" tenors such as "25–27 years". Only maturity years for specific ACWA facilities and contract lengths are verified.
- JBIC "co-financing amounts" as total project debt: the releases give JBIC's portion and its co-financing group, which may not be the whole debt package.
- Shadwan Wind gearing: IFC's "up to" amounts (USD 660 million against a cost of up to USD 700 million) are ceilings, not a capital structure.
- Margins on Skouries' commercial loan, QB2, the Colombian toll-road loans or the Saudi IPPs: they are described only as "fixed margin" or "varying margins", or not at all.
- Gearing, tenor or pricing for Inch Cape, East Anglia THREE or the Baltic Power facility beyond what t-market-norms.md already states.
- Any single "market" margin for Latin American renewables in 2023–2026: the Atlantica figures are legacy loans (2013–2020) reported in 2024.
- Syrdarya capacity as a single number (IFC 1,600 MW; ACWA 1,500 MW).
- Cap des Biches tenor and size as final: IFC bracketed them as indicative.
