# Fleet-wide OEM equipment problems and owners' remedies: GE HA-class blades (2018), Siemens Gamesa onshore platforms (2023), GE Vernova Haliade-X (2024) and related cases

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: YPF Luz F-1/A prospectus, 23 July 2026, on its ICC arbitration against GE Vernova affiliates; CMS Energy 10-K, February 2026, on the Ludington jury verdict of December 2025)

Cross-references: delay-LD doctrine is in facts/triple-point.md and facts/t-contract-law.md. Construction-contract norms, including the Siemens Energy guarantee facility, are in facts/t-construction-norms.md. LTSA and O&M terms are in facts/t-om-ltsa-norms.md. The UK offshore wind projects that use Haliade-X are covered in facts/dogger-bank.md.

## Summary

Serial defects are the equipment risk that a single project's contracts handle worst. The problem shows up across a whole fleet, the OEM's own balance sheet absorbs the cost, and each owner's recovery depends on its warranty, LTSA and EPC terms. Four disclosed episodes illustrate this.

- **GE HA turbines (2018).** In the third quarter of 2018 GE recorded a USD 0.2 billion pre-tax charge for "an oxidation issue" in the Stage 1 turbine blades of its HA and 9FB gas turbines, raising warranty and maintenance reserves. In the fourth quarter it took a further USD 0.4 billion for long-term service agreement (LTSA) assumption updates and USD 0.4 billion for project execution issues, including liquidated damages.
- **Siemens Gamesa onshore platforms (2023).** On 22 June 2023 Siemens Energy withdrew its profit guidance. It said failure rates of certain wind turbine components had risen substantially and that onshore quality measures would likely cost more than EUR 1 billion. Its third-quarter results then booked EUR 2.2 billion of charges at Siemens Gamesa, including EUR 1.6 billion for quality issues on certain onshore platforms. Siemens Gamesa ended fiscal 2023 with a loss before special items of EUR 4.347 billion. Sales of the 4.X and 5.X onshore platforms were suspended. Successor models came back to market in 2024 and 2025.
- **GE Vernova Haliade-X (2024).** A blade failed at Vineyard Wind on 13 July 2024, which GE Vernova attributed to a manufacturing deviation. The US regulator suspended power production. GE Vernova recorded incremental contract losses of approximately USD 0.9 billion in the second half of 2024 across Vineyard Wind and Dogger Bank, including the cost of removing previously installed blades.
- **Owners' remedies.** Owners' recoveries run through contract and dispute resolution:
  - Consumers Energy and DTE Electric won a USD 383 million jury verdict (plus USD 11 million of stipulated delay LDs) against Toshiba America Energy Systems over a defective overhaul of the Ludington plant.
  - YPF Luz is pursuing ICC arbitration against GE Vernova affiliates over an LMS100 plant that missed its LTSA availability guarantee after a forced outage running from May 2023 to August 2024.

## Verified facts

### A. GE HA-class and 9FB blade oxidation (2018)

1. In its Form 10-Q for the third quarter of 2018 (filed 30 October 2018), GE disclosed that "during the third quarter of 2018, Gas Power Systems recorded a $0.2 billion pre-tax charge related to an oxidation issue within the HA and 9FB Stage 1 turbine blades, resulting in increased warranty and maintenance reserves." In the same quarter, Power recognised pre-tax charges of approximately USD 0.4 billion for issues on existing projects driven by execution and by partner and customer challenges. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. GE's 2018 Form 10-K (filed 26 February 2019) repeats the USD 0.2 billion Q3 2018 blade-oxidation charge. It adds that in Q4 2018 GE recorded USD 0.8 billion of pre-tax charges in Power:
   - USD 0.4 billion "related to various assumption updates for unfavorable pricing, lower utilization, and cost updates on our long-term service agreements";
   - USD 0.4 billion "related to execution issues resulting in liquidated damages and partner execution issues on our long-term equipment projects at Gas Power Systems". [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. The same 10-K reports that Power segment revenues fell USD 7.6 billion (22%) and segment profit fell USD 2.8 billion in 2018. GE attributed this to industry overcapacity, lower utilisation, price concessions and other factors. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

### B. Siemens Gamesa onshore quality problems (2023)

4. Siemens Energy ad hoc announcement under Article 17 MAR, 22 June 2023:
   - "Following the substantial increase in failure rates of wind turbine components, the board of Siemens Gamesa initiated an extended technical review of Siemens Gamesa's installed fleet and product designs."
   - Reaching targeted quality on "certain Onshore platforms" would cost "significantly higher costs ... than previously assumed".
   - The costs were "likely to be in excess of 1 bn Euro".
   - Siemens Energy withdrew its fiscal 2023 profit guidance. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
5. In its Q3 FY2023 earnings release (7 August 2023), Siemens Energy reported that Siemens Gamesa's profit before special items was negative EUR 2,550 million for the quarter. It said this was "burdened by charges totaling €2.2bn which included €1.6bn related to quality issues of certain onshore platforms"; the other charges mainly related to increased product costs and offshore ramp-up challenges. Group profit before special items for the quarter was negative EUR 2,048 million. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. Siemens Energy's Q4 FY2023 release (15 November 2023) gave the following for fiscal 2023:
   - Siemens Gamesa revenue EUR 9,092 million;
   - Siemens Gamesa profit before special items negative EUR 4,347 million;
   - Group net loss EUR 4.588 billion;
   - Siemens Gamesa break-even now expected in fiscal 2026.
   The release says the technical analysis of the quality issues "for the onshore platforms 4.X and 5.X is almost complete" and that Siemens Gamesa "has still suspended the commercial activities on the 5.X platform". [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. Siemens Energy's Annual Report 2025 (fiscal year to 30 September 2025) says:
   - Sales of the successor to the 4.X platform (SG 5.0-145 (2.0)) resumed in September 2024. The successor to the 5.X platform (SG 7.0-170) was brought to market in fiscal 2025.
   - The first order for the SG 5.0-145 (2.0), 40 MW in Spain, came in fiscal 2025.
   - The quality task force's responsibilities moved into the regular organisation on 1 October 2025.
   - Siemens Gamesa's profit "remained clearly negative ... due to the quality issues in the onshore area".
   - Updates to warranty provisions, including "the regular annual update of the statistical models used to evaluate the entire wind turbine fleet", again had negative effects. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
8. The same report lists fleet-wide quality issues as a significant risk in Siemens Gamesa. It notes that "existing warranty or service contract provisions may not reflect updated failure rates" and that "Legal is reviewing critical serial defect clauses in new bids". [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### C. GE Vernova Haliade-X blade events (2024)

9. GE Vernova's 2024 Form 10-K (filed 6 February 2025) gives the following account:
   - On 13 July 2024 "a wind turbine blade event occurred, related to a manufacturing deviation, at the Vineyard Wind offshore wind farm", where GE Vernova supplies Haliade-X 220m turbines.
   - On 15 July 2024 the US Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE) ordered a halt to power production and to installation of new turbines.
   - BSEE allowed tower and nacelle installation to resume on 10 August 2024 and blade installation on 22 October 2024, both subject to conditions.
   - BSEE terminated the suspension order on 17 January 2025. Previously installed blades had to be removed. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
10. The same filing says there had been "blade events in prior quarters related to commissioning and installation at the Dogger Bank offshore wind farm". It reports "incremental contract losses of approximately $0.9 billion in the third and fourth quarters [of 2024] for both projects", covering:
    - changes in execution timelines;
    - project-related commercial liabilities;
    - costs to remediate quality issues, including removal of previously installed blades at Vineyard Wind;
    - supply chain and manufacturing costs. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
11. Avangrid, whose renewables arm holds 50% of Vineyard Wind 1, reported in its Q2 2024 10-Q that the blade "was under GE Vernova's control" at the time of the incident and that GE Vernova was undertaking a root cause analysis. To that date it reported no material financial impact. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

### D. Owners' remedies in disclosed disputes

12. Ludington (Michigan):
    - Consumers Energy and DTE Electric, co-owners of Ludington, signed a 2010 EPC agreement with Toshiba International for a major overhaul and upgrade. The contract was later assigned to Toshiba America Energy Systems (TAES).
    - The owners describe TAES's work as "incomplete, defective, and non-conforming". They made a formal demand under a parent guaranty from Toshiba, terminated the contract and sued TAES and Toshiba in the US District Court for the Eastern District of Michigan in 2022.
    - In December 2025 a jury found TAES in breach and awarded USD 383 million in damages. The parties separately stipulated USD 11 million of additional liquidated damages for late performance. The jury rejected TAES's counterclaim of approximately USD 15 million.
    - The parent guaranty lets the owners recover legal costs. Post-verdict proceedings and any appeal "could take two years or more". [confidence: high] [source: 9]
13. Loma Campana I (Argentina):
    - YPF Luz's 105 MW plant uses a GE Vernova LMS100 aeroderivative turbine. It was built under a turnkey EPC contract with a GE Vernova affiliate and began commercial operations in November 2017.
    - Its LTSA with GE Vernova guarantees a minimum plant availability. The LTSA runs to the later of December 2037, 100,000 operating hours, or completion of the agreement overhaul.
    - The plant had "availability factors significantly below the availability guarantee due to the successive outages", including a forced outage from May 2023 to August 2024, and resumed partial operations in September 2024.
    - YPF Luz filed an ICC arbitration request against certain GE Vernova affiliates on 29 August 2024 and a statement of claim on 10 November 2025 for reduced availability and lost energy sales.
    - The outage gave YPF (the offtaker) an option to terminate the plant's PPA. As of the prospectus date YPF had not exercised it. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

## Financing and structure details

- **Where the cost lands.**
  - GE booked the HA problem as higher warranty and maintenance reserves, so the cost sat in the OEM's warranty/LTSA accounts rather than in any project. [1]
  - Siemens Gamesa booked onshore quality costs as provisions and kept updating fleet-wide statistical warranty models. [5, 6]
  - GE Vernova booked Haliade-X costs as contract losses on the affected supply contracts. [7]
- **Contract chain at Loma Campana I.** The OEM was EPC contractor, equipment supplier and LTSA provider at once. The availability guarantee in the LTSA is the hook for the owner's claim, and the PPA offtaker gained a termination option from the same outage. Those terms create a back-to-back mismatch the owner has to manage. [10]
- **Contract chain at Ludington.** A parent guaranty from Toshiba backed the contractor's obligations and gave access to legal costs. Delay LDs (USD 11 million) were small relative to general damages (USD 383 million). [9]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Scale-up and new platforms.** Siemens Energy's own risk disclosure links fleet issues to "the introduction of new onshore and offshore platforms, alongside rapid factory ramp-up". GE Vernova's 10-K says fleet-quality risk "is pronounced in connection with the introduction of new technology". [6, 7]
- **Warranty reserves lag failure data.** Siemens Energy warns that existing provisions "may not reflect updated failure rates" and updates statistical fleet models every year. For owners, the OEM's provision is not a measure of their own exposure. [6]
- **Owners get paid through contract terms, slowly.** Recovery came through availability guarantees (Loma Campana I), parent guarantees and litigation (Ludington), and OEM-funded remediation (Vineyard Wind, where GE Vernova bore the blade-removal costs). Ludington took from a 2022 filing to a December 2025 verdict, with appeals possibly two more years. [7, 9, 10]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 23 (Construction structures beyond the single EPC):** Use Siemens Gamesa and Haliade-X to show that equipment-supplier credit and technology maturity are distinct risks from EPC wrap risk. A wrapped EPC may pass through only the OEM's capped warranty. Teach serial-defect clauses (the fleet-wide trigger, extended warranty, and root-cause and retrofit obligations), citing Siemens Energy's statement that its lawyers were reviewing them in new bids. Ludington shows the value of a parent guaranty from the equipment contractor's group.
- **Ch 24 (Operations and maintenance contracts):** Loma Campana I is the clean case. An LTSA availability guarantee was missed, the offtaker gained a PPA termination right, and the owner had to arbitrate. GE's 2018 LTSA assumption charge shows the provider side: LTSAs are long-dated fixed-price bets on utilisation and failure rates. Ask students to map which party bears a fleet defect under a typical LTSA, and what the liability cap does to that answer.

## Do not state

- The number of HA units affected in 2018, which plants were shut down, or which owners were affected (for example, specific Texas plants often named in press coverage). GE's filings give only the charge, and owner-side filings were not found.
- The specific failed components behind the Siemens Gamesa onshore problem (blades, bearings, etc.). The primary sources say "certain wind turbine components".
- Any total final cost of the Siemens Gamesa quality programme beyond the figures above, or any compensation paid to individual wind-farm owners.
- The amounts claimed in the YPF Luz arbitration, the LTSA's liability cap or the guaranteed availability percentage. None were disclosed.
- The cause of Vineyard Wind's blade failure beyond "manufacturing deviation", any allocation of Vineyard Wind's own losses, or insurance recoveries.
- That the Ludington verdict is final or has been collected. The verdict may be appealed.
