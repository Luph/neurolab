# Transfer pricing for project structures: OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (2022) and Chapter X on financial transactions (shareholder loans, guarantees)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest document covered: OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, January 2022 edition, incorporating Chapter X adopted 20 January 2020)

## Summary

Project sponsors fund project companies with shareholder loans, provide completion and other guarantees, and charge development, management and technical-service fees. Tax administrations test all of these against the arm's length principle in Article 9 of the OECD Model, applied through the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (TPG). The current consolidated edition is dated January 2022.

The BEPS Actions 8–10 and 13 reports of 2015 were folded into the Guidelines in 2016. In 2020 the OECD added **Chapter X**, its first detailed guidance on financial transactions, based on a report adopted by the Committee on Fiscal Affairs on 20 January 2020.

Chapter X does three things that matter for projects:

- **Delineation before pricing.** It first asks whether a purported loan is really a loan, or partly equity, and how much an independent lender would have lent. Indicators include a fixed repayment date, an obligation to pay interest, creditor ranking, covenants and security, the source of interest payments and debt capacity.
- **Loan pricing.** It prices accurately delineated loans mainly by the comparable uncontrolled price (CUP) method, using the borrower's credit rating with "implicit support" from group membership taken into account. Cost of funds, credit default swaps and economic models are recognised alternatives. Bank "bankability" letters are generally not evidence of arm's length terms.
- **Guarantees.** It treats a guarantee fee as payable only for benefit beyond implicit support. It describes five pricing approaches (CUP, yield, cost, valuation of expected loss and capital support). The yield approach sets the borrower's maximum fee and the cost approach the guarantor's minimum. A guarantee that lets a borrower borrow more than it could alone may be recharacterised in part as a loan to the guarantor plus an equity contribution.

Country-by-country reporting (BEPS Action 13) applies to groups with annual consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Interest-deductibility limits (BEPS Action 4) sit alongside transfer pricing and are covered in t-interest-limitation.

## Verified facts

### The Guidelines and their history

1. **Current edition.** The OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and Tax Administrations 2022 were published by OECD Publishing in January 2022 (DOI 10.1787/0e655865-en). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Earlier revisions.** The Foreword records these milestones:
   - 2016: substantial revision to reflect the 2015 BEPS reports on Actions 8–10 ("Aligning Transfer Pricing Outcomes with Value Creation") and Action 13 ("Transfer Pricing Documentation and Country-by-Country Reporting");
   - 2018: revision of the profit split guidance and a new annex on hard-to-value intangibles;
   - 2020: a new Chapter X on financial transactions.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Chapter X adoption.** The report on the transfer pricing aspects of financial transactions was adopted by the Committee on Fiscal Affairs on 20 January 2020 and incorporated in Section D.1.2.2 of Chapter I and in Chapter X. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **Chapter X scope (paras 10.1–10.2).**
   - Section B: accurate delineation.
   - Section C: treasury activities, meaning intra-group loans, cash pooling and hedging.
   - Section D: financial guarantees.
   - Section E: captive insurance.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### Debt or equity: accurate delineation

5. **Article 9 covers recharacterisation.** Chapter X cites the Commentary on Article 9 of the OECD Model. Article 9 is relevant not only to whether an interest rate is arm's length but also to whether a prima facie loan should be regarded as some other kind of payment, in particular a contribution to equity capital (para 10.5). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. **Domestic approaches remain valid.** The guidance does not stop jurisdictions from using other domestic-law approaches to the balance of debt and equity and to interest deductibility, such as multi-factor tests or thin-capitalisation rules. Nor does it mandate accurate delineation as the only approach (paras 10.8–10.10). Labels do not constrain the analysis (para 10.11). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. **Indicators (para 10.12).** In delineating an advance of funds, useful indicators include:
   - presence or absence of a fixed repayment date;
   - obligation to pay interest;
   - right to enforce payment of principal and interest;
   - the funder's status compared with regular corporate creditors;
   - financial covenants and security;
   - the source of interest payments;
   - the recipient's ability to borrow from unrelated lenders;
   - the extent to which the advance is used to acquire capital assets;
   - failure to repay on the due date or requests for postponement.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. **Debt capacity example (para 10.13).** Where good-faith projections show a borrower could not service a 10-year related-party loan of the amount advanced, the amount accurately delineated as a loan is the maximum an unrelated lender would have advanced and an unrelated borrower would have borrowed, possibly nil. The remainder is not treated as a loan when pricing interest. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. **Lender lacking control earns a risk-free return (para 10.25).** If the funding entity does not exercise control over the risks of the advance, or lacks the financial capacity to bear them, those risks are allocated to the entity that does. The funder is then entitled to no more than a risk-free return (cross-referring to Chapter I, Section D.1.2.1). [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### Pricing intra-group loans

10. **Both perspectives (paras 10.51–10.55).** Both the lender's and the borrower's perspectives must be considered. An associated lender may not follow an independent lender's processes, but the same commercial considerations (creditworthiness, credit risk, economic circumstances) apply. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. **Credit ratings (paras 10.62–10.63).** Creditworthiness is among the main pricing factors, and credit ratings, for the issuer or a specific issuance, help identify comparables or apply economic models. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
12. **Implicit support (paras 10.76–10.80).** The incidental benefit an entity gets from group membership ("passive association") is called implicit support. Its effect on the borrower's credit rating or borrowing cost requires no payment or comparability adjustment. How likely support is depends on several factors:
    - the entity's strategic importance and integration;
    - shared name;
    - legal or regulatory obligations;
    - reputational consequences;
    - history of support.

    Where there is evidence no support would be given, a stand-alone rating may be appropriate. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
13. **Group rating (para 10.82).** The MNE group's credit rating may be used to price a loan where the facts so indicate, particularly where the borrower is important to the group and its indicators of creditworthiness do not differ significantly from the group's. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
14. **Covenants (paras 10.83–10.86).** Intra-group loans often lack covenants. Where none are written, it may be appropriate to consider whether there is, in practice, the equivalent of a maintenance covenant and its effect on price. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
15. **CUP method (paras 10.89–10.95).** Once the loan is delineated, arm's length rates can be sought by reference to the borrower's or issuance's rating and the loan's terms.
    - Features that raise the rate: long maturity, absence of security, subordination, or application of the loan to a risky project.
    - Features that lower it: strong collateral, a high-quality guarantee, or restrictions on the borrower.
    - In practice there is usually a range of rates, not one market rate.
    - A group's average external borrowing rate is unlikely to qualify as an internal CUP.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
16. **Other approaches.**

    | Approach | Paras | How it works | Caveat in the Guidelines |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Cost of funds | 10.97–10.100 | Lender's borrowing cost plus arranging and servicing costs, a risk premium and a profit margin | Must be benchmarked against other lenders' costs and the borrower's realistic alternatives |
    | Credit default swap spreads | 10.101–10.103 | May proxy the risk premium | Volatility and liquidity effects |
    | Economic models | 10.104–10.106 | A risk-free rate plus premiums for default, liquidity, inflation or maturity | — |

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
17. **Bank opinions (paras 10.107–10.108).** Written "bankability" opinions from banks stating what rate they would charge depart from a comparability-based approach and are not actual offers to lend. They "would not therefore generally be regarded as providing evidence of arm's length terms and conditions". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
18. **Fees (para 10.96).** Arrangement or commitment fees on related-party loans are evaluated like any other intra-group transaction, bearing in mind that independent lenders' fees partly reflect regulatory and capital-raising costs that associated enterprises may not incur. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### Financial guarantees

19. **Definition (para 10.155).** For Chapter X, a guarantee is a legally binding commitment to assume a specified obligation of the guaranteed debtor if it defaults. "Letters of comfort" and lesser support involve no explicit assumption of risk (para 10.163). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
20. **Benefit test (paras 10.156–10.160, 10.164).** A guarantee fee is warranted only for an economic benefit beyond implicit support: better terms, or access to more debt. A formal guarantee may confer no benefit where group members are already financially interdependent, for example through cross-default clauses in other group facilities. In that case no guarantee fee is due. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
21. **Guarantees that increase borrowing capacity (para 10.161).** Where a guarantee lets the borrower borrow more than it otherwise could, part of the loan may be delineated as a loan from the lender to the guarantor followed by an equity contribution from the guarantor to the borrower. Only the remainder carries a guarantee fee. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
22. **Cross-guarantees (para 10.165).** These may be impractical to evaluate. Facts may show no credit enhancement beyond passive association, in which case support given on default is regarded as a capital contribution. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
23. **Guarantor capacity and correlation (paras 10.166–10.168).** The analysis must consider the guarantor's capacity and the correlation between guarantor and borrower. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
24. **Pricing approaches (paras 10.169–10.182).**

    | Approach | What it measures | Role in the Guidelines |
    |---|---|---|
    | CUP | Comparable independent guarantees | Uncontrolled guarantees are the most reliable comparables but are uncommon for bank loans |
    | Yield | Interest saved: rate without the guarantee but with implicit support, minus rate with it | The **maximum** fee the borrower would pay |
    | Cost | Expected loss or capital the guarantor must hold, for example via option or CDS models | The **minimum** fee the guarantor would accept |
    | Valuation of expected loss | Probability of default adjusted for recovery, applied to the guaranteed amount and priced on a return on capital such as CAPM | — |
    | Capital support | Notional capital needed to lift the borrower to the guarantor's rating, priced at a return on that capital | — |

    The arm's length fee should reflect both parties' perspectives and realistic options. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### Documentation (BEPS Action 13)

25. **Three tiers.** Chapter V sets a three-tiered documentation structure: a master file, a local file and a country-by-country report. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
26. **CbCR threshold (paras 5.52–5.53).** The country-by-country report is required of MNE groups except those with annual consolidated group revenue in the preceding fiscal year below EUR 750 million (or a near equivalent in domestic currency as of January 2015). The OECD expected this to exclude about 85–90% of MNE groups while capturing groups controlling about 90% of corporate revenues. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

## Financing and structure details

Not a transaction. How Chapter X applies to typical project features:

- **Shareholder loans in the funding plan.** Sponsors often provide the "equity" tranche partly as subordinated shareholder loans. Under paras 10.12–10.13 a tax authority may look at several features of such loans:
  - deep subordination to senior lenders;
  - interest payable only from distributable cash after the senior debt service cover ratio (DSCR) lock-up tests;
  - no enforcement rights;
  - an amount beyond what an independent lender would provide.

  Any of these may lead it to delineate part of the loan as equity, so the interest becomes a non-deductible distribution. Thin-capitalisation and interest-limitation rules (t-interest-limitation) operate alongside.
- **Pricing the subordinated rate.** The arm's length rate for a shareholder loan should reflect its subordination and the project's risk profile. A useful reference point is the senior project-finance margin plus a premium for subordination, supported by CUPs or economic models (paras 10.89–10.106). A bank comfort letter is weak evidence (para 10.108).
- **Sponsor guarantees.** Where a sponsor parent guarantees construction-period debt (a completion guarantee), the project company's benefit is the reduced margin or increased debt quantum beyond implicit support. The yield and cost approaches bracket the fee (fact 24). If the guarantee lets the project borrow more than its stand-alone capacity, part of the debt may be treated as borrowed by the sponsor and on-lent as equity (para 10.161).
- **Development and management fees.** These fall under the services guidance (Chapter VII) rather than Chapter X. They must reflect services actually rendered and a benefit to the project company.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Why Chapter X was needed.** Before 2020 the Guidelines had no comprehensive financial-transactions guidance, though Article 9 and the 1986 thin-capitalisation report already allowed recharacterisation of loans as equity (paras 10.5, footnote 2). The 2020 guidance codified accurate delineation, implicit support and guarantee pricing, but deliberately left room for domestic approaches (paras 10.8–10.10). That flexibility means outcomes still differ by country. [source: 1]
- **Why implicit support matters.** Paras 10.76–10.80 and 10.175–10.177 make the benchmark the borrower's rating with group support, not its stand-alone rating. That lowers the arm's length rate on an intra-group loan, and lowers any guarantee fee, compared with a stand-alone analysis. [source: 1]
- **Information asymmetry.** The Guidelines acknowledge that tax administrations may not have the information a group uses to decide whether to support a subsidiary (para 10.80). That is one reason group ratings may be used (para 10.82). [source: 1]

## Teaching angles by chapter

**Chapter 67 (Tax structuring).**

- Use Case P's 80% shareholder-loan funding to walk through Chapter X in order:
  1. Is it a loan? Apply the para 10.12 indicators and debt capacity.
  2. What rate? Apply CUP with implicit support, and explain why a bank opinion letter is not enough.
  3. What happens if part is equity? Interest is disallowed and may be treated as a dividend, bringing dividend WHT (see t-tax-treaties-wht).
- Then price a sponsor completion guarantee using the yield (maximum) and cost (minimum) bounds.
- Tie this to thin-capitalisation and interest-limitation tests, which are separate legal hurdles.
- Teach the drivers that move an arm's length shareholder-loan rate: base rate, project risk and rating, subordination, tenor, currency and security. Do not present any rate as standard.

Their loan amounts, rates and holdings were not retrieved from the judgments for this sheet.
