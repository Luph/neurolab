# Accounting for projects and sponsors, part 2: IFRS 9 expected credit losses, IBOR reform amendments, IAS 28, IFRS 10 loss of control, lessor classification (IFRS 16 / ASC 842), ASC 810 VIEs, IFRIC 12 practice for IPPs, IPSAS 32

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest documents covered: EU consolidated IFRS text, Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; ReNew Energy Global plc Form 20-F filed 30 July 2026; Ellomay Capital Form 20-F filed 1 May 2026)

## Summary

This sheet extends t-accounting (IFRIC 12, IFRS 16, IFRS 9 overview, IFRS 10/11, ASC 853/842/815, IFRS 18) with the detailed rules a project sponsor's accountant needs. All IFRS paragraph references are to the IFRS text as adopted in the EU by Regulation (EU) 2023/1803.

- **IFRS 9 impairment.**
  - The loss allowance is 12-month expected credit losses (ECL) unless credit risk has increased significantly since initial recognition, in which case it is lifetime ECL. Credit-impaired assets earn interest on the net amount. Practitioners call these Stages 1, 2 and 3.
  - There is a rebuttable presumption of significant increase at more than 30 days past due, and of default at 90 days.
  - For trade receivables and contract assets without a significant financing component, the **simplified approach** always uses lifetime ECL, often via a provision matrix.
  - Credit enhancements that are part of a receivable's contractual terms, such as a state guarantee of an offtaker's payments, reduce the measured ECL.
- **IBOR reform.**
  - Phase 1 (September 2019, effective 2020) kept hedge relationships alive during uncertainty before replacement.
  - Phase 2 (August 2020, effective 2021) let an entity reset the effective interest rate rather than book a modification gain or loss when LIBOR was replaced by an economically equivalent rate. It also let hedge documentation be amended without discontinuing the hedge. This is how LIBOR-to-SOFR transitions of project swaps and loans were accounted for.
- **IAS 28 and IFRS 10.**
  - IAS 28 presumes significant influence at 20% or more of voting power and requires the equity method.
  - When a sponsor sells down and loses control, IFRS 10 requires it to derecognise the subsidiary, remeasure the retained stake to fair value, recognise the gain or loss in profit or loss, and recycle OCI (for example the cash flow hedge reserve) as if it had disposed of the underlying items directly.
- **Lessor classification.**
  - IFRS 16 uses risks-and-rewards indicators.
  - ASC 842 uses five criteria plus a "reasonable approach" of 75% of remaining economic life and 90% of fair value.
  - IFRS 16 excludes IFRIC 12 arrangements from its scope.
- **ASC 810.** The VIE model consolidates a legal entity in the hands of the party with power over the activities that most significantly affect economic performance and with significant economics.
- **IFRIC 12 for IPPs.** Real filings show IFRIC 12 applied to IPPs and transmission assets selling to state entities, and why the choice matters for covenants and exit gains.
- **IPSAS 32** (grantor side) is covered in t-ppp-frameworks and only cross-referenced here.

## Verified facts

### IFRS 9: expected credit losses

1. **Scope of impairment (para 5.5.1).** A loss allowance is recognised on:
   - financial assets at amortised cost or at fair value through OCI;
   - lease receivables;
   - contract assets;
   - loan commitments and financial guarantee contracts within scope.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **General approach (paras 5.5.3, 5.5.5, 5.5.7).**
   - If credit risk has increased significantly since initial recognition, the allowance equals lifetime ECL.
   - If not, it equals 12-month ECL.
   - An asset returns to 12-month ECL if the significant-increase condition is no longer met.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Interest on credit-impaired assets (para 5.4.1).** Interest revenue is normally the effective interest rate applied to the gross carrying amount. For assets that have become credit-impaired after origination, it is applied to amortised cost, which is net of the allowance. Purchased or originated credit-impaired assets use a credit-adjusted effective interest rate. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **Assessing significant increase (paras 5.5.9–5.5.11).**
   - The test compares the risk of default over the expected life at the reporting date with that at initial recognition.
   - A "low credit risk" instrument may be assumed not to have increased significantly.
   - There is a rebuttable presumption of significant increase when contractual payments are more than 30 days past due.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. **Default (para B5.5.37).** The definition of default must be consistent with internal credit risk management. There is a rebuttable presumption that default occurs no later than 90 days past due. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. **Simplified approach (paras 5.5.15–5.5.16).** The allowance is always lifetime ECL for:
   - trade receivables and contract assets within IFRS 15 that have no significant financing component (or where the practical expedient applies);
   - by accounting-policy choice, those that do have one;
   - by policy choice, lease receivables.

   Policies may be chosen separately for trade receivables, lease receivables and contract assets. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. **Provision matrix (para B5.5.35).** A provision matrix based on historical loss experience, adjusted for current and forward-looking information, is an acceptable practical expedient for trade receivables. The standard's illustration uses rates of 1% not past due, 2% under 30 days, 3% for 30–90 days and 20% for 90–180 days. These are illustrative only. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. **Measurement (paras 5.5.17–5.5.18).** ECL must reflect three things:
   - an unbiased, probability-weighted amount;
   - the time value of money;
   - reasonable and supportable information about past events, current conditions and forecasts.

   The possibility of a loss must be considered even if it is very low. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. **Integral credit enhancements (para B5.5.55).** Expected cash shortfalls reflect cash flows from collateral and other credit enhancements "that are part of the contractual terms and are not recognised separately by the entity". [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### IBOR reform amendments

10. **Phase 1.** "Interest Rate Benchmark Reform" (amending IFRS 9, IAS 39 and IFRS 7) was issued in September 2019 and added IFRS 9 Section 6.8. It applies for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2020. It covers hedging relationships "directly affected" by the reform (uncertainty about the benchmark or the timing or amount of benchmark-based cash flows). For example, in assessing whether a hedged forecast transaction is highly probable, an entity assumes the benchmark is not altered by the reform (paras 6.8.1, 6.8.4–6.8.5, 7.1.8). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. **Phase 2.** "Interest Rate Benchmark Reform – Phase 2" (amending IFRS 9, IAS 39, IFRS 7, IFRS 4 and IFRS 16) was issued in August 2020. It added IFRS 9 paras 5.4.5–5.4.9 and 6.8.13, Section 6.9 and paras 7.2.43–7.2.46. It applies for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2021, retrospectively, but without restating prior periods for new hedge designations. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
12. **Phase 2 practical expedient (paras 5.4.7–5.4.9, B5.4.5).** A change in the basis for contractual cash flows that is "required by" the reform is accounted for by updating the effective interest rate, as for a floating-rate reset, rather than as a modification gain or loss. "Required by" means both conditions hold:
    - the change is necessary as a direct consequence of the reform;
    - the new basis is economically equivalent to the previous one.

    Other simultaneous changes follow the normal modification and derecognition rules. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
13. **Phase 2 hedge accounting (paras 6.9.1, 6.9.7, 6.9.11).**
    - Hedge designations are amended for reform-required changes without discontinuing the hedge.
    - When the hedged item's description is amended, the cash flow hedge reserve is deemed to be based on the alternative benchmark rate.
    - A non-contractually specified risk component of an alternative rate may be designated if it is reasonably expected to become separately identifiable within 24 months.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
14. **IFRS 16 lessees (paras 104–105).** Lessees apply a similar practical expedient to lease modifications required by the reform. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### IAS 28: associates and joint ventures

15. **Significant influence (paras 3, 5–6).**
    - An associate is an entity over which the investor has significant influence.
    - Holding 20% or more of the voting power (directly or through subsidiaries) creates a rebuttable presumption of significant influence; less than 20% creates the opposite presumption.
    - A majority holding by another investor does not necessarily preclude significant influence.
    - Board representation is the first listed indicator.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
16. **Equity method (paras 10, 16).** Investments in associates and joint ventures are recognised at cost and adjusted for the investor's share of profit or loss and OCI. Distributions reduce the carrying amount. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
17. **Losses and long-term interests (paras 14A, 38–39).**
    - Once the investor's share of losses reaches its interest, further losses are not recognised unless it has legal or constructive obligations.
    - The "interest" includes long-term interests, such as shareholder loans not expected to be settled, that in substance form part of the net investment.
    - Under amendments issued in October 2017 (effective for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2019), IFRS 9, including impairment, is applied to such long-term interests before IAS 28's loss allocation.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
18. **Upstream and downstream transactions (para 28).** Gains on such transactions with an associate or joint venture are recognised only to the extent of unrelated investors' interests. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
19. **Discontinuing the equity method (paras 22–24).** When the equity method is discontinued, OCI that would be reclassified on disposal is reclassified to profit or loss. A change between associate and joint venture does not trigger remeasurement of the retained interest. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### IFRS 10: loss of control

20. **Control (para 7).** An investor controls an investee only if it has all three of:
    - power over the investee;
    - exposure, or rights, to variable returns;
    - the ability to use its power to affect those returns.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
21. **Changes without loss of control (para 23).** Changes in ownership that do not result in loss of control are equity transactions. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
22. **On loss of control (paras 25, B98).** The parent:
    - derecognises the subsidiary's assets, including goodwill, and liabilities, and the non-controlling interests, at their carrying amounts;
    - recognises consideration received at fair value;
    - recognises any retained investment at fair value, which becomes its cost as an associate or joint venture, or its initial fair value under IFRS 9;
    - recognises the resulting gain or loss in profit or loss attributable to the parent.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
23. **OCI recycling (para B99).** Amounts in OCI relating to the subsidiary are accounted for as if the parent had directly disposed of the related assets or liabilities. Gains or losses that would be reclassified on disposal, such as a cash flow hedge reserve or foreign currency translation, are reclassified to profit or loss. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
24. **Multiple arrangements (para B97).** Arrangements that lose control in steps are treated as a single transaction where indicators apply, for example being entered into at the same time or in contemplation of each other, or forming a single transaction designed to achieve an overall commercial effect. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### Lessor classification

25. **IFRS 16 scope (para 3(c)).** IFRS 16 does not apply to service concession arrangements within the scope of IFRIC 12. IFRIC 12 para AG8 says a lease may still exist for separable unregulated infrastructure or ancillary facilities, and that lease follows IFRS 16. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
26. **IFRS 16 lessor test (paras 61–66).** A lease is a finance lease if it transfers substantially all the risks and rewards incidental to ownership; otherwise it is an operating lease.
    - Examples that normally lead to finance lease classification (para 63):
      - ownership transfers to the lessee by the end of the term;
      - the lessee has a purchase option reasonably certain to be exercised;
      - the term is for the major part of economic life;
      - the present value of lease payments amounts to at least substantially all of fair value;
      - the asset is so specialised that only the lessee can use it without major modification.
    - Further indicators (para 64):
      - the lessee bears the lessor's cancellation losses;
      - residual value gains or losses accrue to the lessee;
      - a secondary period at a rent substantially below market.
    - The examples are not conclusive (para 65).
    - Classification is set at inception and reassessed only on a lease modification (para 66).
    - IFRS 16 gives no numerical thresholds.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
27. **ASC 842 lessor test (842-10-25-2, 25-3).** A lessor classifies a lease as **sales-type** if any of five criteria is met:
    - (a) ownership transfers;
    - (b) a purchase option the lessee is reasonably certain to exercise;
    - (c) the term is for the major part of the remaining economic life (not used near the end of life);
    - (d) the present value of lease payments plus lessee-guaranteed residual value equals or exceeds substantially all of fair value;
    - (e) the asset is so specialised it has no alternative use to the lessor.

    If none is met, the lease is a **direct financing** lease where both of the following hold, and otherwise an **operating** lease:
    - the present value of lease payments plus residual value guaranteed by the lessee and/or unrelated third parties is substantially all of fair value;
    - collection is probable.

    [confidence: high] [source: 2]
28. **ASC 842 reference points (842-10-55-2).** "One reasonable approach" is to treat:
    - 75% or more of remaining economic life as a major part;
    - a commencement within the last 25% of total economic life as "at or near the end";
    - 90% or more of fair value as substantially all.

    [confidence: high] [source: 2]
29. **ASU 2021-05.** Issued in July 2021. A lessor must classify a lease with variable payments not based on an index or rate as operating if both:
    - it would otherwise be sales-type or direct financing;
    - the lessor would otherwise recognise a day-one loss.

    Effective for fiscal years beginning after 15 December 2021. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### ASC 810: variable interest entities

30. **VIE conditions (810-10-15-14, as amended by ASU 2015-02, February 2015).** A legal entity is a VIE if, by design, any of these applies:
    - its equity investment at risk is insufficient to finance its activities without additional subordinated financial support;
    - the equity holders as a group lack the power, through voting or similar rights, to direct the activities that most significantly affect its economic performance;
    - the equity holders lack the obligation to absorb expected losses or the right to receive expected residual returns, or have voting rights disproportionate to their economics.

    Equity at risk excludes amounts financed for the equity investor by the entity or other involved parties. [confidence: high for (a) and the power limb as quoted; the third limb is paraphrased] [source: 4]
31. **Primary beneficiary (810-10-25-38A).** A reporting entity consolidates a VIE if it has both:
    - (a) the power to direct the activities of the VIE that most significantly affect its economic performance;
    - (b) the obligation to absorb losses, or the right to receive benefits, that could potentially be significant to the VIE.

    The quantitative expected-losses approach "is not required and shall not be the sole determinant". Only one reporting entity, if any, is expected to be the primary beneficiary. [confidence: high] [source: 4]

### IFRIC 12 practice for IPPs and grid assets (company filings)

32. **When an IPP falls under IFRIC 12 (para AG2).** Condition (a), grantor control or regulation of services, users and price, "includes circumstances in which the grantor buys all of the output". If the grantor is a public-sector entity, the public sector as a whole and regulators acting in the public interest are treated as related to it. A single-buyer PPA with a state utility can therefore meet condition (a). Condition (b), grantor control of the significant residual interest, must also be met (AG4). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
33. **Ellomay Capital (Israel solar, IFRIC 12 financial asset).**
    - Ellomay presented its Talmei Yosef solar plant in Israel as a financial asset under IFRIC 12.
    - It agreed the sale on 31 December 2023 and completed it in June 2024. Although the price reflected a market value above its investment, the IFRIC 12 accounting produced a net loss of approximately EUR 3.2 million on the sale.
    - Ellomay's Series D, E and F bond covenants test net financial debt to EBITDA with revenues "calculated based on the fixed asset model and not based on the financial asset model (IFRIC 12)".

    [confidence: high] [source: 5]
34. **ReNew Energy Global (India transmission, IFRIC 12 to IFRS 16).**
    - ReNew had two transmission projects under 35-year build-own-operate-maintain (BOOM) Transmission Services Agreements, against a 50-year economic life.
    - After a change in regulations, it concluded in the prior year that IFRIC 12 no longer applied. The TSAs instead contain an operating lease of the line under IFRS 16, plus a service element under IFRS 15. Rentals step down for eight years and are then constant, subject to minimum availability.
    - Both assets were sold in the year ended 31 March 2026.
    - ReNew's accounting policy applies the IFRIC 12 financial asset model where it has an unconditional contractual right to cash from the grantor.

    [confidence: high] [source: 6]

### IPSAS 32 (grantor)

35. See t-ppp-frameworks, facts 19–20. IPSAS 32, *Service Concession Arrangements: Grantor* (2011), uses a control approach mirroring IFRIC 12: the public partner recognises the asset if it controls use, users and price. That can differ from Eurostat's risks-and-rewards test. [confidence: high, as verified in t-ppp-frameworks] [source: 7]

## Financing and structure details

Not a transaction. Structural points that follow from the verified rules:

- **Offtaker receivables.** A project company selling to a state utility will usually hold trade receivables without a significant financing component. The simplified lifetime-ECL approach and a provision matrix are then natural (facts 6–7). If the PPA is backed by a sovereign guarantee or letter of credit forming part of the contract, the expected shortfall is measured net of that enhancement (fact 9). A separately purchased guarantee is not netted this way.
- **Swaps after LIBOR.** For a project's LIBOR loan and swap that moved to SOFR plus a fixed spread adjustment, Phase 2 let the borrower update the effective interest rate on the loan. It also let the borrower amend the hedge documentation without discontinuing the cash flow hedge, keeping the hedge reserve intact (facts 11–13).
- **Sell-downs.** A sponsor selling from 60% to a non-controlling 36% stake:
  1. loses control under IFRS 10;
  2. books a gain on both the sold and the retained portions, since the retained stake is remeasured to fair value;
  3. recycles its share of the cash flow hedge reserve (facts 22–23).

  The retained stake is then equity-accounted if significant influence or joint control exists (facts 15–16).
- **Lessor or concession for the project company.** The same asset can fall under:
  - IFRIC 12, where a public grantor controls services, price and residual (financial or intangible asset);
  - IFRS 16 as a lessor (finance or operating lease);
  - IFRS 15/IAS 16 (PP&E plus revenue).

  ReNew's reclassification after a regulatory change shows the answer can change mid-life (fact 34). Ellomay's covenants show lenders often redefine EBITDA to strip out the IFRIC 12 financial-asset model (fact 33).
- **US GAAP sponsors.** A project company with thin equity, or whose decisions sit with a managing member, may be a VIE. The consolidator is the party with power over the most significant activities (often operations and maintenance, dispatch or remarketing) and significant economics. Tax-equity structures make this analysis common (facts 30–31).

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Ellomay's exit loss.** Ellomay explains that it had recognised "relatively high profits" from the plant since acquisition under the IFRIC 12 financial-asset treatment. As a result, a sale at a price above its investment still produced a net accounting loss of approximately EUR 3.2 million. The general lesson is that accounting carrying amounts under IFRIC 12 can diverge from the economic value a buyer pays. [source: 5]
- **Why lenders adjust covenants.** Ellomay's bond deeds compute EBITDA on a "fixed asset model" rather than the IFRIC 12 model, which neutralises the effect of the accounting choice on leverage tests. [source: 5]
- **Why Phase 2 mattered.** Without the practical expedient, replacing LIBOR in thousands of contracts could have triggered modification gains or losses and hedge discontinuations across project portfolios. The IASB limited relief to changes "required by" the reform and "economically equivalent" (fact 12). [source: 1]

## Teaching angles by chapter

**Chapter 66 (Accounting for projects and sponsors).**

- Work through Case P in sequence:
  1. Bélanou's ECL on state-utility receivables (simplified approach, provision matrix, credit enhancement netting).
  2. The LIBOR-to-SOFR swap under Phase 2.
  3. Kilnworth's 2026 sell-down from control to a 36% stake (IFRS 10 paras 25/B98–B99, then IAS 28).
- Contrast IFRS 16's judgement-based lessor indicators with ASC 842's 75%/90% reference points.
- Show the VIE test for a US-GAAP co-investor.
- Use Ellomay and ReNew as real evidence that IFRIC 12 choices move exit gains and covenant metrics.

**Chapter 7 (Accounting from zero).**

- Use the three ECL buckets to introduce allowances as an accrual concept: a receivable is shown net of expected losses, not only when a customer defaults.
- Use the IFRIC 12 financial-asset model (Ellomay) to show that a plant can appear as a receivable rather than PP&E, and that accounting profit and cash differ.

**Chapter 57 (The public-sector case for PPPs).**

- Pair IFRIC 12 (operator) with IPSAS 32 (grantor) through t-ppp-frameworks. Both use control, so in a well-designed availability PPP the asset sits on the government's IPSAS balance sheet and the operator holds a receivable.
- Contrast this with Eurostat's risks-and-rewards test for national accounts.

## Do not state

- That IFRS 9 uses the words "Stage 1, 2, 3". These are practice shorthand; the standard speaks of 12-month and lifetime ECL and credit-impaired assets.
- That the 1%/2%/3%/20% provision-matrix rates are typical market rates. They are the standard's illustration.
- The outcome or status of the IASB's amendments to IFRS 10 and IAS 28 on "Sale or Contribution of Assets between an Investor and its Associate or Joint Venture". Commonly said to have been deferred indefinitely; not verified here, and absent from the EU-endorsed text.
- Any IASB post-implementation review conclusions on IFRS 9 impairment or IFRS 16, and any 2025–2026 IASB amendments not listed above. Not verified here.
- The specific Indian regulation that led ReNew to drop IFRIC 12 for its TSAs. ReNew's filing refers to "a change in the regulations" but the excerpt reviewed does not name it.
- The counterparty identity and tariff of Ellomay's Talmei Yosef PPA. Not verified in the excerpts reviewed.
- IPSAS 32 paragraph-level requirements (financial liability versus grant-of-a-right model). Not retrieved; use t-ppp-frameworks and the IPSASB Handbook.
