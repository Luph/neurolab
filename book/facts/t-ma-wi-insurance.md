# M&A in infrastructure: locked box vs completion accounts; warranty and indemnity (W&I) insurance limits, retentions, pricing and claims

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest items covered: Aon "Global Claims Study 2026" covering 2025 data; Howden M&A "M&A Claims insights 2020–2024," August 2025; Marsh "Global transactional risk insurance claims report 2026"; CMS European M&A Study 2026, published March 27, 2026, key findings only)

## Summary

Buyers and sellers of infrastructure stakes allocate two kinds of risk in the sale and purchase agreement (SPA): price risk and warranty risk.

**Price risk: locked box or completion accounts.**
- **Locked box.** The price is fixed by reference to a historic balance sheet at a "locked box date." The seller then promises that no value will "leak" to it or its affiliates between that date and closing (dividends, payments, transfers, assumed liabilities), except agreed "permitted leakage." Any leakage is repaid pound for pound.
- **Completion accounts.** The price is adjusted after closing to the actual net debt and working capital at closing.

A real example is the February 2018 SPA under which Sun Hydraulics bought the Faster group from Capvis. It used a locked box date of December 31, 2017, with leakage defined as dividends, payments and transfers of value to the sellers after that date.

**Warranty risk: W&I insurance.** W&I (called representations and warranties, or R&W, insurance in North America) lets the seller exit with nominal liability, often USD/EUR/GBP 1, while the buyer claims against an insurer. Howden's 2024 buy-side guide gives the market parameters:
- limits typically 10–30 percent of enterprise value (EV), up to 100 percent;
- deductibles (retentions) of 0.25–1 percent of EV for operational deals, 0.10–0.25 percent for semi-operational real estate (often tipping to nil), and nil for pure real estate;
- one-off premiums of 0.70–1.50 percent of the limit for operational deals and 0.45–0.80 percent for real estate, up to 3–4 percent for high-risk deals;
- cover periods of 2–3 years for general warranties and 7 years for title and tax.

The 2025 market was soft. Aon reports average EMEA retentions fell from 0.71 percent (2021) to 0.27 percent (2025), excluding nil-retention policies.

**Claims experience.**
- About 18 percent of North American R&W policies bound in 2019–2023 have had a claim notification (Aon). Aon expects EMEA ultimate claim frequency for 2025 policies to exceed the long-standing 20 percent benchmark.
- Financial statement breaches dominate paid losses: about 40 percent of North American paid loss on policies since 2019 (Aon), and 75 percent of indemnity paid on Howden's EMEA book (2020–24).
- Payments are large: North American median USD 8.2 million and average over USD 10 million in 2025; EMEA average USD 5.2 million and median USD 2.1 million. The largest EMEA W&I payment on record is about EUR 120 million (B&C/Schur Flexibles, 2024).
- Disputes are rare: 2 percent of Howden's paid claims required litigation or arbitration, and 3 percent of Aon's North American claims were denied.

## Verified facts

**Locked box and leakage (illustrative primary document)**
1. **The Faster SPA.** The share purchase agreement dated February 18, 2018 between Capvis IV Co-Investors Faster L.P. and co-investors (sellers) and Sun Hydraulics Corporation (purchaser) defines "Locked Box Date" as December 31, 2017. It defines "Leakage" as specified items occurring after the Locked Box Date and on or before Closing, including:
   - dividends, distributions, or returns or reductions of capital to or for the benefit of sellers or their affiliates;
   - other payments or transfers of value or assets to them;
   - indebtedness or liabilities assumed or incurred for their benefit.

   "Permitted Leakage" is excluded. The SPA also has a clause on a warranty and indemnity insurance policy (Clause 9). It was filed as Exhibit 2.1 to Sun Hydraulics' Form 8-K. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Why leakage matters to W&I.** Howden's buy-side guide lists purchase price adjustments (other than those arising from warranty or indemnity claims) and leakage among the market-standard exclusions from W&I cover. Leakage protection therefore has to come from the seller's covenant in the SPA, not from the policy. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. **Market use (Europe).** The CMS European M&A Study 2026 (601 deals CMS advised in 2025) reports that purchase price adjustments "stayed steady" in 2025 compared with 2024. The study covers locked box and earn-out usage, but the percentages are in the gated full report, which was not read. The 2025 edition reported "a notable 8% increase" in W&I adoption, with the UK leading. [confidence: high for the statements quoted; percentages not verified] [source: 3, 4]

**W&I market parameters (Howden buy-side guide, document dated 2023, updated September 2024)**
4. **Limits and cover periods.**
   - Policy limits typically range from 10 to 30 percent of the target's EV. Up to 100 percent can be bought, and capacity of EUR 1 billion or more per deal is available.
   - A buy-side policy typically survives 2 years from completion for general warranties (extendable to 3 years for most, and in some jurisdictions 5 years for employment and environmental).
   - It survives 7 years for title and capacity, tax warranties and the tax indemnity. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
5. **Deductibles (retentions).**
   - Operational deals: 0.25–1 percent of EV.
   - Semi-operational real estate (for example hotels): 0.10–0.25 percent, often able to tip to nil.
   - Pure real estate: usually nil.
   - Higher-risk jurisdictions or sectors may need higher deductibles. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. **Premiums.** Premiums are a one-off cost. Typical rates:
   - real estate: 0.45–0.80 percent of the policy limit;
   - operational transactions: 0.70–1.50 percent;
   - higher-risk deals: as much as 3–4 percent.

   Insurers' legal fees are typically EUR/GBP 10,000–30,000. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
7. **Standard exclusions and process.**
   - Standard exclusions: matters within the buyer deal team's actual knowledge; fairly disclosed matters; purchase price adjustments and leakage; the physical condition of properties; and secondary tax, transfer pricing and the availability of tax losses (which can be brought back with diligence).
   - Placement typically takes three weeks, or as little as three days, with the policy incepting at SPA signing. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
8. **Retention trend (EMEA).** Aon (claims study 2026) reports average W&I retentions in EMEA "dropped from 0.71% in 2021 to just 0.27% in 2025 (not including nil retention policies)." It attributes this to soft-market competition, which means more claims now respond from the first dollar. [confidence: high] [source: 5]

**Claims data**
9. **Aon, North America (R&W).**
   - About 18 percent of policies bound 2019–2023 have seen at least one claim notification. Aon expects frequency to stay in the historic 16–20 percent range.
   - In 2025 clients recovered over USD 440 million on R&W claims (a record), with a median payment of about USD 8.2 million and an average of about USD 10.4 million.
   - 51 percent of claims were notified more than 12 months after closing, often after seller escrows expired.
   - Financial statement breaches account for about 38–40 percent of paid loss on policies placed since 2019, followed by material contracts (21 percent) and compliance with laws (about 15 percent).
   - In 2025, 68 percent of the amount paid was calculated on a multiple basis.
   - Only 3 percent of claims were denied, and 1 percent went to arbitration or litigation. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
10. **Aon, limits.** Of North American paid claims, 23 percent exceeded 60 percent of the policy limit, and 8 percent were full-limit payments. Aon suggests that buying a limit of 10 percent of EV "may be" commercially reasonable in many circumstances. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
11. **Aon, EMEA (W&I).**
    - Notifications rose from 70 in 2024 to 119 in 2025, a 47 percent increase in submitted notifications.
    - 9.5 percent of 2025 policies had a notification by year-end 2025, as did 12.5 percent of 2024 policies and 14.4 percent of 2023 policies.
    - Projected ultimate frequency for 2025 policies exceeds the "long-standing 20% benchmark."
    - The average R&W payment in EMEA in 2025 was USD 5.2 million, and the median USD 2.1 million. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
12. **Howden, EMEA (2020–2024).**
    - 8 percent of W&I transactions received a notified claim.
    - 65 percent of closed claims with an actual loss were paid or eroded the retention; 35 percent were validly declined. The main grounds were property defects (22 percent), disclosed matters (19 percent) and bespoke exclusions (16 percent).
    - The average time to pay was 16 months.
    - Financial statements accounted for 36 percent of paid claims and 75 percent of total indemnity paid, tax for 20 percent of indemnity, and material contracts for 15 percent.
    - Only 2 percent of paid claims required litigation or arbitration. No insurer subrogated against a seller.
    - Over EUR 260 million was paid in 2020–2024, including about EUR 190 million in 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
13. **Largest EMEA W&I claim.** In 2024 B&C KB Holding GmbH received about EUR 120 million from RiskPoint Group and Liberty Global Transaction Solutions. The claim related to its acquisition of the Schur Flexibles Group (Austrian packaging), after irregularities were found in prior-year balance sheets. Howden describes it as the largest W&I claim paid to date in EMEA. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
14. **Marsh (2026 report, summary).** Marsh's Global transactional risk insurance claims report 2026 (2025 data) states that financial statement breaches were the primary driver of paid losses globally, that tax breaches were among the most frequently notified, and that claims activity continues to rise. Detailed figures are in a gated download that was not read. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

## Financing and structure details

Interaction with project finance and infrastructure stake sales:
- **Locked box in a project company sale.** Distributions from the SPV to the seller after the locked box date are leakage unless listed as permitted. Under the project's finance documents, distributions are anyway gated by distribution lock-up tests. The buyer typically pays a daily "ticker" (interest-like accrual) in lieu of post-locked-box profits. The ticker is a common feature but is not verified in the example SPA here.
- **W&I sizing.** Limit as a percentage of EV (10–30 percent typical), retention as a percentage of EV (0.25–1 percent operational, falling toward 0.27 percent average in EMEA in 2025), and premium as a percentage of limit (0.70–1.50 percent operational). Lenders financing the acquisition may take an assignment of the policy proceeds. That is common practice, but not verified in sources read here.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Soft market.** Howden explains 2022–2025 pricing and terms through the insurance cycle. Lower M&A volume and competition among insurers produced lower premiums and broader cover. It warns insurers may "recalibrate pricing and tighten terms" as claims develop. Aon links lower retentions to more small claims being pursued (sources 5, 6).
- **Severity drivers.** Both Aon and Howden find financial statement breaches dominate paid losses, often valued on a multiple of the earnings shortfall rather than pound for pound. Aon reports more insurers seeing multiple-based claims (67 percent of insurer respondents, up from 41 percent) (sources 5, 6).

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 47 (Bidding, acquisitions and funds):**
  - For the Case R acquisition, teach the choice between locked box and completion accounts. Use the Faster leakage definition as a model clause and explain permitted leakage, for example scheduled debt service and management fees.
  - Then size a W&I policy using Howden's parameters, and show how seller cap and escrow terms change when W&I is bought.
  - Stress that leakage and price adjustments are excluded from W&I.
- **Ch 63 (Refinancing, repricing and secondary sales):**
  - For the Case P partial sale, use the claims data (frequency of about 18–20 percent, financial statements as the main loss driver, 51 percent notified after 12 months) to show why buyers diligence historical accounts and why W&I cover beyond a 12-month escrow matters.
  - Discuss how distribution lock-ups in the project's loan agreement interact with the locked box.

## Do not state

- Do not state that "most infrastructure deals use a locked box," or give a percentage split between locked box and completion accounts for infrastructure or for Europe. The CMS percentages were not read, and no infrastructure-specific data were found.
- Do not present Howden's ranges as current pricing for 2026. The guide is dated 2023 (updated September 2024), and the market softened afterwards.
- Do not give a single "market" W&I rate-on-line for 2025–26. Not verified.
- Do not equate Aon's North American R&W statistics with European W&I. The products, retentions and loss rules differ, and Aon reports them separately.
- Do not name infrastructure-specific W&I claims. None was verified.
