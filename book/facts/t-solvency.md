# Solvency II infrastructure asset classes and the review

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: EIOPA completes its Solvency II review mandate with final guidelines and draft technical standards, July 2026; amended rules apply from 30 January 2027)

## Summary

Solvency II is the EU prudential regime for insurers. It has applied since 1 January 2016, and its standard formula sets a solvency capital requirement (SCR) by shocking each asset class. Insurers are natural holders of long-dated, illiquid, predictable cash flows, but the original calibration treated unlisted project equity as "type 2" equity (a 49% shock) and charged project bonds and loans like ordinary corporate credit. Under the Capital Markets Union agenda, the European Commission added two infrastructure asset classes to Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35. Regulation (EU) 2016/467, effective 2 April 2016, created "qualifying infrastructure investments" (project-level SPVs): a 30% equity shock and spread-risk factors cut by roughly 30-40%. Regulation (EU) 2017/1542, effective 15 September 2017, created "qualifying infrastructure corporates": a 36% equity shock and debt factors cut by about 25% on average. To qualify, investments must meet detailed criteria on predictable, contracted or regulated revenues, stress-tested debt service, creditor protections and the insurer's own due diligence and monitoring (Articles 164a, 164b and 261a). The 2020-2025 review produced Directive (EU) 2025/2 (published 8 January 2025) and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/269 (published 18 February 2026). Both apply from 30 January 2027. They leave the infrastructure classes broadly as they are. Instead they free capital through a lower risk margin, an easier long-term equity (LTE) route at 22%, a wider symmetric adjustment corridor and reformed securitisation charges. In the UK, the separate "Solvency UK" regime took a different route. It widened matching adjustment (MA) eligibility to "highly predictable" assets from 30 June 2024, and in October 2025 it finalised an MA Investment Accelerator.

## Verified facts

**Legal architecture (EU)**

1. The framework Directive is 2009/138/EC (Solvency II). The detailed standard-formula calibrations sit in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35 (the "Delegated Regulation"). The regime has applied since 1 January 2016. Current rules: EUR-Lex consolidated text of Regulation 2015/35 and the Commission's "Implementing and delegated acts – Solvency II" page. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 9]
2. Before the infrastructure amendments, the standard-formula equity shocks were 39% for type 1 equity (listed in EEA/OECD) and 49% for type 2 equity (including unlisted equity). Both are before the symmetric adjustment (Article 169). Unlisted project equity therefore typically attracted 49%. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

**Qualifying infrastructure investments (projects), Regulation (EU) 2016/467**

3. Regulation (EU) 2016/467 introduced "qualifying infrastructure investments" and became effective on 2 April 2016. It initially covered investment through entities that do nothing other than own, finance, develop or operate infrastructure assets. The primary source of payments to debt and equity investors had to be the income from the assets being financed. Infrastructure assets are physical assets, structures or facilities, systems or networks that provide or support essential public services. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
4. The equity shock for qualifying infrastructure equity is 30%, against 49% for type 2 equity. The symmetric adjustment applied to these holdings is 77% of the standard symmetric adjustment. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
5. Qualifying infrastructure bonds and loans receive reduced spread-risk factors (Article 180). Rated debt must be investment grade, which means credit quality step (CQS) 0-3. Unrated debt must be senior to, or pari passu with, all other claims except statutory claims and claims of liquidity facility providers, trustees and derivative counterparties. Milliman's worked example: a 20-year CQS 3 bond moves from a 30% to a 20% spread-risk factor, a reduction of over 30% for CQS 3. KPMG summarises the average reduction in the spread SCR as about 40% for project debt. [confidence: high for the 30%-to-20% example and the eligibility gates; medium for the 40% average] [source: 2, 3, 4]
6. To qualify, the cash flows must be shown to meet all financial obligations under sustained stresses relevant to the project's risks. The cash flows must also be predictable. "All except an immaterial part" of revenues must be availability-based, subject to rate-of-return regulation, under a take-or-pay contract, or have output/usage and price that are each regulated, contractually fixed or sufficiently predictable because demand risk is low. Unrated debt and equity face extra conditions, including EEA/OECD location and contractual protections for investors. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
7. Article 261a imposes risk-management duties on the insurer: [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4]
   - adequate due diligence before investing;
   - regular monitoring and stress testing of the supporting cash flows and collateral;
   - written procedures for monitoring the construction phase and for maximising recoveries in a work-out;
   - asset-liability management that ensures debt can be held to maturity.
8. Qualifying infrastructure equity held through a European Long-Term Investment Fund (ELTIF) keeps the 30% calibration under the look-through approach. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

**Qualifying infrastructure corporates, Regulation (EU) 2017/1542**

9. EIOPA published its advice on infrastructure corporates on 30 June 2016. It recommended two things:
   - extending the project class to "project-like" corporate groups;
   - a new class of qualifying infrastructure corporate equity with a 36% shock, a 75% correlation with type 1 equity and 100% with type 2.

   EIOPA said it had not found sufficient evidence to justify lower charges for infrastructure corporate debt. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
10. Under EIOPA's definition, an infrastructure corporate derives the substantial majority of its revenues from owning, financing, developing or operating infrastructure assets in the EEA or OECD, in six sectors: [confidence: high] [source: 4]
    - electricity or thermal energy (generation, transmission, distribution);
    - gas transmission or distribution;
    - water and wastewater;
    - waste management and recycling;
    - transport networks and assets;
    - social infrastructure.

    Telecoms is not on the list. Rated corporates need at least CQS 3; otherwise at least three years of operation in those lines of business.
11. Regulation (EU) 2017/1542 took effect on 15 September 2017. It set the shock for qualifying infrastructure corporate equity at 36% (22% where the holding is strategic), with the symmetric adjustment multiplied by 92%. Milliman reports that debt calibrations for qualifying infrastructure corporates fell by about 25% on average against the standard formula; KPMG gives about 30%. The Commission went beyond EIOPA's advice by giving a debt reduction. [confidence: high for 36%/22%/92% and the date; medium for the average debt reduction] [source: 2, 3]

**Related Capital Markets Union changes, Regulation (EU) 2019/981**

12. A 2019 amendment did three things: [confidence: high] [source: 2]
    - it let insurers assign unrated corporate bonds and loans to CQS 2 or 3 through an internal credit assessment meeting set criteria;
    - it gave "qualifying unlisted equity" portfolios type 1 treatment (39%);
    - it created a 22% long-term equity class.

    Its internal-assessment route excludes issuers that are themselves infrastructure entities, because those are covered by the infrastructure classes.

**The Solvency II review**

13. Directive (EU) 2025/2, amending Solvency II, was adopted on 27 November 2024 and published in the Official Journal on 8 January 2025. Member states must transpose it, and the new rules apply, from 30 January 2027 (sources describe the deadline as "end-January 2027"). [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]
14. The Directive cuts the cost-of-capital rate used in the risk margin to 4.75%, down from 6%. The Commission and law-firm summaries put the overall reduction in the risk margin at approximately 21%. [confidence: high for 4.75%; medium for 6% as the prior rate; approximately for 21%] [source: 5, 7]
15. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/269 amends Delegated Regulation 2015/35. It was adopted on 29 October 2025, published on 18 February 2026, entered into force on 10 March 2026 and applies from 30 January 2027. [confidence: high] [source: 7, 8]
16. Main capital changes in Regulation 2026/269: [confidence: high] [source: 7, 8]
    - a 22% factor for long-term equity, where the insurer can show it can avoid forced sales for at least five years, on an ongoing basis and under stressed conditions;
    - a proportionately lower factor for equity under EU legislative programmes with public guarantees (the Commission's example: a 20% credit-risk reduction gives 17.6%);
    - a symmetric adjustment corridor widened to -13% to +13%;
    - lower securitisation charges, with STS senior tranches aligned with corporate or covered bonds of comparable rating;
    - a 20-year last liquid point for the euro, with an 11% convergence parameter (40% for SEK);
    - a time-dependent decay in the risk margin (an annual risk reduction of at least 3.5%, capped at 50% in total);
    - a revised volatility adjustment.
17. Neither the Commission's Q&A on Regulation 2026/269 nor Debevoise's summary of it reports any change to the qualifying infrastructure criteria or factors (Articles 164a, 164b). The review's effect on infrastructure is indirect: through the LTE route, the lower risk margin and general capital release. [confidence: medium; this rests on the summaries, not a line-by-line review of the amending act] [source: 7, 8]
18. EIOPA announced in July 2026 that it had completed its review mandate with final guidelines and draft technical standards. It intends to launch a coordinated data collection in 2027 to assess the new regime's impact, including use of the expected capital relief. The Commission's Q&A says EIOPA's first monitoring report is due by 31 December 2028. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 10]

**UK (Solvency UK): different mechanism**

19. The PRA published PS10/24, "Reform of the Matching Adjustment," on 6 June 2024, effective 30 June 2024. It admits assets with "highly predictable" (HP) cash flows to MA portfolios. HP cash flows must be contractually bound in time and amount, with default if those terms are not met. No more than 10% of a firm's aggregate MA benefit may come from HP assets. Commentators note that HP assets can open up construction-phase and other infrastructure lending. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
20. The PRA finalised the Matching Adjustment Investment Accelerator in PS17/25 on 23 October 2025, after consulting in CP7/25 (8 April 2025). A firm with an accelerator permission can hold a limited quantity of self-assessed MA-eligible assets in its MA portfolio for up to 24 months before regularising them through a formal application. [confidence: high] [source: 12]

**Indicative magnitudes (for teaching, standard formula, before diversification and symmetric adjustment)**

21. Equity shocks:

    | Category | Shock |
    |---|---|
    | Type 2 / unlisted equity (default) | 49% |
    | Type 1 | 39% |
    | Qualifying infrastructure corporate equity | 36% |
    | Qualifying infrastructure project equity | 30% |
    | Long-term equity and strategic infrastructure corporate equity | 22% |

    For debt, the illustrative reduction is a 20-year CQS 3 bond moving from 30% to 20%. Drivers of actual capital: [confidence: high for the factors] [source: 2, 3, 7]
    - rating/CQS and modified duration (spread risk);
    - whether the holding qualifies at all;
    - the symmetric adjustment;
    - diversification within the SCR;
    - whether the insurer uses an internal model rather than the standard formula. Large insurers often do, and the standard-formula relief is then less relevant.

## Financing and structure details

How the classes shape deal structure (from the qualification criteria above):

- **Revenue model:** availability payments, rate-of-return regulation and take-or-pay qualify. Merchant revenue qualifies only where demand risk is low and price is regulated or fixed. Merchant-heavy renewables and demand-risk toll roads often fail, or need structuring.
- **Offtaker quality:** where revenues are not paid by many users, the purchaser must be a public entity listed in Article 180(2), a listed regional or local authority, an entity rated at least CQS 3, or replaceable without a significant change in revenue.
- **Debt features:** rated debt must be CQS 3 or better. Unrated debt must be senior or pari passu, have security or equivalent protections, carry covenants restricting new debt or permit it only above thresholds, and come with a stress-tested base case. These features match standard project bond and loan terms (Chapters 34-35), which is why investment-grade senior project debt is the insurers' sweet spot.
- **Holding route:** direct holdings, private placements and funds all work, provided the look-through approach can be applied (for example ELTIFs).

## What went wrong or right, and why

- EIOPA's own advice process showed the evidence problem. EIOPA found enough default and recovery data to support lower charges for project debt and project-like entities, but not for infrastructure corporate debt. The Commission nevertheless reduced corporate debt charges in 2017. [source: 3, 4]
- Milliman (2019) records the Commission's observation that the early Capital Markets Union measures had produced limited change in insurer investment behaviour. This was one reason for the 2019 package and for the LTE reform in the 2025-2026 review. [source: 2]
- Market commentators often cite the qualification criteria and the documentation burden as reasons uptake has been modest. No authoritative EU-wide uptake figure was verified (see Do not state).

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 68 (Capital rules for banks and insurers):** Teach the standard formula as a price signal. A 49%-to-30% equity shock, or a 30%-to-20% spread factor, lowers the capital, and so the return on capital, an insurer needs, and that shows up in the spread it will accept. Walk through the qualification tests as a checklist an arranger uses to make a project bond "Solvency II-qualifying." Contrast the EU route (lower charges for a defined class) with the UK route (MA eligibility for highly predictable assets), and compare both with Basel slotting for banks. Stress that internal-model insurers are less affected, and give the as-of date (rules applying from 30 January 2027) and where to find them (EUR-Lex consolidated 2015/35; the PRA Rulebook).
- **Cross-reference, Chapters 34-35 and 47 (bonds, institutional investors):** Use the qualifying criteria to explain why insurers favour availability-based PPP and regulated-utility debt rated BBB or better, and why they want construction-phase support or monitoring rights.

## Do not state

- Any single "average" capital saving as precise. Sources give about 30-40% (projects) and about 25-30% (corporates) for debt; mark them approximate and attribute them.
- That the 2025-2026 review recalibrated the 30%/36% infrastructure equity factors or the infrastructure spread tables. No verified source says so.
- Any figure for the volume of insurer assets flagged as qualifying infrastructure across the EU. Not verified.
- The full Article 180(12) spread-risk table by CQS and duration. Not reproduced here because it was not verified line by line; cite the official text instead.
- That Regulation 2017/1542 followed EIOPA's advice on debt. It went further than the advice.
- Exact Solvency UK risk-margin parameters (for example the reduction for life insurers). Not verified for this sheet.
- That telecoms qualifies as an infrastructure corporate. It is excluded from the corporate sector list, though project-level telecoms assets may qualify as projects.
