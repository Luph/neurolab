# Ichthys LNG (Browse Basin and Darwin, Australia)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: the 2022 refinancing and KBR's 12 April 2022 settlement with the power-plant subcontractor; no later financing event verified)

## Summary

Ichthys LNG is an INPEX-operated gas and condensate development that brings gas from the Ichthys field in the Browse Basin offshore Western Australia through an approximately 890 km export pipeline to a two-train onshore liquefaction plant near Darwin, Northern Territory. INPEX and Total (now TotalEnergies) took the final investment decision on 13 January 2012 at a capital cost of USD 34 billion (100 percent basis); it was the first world-scale LNG project led by a Japanese company as operator. On 18 December 2012 the borrower, Ichthys LNG Pty Ltd (the incorporated joint venture for the downstream), finalized USD 20 billion of project finance, which INPEX called the largest project financing ever arranged in the international market: USD 5.8 billion of direct loans from JBIC, KEXIM and EFIC, USD 5.4 billion of ECA-insured or guaranteed commercial loans (NEXI, KEXIM, K-sure, Atradius, Euler Hermes, Coface), USD 4.8 billion of uncovered commercial loans from 24 banks, and USD 4.0 billion of senior sponsor loans from the project participants, with the participants severally guaranteeing the debt during construction. Start-up slipped from the end of 2016 to October 2018 (first LNG cargo shipped 22 October 2018), and the operator's final capital estimate reached approximately USD 45 billion, against USD 34 billion at FID. A key driver was the collapse of the combined-cycle power plant subcontract: the UGL/CH2M/GE consortium served notice of termination on the EPC contractor JKC on 25 January 2017, leading to years of arbitration and litigation. Despite the overrun, Ichthys passed its 90-day lender completion test on 7 December 2019 (financial completion announced 20 December 2019, releasing the sponsors' completion guarantees), refinanced about USD 8.3 billion of its USD 15.6 billion of loans in June 2020, and completed a further USD 4.9 billion bank-and-bond refinancing in 2022.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Cost and schedule: Total's own disclosure puts the operator's final capex at around USD 45 billion against USD 34 billion at FID, roughly a one-third overrun, with an intermediate estimate of about USD 40 billion in 2017. [source: 5, 3] The best-documented single cause in primary filings is the power-plant subcontract failure: KBR's 10-K records the consortium's termination notice of January 2017 and JKC's allegations of defective design, workmanship and materials, after which JKC had to complete the plant with others. [source: 13] INPEX's own notices show the owner advancing AUD 758 million of interim funding to JKC to keep subcontractor claims from halting the work, then having to arbitrate and litigate to recover it. [source: 10, 11]
- What worked for lenders: the structure put construction risk on the sponsors through several completion guarantees, backed in INPEX's case by JOGMEC, so the overrun was funded by sponsors rather than by lender losses; once the 90-day test was passed, the debt became non-recourse and was refinanced on better terms in 2020 and 2022. [source: 1, 7, 8, 9]
- What worked for sponsors: a large Japanese and Taiwanese utility offtake book signed before financing (about 8.3 mtpa of 15-year contracts) supported ECA and bank appetite. [source: 1]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 29 (The lenders): Ichthys is the cleanest illustration of how a mega-financing is assembled from layers: ECA direct loans (JBIC dominant), ECA-covered commercial tranches from six agencies in several countries, uncovered bank debt and, unusually, a USD 4 billion senior sponsor loan within the debt package. Use it to show why ECAs follow national content and offtake (Japanese buyers, Korean shipyards and equipment, European suppliers), how a government agency (JOGMEC) can stand behind a sponsor's completion guarantee, and how a post-completion refinancing lets ECA-heavy construction debt be partly replaced by cheaper bank and bond debt.
- Ch 76 (LNG and FPSOs): Teach the full LNG chain risk on one project: offshore central processing facility and FPSO, long export pipeline, onshore trains, and the utility offtake book. The lesson is that interface and subcontract risk inside a lump EPC package can drive a multi-billion overrun (the power plant), that sponsor completion support is what protects lenders, and that the 90-day completion test is the moment the credit changes character, enabling refinancing.
