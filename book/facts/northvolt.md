# Northvolt financing and insolvency

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Lyten's binding agreement of August 7, 2025 to acquire Northvolt's remaining Swedish and German assets; completion not verified)

## Summary

Northvolt AB was a Swedish lithium-ion battery cell maker, founded in 2016 by former Tesla executives Peter Carlsson and Paolo Cerruti. It aimed to build Europe's homegrown battery industry. Its first gigafactory, Northvolt Ett in Skellefteå, northern Sweden, was financed as a project. Northvolt Ett AB borrowed about USD 1.6 billion of first- and second-lien "Snowball" facilities, signed July 28, 2020. The lenders included the European Investment Bank (EIB), export credit agency (ECA)-covered tranches backed by Euler Hermes, NEXI and Bpifrance Assurance Export, a direct loan from the Export-Import Bank of Korea, commercial banks and a second-lien tranche. At parent level, Northvolt AB raised large equity rounds and about USD 3.8 billion of convertible instruments from Volkswagen, other investors and Germany's KfW. Between January 2017 and January 2024 it raised more than USD 8 billion in total. In December 2023 / January 2024 it signed what was publicized as a USD 5 billion green, non-recourse expansion financing for Northvolt Ett. The court filings show USD 4.5 billion of debt commitments, including EIB support of about USD 1.04 billion guaranteed partly by the Swedish National Debt Office and InvestEU. These commitments were never drawn. Production ramp-up lagged, European electric vehicle (EV) demand slowed, and losses grew from USD 285 million (2022) to USD 1.2 billion (2023). In September 2024 Northvolt suspended the Ett expansion. Its expansion subsidiary entered Swedish bankruptcy in October 2024, and the expansion commitments were unwound. On November 21, 2024 Northvolt filed for Chapter 11 in the Southern District of Texas. It had about USD 30 million of cash and USD 5.84 billion of funded debt. Scania provided a USD 100 million DIP facility, and the senior lenders allowed debt service reserve cash to be released. The restructuring failed, and on March 12, 2025 Northvolt AB and key Swedish subsidiaries filed for bankruptcy in Sweden. On August 7, 2025, US lithium-sulfur battery company Lyten agreed to buy the remaining Swedish and German assets.

## Verified facts

1. Northvolt was formally founded in October 2016 by Peter Carlsson and Paolo Cerruti after they left Tesla in 2015. Between January 2017 and January 2024 it raised more than USD 8 billion, a figure that excludes the USD 5 billion "green loan," which was never funded. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The EIB supported the Northvolt Labs demonstration line in Västerås in 2018 with a EUR 52.5 million loan under the EU InnovFin program. It then lent USD 350 million (EUR 319 million) for the first phase of Northvolt Ett, backed by the European Fund for Strategic Investments (EFSI). [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. In June 2019 Northvolt completed an equity raise of USD 1 billion for Northvolt Ett, with affiliates of Goldman Sachs and Volkswagen among the key investors. At that time it reported supply agreements worth over USD 13 billion through 2030. Volkswagen and Goldman Sachs were the two largest shareholders of Northvolt AB as of November 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. In July 2020 Northvolt raised USD 1.6 billion of debt for Northvolt Ett from commercial banks, pension funds and public institutions. The court filings describe this as approximately USD 1.6 billion of project financing secured on a first- and second-lien basis at Northvolt Ett AB. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. The Northvolt Ett "Snowball" facilities, all dated July 28, 2020, comprised: EIB term loan USD 350 million (LIBOR, later SOFR, plus 300 bp); commercial uncovered term loan USD 50 million (ING agent); KEXIM direct term loan USD 100 million; Euler Hermes-covered term loan USD 525 million (SMBC agent; margin 1.3%); NEXI-covered term loan USD 150 million (margin 1.3%); BPIAE-covered term loan USD 75 million; revolving working capital facility USD 110 million; and a second-lien facility of USD 255 million (Danske Bank agent; 5% cash interest or PIK alternatives). The first-lien facilities mature December 31, 2029. The second lien matures June 30, 2030. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. The first-lien facilities were secured on substantially all of Northvolt Ett AB's assets, certain property of the PropCo (Northvolt Ett Fastighetsförvaltning AB), and pledges of Northvolt AB's shares in Northvolt Ett AB and Northvolt Labs AB. The Labs pledge secured only a pre-petition release of debt service reserve cash. A Security Agency and Intercreditor Deed dated July 28, 2020 governed priorities. Northvolt Ett AB had to hedge 70–110% of projected first-lien debt and had interest rate swaps fixed at 4.65% that run to June 30, 2027. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. Parent-level convertible instruments at the November 2024 petition date were:
   - VW convertible (agreement dated September 5, 2019): EUR 200 million for Ett expansion plus EUR 200 million for the German JV equity; approximately USD 330 million outstanding; matures December 31, 2025.
   - Volta convertible (agreement dated July 1, 2022): 8% interest stepping up 1% per period to a 12% cap; approximately USD 1.063 billion at first closing and USD 2.764 billion outstanding; matures August 17, 2027.
   - KfW convertible (agreement dated October 30, 2023): EUR 600 million at 3-month EURIBOR plus 100 bp, provided to fund equity for the Heide (Northvolt Drei) gigafactory; approximately USD 670 million outstanding; matures June 30, 2028.
   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. During 2023 Northvolt AB secured over EUR 1.3 billion in convertible instruments and grants from German governmental institutions for Northvolt Drei in Heide. Northvolt Six near Montréal secured approximately USD 5.8 billion of federal and provincial commitments in grants, debt and convertibles. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. On January 16, 2024 the EIB announced a lending package of about USD 1.038 billion (EUR 942.6 million) for the Northvolt Ett expansion. The loan was signed December 22, 2023. It formed part of a USD 5 billion non-recourse project financing that the EIB described as "the biggest green debt deal in Europe to date." Within the EIB package, USD 500 million was guaranteed by the Swedish National Debt Office (Riksgälden) and USD 400 million under InvestEU, and USD 138.7 million was intermediated through commercial banks. The expansion was meant to raise capacity to up to 60 GWh. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
10. The court filings describe the 2023 expansion package ("Northern Lights Finance Arrangements," coordination agreement dated December 22, 2023, Crédit Agricole CIB as agent) as USD 4.5 billion of debt commitments. The package was meant to refinance the Snowball facilities and fund the expansion. Drawing was subject to conditions precedent, including expansion milestones. After the expansion was abandoned, the agent unwound the arrangements effective November 14, 2024. About USD 44 million of fees remained owed. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. Northvolt Ett began operating in late 2021 and assembled its first cell in December 2021. It has supplied automakers since Q2 2022. In November 2024 it had a current capacity of 16 GWh against a 60 GWh target, and peak output reached 60,000 cells per week in September 2024. The company says Northvolt Ett had been intended to be fully operational by 2023 but lagged its targets. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
12. Northvolt reported USD 55 billion of customer orders. Its net loss widened from USD 285 million in 2022 to USD 1.2 billion in 2023. The company attributed its distress to the EV slowdown, Asian price competition, production shortfalls and reliance on Chinese suppliers for some materials and machinery. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
13. On September 23, 2024 Northvolt suspended the Northvolt Ett expansion (an additional 30 GWh) and announced about 1,600 redundancies: 1,000 in Skellefteå, 400 in Västerås and 200 in Stockholm. It refocused on ramping up the first 16 GWh phase of Ett. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
14. On October 8, 2024 the subsidiary Northvolt Ett Expansion AB, which had no direct employees and managed the expansion construction project, filed for bankruptcy at the District Court of Stockholm. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
15. In August 2024 existing shareholders provided a USD 154 million unsecured shareholder bridge facility. In October 2024 an out-of-court "stable platform" plan released about USD 25 million from the Ett debt service reserve account (DSRA) and about USD 25 million of unapplied bridge funds. Advisors were Teneo, Rothschild & Co and Kirkland & Ellis. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
16. On November 21, 2024 Northvolt AB and affiliates filed for Chapter 11 in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas (lead case 24-90577). Total funded debt was approximately USD 5.841 billion, of which about USD 5.66 billion sat at Debtor entities: USD 3.918 billion at Northvolt AB, USD 1.742 billion at Northvolt Ett AB and USD 181 million under a Canadian land loan. Available cash was about USD 30 million, enough for about one week. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
17. The Chapter 11 financing was a USD 100 million senior secured, superpriority multi-draw DIP term loan from Scania, a key customer and Volkswagen subsidiary, funded in tranches of USD 51 million, USD 25 million and USD 24 million. The senior lenders also consented to the use of cash collateral: about USD 110 million released from the Ett DSRA (USD 28 million of it to stay held there) and about USD 35 million from other Northvolt Ett AB accounts. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
18. On February 18, 2025 Northvolt announced a sale and purchase agreement with an unnamed "leading industrial group" for its Systems Industrial division (about 300 employees). [confidence: high] [source: 5]
19. On March 12, 2025 Northvolt AB, Northvolt Ett AB, Northvolt Labs AB, Northvolt Revolt AB and Northvolt Systems AB filed for bankruptcy in Sweden. Northvolt nominated Mikael Kubu as trustee. Northvolt Germany and Northvolt North America did not file. The company said it had been unable to secure the financial conditions to continue despite the Chapter 11 process and liquidity support from lenders and counterparties. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
20. On March 26, 2025 the debtors filed an emergency motion in the Texas court to dismiss certain of the Chapter 11 cases and set procedures to dismiss the rest. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
21. On August 7, 2025 Lyten announced a binding agreement to acquire Northvolt's remaining assets in Sweden and Germany: Northvolt Ett and Ett Expansion, Northvolt Labs, Northvolt Drei, and all remaining Northvolt IP. Lyten had earlier acquired Northvolt's Cuberg facility in California (November 2024) and its BESS product and IP portfolio (July 2025), and had announced the acquisition of Northvolt Dwa in Poland (early July 2025). [confidence: high] [source: 8]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Ramp-up and execution risk at a first-of-a-kind plant: the debtors' own declaration says Northvolt Ett was meant to be fully operational by 2023 but lagged. It reached 16 GWh of capacity against a 60 GWh target, and the company relied on Chinese suppliers for some critical materials and machinery. [source: 1]
- Expansion before stabilization: Northvolt committed to the Ett expansion and to new plants in Germany, Canada, Poland, Portugal and elsewhere while the first phase was still ramping up. Expansion financing hinged on milestones the company could not meet, and abandoning the expansion led to the unwinding of USD 4.5 billion of commitments. [source: 1, 3, 4]
- Market and offtake risk: the company says its capital structure and plan assumed continued EV growth. In 2023–2024, slowing EV sales, cancelled and reduced orders and Asian price competition hit revenue. [source: 1]
- Structural subordination and ring-fencing: Ett's secured lenders controlled the DSRA and the project collateral. Rescue liquidity therefore came from DSRA releases negotiated with secured lenders and from a strategic customer (Scania) as DIP lender. The parent's convertibles and equity (Volkswagen, Goldman Sachs, KfW, pension funds) sat behind them. [source: 1]
- Northvolt's own statement at the Swedish filing cites rising capital costs, geopolitical instability, supply chain disruption, shifting demand, and internal ramp-up challenges, some expected and some unforeseen. [source: 6]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 77 (Refining, petrochemicals and manufacturing (gigafactory) finance): Northvolt Ett is the anchor case for financing a manufacturing plant as a project. Show the stack: EIB, ECA-covered tranches (Euler Hermes, NEXI, BPIAE), KEXIM, commercial uncovered debt, a revolver, a second lien, a DSRA and mandatory hedging. Then show why manufacturing-yield and ramp-up risk differ from infrastructure availability risk. Offtake "orders" (USD 55 billion) were not take-or-pay revenue protection once the plant could not deliver on spec and on time.
- Chapter 88 (The frontier: evaluating new structures): Use Northvolt to build a framework for public-private financing of strategic industrial policy assets. Public lenders and guarantors (EIB, Riksgälden, InvestEU, KfW, Canadian governments) took large exposures, and the largest "green loan" headline was conditional commitments never drawn. Teach readers to ask what is funded and what is committed, which milestones gate the draws, and who can provide rescue liquidity when parent equity runs out.

## Do not state

- That Northvolt "borrowed USD 5 billion" in 2024 or that the green loan was drawn. The commitments (USD 4.5 billion per the court filing) were conditional and never funded.
- That Scania was the buyer of Northvolt Systems Industrial. Northvolt's release does not name the buyer, and that was not verified here.
- That Lyten completed the acquisitions of Ett, Labs or Drei, or the price paid. Only the August 7, 2025 binding agreement is verified.
- That BMW cancelled a EUR 2 billion contract in June 2024. This was widely reported (citing Handelsblatt) but not verified from a primary source here.
- Exact equity ownership percentages for Volkswagen, Goldman Sachs or others at any date, and individual investor write-down amounts (for example, the IMCO and CPP Investments figures).
- Claims about the founder's personal gains, about worker deaths, or about whistleblower or book allegations. These are unverified or contested.
- The EU state aid amount for Northvolt Drei (EUR 902 million is often reported) unless checked against the European Commission's decision. Only the debtors' statement of "over EUR 1.3 billion in convertible instruments and grants from German governmental institutions" is verified.
- The final dismissal date of the Chapter 11 cases. Only the March 26, 2025 motion is verified.
- Any outcome of creditor recoveries in the Swedish bankruptcy.
