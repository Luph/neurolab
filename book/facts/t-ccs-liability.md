# CCS long-term liability transfer rules: EU CCS Directive, UK, US Class VI wells and 45Q

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: EPA proposed approval of Colorado Class VI primacy, signed 16 March 2026; EPA extension of the 2025 GHGRP reporting deadline, published 27 February 2026; Texas Class VI primacy final rule, 14 November 2025; OBBBA §70522 changes to 45Q, 4 July 2025)

Cross-references: facts/northern-lights.md (the Norwegian state-aid model and UK Track-1 cluster financings, including the dates of the UK CCUS contracts); facts/t-us-tax-credits.md (45Q transferability and FEOC rules); facts/t-insurance-market.md (insurance capacity generally).

## Summary

A CO2 storage site has to be watched for decades after injection stops. No private lender will finance a project whose operator carries unlimited, perpetual leakage liability. Each jurisdiction answers the question "who holds the risk after closure, and for how long?" in its own way. The EU CCS Directive (2009/31/EC) gives the answer most clearly. After closure the operator stays responsible until the competent authority accepts a transfer. That needs evidence that the CO2 will be permanently contained, a minimum of 20 years after closure (shorter only if the authority is convinced earlier), a sealed site, and a financial contribution from the operator covering at least 30 years of monitoring. The operator must also post financial security before injecting. The UK copied this through the Storage of Carbon Dioxide (Termination of Licences) Regulations 2011: leakage liabilities pass to the licensing authority when the licence ends, after a minimum period of at least 20 years. In addition, the UK government's support package for transport-and-storage companies (T&SCos) acts as insurer of last resort, paying losses above commercial insurance limits or where insurance is unavailable. The US has no federal transfer. EPA's Class VI rule requires post-injection site care for 50 years by default and financial responsibility instruments, and EPA has said the Safe Drinking Water Act gives it no power to transfer liability. States with Class VI primacy may legislate their own transfers if the provisions are compatible with federal rules; Louisiana's was accepted in 2024. Tax adds a separate layer. 45Q credits can be recaptured if stored CO2 leaks within a three-year lookback window, and since July 2025 utilisation and EOR earn the same rate as dedicated storage.

## Verified facts

### A. European Union (where to find the current rule: Directive 2009/31/EC as amended, consolidated on EUR-Lex; national transposing law; Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 for the injection-capacity target)

1. Closure (Article 17): after closure at the operator's request or under the permit, the operator remains responsible for monitoring, reporting and corrective measures. It also remains liable to surrender EU ETS allowances for any leakage and to take environmental-liability prevention and remediation measures, until responsibility is transferred. It must also seal the site and remove injection facilities. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Transfer conditions (Article 18(1)):
   - (a) all available evidence indicates the stored CO2 will be completely and permanently contained;
   - (b) a minimum period set by the competent authority has passed, which "shall be no shorter than 20 years" unless the authority is convinced that (a) is met earlier;
   - (c) the Article 20 financial obligations are met;
   - (d) the site is sealed and injection facilities removed.

   The operator's report must show that the CO2's actual behaviour matches the model, that there is no detectable leakage, and that the site is moving towards long-term stability. The Commission may give a non-binding opinion on the draft decision within four months. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. After transfer (Article 18(6)–(7)), routine inspections stop and monitoring may be reduced. The authority can recover costs from the former operator only for fault (deficient data, concealment, negligence, wilful deceit, lack of due diligence); otherwise there is no further cost recovery. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. Financial security (Article 19): a would-be operator must prove, as part of the permit application, that adequate financial security or an equivalent can be provided. It must be effective before injection starts, adjusted periodically for leakage risk and cost estimates, and kept in place until transfer. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Financial mechanism (Article 20): before transfer the operator must make a financial contribution available to the competent authority covering "at least the anticipated cost of monitoring for a period of 30 years". The contribution may be used for the authority's post-transfer costs. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. The Net-Zero Industry Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1735) sets a Union target of 50 million tonnes a year of operational CO2 injection capacity by 2030. Licensees of oil and gas production in the EU are to contribute pro rata. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

### B. United Kingdom (where to find the current rule: Energy Act 2008 Part 1 Chapter 3; Storage of Carbon Dioxide (Licensing etc.) Regulations 2010; Storage of Carbon Dioxide (Termination of Licences) Regulations 2011, SI 2011/1483, on legislation.gov.uk; DESNZ "CCUS business models" collection on gov.uk)

7. The 2011 Regulations implement Articles 18 and 20 of the Directive. When the licensing authority approves a post-closure plan, it must set a minimum period between closure and licence termination of "no less than twenty years". It may shorten the period if satisfied that the CO2 will be completely and permanently contained. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
8. The transfer conditions are the four EU conditions plus one more: the abandonment programme must have been carried out under Part 4 of the Petroleum Act 1998. The authority sets the amount and form of the operator's financial contribution "sufficient to cover the expected post-transfer costs" (applying the Article 20 factors), after giving the operator at least 28 days to make representations. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
9. When the licence terminates, the operator's monitoring and related obligations, and "any leakage liabilities" incurred before termination, pass to the authority. "Leakage liabilities" covers any present or future, actual or contingent liabilities arising from leakage from the storage complex, including personal injury, property damage and economic loss. Debts already fixed and payable are excluded. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
10. During operations, the UK T&S business model adds a government support package. Its Supplemental Compensation Agreement (SCA) between the Secretary of State and the T&SCo requires the T&SCo to buy commercial insurance for specified insured risks. Those risks include leakage that forces suspension of injection, and leakage or significant irregularities in the post-closure period. Insured losses include environmental remediation and the cost of buying UK ETS allowances for leakage. The Secretary of State pays "Excess Loss Compensation" above the applicable policy limit (up to scheduled sums insured) and "Unavailability Loss Compensation" where commercial insurance is unavailable. A separate Discontinuation Agreement covers the government's right to discontinue the project and the compensation payable. [confidence: high for structure as at the December 2023 heads of terms; medium for final signed terms, which were not read] [source: 4]
11. The licensing authority: the 2011 Regulations refer to "the authority". For most offshore UK storage this is the North Sea Transition Authority (formerly the Oil and Gas Authority). Licences issued by Scottish Ministers fall under the Scottish 2011 Regulations. [confidence: medium (inferred from the legislation's structure and the NSTA's role in the T&S heads of terms; the transfer of functions was not read directly)] [source: 3, 4]

### C. United States (where to find the current rule: 40 CFR Part 146 Subpart H (Class VI) on ecfr.gov; EPA UIC primacy page; 26 U.S.C. §45Q and Treas. Reg. §§1.45Q-1 to -5; state statutes for liability transfer)

12. Post-injection site care (40 CFR 146.93): after injection ends, the operator must monitor under the approved plan "for at least 50 years" or an approved alternative timeframe. The Director may authorise earlier closure only on substantial evidence that the project no longer poses a risk of endangering underground sources of drinking water (USDWs). [confidence: high] [source: 5]
13. Financial responsibility (40 CFR 146.85): the operator must demonstrate and maintain financial responsibility through trust funds, surety bonds, letters of credit, insurance, self-insurance (financial test and corporate guarantee), escrow or other approved instruments. Cover must extend to corrective action, well plugging, post-injection site care and site closure, and emergency and remedial response. A self-insurer must have net working capital and tangible net worth each at least six times the sum of current plugging, PISC and closure costs. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
14. No federal transfer: in approving Louisiana's primacy (final rule, 89 FR 703, 5 January 2024, effective 5 February 2024), EPA restated its 2010 position that the SDWA does not authorise EPA to transfer liability from one entity to another. EPA said this does not stop primacy states legislating post-closure transfers. Such provisions must not take effect before all federal site-closure requirements are met. They must not release a former permittee from liability for earlier violations, and they must not bar emergency orders where there is imminent and substantial endangerment. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
15. Louisiana's transfer statute (La. R.S. 30:1109) was revised by Act No. 378 (HB 571) during the comment period. The Louisiana Department of Natural Resources (LDNR) agreed in a memorandum-of-agreement addendum not to issue a certificate of completion until the operator submits the federal site-closure report and complies with 40 CFR 146.93. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
16. Class VI primacy (state permitting):
    - Louisiana (effective 5 February 2024);
    - West Virginia (final rule 26 February 2025);
    - Arizona (final rule 15 September 2025);
    - Texas (final rule 14 November 2025, effective 15 December 2025);
    - Colorado: approval proposed 16 March 2026; comment period ended 4 May 2026.

    North Dakota and Wyoming obtained Class VI primacy earlier. [confidence: high for LA, WV, AZ, TX and CO; medium for the ND and WY dates, not read in a primary source here] [source: 7, 8, 9]
17. 45Q recapture (Treas. Reg. §1.45Q-5): if stored qualified carbon oxide leaks to the atmosphere during the recapture period, the leaked tonnes are first netted against that year's stored tonnes. Any excess is recaptured on a last-in-first-out basis against the previous three taxable years only. The recapture period ends at the earlier of three years after the last taxable year in which credit was claimed (or carried forward) and the end of monitoring under subpart RR or ISO 27916. Volumes must be measured under EPA GHGRP subpart RR or CSA/ANSI ISO 27916:2019 (for EOR, certified by an independent engineer or geologist). [confidence: high] [source: 10]
18. OBBBA §70522 (4 July 2025): 45Q now bars specified foreign entities and foreign-influenced entities, for taxable years beginning after enactment. For facilities placed in service after enactment, it removes the lower rate for utilisation and EOR: the base amount is USD 17/t for 2025–2026 (inflation-indexed from 2027) for storage, EOR and utilisation alike, and USD 36/t for direct air capture. The prevailing wage and apprenticeship multiplier of five (USD 85 and USD 180) continues under the existing statute. [confidence: high for the USD 17/36 amounts and parity; medium for the ×5 multiplier, which comes from the unamended statute and was not re-read] [source: 11]
19. Measurement risk: on 16 September 2025 EPA proposed removing most Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program obligations, including subpart RR, the geologic sequestration reporting on which the 45Q regulations rely. The proposal notes the 45Q link. On 27 February 2026 EPA finalised only an extension of the 2025 reporting deadline to 30 October 2026 and said the rest would be addressed later. No final removal of subpart RR was found as of this sheet's date. [confidence: high for the proposal and extension; medium for "no final action"] [source: 12, 13]

## Financing and structure details

- How the regimes allocate liability over time:
  - EU and UK: the operator carries liability through operation and closure, backed by financial security. After at least 20 years (or sooner on evidence), liability moves to the state against a contribution of at least 30 years' monitoring cost, with clawback only for fault.
  - UK during operation: commercial insurance first, then the government (SCA) above policy limits or where cover is unavailable.
  - US: the operator holds liability indefinitely unless a primacy state statute provides a transfer that EPA accepts. The federal floor is 50 years of post-injection care plus financial responsibility instruments.
- Lender relevance: transfer rules set the length of the debt tail that must be reserved for. Financial security instruments (letters of credit, trusts, bonds) are contingent liabilities that use up credit lines. 45Q recapture is a tax indemnity issue between credit buyer and seller, limited by the three-year lookback.
- The UK ETS allowance cost for leakage is an explicitly insured loss in the UK model. Under the EU Directive the operator surrenders allowances for leakage until transfer.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- EPA (2024) explained why there is no federal transfer: the SDWA does not give EPA the power. That pushes transfer policy to the states and creates a patchwork that varies with primacy status. [source: 7]
- The UK designed government insurance-of-last-resort because commercial cover for some storage risks was unavailable at licence award. The heads of terms say this directly. [source: 4]
- The 45Q verification chain depends on an EPA reporting programme that EPA has proposed to remove. That is a regulatory-dependency risk for credit buyers and lenders. [source: 12]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 83 (Hydrogen and derivatives, CCS and sustainable fuels):** Draw the liability curve over a CCS project's life: construction, injection, closure, the 20-year (EU/UK) or 50-year (US default) post-closure period, then transfer. Mark who pays at each stage and with which instrument. Compare a Norwegian or UK state-backed model (northern-lights.md) with a US 45Q-funded project whose operator carries the tail itself. Have students size a financial-security letter of credit against a monitoring cost estimate and discuss what happens to it at debt maturity.

## Do not state

- That the EU or UK transfer happens "automatically after 20 years". Twenty years is a minimum, and the evidence and financial conditions must also be met.
- That US federal law transfers CCS liability to the government after 50 years. It does not. The 50-year figure is the default post-injection site-care period.
- Specific North Dakota, Wyoming or Texas liability-transfer periods (for example "10 years after cessation"). State statutes were not read for this sheet.
- That GHGRP subpart RR has been repealed. It was only proposed (September 2025).
- The sums insured or the government's cap under signed UK SCAs. Only the heads-of-terms structure was read.
- That the NSTA is the authority in every UK case (Scottish territorial waters and onshore differ).
