# LaGuardia Airport Central Terminal B (New York): a terminal PPP financed with tax-exempt bonds

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: project completion in 2022, as described by LaGuardia Gateway Partners and Vantage Group)

## Summary

In 2015 the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey (PANYNJ) selected LaGuardia Gateway Partners (LGP) to design, build, finance, operate and maintain a replacement for LaGuardia's Central Terminal B. Terminal B opened in 1964 for 8 million passengers a year and was handling more than 16 million. LGP reached financial close in June 2016 and signed a lease with PANYNJ running to 2050, about 35 years. Today LGP describes itself as Vantage Group (terminal manager and lead developer) and Meridiam (equity). The project, valued at USD 5.1 billion and described as the largest aviation PPP at the time, built a 35-gate, 1.3 million square foot terminal while the old one stayed in operation. It was completed in 2022, on time and on budget according to LGP. Vantage says the financing relied mainly on tax-exempt Special Facilities Revenue Bonds rated BBB (Fitch) and Baa3 (Moody's), together with USD 200 million of sponsor equity and future passenger facility charges (PFCs). It says the bonds were more than ten times oversubscribed by more than 150 investors. The case shows how US airport terminal PPPs use municipal-market conduit debt rather than bank project loans, and how airline consultation on rates supports the credit.

## Financing and structure details

- Grantor and form: PANYNJ, as landlord, leases the terminal to LGP, a private SPV, for about 35 years (to 2050). LGP takes design, construction, finance, operation and maintenance. [source: 1, 2]
- Debt: predominantly tax-exempt Special Facilities Revenue Bonds, which are municipal conduit bonds payable from terminal revenues, not PANYNJ general obligations. Ratings: BBB (Fitch), Baa3 (Moody's). [source: 2]
- Equity: USD 200 million, contributed equally by the sponsors. [source: 2]
- Other funding: future passenger facility charges. [source: 2]
- Revenue logic: airline rates agreed through consultation, plus concessions income. Precise rate-setting mechanics were not verified.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right (sponsor view): Vantage attributes the strong bond demand to construction de-risking, the consortium's track record and airline buy-in, and LGP reports on-time, on-budget completion while operating the live terminal. [source: 2, 3] These are sponsor statements; no independent evaluation was reviewed.
- Structural point: phasing construction "behind, above, and in front of" the existing terminal (Vantage) is what made a brownfield replacement financeable while revenues continued. [source: 2]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 80 (Rail, urban transit, airports and ports): Use Terminal B to show the US airport-terminal PPP model. The airport authority remains landlord, a private SPV leases one terminal, debt is tax-exempt conduit revenue bonds bought by municipal investors, and airline rate consultation acts as the credit's backbone. Contrast it with whole-airport concessions such as ADR in Rome, where traffic risk and regulatory rebalancing dominated during COVID-19 (facts/covid-toll-roads.md). Ask what passenger-traffic risk the bondholders carry and how PFCs and airline rates mitigate it.
