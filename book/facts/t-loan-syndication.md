# Primary loan syndication: arranger roles, underwritten vs best efforts vs club, market flex, sell-down timelines, and documented flex use 2020–2026

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Norton Rose Fulbright "Cost of Capital: 2026 Outlook", January 2026)

## Summary

In a syndicated project loan, one or more **mandated lead arrangers (MLAs)** win the mandate, structure the deal and sell it to other lenders. The arranger's risk depends on the syndication strategy:

- **Underwritten:** the arrangers commit to the whole amount and carry any unsold portion.
- **Best efforts:** the arrangers try to sell down but are not responsible for unsold amounts. The European Central Bank's definition adds that the arranger often commits to fund a small "final take".
- **Club deal:** pre-marketed to a small group of relationship lenders who agree their allocations before closing.

The LMA publishes separate recommended-form mandate letters for underwritten and for best-efforts transactions, and a confidentiality and no-front-running undertaking for primary syndication. **Market flex** lets the arrangers change pricing, and sometimes structure or terms, during syndication if needed to clear the market. In institutional loan markets, flex makes syndication work like a book-building exercise: an over-subscribed deal "reverse flexes" (tightens) and a weak one flexes wider.

The ECB's 2017 guidance on leveraged transactions treats a deal not syndicated within 90 days of commitment as a "failed syndication" ("hung transaction"). It requires banks to set underwriting limits, but it excludes specialised lending, including project finance, from its scope.

Documented flex use 2020–2026 comes mainly from the US leveraged loan market. LSTA/LCD commentary reports:

- **January 2021:** virtually all flexes were borrower-friendly.
- **October 2021:** lender-friendly spread flexes rose to their 2021 high.
- **April 2023:** a few upsizings and reverse flexes.

In 2022, rising rates left banks holding underwritten acquisition debt. The Bank of England reported that loan syndication and underwriting income had fallen in 2022. The USD 13 billion debt commitment for the Twitter acquisition is a documented example of a large underwritten commitment.

In US project finance, MUFG and Apterra (Apollo) practitioners reported in 2024–2026 that:

- full underwriting is more common for acquisition debt than for construction loans and costs about 25–50 bp on top of other fees;
- banks underwrite large year-end deals alone or in pairs and sell them down in the new year;
- in January 2026 there were "hung" underwritings in data-centre-related loans.

Fee components are covered in facts/t-market-norms.md (item 10). Documentation is covered in facts/t-loan-docs.md.

## Verified facts

### Definitions and documentation

1. **ECB definitions (May 2017):**
   - A "best efforts deal" is one where the arranger agrees to use all efforts to sell down as much of the loan as possible. It often commits to fund a small portion (the "final take") if the loan is fully syndicated, but it is not responsible for any unsold portion.
   - A "club deal" is pre-marketed to a small group of lenders, who agree individual allocations before closing. It may not be governed by a single loan agreement, but participants usually agree very similar terms. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **ECB expectations for syndication (leveraged transactions):**
   - Banks should define their syndication appetite, with dedicated underwriting limits and sub-limits.
   - Pricing should be verified by a function independent of the syndication unit.
   - Every deal with underwriting or syndication risk needs prior approval, with an analysis of market absorption and pricing risk.
   - Banks should stress-test the underwriting pipeline.
   - A transaction not syndicated within 90 days of the commitment date is a "failed syndication" ("hung transaction"), with a dedicated framework for holding strategy, accounting, regulatory classification and capital.

   [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. **Scope carve-out:** the ECB guidance excludes loans classified as specialised lending, which it says comprises project finance, real estate, object finance and commodities finance. Its 90-day and 6.0x leverage expectations therefore do not apply directly to project finance. They are a useful benchmark for internal practice, not a rule for PF. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **LMA primary documents:** the LMA lists separate forms of mandate letter for underwritten and for best-efforts transactions, corporate and real estate term sheets, and a form of confidentiality and no-front-running undertaking designed for primary syndication. The full texts are available to members only. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
5. **Underwritten vs best efforts vs club (market description):** an underwritten deal guarantees the whole commitment; if it is not fully subscribed, the arrangers keep the shortfall and may later sell it at a discount. Underwriting is used to win mandates and earns higher fees. In a best-efforts deal, an undersubscribed loan may not close or may need repricing. The European leveraged market has been mostly underwritten and the US mostly best efforts. [confidence: medium (Wikipedia summary of S&P LCD primer; consistent with the ECB definitions)] [source: 3, 1]
6. **Market flex (market description):** flex language lets arrangers change spread (and sometimes structure) after investor feedback. Launch is at price talk, often a range, and is then tightened (reverse flex) or widened, which makes syndication a book-building exercise. [confidence: medium (secondary source; mechanism consistent with LSTA commentary below)] [source: 3, 4]
7. **The European MLA title:** in Europe the lead role is commonly titled "mandated lead arranger" (MLA). [confidence: medium] [source: 3]

### Documented market flex and underwriting conditions, 2020–2026

8. **US leveraged loans, January 2021:** investor demand outpaced supply, repricings outnumbered new-money deals, and "virtually all price flexes on loans during syndication in January were to the borrower's benefit". [confidence: high] [source: 4]
9. **US leveraged loans, October 2021:** the number of lender-friendly spread flexes rose to its highest level of 2021, as conditions tilted toward lenders after a record M&A-driven quarter. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
10. **US leveraged loans, April 2023:** after the March 2023 banking stress, new-issue activity resumed, with "a few upsizing and reverse-flexes" reflecting investor demand (commentary by Marina Lukatsky, PitchBook LCD). [confidence: high] [source: 6]
11. **UK, 2022:** the Bank of England's December 2022 Financial Stability Report said that banks' income from loan syndication and underwriting had fallen in 2022, driven by persistently weak issuance and underwriting conditions. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
12. **Example of an underwritten acquisition commitment (2022):** on 20 April 2022, Morgan Stanley Senior Funding and other commitment parties signed a debt commitment letter for USD 13 billion to finance Elon Musk's proposed acquisition of Twitter. It comprised:
    - a USD 6.5 billion senior secured term loan;
    - a USD 500 million revolving facility;
    - up to USD 3 billion of secured bridge loans;
    - up to USD 3 billion of unsecured bridge loans.

    There was a separate USD 12.5 billion margin loan commitment. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
13. **US project finance, early 2024 (Ralph Cho, Apterra):**
    - all-in upfront economics of about 200–300 bp;
    - an underwriting fee of about 25–50 bp is charged where the whole deal is underwritten, which is seen more often for acquisition debt than construction financings;
    - participation fees of about 100–200 bp, wider when the loan is sold "wholesale" (further syndication expected) and tighter when sold "retail".

    [confidence: high (as reported practitioner view)] [source: 9]
14. **US project finance, late 2024 (Beth Waters, MUFG, January 2025):**
    - year-end demand meant some very large underwritings were done by one or two banks so that deals closed before year end, with the plan to syndicate in the new year;
    - many project finance banks' budgets are exhausted in the fourth quarter;
    - MUFG counted USD 164 billion of North American project finance lending in 2024, against USD 137 billion in 2023.

    [confidence: high (as reported)] [source: 10]
15. **US project finance and data centres, January 2026 (Ralph Cho):**
    - banks had sold billions of dollars of Oracle-related credit at around SOFR + 250 bp;
    - there were some large "hung" underwritings;
    - there was talk of pricing up to SOFR + 350 bp, and structural enhancements might be needed to complete syndication;
    - deals with CoreWeave as tenant had been sold at SOFR + 325–425 bp.

    [confidence: high (as reported; single practitioner)] [source: 11]

## Financing and structure details

- **Roles in a project syndicate:** MLAs, sometimes bookrunners, a facility agent, a security agent or trustee, an intercreditor agent and an account bank. The MLA group usually also allocates technical, modelling, insurance and documentation responsibilities among themselves. (Role allocation is standard practice; titles and fee splits vary by deal and are not verified here.)
- **Fee mapping (see facts/t-market-norms.md):** a structuring or arrangement fee goes to the MLAs, an underwriting fee is added only if the deal is underwritten, and participation (upfront) fees go to the joining banks, tiered by ticket size [source: 9].
- **Mandate letter and fee letter:** the underwritten and best-efforts mandate letters are separate LMA forms [source: 2]. Flex terms commonly sit in a separate, confidential fee or flex letter. That is common practice and was not verified from an LMA text.
- **Sell-down timing:** the only published numeric marker found is the ECB's 90 days after commitment for leveraged transactions [source: 1]. Practitioner commentary shows timing driven by bank budget cycles, with Q4 capacity tight [source: 10].

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Underwriting risk is market risk.** When rates rose in 2022, syndication and underwriting income fell (Bank of England [source: 7]). Banks that had underwritten fixed or capped economics before the move had to hold or discount positions. This is why flex rights and caps are negotiated hard.
- **Flex runs both ways.** LSTA/LCD data show borrower-friendly reverse flex in hot markets (January 2021, April 2023) and lender-friendly flex as conditions turn (October 2021) [source: 4, 5, 6].
- **Concentrated data-centre exposure (2026).** Cho described hung underwritings and widening price talk in data-centre-related credits, where syndication may need structural enhancements rather than pricing alone [source: 11].

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 38 (pricing project debt):** Separate the fee stack into arrangement, underwriting, participation, commitment and agency fees, and price underwriting as an option the borrower buys from the arranger. Market flex is the arranger's partial hedge of that option. Show how a flex cap on margin and fees shifts market risk back to the arranger, and use the 2021–2023 flex swings as evidence that the option has real value.
- **Ch 55 (running a financing to close):** Choose between club, best-efforts and underwritten execution based on deal size, timing certainty, and how many banks must say yes. Plan the timeline around bank budget cycles: Q4 capacity is scarce, so either close early or pay for a year-end underwrite and sell down in the new year. Use the ECB 90-day "hung" marker to explain why arrangers press for a fast general syndication after signing.

## Do not state

- Specific flex statistics for project finance loans (counts, frequencies, average bp moved). None were found. LSTA/LCD data are for US leveraged loans and are members-only beyond headline commentary.
- The outcome of the Twitter/X debt (for example when or at what price banks sold it in 2025). Reported in the press but not verified here.
- A standard syndication timeline in weeks (for example "general syndication takes 4–8 weeks"). No authoritative source was found.
- That the ECB 90-day test applies to project finance. Specialised lending is excluded from the guidance.
- Exact LMA mandate-letter clause wording (flex, clear market, "successful syndication"). Member-only texts were not checked.
