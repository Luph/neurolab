# Gulf IWPP programs (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman): procurement, low tariffs

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: EWEC award of the Taweelah C IPP, 3 June 2026; Oman 7-Year Statement 2025-2031)

## Summary

Saudi Arabia, Abu Dhabi and Oman each buy most new power and desalinated water through a state-owned single buyer. The buyer runs competitive tenders and signs long-term power and/or water purchase agreements with privately financed project companies.

- **Abu Dhabi:** the procurer is EWEC (Emirates Water and Electricity Company), which succeeded ADWEC in November 2018. The emirate's independent water and power projects (IWPPs) and power-only projects (IPPs) typically pair a 60% local government-linked shareholder, now usually TAQA, with 40% held by international developers.
- **Saudi Arabia:** water is procured by Sharakat, which began as the Water and Electricity Company (WEC) set up in 1423H (2002), became the Saudi Water Partnership Company (SWPC) in 2019 and was rebranded in 2026. It is owned by the Ministry of Finance.
- **Oman:** the single buyer and planner is Nama Power and Water Procurement Company (PWP, formerly OPWP).

The first generation of plants were large gas-fired IWPPs that combined power with thermal desalination. Abu Dhabi examples are Shuweihat S1 (2004) and Umm Al Nar (2007); Saudi Arabia had Shuaibah 3 and Shuqaiq 2; Oman had Barka and Salalah.

From the late 2010s the procurers deliberately split water from power. They tendered stand-alone reverse-osmosis (RO) independent water projects (IWPs) and solar IPPs, and contracted tariffs fell sharply. Saudi official data show water tariffs falling from SAR 4.3/m³ (Shuqaiq 2, 2011) and SAR 3.71/m³ (Shuaibah expansion, 2009) to SAR 1.55-1.99/m³ for RO plants commissioned in 2021-2024. Dubai's Hassyan IWP set a then-record USD 0.365/m³ in 2023. Abu Dhabi's Al Dhafra solar PV tariff was USD 1.35 cents/kWh in 2020. These programs combine standardized contracts, creditworthy single buyers, repeat bidders (ACWA Power, ENGIE, TAQA, Sembcorp, Marubeni and others) and high-leverage bank debt, so they are the reference case for competitive tariff bidding and its pressures.

## Verified facts

### Saudi Arabia

1. Saudi Arabia opened the power and water sector to private participation in 2002, according to ACWA Power. ACWA Power was established in 2004, PIF became its largest shareholder (44%) in 2020, and it listed on Tadawul in 2021. It was renamed "Acwa" in 2026. [confidence: high, company source] [source: 1]
2. Sharakat (formerly SWPC) states the following. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
    - It began as the Water and Electricity Company (WEC), created by Supreme Economic Council decision No. 5/23 dated 23/3/1423H (2002), owned equally by Saudi Electricity Company and the Saline Water Conversion Corporation.
    - Council of Ministers decision No. 494 (5/8/1438H, 2017) made it the principal buyer of desalinated, treated and other water, including co-production.
    - It adopted the SWPC identity in 2019 and the Sharakat identity in 2026.
    - It is owned by the Ministry of Finance, and its stated mission is to procure water at acceptable cost "through transparent competitions".

### UAE (Abu Dhabi and Dubai)

8. EWEC was formed in November 2018, succeeding ADWEC. It is the "sole procurer" of water and power in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi, is part of ADQ, and carries out planning, forecasting, purchasing and system despatch. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
9. TAQA was founded in June 2005 under an Emiri decree. Plants in the Abu Dhabi program have typically been owned 54% by TAQA, 6% by ADWEA and 40% by international shareholders, selling under long-term power and water purchase agreements (PWPAs). [confidence: medium, secondary] [source: 7]
10. EWEC lists the following ownership of existing IWPPs and IPPs. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
    - Shuweihat S1 IWPP: in operation since 2004, 1.5 GW and 455,000 m³/day; TAQA 60%, ENGIE 20%, Sumitomo 20%.
    - Shuweihat S2 IWPP: first tendered July 2007, 1.5 GW and about 455,000 m³/day; TAQA 60%, ENGIE 20%, Marubeni 10%, Osaka Gas 10%.
    - Shuweihat S3 IPP: 1.6 GW; TAQA 60%, Sumitomo 20.4%, KEPCO 19.6%.
    - Umm Al Nar: 1.5 GW and 429,000 m³/day, operating since 2007, the first plant in the Gulf to use GE Frame F turbines; TAQA 60%, ENGIE 20%, JERA 20%.
    - Taweelah A1: TAQA 60%, ENGIE 20%, Total 20%.
    - Taweelah B: TAQA 60%, Marubeni 14%, BTU Power 10%, Powertek 10%, Kyuden 6%.
    - Fujairah F1: hybrid multi-stage flash (MSF) and RO, 591,000 m³/day, described as the first hybrid desalination plant in the Middle East; TAQA 60%, Sembcorp 40%.
    - Fujairah F2: 591,000 m³/day; TAQA 60%, ENGIE 20%, Marubeni 20%.
11. Taweelah RO IWP reached financial close on 19 October 2019. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
    - Cost was AED 3.19 billion. Senior project finance loans of AED 2.71 billion (about 85% of cost, computed) came in conventional and Islamic tranches from Emirates NBD, Natixis (agent), Mizuho, Siemens Bank, Boubyan and Norinchukin.
    - The loans are structured as a "Soft Mini Perm", with expected refinancing within at most three years after commercial operation.
    - Capacity is 909,200 m³/day, 44% larger than the previous largest RO plant (624,000 m³/day). Ownership is 60% ADPower/Mubadala and 40% ACWA Power.
    - EWEC's CEO said it would cut gas use "by decoupling power and water production".
12. Mirfa 2 RO IWP was awarded on 21 February 2023 to TAQA (60%) and ENGIE (40%). [confidence: high] [source: 10]
    - Capacity is 120 MIGD (about 550,000 m³/day).
    - EWEC expected over 90% of its water to come from RO by 2030, with water-production emissions falling from 14.6 Mt CO2 (2020) to 2.1 Mt (2030).
13. Taweelah C IPP was awarded on 3 June 2026 to TAQA (60%) with Aljomaih Energy and Water Company and Sembcorp (40%). [confidence: high] [source: 11]
    - It is a 2.6 GW carbon-capture-ready CCGT with Siemens Energy H-class turbines, under a PPA running to 2050, with COD forecast in 2029.
    - EWEC stated it had "one of the region's lowest capital expenditure rates" and "the lowest levelised cost of electricity".
14. On 28 April 2020 ADPower announced a solar PV tariff of AED 4.97 fils/kWh (USD 1.35 cents/kWh) on a levelized basis for the Al Dhafra project, about 44% below Noor Abu Dhabi's tariff three years earlier. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
15. In August 2023 DEWA selected ACWA Power for the first phase of the Hassyan IWP (818,280 m³/day) at USD 0.365/m³. Arab News reported it as a world record. ACWA's CEO said it was the first IWP "to break the $0.4/m3 barrier" for a seawater RO plant of this scale. [confidence: high, press report] [source: 13]

It operates under the Sector Law and a licence from the Authority for Public Services Regulation (APSR). Its licence (Condition 5) requires an annual 7-Year Statement; the 19th edition covers 2025-2031. [confidence: high] [source: 14, 15]

    - Salalah 1 IWPP (Sembcorp Salalah): 445 MW, COD 25 July 2012.
    - Sur IPP (Phoenix Power): 1,980 MW, 11 December 2014.
    - Ibri IPP: 1,505 MW, 10 May 2019.
    - Sohar III IPP: 1,710 MW, 4 May 2019.
    - Ibri II Solar: 500 MW, 6 August 2021.
    - Manah I and II Solar: 500 MW each, 2025.
18. PWP reports that system gas intensity fell from 374 Sm³/MWh (2005) to 200 Sm³/MWh (2024). It credits efficient CCGTs procured from 2019, renewables and RO replacing MSF. It targets about 30% renewable generation by 2030. [confidence: high] [source: 15]

## Financing and structure details

- **Contract model:** BOO project companies sell all capacity and output to a single state buyer under 20- to 25-year water purchase agreements (Saudi: 20 years in 2009, 25 years since) or PWPAs/PPAs (Taweelah C to 2050). [source: 3, 11]
- **Ownership:** in Abu Dhabi a 60% government-linked stake (TAQA, ADPower/Mubadala) plus 40% international developer is the standard pattern across every plant EWEC lists. TAQA's stake in the operations and maintenance (O&M) company can differ: at Taweelah C TAQA holds 40% and the consortium 60%. [source: 8, 9, 11]
- **Leverage and debt:** Taweelah RO was about 85% debt (AED 2.71bn of AED 3.19bn, computed). It used a soft mini-perm, a short-dated loan whose step-ups and cash sweeps push refinancing, with mixed conventional and Islamic tranches and a club of Gulf, Japanese and European banks. [source: 9]
- **Developers:** recurring bidders include ACWA Power/Acwa, TAQA, ENGIE, Sembcorp, Marubeni, Sumitomo, JERA, KEPCO and Aljomaih. [source: 1, 8, 11]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Right: credible single buyers and standard contracts.** Each program centralizes planning and procurement in one buyer with a statutory or licence mandate (EWEC, Sharakat, Nama PWP), which repeat bidders and lenders can price. [source: 2, 6, 14]
- **Right: unbundling water from power.** EWEC says RO decoupling reduces gas burn and emissions, and PWP credits RO replacing MSF for efficiency gains. The tariff decline in Sharakat's data coincides with the shift from thermal or integrated plants to large stand-alone RO. [source: 3, 9, 10, 15]
- **Pressure: tariff competition.** Record tariffs (Sakaka 2018, Al Dhafra 2020, Hassyan 2023) were announced by procurers and winners as world records. This sheet has not verified any independent analysis of bidder returns or of whether the tariffs are sustainable; treat claims about winner's curse in Gulf tenders as unverified. [source: 5, 12, 13]
- **Legacy assets being restructured.** Shuaibah 3, an IWPP built in the 2000s, was converted to RO under a negotiated restructuring with SWPC (2022). It shows that long-term contracts can be reopened when the buyer's technology preference changes. [source: 1, 4]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 47 (Bidding, acquisitions and infrastructure funds).** Use the Sharakat tariff series and the record PV and RO tariffs to teach bid pricing in repeat, standardized tenders. Cover what drives the tariff (capex per m³, energy per m³, cost of debt, tenor, leverage near 85%, refinancing assumptions such as mini-perms, equity IRR targets) and why winners' tariffs embed aggressive refinancing and operating assumptions. Have students rebuild a levelized tariff and test it against a winner's-curse check; present the winner's curse as a hypothesis to test, not an established finding for the region.
- **Ch 69 (Thermal power).** The Abu Dhabi IWPPs (Shuweihat, Umm Al Nar, Taweelah, Fujairah) are the template for gas-fired IWPP and IPP project finance: 60/40 government-private equity, long-term PWPAs with a single buyer, and fuel supplied by the state. Taweelah C (2026, 2.6 GW, PPA to 2050, carbon-capture ready) shows the thermal IPP repositioned as firming capacity for solar. Oman's gas-intensity fall from 374 to 200 Sm³/MWh quantifies the value of competitive CCGT procurement.
- **Ch 81 (Social infrastructure, water, desalination and waste-to-energy).** Use Sharakat's portfolio for IWP economics: capacity payments plus output charges, 25-year BOO terms, and the move from MSF/MED co-production to RO. Cover the Taweelah RO financing (AED 3.19bn, 85% debt, Islamic and conventional tranches, soft mini-perm) and the Shuaibah 3 conversion as a case of contract restructuring.
