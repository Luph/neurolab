# Sanctions since 2022 and live projects: Nord Stream 2 and Arctic LNG 2

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: TotalEnergies' completed exit from Arctic LNG 2, 27 August 2026; EU 21st sanctions package, 23 July 2026; OFAC SDN list checked 3 October 2026)

## Summary

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 turned sanctions from a background compliance item into a risk that can strand projects. Two large Russian gas projects show the range of outcomes. Nord Stream 2 was a twin offshore gas pipeline from Russia to Germany, owned by Gazprom's Swiss subsidiary Nord Stream 2 AG, and half-financed by loans from five European energy companies. It was physically complete but never certified. Germany halted certification on 22 February 2022. On 23 February 2022 the United States sanctioned Nord Stream 2 AG and its corporate officers. The European lenders wrote off their loans within weeks: Uniper took a EUR 987 million impairment and Wintershall Dea wrote off about EUR 1 billion. One of the two NS2 strings was damaged by the September 2022 explosions. In July 2025 the EU's 18th package banned EU operators from any transaction involving Nord Stream 1 or 2. Arctic LNG 2 is Novatek's 19.8 mtpa Arctic LNG project on the Gydan peninsula, with TotalEnergies, CNPC, CNOOC and a Mitsui/JOGMEC vehicle holding 10% each. It was still under construction when sanctions arrived. The United States designated the project company on 2 November 2023, and it then extended designations to shipping, engineering and trading counterparties in China, India, Hong Kong, Singapore and the UAE. The United Kingdom followed in February 2024. The EU (June 2024) prohibited new investment and supplies for completing Russian LNG projects "such as Arctic LNG 2" and banned EU transshipment of Russian LNG. The foreign shareholders reportedly declared force majeure in December 2023. TotalEnergies completed the transfer of its 10% to a Novatek subsidiary on 27 August 2026, keeping only a sanctions-contingent right to be repaid about USD 1.3 billion of shareholder loans. Both entities remain on the US SDN list as of 3 October 2026.

## Verified facts

### Nord Stream 2

1. Nord Stream 2 AG, the developer and owner, is a Gazprom subsidiary headquartered in Zug, Switzerland. The project was two parallel offshore lines from Russia to Germany with a planned capacity of 55 bcm a year. [confidence: medium, Wikipedia summary of company and press sources] [source: 6]
2. In 2017 Uniper, Wintershall (later Wintershall Dea), OMV, Engie and Royal Dutch Shell signed financing agreements with Nord Stream 2 AG to provide long-term loans covering 50% of the projected cost of EUR 9.5 billion (reported as up to EUR 950 million each), with Gazprom funding the rest. [confidence: medium, Wikipedia citing a Shell press release that could not be loaded directly] [source: 6]
3. The US Congress imposed sanctions on firms helping build the pipeline in the FY2020 National Defense Authorization Act (December 2019). On 19 May 2021 the Biden administration waived sanctions on Nord Stream 2 AG and its CEO Matthias Warnig while sanctioning certain Russian vessels and entities. [confidence: medium] [source: 6]
4. Germany announced on 22 February 2022 that it would halt certification of the pipeline. On 23 February 2022 President Biden directed his administration to impose sanctions on Nord Stream 2 AG and its corporate officers as part of the "initial tranche" of sanctions in response to Russia's actions in Ukraine. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. As of 3 October 2026, NORD STREAM 2 AG is on OFAC's Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list under the RUSSIA-EO14024 and PEESA-EO14039 programs, with a secondary sanctions risk notation. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
6. On 9 March 2022 Uniper reported an impairment of EUR 987 million on its Nord Stream 2 financing: EUR 695 million of loan principal plus EUR 292 million of accrued interest. It also expected to forgo about EUR 100 million of interest a year. [confidence: medium-high, trade press reporting company statements] [source: 7]
7. Wintershall Dea announced on 3 March 2022 that it would write off its Nord Stream 2 financing of about EUR 1 billion. [confidence: medium-high] [source: 8]
8. Nord Stream 2 never delivered gas. On 26 September 2022 explosions damaged both Nord Stream 1 lines and one of the two Nord Stream 2 lines (line A). Line B was not damaged. [confidence: medium] [source: 6]
9. On 18 July 2025 the EU's 18th sanctions package introduced a transaction ban for Nord Stream 1 and 2, meaning that no EU operator may engage in any transaction regarding the Nord Stream pipelines. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### Arctic LNG 2

10. Arctic LNG 2 is a Novatek project on the Gydan peninsula in Russia's Yamal-Nenets region. It has three trains with a combined planned capacity of 19.8 million tonnes a year (about 6.6 mtpa each) and a projected total cost of approximately USD 21 billion. Shareholders (as of 2021) were Novatek 60%, TotalEnergies 10%, CNPC 10%, CNOOC 10% and Japan Arctic LNG (Mitsui and JOGMEC) 10%. [confidence: medium-high for capacity and shareholdings; medium for cost] [source: 9]
11. On 2 November 2023 OFAC added LIMITED LIABILITY COMPANY ARCTIC LNG 2 (Novy Urengoy) to the SDN list under Executive Order 14024. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
12. As of 3 October 2026, the project company remains on the SDN list (UKRAINE-EO13662 and RUSSIA-EO14024, secondary sanctions risk). More than a dozen further SDN entries are marked "Linked To" Arctic LNG 2, including shipping, engineering and service companies registered in China, Hong Kong, India, Singapore and the UAE. Among them are Penglai Jutal Offshore Engineering Heavy Industries and Zhoushan Wison Offshore and Marine. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
13. On 22 February 2024 the UK sanctioned Arctic LNG 2 and its director, and six directors of PJSC Novatek, its majority owner. On 26 September 2024 the UK sanctioned 5 vessels and 2 entities involved in shipping Russian LNG, including from Arctic LNG 2. The UK said the project had been forced to "slash production" since its designation. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
14. EU Council Regulation (EU) 2024/1745 of 24 June 2024 (the 14th package, amending Regulation 833/2014) did three relevant things. It inserted Article 3r, which prohibits reloading services in EU territory for transshipment of Russian LNG, along with related technical assistance, brokering and financing, subject to a security-of-supply derogation. It referred to Council Decision (CFSP) 2024/1744, which prohibits new investment and the supply of goods, technology and services to complete LNG projects "such as Arctic LNG 2 and Murmansk LNG", without affecting purchases of LNG from Russian terminals or financial services to those projects. And it added a vessel list (Annex XLII) that includes the floating storage units Saam FSU and Koryak FSU. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
15. The EU's 18th package (18 July 2025) delisted 3 LNG tankers after firm commitments that they would no longer carry Russian energy for the Yamal and Arctic 2 projects. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
16. Reports in December 2023 said the foreign shareholders had declared force majeure under their project agreements and suspended participation. Novatek sent force majeure notices to prospective buyers on 21 December 2023. [confidence: medium, Russian press (Kommersant) via secondary outlets] [source: 9]
17. Reuters reported on 2 April 2024 that Novatek had suspended production at Arctic LNG 2 because of sanctions and a shortage of tankers. Systematic deliveries to China were reported from August 2025. [confidence: medium] [source: 9]
18. On 27 August 2026 TotalEnergies said it had completed the transfer of its 10% interest in Arctic LNG 2 to NordLine, a Novatek subsidiary, and was no longer a shareholder. It retains its right to be reimbursed by Arctic LNG 2 for its share of shareholder loans, around USD 1.3 billion, which "might be implemented in the future subject to applicable sanctions." [confidence: high] [source: 11]

### Wider EU framework (latest)

19. The EU's 21st package (23 July 2026) introduced a notification obligation for sales of LNG tankers to third countries. It clarified that the LNG terminal services ban introduced in the 20th package also covers third-country operators controlled by Russian companies, and it brought the number of listed shadow-fleet vessels to 673 (41 added to 632). [confidence: high] [source: 12]

## Financing and structure details

- Nord Stream 2: corporate-style sponsor financing rather than non-recourse bank debt. Gazprom was the sole shareholder, and five Western energy companies provided long-term loans for 50% of the EUR 9.5 billion budget. When sanctions and non-certification made repayment unlikely, the lenders impaired loan principal and accrued interest (Uniper: EUR 695 million plus EUR 292 million). [source: 6, 7, 8]
- Arctic LNG 2: Novatek-led equity with 10% stakes for each of four foreign partners, which also funded the project through shareholder loans (TotalEnergies' share is about USD 1.3 billion). The exit structure was a transfer of shares to a Novatek subsidiary, with the right to repayment of the shareholder loan kept but made contingent on sanctions. Details of any third-party project-finance debt (for example Chinese or Japanese lender participation) were not verified. [source: 9, 11]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The US President's statement (23 February 2022) framed the NS2 sanctions as part of an initial tranche that had been coordinated with Germany in advance. This political risk was flagged for years (2017 CAATSA debates, 2019 NDAA sanctions, the 2021 waiver), yet the lenders stayed exposed until the asset could not be used. [source: 1, 6]
- UK government (26 September 2024): sanctions on the project company and then on its shipping chain forced Arctic LNG 2 to cut production. The lesson is that a liquefaction plant without sanctioned-compliant ice-class shipping and buyers is a stranded asset even when complete. [source: 5]
- TotalEnergies (27 August 2026): the exit terms show the typical end state for a Western minority partner in a sanctioned project. It gives up its equity, keeps a creditor claim that cannot be enforced while sanctions last, and its recovery depends on future policy. [source: 11]
- The spread of US designations to Chinese, Indian and UAE counterparties "linked to" Arctic LNG 2 (SDN list) shows how secondary sanctions risk reaches non-Western participants and service providers, not only Western sponsors. [source: 2]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 60 (Political risk and its protection): use these two projects to teach sanctions as a distinct political risk that is not reliably covered by classic PRI and that host-government support cannot cure, because the binding measures come from the sponsors' and lenders' home states. Walk through the mechanics: designation (SDN, UK and EU lists), secondary sanctions risk, sectoral prohibitions on investment and services (EU Regulation 833/2014 Article 3r and the CFSP 2024/1744 ban on completing LNG projects), and transaction bans on a named asset (EU 18th package on Nord Stream). Then show the contractual consequences: force majeure and illegality declarations by shareholders (Arctic LNG 2), lender write-offs (Uniper and Wintershall Dea on NS2), and exit at nominal or contingent value (TotalEnergies). Close with drafting and diligence lessons: sanctions representations and covenants, illegality and mandatory prepayment clauses, change-of-law triggers in offtake and EPC contracts, multi-jurisdiction screening of counterparties and shipping, and recognising when a political signal (the 2017 to 2021 NS2 sanctions history) should change the credit view before the event.

## Do not state

- Do not state that Nord Stream 2 AG "filed for bankruptcy" or was declared bankrupt. Reports in March 2022 conflicted, the company denied filing, and its later Swiss insolvency or moratorium proceedings were not verified here.
- Do not attribute the September 2022 explosions to any state or person. Investigations and prosecutions have been contested. Swedish and Danish investigations closed in February 2024 without naming perpetrators, and German proceedings against individuals were reported, but none of this was verified from primary sources here.
- Do not give exact OMV, Engie or Shell NS2 impairment amounts. They were not verified.
- Do not state TotalEnergies' 2022 impairment amount for Arctic LNG 2. It is often cited (about USD 3.7 billion to 4.1 billion) but was not verified here.
- Do not describe the Arctic LNG 2 third-party project-finance package (Chinese, Japanese or Russian bank loans, JBIC or ECA participation) or the status of other foreign shareholders' stakes (CNPC, CNOOC, Japan Arctic LNG) after 2023. These were not verified.
- Do not say the EU has banned all imports of Russian LNG or give a date for a full ban (for example 1 January 2027) without checking the current EU regulations. The 19th and 20th packages and REPowerEU phase-out legislation were not verified here.
- Do not state current Arctic LNG 2 production or export volumes. These figures are fragmentary and change quickly.
- Do not state that the US or EU sanctions described here have been eased or lifted. As of 3 October 2026 both entities remain on the SDN list. Re-check the OFAC, UK and EU lists before publication.
