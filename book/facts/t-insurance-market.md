# Insurance market cycle 2019-2026 for energy and infrastructure

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest data: Marsh Global Insurance Market Index (GIMI) Q2 2026, published July 2026; Lloyd's half-year results 2026; Gallagher Re 1st View July 2026 as reported by Artemis, 13 July 2026)

## Summary

Commercial insurance pricing moves in cycles, and project finance budgets, insurance covenants and lender requirements must be written to survive both phases. According to Marsh's GIMI, global commercial rates rose in every quarter from late 2017 to the end of 2023 (25 consecutive quarters to Q4 2023). The increase peaked at +22% in Q4 2020, the largest since the index began in 2012. Rates were flat in Q2 2024, then fell from Q3 2024. By Q2 2026 they had fallen for eight consecutive quarters, with global property down 12% while casualty still rose (+2% globally, +7% in the US).

Lloyd's illustrates the underwriting side. It lost about GBP 1.0 billion in 2018 (combined ratio 104.5%) and launched the "Decile 10" remediation of its worst-performing business. It lost GBP 887 million in 2020, a year of COVID-19 losses (combined ratio 110.3%). It then earned GBP 9.6-10.7 billion a year in 2023-2025, with combined ratios of 84-88%. Capital flowed back in on these profits.

Energy markets show the soft phase at its extreme. WTW's April 2026 Energy Market Review calls the market "deeply soft". Upstream theoretical capacity is a record of about USD 10.7 billion, and rate reductions continue even though 2025 downstream losses (USD 6.8 billion gross) were roughly double the estimated global downstream premium (USD 3-3.5 billion). WTW warns that a capacity withdrawal, severe loss or change in reinsurance appetite could turn the cycle quickly.

The drivers are capacity (insurer and reinsurer capital, including insurance-linked securities (ILS) of USD 135 billion at end-2025 per Gallagher Re), loss experience, reinsurance pricing, interest rates and investment returns, and competition from new entrants such as managing general agents (MGAs) and broker facilities.

## Verified facts

### Global commercial pricing: Marsh GIMI (market: global commercial insurance placed by Marsh, composite renewal rate change)

1. Marsh Q4 2020: global commercial insurance pricing rose 22%, the largest increase since the index was launched in 2012. Q3 2020 was +20% and Q2 2020 +19%. Property was up 20%, financial and professional lines 47% and casualty 7%. Q4 2020 was the ninth consecutive quarter in which composite pricing rose in all regions. Regional rises were UK 44%, Pacific 35%, US 17% and Latin America 9%. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Marsh Q4 2023: rates rose 2%, the 25th consecutive quarterly increase, "the longest run of increases since the inception of the index in 2012". [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. Marsh Q2 2024: composite rates were flat, the first quarter without an increase since Q3 2017. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. Marsh Q3 2024: rates declined 1%, "the first decrease in the composite rate in seven years". The UK (-5%) and Pacific (-6%) fell most, while the US and Latin America rose 3%. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
5. Subsequent global composite changes per Marsh: Q4 2024 -2%; Q1 2025 -3%; Q2 2025 -4%; Q3 2025 -4%; Q4 2025 -4%; Q1 2026 -5%; Q2 2026 -6%. Q2 2026 was the eighth consecutive quarterly decrease. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]
6. Global property rates per Marsh: Q4 2024 -3%; Q1 2025 -6%; Q2 2025 -7%; Q3 2025 -8%; Q4 2025 -9%; Q1 2026 -9%; Q2 2026 -12%. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]
7. Global casualty rates kept rising through the soft phase, driven by US casualty and especially excess and umbrella layers, which Marsh links to claim severity and large jury verdicts. Global casualty: +4% in Q4 2024, Q1 2025 and Q2 2025, +3% Q3 2025, +4% Q4 2025, +3% Q1 2026 and +2% Q2 2026. US casualty was +8% in Q1 2025, +9% in Q2 2025, +8% in Q3 2025, +9% in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, and +7% in Q2 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]
8. Marsh Q2 2026 regional composites: UK -8%; Europe -6%; Asia -5%; Canada -7%; Pacific -13%; Latin America and Caribbean -9%; India, Middle East and Africa (IMEA) -16%. The US decreased; the exact US figure was not captured. Marsh attributes the Q2 2026 declines to available capacity and favourable reinsurance conditions. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

### Lloyd's of London results (market: Lloyd's, all classes; figures in GBP)

9. 2018: loss of about GBP 1.0 billion, combined ratio 104.5%. 2019: profit of GBP 2.5 billion, combined ratio 102.1%, with investment income (GBP 3.5 billion) the driver of profit. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
10. In 2018 Lloyd's asked syndicates to identify their poorest-performing business, called "Decile 10", and required credible plans to fix or exit it. Around GBP 3 billion of the poorest-performing business was exited. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
11. 2020: loss before tax of GBP 887 million, combined ratio 110.3% (97.0% excluding COVID-19 losses of GBP 3.4 billion). Risk-adjusted rate change was +10.8%, the 13th consecutive quarter of positive rate movement. Remediation removed 12% of unprofitable business in 2020 compared with 2019. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
12. 2023: profit before tax GBP 10.7 billion; combined ratio 84.0%; gross written premium GBP 52.1 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 14]
13. 2024: profit before tax GBP 9.6 billion; combined ratio 86.9%; gross written premium GBP 55.5 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
14. 2025: profit before tax GBP 10.6 billion; combined ratio 87.6%; gross written premium GBP 57.9 billion; return on capital 22.0%. [confidence: high] [source: 16]
15. H1 2026: gross written premium GBP 34.7 billion; combined ratio 90.8%; profit before tax GBP 3.5 billion. Lloyd's cites a "softening rate environment and rising geopolitical tensions". [confidence: high] [source: 17]
16. WTW reproduces Lloyd's data for the Lloyd's casualty sector. Combined ratios were above 100% every year from 2015 to 2021 (peak 110.3% in 2020), then 93.7% in 2022, 93.6% in 2023 and 95.5% in 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 19]

### Energy insurance (market: global energy upstream, downstream and liability, per WTW Energy Market Review)

17. WTW, April 2025: the softening cycle continued, with capacity "at an all-time high". Reductions for offshore operating rates widened from 5-10% at the close of 2024 to 5-12.5% in some cases. Upstream construction (especially subsea) moved the other way, with rate increases and restricted capacity after losses. Upstream premium was estimated at about USD 2.5-2.6 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 19]
18. WTW, April 2026: the energy market "remains deeply soft". Upstream theoretical capacity reached about USD 10.7 billion, "the highest level ever recorded", with a further USD 200-250 million expected from expansions and new entrants. Broker facilities add non-clashing capacity. [confidence: high] [source: 18]
19. WTW, April 2026, upstream pricing: [confidence: high] [source: 18]
    - Early-2026 renewals with incumbent leaders typically saw about 15% reductions, with larger cuts where leaders are tested.
    - Core risks with clean loss histories and substantial premium saw 10-20%, and more than 40% in exceptional cases.
    - Contractors saw 7.5-15% and midstream 7.5-10%.
    - Construction all risks (CAR) was "largely flat".
    - Long-term agreements carried 5-7.5% upfront discounts.
    - The Willis Energy Loss Database recorded USD 842 million of upstream losses across 58 events in 2025; the largest was USD 130 million.
20. WTW, April 2026, downstream: 2025 losses were USD 6.8 billion gross and USD 4-4.5 billion net. Refining produced the three largest losses, in a segment generating only about USD 2-2.5 billion of premium a year. Global downstream premium is about USD 3-3.5 billion. Rates still fell: about 10% standard, 15% commonplace for well-performing accounts, 15-20% for US downstream, and 25% for well-engineered business with substantial premium. Cat-exposed or loss-hit risks saw smaller cuts. [confidence: high] [source: 18]
21. WTW, April 2026, regional pricing: [confidence: high] [source: 18]
    - Latin America: 30-40% reductions on loss-free, well-managed renewals.
    - Middle East upstream: 40-45% in more competitive tenders, with pricing "increasingly fragmented". Dubai is described as among the most competitive quoting centres.
    - China: capacity of about USD 400-450 million for upstream and USD 5-6 billion for downstream.
    - North America: double-digit property reductions "becoming the benchmark".
22. WTW, April 2026, outlook: the market is "built on fragile foundations". A capacity withdrawal, severe loss event or shift in treaty (reinsurance) appetite could trigger "a rapid turn in the cycle". WTW also expects the Middle East conflict to generate significant wider-market losses, with oil prices up more than 25% since it began. It advises buyers to update business interruption values for higher commodity prices. [confidence: high] [source: 18]

### Reinsurance and capital (Gallagher Re, as reported by Artemis)

23. Gallagher Re's 1st View for the April 2026 renewals reported total non-life ILS assets of USD 135 billion at year-end 2025, up 19% year on year. Gallagher Re's CEO said this capital influx was reinforcing underwriter conviction even as the cycle softened. [confidence: medium; secondary report of broker publication] [source: 20]
24. Gallagher Re's 1st View for the July 2026 renewals reported non-marine retrocession catastrophe rates down by up to 20% for loss-free accounts. This continued the buyer-friendly January and April 2026 renewals, with the sharpest reductions in remote layers. [confidence: medium; secondary report] [source: 21]

## Financing and structure details (parties, tranches, amounts, tenors, guarantees, where verified)

Not a transaction sheet. The cycle reaches the project in four ways; this is practice description, to be taught as principle.
- Base-case insurance premiums in the financial model and the operating budget.
- The availability and price of construction all risks (CAR) and delay in start-up (DSU) cover at financial close.
- Lender insurance covenants. Facility agreements typically allow covenant relief or substitution where required cover is "not available on commercially reasonable terms". This is market practice, not verified to a specific source here.
- Reserve sizing for deductibles and uninsured risks.

## What went wrong or right, and why (well-established analysis only, attributed to named sources)

- **Hard phase (about 2018-2023).** Lloyd's itself attributes its 2018-2019 turnaround to remediation of unprofitable business ("Decile 10") and rate increases, with 2020 results distorted by COVID-19 losses. [source: 11, 12, 13]
- **Soft phase (from Q3 2024).** Marsh attributes the declines to capacity, insurer competition and favourable reinsurance conditions, with US casualty the exception because of claim severity and jury awards. [source: 5-10] WTW says capacity oversupply is "the central driver" in energy and that loss severity has been "insufficient to counteract" it, so pricing is "increasingly disconnected from underlying risk". [source: 18]

## Teaching angles by chapter (for each listed chapter: the transferable lesson and the angle to take, 2–4 sentences)

- **Chapter 27 (Insurance):** Teach the cycle as capital chasing returns. Losses and thin margins (Lloyd's 2018-2020) bring remediation and rising rates. Profits (2023-2025) attract capital and ILS, and capacity then pushes rates down even as losses rise (WTW downstream, 2025). For the Case P insurance programme, show how a sponsor locking in multi-year cover or long-term agreements in a soft market trades upside for stability. Explain why lenders insist on "commercially reasonable availability" language and periodic insurance-adviser reviews so the project survives the next hard market. Use the Marsh index as the reader's tool for updating premium assumptions, and stress that construction and US-casualty lines can harden while property softens.
