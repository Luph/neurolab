# Sustainable finance II: EU Taxonomy gas-power criteria, IPCC gas emission factor, TCFD-to-ISSB handover, NGFS scenarios, Climate Bonds Standard, CBAM and ETS2 status (2026)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: CBAM certificate price for Q2 2026 published 6 July 2026; CBAM default values corrected July/August 2026; Regulation (EU) 2026/667 published 18 March 2026; NGFS cover note, November 2025; Climate Bonds Standard v4.3, August 2025; General Court judgment in T-625/22, 10 September 2025)

## Summary

This sheet complements t-sustainable-finance.md, which covers ICMA/LMA principles, the core EU Taxonomy, the EU Green Bond Standard, ISSB adoption and Article 6.

**EU Taxonomy and gas power.** The Complementary Climate Delegated Act (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/1214 of 9 March 2022, applying from 1 January 2023) made gas-fired power a "transitional" activity (Section 4.29). A plant qualifies either on life-cycle emissions below 100 gCO2e/kWh, or, if its construction permit is granted by 31 December 2030, on all of the following:
- direct emissions below 270 gCO2e/kWh, or an average of no more than 550 kgCO2e/kW a year over 20 years;
- replacement of a solid or liquid fossil-fuel plant;
- capacity no more than 15% above the plant replaced;
- a switch to renewable or low-carbon gases by 31 December 2035;
- a lifetime emissions cut of at least 55%;
- independent verification.
The EU General Court upheld the act against Austria's challenge on 10 September 2025 (T-625/22).

**Emission factor.** The IPCC 2006 Guidelines' default CO2 factor for natural gas in energy industries is 56,100 kg CO2/TJ (range 54,300–58,300) on a net calorific basis, about 202 gCO2 per kWh of fuel. An unabated CCGT at 55% efficiency therefore emits about 367 gCO2/kWh, above the 270 g threshold.

**Disclosure and scenarios.** The TCFD published its final status report in October 2023 and disbanded. The FSB asked the IFRS Foundation to monitor climate disclosures from 2024, as IFRS S1 and S2 "fully incorporate" the TCFD recommendations. The NGFS's current long-term scenarios are Phase V (5 November 2024): seven scenarios in four categories. In November 2025 the NGFS flagged an academic debate over the physical-damage function behind Phase V, and it plans Phase VI in 2026.

**Certification.** The Climate Bonds Standard is at version 4.3 (August 2025). It certifies use-of-proceeds instruments, assets, entities and sustainability-linked debt.

**Carbon pricing.** CBAM entered its definitive regime on 1 January 2026. It covers cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen, with a 50-tonne importer threshold introduced by the simplification Regulation (EU) 2025/2083. The 2026 certificate price is set quarterly (EUR 75.36 for Q1 and EUR 75.28 for Q2 2026), and certificate sales on the central platform start in February 2027. ETS2 (buildings, road transport and small industry) is to be fully operational from 2028, one year later than originally legislated.

## Verified facts

### A. EU Taxonomy: gas-fired power (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/1214, Annex I Section 4.29; EUR-Lex; also 4.30 CHP and 4.31 heat/cool)

1. Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/1214 was adopted on 9 March 2022. It amends the Climate Delegated Act (EU) 2021/2139 and the Disclosures Delegated Act (EU) 2021/2178. It applies from 1 January 2023. Gas-fired generation meeting its criteria is a transitional activity under Article 10(2) of the Taxonomy Regulation. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Substantial contribution test, route (a): life-cycle GHG emissions below 100 gCO2e/kWh. These are calculated with project-specific data under Recommendation 2013/179/EU, ISO 14067:2018 or ISO 14064-1:2018, and verified by an independent third party. Captured CO2 must be transported and stored in line with Sections 5.11–5.12. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. Route (b), for facilities whose construction permit is granted by 31 December 2030; all conditions must be met:
   - (i) direct emissions below 270 gCO2e/kWh, or annual direct emissions averaging no more than 550 kgCO2e per kW of capacity over 20 years;
   - (ii) the power cannot be generated from renewables, based on a published comparative assessment subject to stakeholder consultation;
   - (iii) it replaces an existing high-emitting plant using solid or liquid fossil fuels;
   - (iv) new capacity is no more than 15% above the replaced plant;
   - (v) it is designed for renewable and/or low-carbon gases, with a full switch by 31 December 2035 under a board-approved, verifiable plan;
   - (vi) it cuts lifetime GHG emissions by at least 55%;
   - (vii) where located in a member state using coal, that state has committed to a coal phase-out.
   An independent verifier reports annually to the Commission on emissions levels and trajectory. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. On 10 September 2025 the General Court dismissed Austria's action for annulment of Delegated Regulation 2022/1214 (Case T-625/22 Austria v Commission; press release No 113/25). It held that the Commission did not exceed its delegated powers. It also held that nuclear and fossil gas activities can, under conditions, contribute substantially to climate change mitigation, describing the act as a "gradual approach" that cuts emissions in stages while allowing for security of supply. An appeal on points of law to the Court of Justice was possible. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

### B. IPCC default emission factor for natural gas (2006 IPCC Guidelines, Vol. 2, Ch. 2, Table 2.2; the IPCC Task Force on National Greenhouse Gas Inventories publishes the guidelines)

5. Default stationary-combustion factors for natural gas in the energy industries, in kg per TJ on a net calorific value basis:
   - CO2: 56,100 (lower 54,300; upper 58,300);
   - CH4: 1 (0.3–3);
   - N2O: 0.1 (0.03–0.3).
   [confidence: high] [source: 3]
6. Conversions (author's calculation from fact 5):
   - 56,100 kg/TJ × 0.0036 TJ/MWh = 202 kg CO2 per MWh of fuel (net calorific basis);
   - at 55% net electrical efficiency, about 367 gCO2/kWh of electricity; at 60%, about 337 gCO2/kWh.
   Both are above the Taxonomy's 270 g direct-emissions threshold, so an unabated CCGT can qualify under route (b)(i) only through the 550 kgCO2e/kW-year average (limiting running hours), or with blending or CCS. [confidence: high as arithmetic] [computed; source: 1, 3]

### C. TCFD to ISSB (FSB; IFRS Foundation)

7. The TCFD published its final recommendations in June 2017. Its 2023 status report (October 2023) was its sixth and final report. It reported:
   - 19 jurisdictions, accounting for close to 60% of 2022 global GDP, with final or proposed TCFD-aligned disclosure requirements;
   - over 4,800 supporting organisations (4,850+ in its chart).
   It also stated that the FSB had said the ISSB standards represent the culmination of the Task Force's work and that the TCFD would be disbanded on release of that report. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
8. In July 2023 the IFRS Foundation announced that the FSB had asked it to take over monitoring of companies' climate-related disclosures from the TCFD from 2024, because IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 "fully incorporate" the TCFD recommendations. The FSB called the standards "the culmination of the work of the TCFD". For ISSB adoption status, see t-sustainable-finance.md. [confidence: high] [source: 5]

### D. NGFS scenarios (ngfs.net; data via the IIASA NGFS Scenario Explorer)

9. Phase V of the NGFS long-term scenarios was published on 5 November 2024. It uses SSP version 3.0 and country commitments as of March 2024, and introduces a new damage function for chronic physical risk. NGFS said GDP losses by 2050 could be two to four times greater than previously estimated. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
10. Phase V contains seven scenarios in four categories:
    - Orderly: Low Demand, Net Zero 2050, Below 2°C;
    - Disorderly: Delayed Transition;
    - Hot house world: Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), Current Policies;
    - Too little, too late: Fragmented World.
    [confidence: high] [source: 7]
11. In its November 2025 cover note, the NGFS warned users of academic critiques ("Matters Arising" at Nature) of Kotz et al. (2024), the paper underlying Phase V physical-risk estimates. It said outputs not using those estimates, and its short-term scenarios, are unaffected. It said the next vintage (Phase VI) will be released in 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

### E. Climate Bonds Standard (climatebonds.net)

12. The Climate Bonds Standard and Certification Scheme was launched in 2012 by the Climate Bonds Initiative. It is overseen by an independent Climate Bonds Standard Board of investor representatives, and uses approved verifiers. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
13. Version 4.3 (updated August 2025) covers certification of:
    - (A) use-of-proceeds debt instruments;
    - (B) assets;
    - (C) non-financial entities with climate mitigation performance targets (CMPTs) aligned with Climate Bonds Sector Criteria now or by 2030, with transition-plan and disclosure requirements, and general-purpose debt they issue;
    - (D) sustainability-linked debt of non-financial entities.
    Debt certification applies per instrument, not per framework. Version 4.0 (2023) first introduced entity certification; v4.1 and v4.2 followed in 2024. [confidence: high for v4.3 content; medium for earlier version dates] [source: 9, 10]

### F. CBAM (Regulation (EU) 2023/956 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2025/2083; EC DG TAXUD CBAM pages)

14. CBAM ran a transitional (reporting-only) phase from 2023 to 2025 and has applied in its definitive regime since 1 January 2026. It covers imports of cement, iron and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
15. Under the definitive regime:
    - importers bringing in more than a single mass-based threshold of 50 tonnes of CBAM goods a year must be authorised CBAM declarants;
    - declarants declare embedded emissions and surrender certificates each year;
    - carbon prices paid in the country of production can be deducted;
    - certificate prices follow EU ETS auction prices: quarterly averages in 2026, weekly from 2027;
    - all certificates will be bought on the common central platform from February 2027.
    The simplification amendment is Regulation (EU) 2025/2083. A proposal (COM(2025) 989) would extend scope to downstream goods and add anti-circumvention measures. [confidence: high] [source: 11, 12, 13]
16. Published 2026 CBAM certificate prices: Q1 2026 EUR 75.36 (published 7 April 2026); Q2 2026 EUR 75.28 (published 6 July 2026). The Q3 price was due on 5 October 2026. Default values were set in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621 and corrected by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1740 (July 2026). [confidence: high] [source: 12, 13]

### G. ETS2 (Directive 2003/87/EC, Chapter IVa, as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/959; EC DG CLIMA ETS2 page)

17. ETS2 covers fuel combustion in buildings, road transport and additional sectors. It regulates upstream fuel suppliers, not end users. Monitoring and reporting began in 2025, with verified reporting from 2026. The Commission states that ETS2 "will become fully operational in 2028". From 2028, regulated entities surrender allowances by 31 May of the following year, and 30% more 2028 allowances will be auctioned for liquidity. The Social Climate Fund is expected to mobilise at least EUR 86.7 billion in 2026–2032. [confidence: high] [source: 14]
18. The one-year delay from the originally legislated 2027 start was agreed during the negotiation of the 2040 amendment to the European Climate Law:
    - the Council's general approach (5 November 2025) added a provision applying the Article 30k postponement mechanism;
    - Parliament supported it (13 November 2025).
    The resulting Regulation (EU) 2026/667 (11 March 2026; OJ 18 March 2026) sets a binding 2040 target of a 90% net GHG reduction from 1990. ICAP reports the ETS2 market stability reserve price trigger at EUR 45 (2020 prices). [confidence: medium for the legislative mechanics (secondary sources); high for the 90% target] [source: 15, 16, 17]

## Financing and structure details

- **Gas plants and green labels**: a gas plant can be Taxonomy-aligned (and so EuGB-eligible) only through the 4.29 conditions, including verified decarbonisation by 2035. Most labelled green instruments under ICMA principles exclude unabated gas. Taxonomy alignment and a label are separate questions (see t-sustainable-finance.md).
- **CBAM in project revenue**: projects exporting cement, steel, aluminium, fertiliser, hydrogen or electricity into the EU face a cost linked to the ETS price, less any carbon price paid at origin. Offtake contracts need a CBAM cost-allocation clause.
- **Scenario analysis**: lenders' climate stress tests commonly use NGFS scenarios. The Phase V damage-function caveat means physical-risk outputs should be presented with ranges.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The CCDA was contested as greenwashing (Austria). The General Court found the Commission's "gradual approach" within its discretion [2].
- The NGFS Phase V physical-damage estimates drew academic critique, showing the model risk in climate scenarios used for capital and lending decisions [8].
- The CBAM simplification concentrates obligations on larger importers through the 50-tonne threshold [11].

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 84 (Sustainable finance and climate risk)**:
  - Use the 4.29 test with the IPCC factor to show students why an unabated CCGT (about 367 g/kWh) fails the 270 g test and must rely on the 550 kg/kW-year average, hydrogen readiness or CCS.
  - Present the TCFD-to-ISSB handover as the institutional end of voluntary climate disclosure.
  - Use the NGFS seven-scenario grid for project stress tests, with the damage-function caveat.
  - Contrast the Climate Bonds certification (per instrument, sector criteria) with ICMA self-labelling.
  - Treat CBAM and ETS2 as carbon-price exposures in project cash flows, quoting dated prices (EUR 75.36 for Q1 2026) and the 2028 ETS2 start.
