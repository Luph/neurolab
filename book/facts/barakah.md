# Barakah Nuclear Energy Plant (UAE): government-led financing

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Unit 4 commercial operation, September 2024; latest financing event: July 2023 refinancing of the KEXIM loans by Emirati banks)

## Summary

Barakah is a four-unit nuclear plant in the Al Dhafra region of Abu Dhabi, built to the Korean APR-1400 design by a consortium led by Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), which the Emirates Nuclear Energy Corporation (ENEC) selected on 27 December 2009 under a prime contract of approximately USD 20 billion covering construction, commissioning and first fuel loads, a high percentage of it fixed-price. Construction started in July 2012. All four units are now in commercial operation (Unit 1 in April 2021, Unit 2 in March 2022, Unit 3 in February 2023 and Unit 4 in September 2024) and produce about 40 TWh a year, around a quarter of the UAE's electricity. Financing reached financial close on 20 October 2016 through Barakah One PJSC (now ENEC Commercial), owned 82 percent by ENEC and 18 percent by KEPCO. Of about USD 24.4 billion, about USD 19.6 billion was direct loans: up to USD 16.2 billion from the Abu Dhabi Department of Finance, USD 2.5 billion from the Export-Import Bank of Korea (KEXIM) and USD 250 million from commercial banks. The remaining USD 4.7 billion was equity from ENEC and KEPCO. A 60-year power purchase agreement with the Abu Dhabi Water and Electricity Company (ADWEC, now EWEC) gives the revenue framework. Barakah is the clearest example of a sovereign-financed nuclear new build. The host government's own treasury supplied about two-thirds of the money, the vendor-country ECA added a minority tranche, and the vendor took equity in both the financing company and the operator, so the vendor had money at stake in the plant's operation.

## Verified facts

1. On 27 December 2009 ENEC announced it had selected a team led by KEPCO, after reviewing three bids, to design, build and help operate four civil nuclear units of about 1,400 MW each. ENEC stated the contract value for construction, commissioning and fuel loads for the four units was approximately USD 20 billion, "with a high percentage of the contract being offered under a fixed-price arrangement." ENEC and KEPCO also agreed key terms for Korean investors to take an equity interest. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. The KEPCO-led team included Samsung, Hyundai, Doosan, Westinghouse, Korea Hydro and Nuclear Power (KHNP), Korea Power Engineering, Korea Nuclear Fuel and Korea Plant Service and Engineering. World Nuclear Association gives the contract value as approximately USD 20.4 billion. [confidence: medium; from World Nuclear Association, not checked against ENEC's member list] [source: 3]
3. Reactor design: APR-1400 pressurized water reactors. ENEC describes them as 1,400 MW units; World Nuclear Association lists net capacity of about 1,337–1,345 MWe each (about 5,348 MWe total). [confidence: high for the design; state capacity as "approximately 1,400 MW gross" or cite the WNA net figure] [source: 2, 3, 4]
4. Construction began in July 2012 after FANR issued the construction license and the Environment Agency – Abu Dhabi issued a no-objection certificate. Unit 2 construction began in 2013, and first safety concrete was poured for Units 3 and 4 in 2014 and 2015. With Unit 4 under way in 2015, the site had four identical reactors under construction at once. Construction was completed for Unit 1 in March 2018, Unit 2 in July 2020, Unit 3 in November 2021 and Unit 4 in December 2023. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
5. Construction start dates by unit (World Nuclear Association): Unit 1, 19 July 2012; Unit 2, 15 April 2013; Unit 3, 24 September 2014; Unit 4, 30 July 2015. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
6. FANR issued operating licenses for Unit 1 in February 2020, Unit 2 in March 2021, Unit 3 in June 2022 and Unit 4 in November 2023, each authorizing operation for 60 years. Fuel load was completed in Unit 1 in March 2020, Unit 2 in March 2021, Unit 3 in June 2022 and Unit 4 in December 2023. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
7. Commercial operation: Unit 1, announced 6 April 2021; Unit 2, announced 23 March 2022; Unit 3, February 2023; Unit 4, September 2024. [confidence: high for Units 1 and 2 (ENEC); medium-high for Units 3 and 4 (World Nuclear Association)] [source: 3, 7, 8]
8. The original plan was for the four units to be completed by 2020 (ENEC, 2016). World Nuclear Association reports that Unit 1 start-up slipped from 2017 to 2018 and was then delayed further after an operational readiness review. FANR identified more than 400 adverse findings on technical, organizational and management issues, and one underlying problem was developing English competence as a bridging language between Arabic- and Korean-speaking staff. [confidence: high for the 2020 target; medium for the delay details, which come from World Nuclear Association] [source: 3, 9]
9. On 20 October 2016 ENEC and KEPCO signed a joint venture agreement creating Barakah One PJSC to represent the project's commercial and financial interests, owned 82 percent by ENEC and 18 percent by KEPCO. The agreement also entitled KEPCO to an 18 percent stake in Nawah Energy Company, the operator of Units 1–4, which was established in 2016 (ENEC gives May 2016 in the 2016 release and June 2016 on its current website). Barakah One is now branded ENEC Commercial and Nawah ENEC Operations. [confidence: high] [source: 9, 10]
10. On 20 October 2016 ENEC and KEPCO announced financial close. Barakah One would manage total financing estimated at USD 24.4 billion, made up of direct loans of about USD 19.6 billion and USD 4.7 billion of equity commitments from ENEC and KEPCO. The direct loans were: KEXIM, USD 2.5 billion; commercial banks, USD 250 million in total (National Bank of Abu Dhabi, First Gulf Bank, HSBC and Standard Chartered); and the Abu Dhabi Department of Finance, up to USD 16.2 billion. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
11. ENEC stated that the loans cover the overnight cost of the prime contract, interest during construction and the cost of initial nuclear fuel, plus allowances for possible inflationary increases in commodity prices such as construction materials during construction. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
12. ENEC Commercial's current website gives slightly different figures: overall financing requirements of about USD 24.5 billion, USD 19 billion as a direct loan from the Government of Abu Dhabi, USD 2.5 billion from KEXIM, USD 250 million from five local and international commercial banks, and USD 4.7 billion of equity shared between ENEC and KEPCO. [confidence: high that the website says this; the difference from the 2016 release is not explained] [source: 10]
13. In July 2023 ENEC Commercial refinanced the full outstanding balance of the KEXIM loan facilities through a competitive process, selecting Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank and First Abu Dhabi Bank. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
14. A power purchase agreement between Barakah One and ADWEC for electricity from the plant for 60 years was announced in a release dated 1 November 2016; ENEC Commercial's website dates the PPA to 2016 and says it sets the price-per-kilowatt structure. ADWEC has since become Emirates Water and Electricity Company (EWEC). ENEC Commercial also holds the generation license from the Department of Energy. [confidence: high for the counterparty, term and year; the tariff level has not been disclosed] [source: 10, 12]
15. KHNP agreed to supply about 400 qualified staff to support Nawah until 2030 under an operating support services agreement of approximately USD 880 million signed in July 2017, according to World Nuclear Association. [confidence: medium] [source: 3]
16. The UAE signed a bilateral nuclear cooperation (123) agreement with the United States in January 2009, under which it committed to forgo domestic enrichment and reprocessing. [confidence: medium; World Nuclear Association] [source: 3]
17. ENEC states the four units produce about 40 TWh a year, around 25 percent of the UAE's electricity needs, and prevent up to 22.4 million tonnes of carbon emissions a year. [confidence: high as ENEC's claim] [source: 4]
18. On 28 July 2023 an electrical fire occurred in a contained location in the Unit 4 turbine generator building, before fuel load. ENEC reported no injuries and no environmental harm, and the three operating units continued at full power. [confidence: high] [source: 13]

## Financing and structure details

- **Owner/developer:** ENEC (its ownership and legal status were not verified from a primary source for this sheet; the Abu Dhabi government's backing is described in ENEC's 2016 financial-close release). [source: 11]
- **Financing company:** Barakah One PJSC (ENEC Commercial), 82 percent ENEC, 18 percent KEPCO; it receives and manages the funds, holds the PPA and generation license, and contracts ENEC Operations (Nawah) to operate the plant. [source: 10, 11]
- **Operator:** Nawah (ENEC Operations), 82 percent ENEC, 18 percent KEPCO. [source: 9, 10]
- **Sources at financial close (October 2016, USD 24.4 billion):** Abu Dhabi Department of Finance direct loan up to USD 16.2 billion (about 66 percent of the total); KEXIM direct loan USD 2.5 billion (about 10 percent); commercial bank loans USD 250 million (about 1 percent); ENEC and KEPCO equity USD 4.7 billion (about 19 percent). The figures as published sum to about USD 23.65 billion against the stated USD 24.4 billion total; ENEC rounds the debt total to "about USD 19.6 billion." [source: 11; percentages computed from the published figures]
- **Use of debt:** overnight prime-contract cost, interest during construction, first fuel, and an escalation allowance. [source: 11]
- **Contract:** prime contract with KEPCO, approximately USD 20 billion, largely fixed price. [source: 1, 2]
- **Revenue:** 60-year PPA with ADWEC/EWEC (the single buyer of Abu Dhabi's power), tariff undisclosed. [source: 10, 12]
- **Refinancing:** KEXIM loans fully refinanced in July 2023 by ADCB and FAB. [source: 10]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Right, on financing design (ENEC):** ENEC presented the October 2016 structure as a framework for "commercially viable and cost-competitive" supply. The structure placed most construction-period funding risk on the Abu Dhabi government, which is both the main lender and the ultimate owner, and gave the vendor (KEPCO) an 18 percent equity stake in both the financing company and the operator. Both the 2009 announcement and the 2016 joint venture statements present that equity as an incentive for on-time delivery and long-term operating performance. [source: 1, 9, 11]
- **Right, on delivery (World Nuclear Association; ENEC):** four identical units were built in series on one site, which ENEC describes as the world's largest nuclear construction site at the time, and all four are now operating. [source: 3, 5]
- **What slipped (World Nuclear Association; ENEC):** the 2016 plan was completion of all four units by 2020; the last unit entered service in 2024. The Unit 1 delay was driven mainly by operational readiness and regulatory findings rather than construction, including the challenge of building a new nuclear workforce and regulator from scratch. [source: 3, 9]
- **Limits of what is public:** ENEC has not published a final outturn cost or the PPA tariff, so cost performance against the USD 20 billion contract cannot be assessed from verified sources.

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 74 (Nuclear power):** set Barakah against Vogtle as the two poles of nuclear finance. At Barakah a sovereign with a deep balance sheet took almost all construction risk through a direct government loan, with the vendor-country ECA as a minority lender and commercial banks almost symbolic at about 1 percent. At Vogtle the risk fell on regulated utilities, their customers and federal guarantees. Teach the vendor-equity mechanism (KEPCO's 18 percent in both the financing company and the operator) as an alternative to relying only on a fixed-price EPC and parent guarantees, and the 60-year offtake with a single state buyer as what makes the long debt possible. The 2023 KEXIM refinancing by local banks after operations began shows how construction-period ECA debt is taken out once the risk profile changes, a pattern readers will see again in other sectors.

## Do not state

- Do not state a final project cost or overrun for Barakah; ENEC has not published one, and figures in circulation (for example, about USD 24–25 billion "total cost") are financing envelopes, not outturn costs.
- Do not state the PPA tariff or its structure beyond "price per kilowatt" and the 60-year term.
- Do not state the interest rates, tenors or guarantees on the Abu Dhabi government loan or the KEXIM loan; not disclosed in the sources reviewed.
- Do not reconcile the USD 16.2 billion (2016 release) and USD 19 billion (current ENEC Commercial site) government loan figures, or the four versus five commercial banks, without a primary explanation; give the 2016 release figures and note the later website figures.
- Do not assert that the prime contract price was fully fixed or that KEPCO absorbed overruns; ENEC said only that a "high percentage" was fixed price.
- Do not state the exact commercial operation days for Units 3 and 4; only the months (February 2023, September 2024) are verified, from World Nuclear Association.
- Do not repeat claims that Barakah was built "on time and on budget" without qualification; the 2020 completion target was missed by about four years for the last unit.
