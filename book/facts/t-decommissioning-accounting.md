# Decommissioning provisions and asset retirement obligations: IAS 37, IAS 16, IFRIC 1, IFRIC 5 and IAS 12 (IFRS) versus ASC 410-20 (US GAAP), including discount-rate and unwinding rules

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: IASB redeliberations of Exposure Draft IASB/ED/2024/8 "Provisions—Targeted Improvements", tentative discount-rate decisions of 24 September 2025 and further meeting on 23 September 2026; IFRS text as in the EU consolidated Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1803)

Cross-references: facts/t-decommissioning-liabilities.md (regulatory financial-security regimes for offshore wind, oil and gas, mining and nuclear, which are the cash side of what this sheet accounts for); facts/t-upstream-decom-security.md; facts/t-nuclear-decom-waste.md; facts/t-accounting.md and facts/t-accounting-2.md (other project and sponsor accounting standards; neither covers decommissioning).

## Summary

Both IFRS and US GAAP require a project company to recognise the present value of its obligation to dismantle and restore a site as a liability. The same amount is added to the cost of the asset and depreciated over its life. The liability then grows each year as the discount unwinds.

The two frameworks differ in several ways that matter for project models and covenant definitions:

| Point | IFRS (IAS 37) | US GAAP (ASC 410-20) |
|---|---|---|
| Obligations in scope | Legal and constructive | Legal only |
| Discount rate | Current pre-tax rate reflecting the time value of money and risks specific to the liability; remeasured every reporting date | Credit-adjusted risk-free rate fixed for each layer when the layer is recognised; not remeasured for rate changes |
| Measurement basis | Best estimate | Fair value via an expected present value technique |
| Revisions | Changes in cash flows or discount rate adjust the asset cost under IFRIC 1, subject to a floor at the asset's carrying amount | Upward revisions use today's rate; downward revisions use the historical rate of the layer |
| Unwinding | A borrowing (finance) cost; capitalisation under IAS 23 is prohibited | "Accretion expense", an operating expense, not interest |

The IASB is finalising targeted amendments to IAS 37. In September 2025 it tentatively confirmed that provisions will be discounted at a risk-free rate with no adjustment for the entity's own non-performance (credit) risk, which ends a long-standing diversity in practice. Once effective, this will widen the measurement gap with US GAAP's credit-adjusted rate. Since May 2021 (effective 2023), IAS 12 has also required deferred tax to be recognised on the decommissioning asset and liability, closing an exemption some entities had used.

## Verified facts

### IFRS (jurisdiction: IFRS as issued by the IASB; EU-endorsed text checked; as of 3 October 2026)

1. Recognition (IAS 37.14): a provision is recognised when there is a present obligation (legal or constructive) from a past event, an outflow is probable, and a reliable estimate can be made. A constructive obligation (IAS 37.10) arises from an established pattern of past practice, published policies or a sufficiently specific current statement that creates a valid expectation in other parties. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Measurement (IAS 37.36-37): the provision is the best estimate of the expenditure required to settle the obligation at the reporting date, meaning the amount the entity would rationally pay to settle it or transfer it to a third party at that date. It is measured before tax (IAS 37.41). Risks and uncertainties are taken into account, without double-counting risk adjustments (IAS 37.42-43). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. Discounting (IAS 37.45-47): where the time value of money is material, the provision is the present value of the expected expenditures. The discount rate shall be "a pre-tax rate (or rates) that reflect(s) current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the liability", and shall not reflect risks for which the cash flow estimates have already been adjusted. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. Future events (IAS 37.48-50): expected cost reductions from increased experience with existing technology may be reflected. Wholly new technology may not be anticipated without sufficient objective evidence. New legislation is reflected only when it is virtually certain to be enacted. Gains from expected asset disposals are excluded (IAS 37.51). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Remeasurement and unwinding (IAS 37.59-60): provisions are reviewed at each reporting date and adjusted to the current best estimate. Where discounting is used, the carrying amount increases each period for the passage of time, and "this increase is recognised as borrowing cost". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. Asset side (IAS 16.16(c)): the cost of property, plant and equipment includes "the initial estimate of the costs of dismantling and removing the item and restoring the site", whether the obligation is incurred on acquisition or through use other than to produce inventories. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
7. IFRIC 1, "Changes in Existing Decommissioning, Restoration and Similar Liabilities" (effective for periods beginning on or after 1 September 2004):
   - under the cost model, changes in estimated timing or amount of outflows, and changes in the current market-based discount rate, are added to or deducted from the asset's cost in the current period;
   - a deduction cannot exceed the asset's carrying amount, and any excess goes to profit or loss;
   - an addition is a possible impairment indicator under IAS 36;
   - the adjusted depreciable amount is depreciated prospectively over the remaining useful life;
   - once the asset reaches the end of its useful life, all later changes go to profit or loss;
   - under the revaluation model, changes go through the revaluation surplus or deficit;
   - the periodic unwinding "shall be recognised in profit or loss as a finance cost", and capitalisation under IAS 23 is not permitted (IFRIC 1.8). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
8. IFRIC 5, "Rights to Interests arising from Decommissioning, Restoration and Environmental Rehabilitation Funds" (effective 1 January 2006):
   - a contributor recognises its decommissioning obligation as a liability and its interest in a fund separately, unless it is not liable even if the fund fails to pay;
   - where it does not control, jointly control or significantly influence the fund, the right to reimbursement is measured at the lower of the obligation recognised and its share of the fair value of the fund's net assets;
   - obligations to make additional contributions are contingent liabilities under IAS 37. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. IAS 12 amendment "Deferred Tax related to Assets and Liabilities arising from a Single Transaction" (issued May 2021, effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2023). The initial recognition exemption no longer applies where equal taxable and deductible temporary differences arise (IAS 12.22A). On transition, deferred tax had to be recognised for decommissioning, restoration and similar liabilities and the corresponding asset amounts (IAS 12.98L). [confidence: high for the text; high for the effective date, which is the IASB's published date] [source: 1]
10. Disclosure (IAS 37.84-85), for each class of provision:
    - a reconciliation of opening and closing balances, showing additions, amounts used, unused amounts reversed, and "the increase during the period in the discounted amount arising from the passage of time and the effect of any change in the discount rate";
    - the nature and expected timing of outflows;
    - uncertainties and major assumptions;
    - expected reimbursements. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
11. Pending amendments: on 12 November 2024 the IASB published Exposure Draft IASB/ED/2024/8 "Provisions—Targeted Improvements" (comments by 12 March 2025). The draft noted that some entities used risk-free rates while others used rates including non-performance risk, which are higher and produce smaller provisions. It proposed requiring a risk-free rate (excluding non-performance risk) without specifying how to determine it. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
12. On 24 September 2025 the IASB (12 of 12 members) tentatively decided:
    - to retain the requirement to discount provisions at a rate reflecting the time value of money, "represented by a risk-free rate", with no adjustment for non-performance risk;
    - to add no application guidance on determining the risk-free rate;
    - to clarify that the best estimate of expenditure is not reduced for non-performance risk;
    - to add no requirement on real versus nominal rates;
    - to require disclosure of the discount rate(s) used and the approach used to determine them.

    Redeliberations continued through 2026 (meetings including 18 May and 23 September 2026, on other aspects). As of the September 2026 IASB Update, no final amendments or effective date had been announced. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4, 5]

### US GAAP (jurisdiction: FASB Accounting Standards Codification; as of 3 October 2026)

13. Scope (ASC 410-20-15-2): legal obligations associated with the retirement of a tangible long-lived asset that result from its acquisition, construction, development or normal operation. Constructive obligations that are not legal obligations are outside the scope. Unlike IAS 37, US GAAP has no separate constructive-obligation concept for AROs. [confidence: high for the scope; the contrast is this sheet's synthesis] [source: 6, 7]
14. Recognition (ASC 410-20-25-4 to 25-8 and 25-11): an ARO is recognised at fair value in the period incurred if a reasonable estimate of fair value can be made, and otherwise when one can be made. The obligation to perform retirement activities is unconditional even if timing or method is uncertain (a "conditional ARO"), and such uncertainty is reflected in measurement rather than delaying recognition. Recognition cannot be delayed merely because management does not intend to retire the asset in the foreseeable future. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
15. Initial measurement (ASC 410-20-30-1; ASC 410-20-55-13): an expected present value technique will usually be the only appropriate technique. Expected cash flows are estimated as a third party would incur them, including overhead, equipment charges, a profit margin and a market-risk premium. They are discounted at a credit-adjusted risk-free rate: a risk-free rate (typically US Treasury yields matched to settlement timing) adjusted for the credit standing of the obligated entity, taking guarantees and other credit enhancements into account. Salvage value does not reduce the ARO. [confidence: high (Big Four guidance quoting the Codification)] [source: 8]
16. Asset retirement cost (ARC): on initial recognition the same amount is capitalised into the carrying amount of the related long-lived asset. It is then expensed systematically and rationally over the asset's useful life (ASC 410-20-35-2). [confidence: high] [source: 6, 9]
17. Accretion (ASC 410-20-35-4 and 35-5): changes due to the passage of time are measured first, by applying an interest method to the beginning liability at the credit-adjusted risk-free rate in effect when each layer was recognised. Accretion is "not ... interest cost", is classified as an operating expense, and is not eligible for interest capitalisation. [confidence: high] [source: 9, 6]
18. Revisions (ASC 410-20-35-8): upward revisions in undiscounted cash flows are discounted at the current credit-adjusted risk-free rate, creating a new layer. Downward revisions are discounted at the rate used when the original liability (or the relevant layer) was recognised, or at a weighted-average rate if the layer cannot be identified. Changes in market rates alone do not cause remeasurement. [confidence: high] [source: 9, 6]
19. Disclosure (ASC 410-20-50-1): a general description of AROs and the related assets; the fair value of assets legally restricted to settle AROs; a reconciliation of the beginning and ending ARO when significant changes occur, showing liabilities incurred, settled, accretion and revisions; and, if fair value cannot be reasonably estimated, that fact and the reasons. [confidence: high] [source: 6]

## Worked numbers (illustrative, computed for this sheet)

Assume decommissioning costs USD 50 million in today's prices, cost inflation of 2.5% a year, and settlement in 25 years. The expected outflow is USD 92.7 million.

- IFRS at a 4.0% nominal risk-free rate: present value USD 34.8 million. Year-1 unwinding (finance cost) is USD 1.39 million. If the rate falls to 3.5% at the next reporting date, the provision rises by about USD 4.5 million (holding the 25 years constant for simplicity), and the asset cost rises by the same amount under IFRIC 1.
- US GAAP at a 6.0% credit-adjusted rate: initial ARO USD 21.6 million. Year-1 accretion (operating expense) is USD 1.30 million. A fall in market rates causes no remeasurement.

The same physical obligation is booked about 38% lower under the higher credit-adjusted rate. The IFRS figure would also be lower if an entity currently includes own-credit risk, which the pending amendments would stop.

## Financing and structure details (why this matters to lenders and sponsors)

- Covenants: unwinding is a finance cost under IFRS but an operating expense under US GAAP. EBITDA, interest cover and leverage ratios therefore treat the same obligation differently. Loan agreements should define whether accretion or unwinding is excluded and whether decommissioning provisions count as "financial indebtedness" (usually not).
- Discount-rate volatility: IFRS remeasures at current rates. A falling-rate environment raises provisions and asset cost, which raises depreciation and can trigger impairment testing. Under US GAAP, layers lock in historical rates.
- Reserve accounts: cash in a decommissioning reserve account is not netted against the provision. Interests in separate decommissioning funds follow IFRIC 5 (IFRS) or are disclosed as legally restricted assets (ASC 410-20-50-1).
- Deferred tax: since 2023 the IFRS single-transaction amendment produces deferred tax balances on both sides.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The IASB's own exposure draft identifies diversity in practice on discount rates (risk-free versus rates including non-performance risk) as the problem it is fixing, because it impairs comparability (source 2). The tentative 2025 decision resolves this in favour of risk-free rates (source 3). The US approach deliberately embeds the obligor's credit standing in the rate (source 8), so a weaker sponsor reports a smaller liability for the same work. Practitioners have long criticised this as counter-intuitive, while the FASB's fair-value rationale is that a third party would demand it.

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 66 (accounting for projects and sponsors): teach the paired entry (provision and asset), then the three movements in a period: unwinding, rate change and estimate change. Use the worked numbers to show how IFRS current-rate remeasurement and the US layered credit-adjusted model diverge, and how the IASB's risk-free decision will move IFRS further from US GAAP. Flag covenant and EBITDA consequences, and the IAS 12 deferred-tax requirement since 2023.
- Ch 65 (end of life, decommissioning and handback): connect the accounting provision to the cash plan. The provision is a discounted estimate, while lenders size decommissioning reserves to the undiscounted, inflated cost at the expected date, often with a contingency. Explain why a provision can rise sharply late in life (shorter discounting, cost escalation, falling rates), and that under IFRIC 1 changes after the asset's useful life go straight to profit or loss. Cross-refer to regulatory security regimes (facts/t-decommissioning-liabilities.md).

## Do not state

- Do not state a final effective date or final wording for the IAS 37 targeted improvements. They had not been issued as of 3 October 2026.
- Do not quote ASC paragraph text verbatim beyond the short phrases given above. The FASB Codification site blocked automated access (HTTP 403), and the Codification paragraphs were verified through Deloitte DART and PwC Viewpoint summaries.
- Do not give "typical" discount rates used by named companies or "market norm" ranges for decommissioning discount rates. None were verified; rates follow government bond yields and, for US GAAP, the entity's credit spread.
- Do not state how IFRS 18 (effective 1 January 2027) classifies provision unwinding in the new income-statement categories. It was not verified here.
- Do not state the FAS 143 or FIN 47 issue dates as verified (the 2001 and 2005 dates above are from general knowledge).
- Do not say IFRS forbids own-credit adjustments today. Current IAS 37.47 is silent, and practice is diverse.
