# Winter Storm Uri (Texas, Feb 2021): hedge shape and volume risk

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: post-event filings and market-rule changes through mid-2021, plus the November 2021 FERC-NERC final report)

## Summary

The FERC-NERC staff report describes a severe cold-weather event from 8 to 20 February 2021. During that event, 1,045 generating units in Texas and the South Central United States had 4,124 outages, derates or failures to start. About 58% of those units were gas-fired and 27% were wind. Freezing (44.2%) and fuel supply problems (31.4%) caused 75% of these events. In ERCOT, the grid operator for most of Texas, an average of 34,000 MW of generation was unavailable from 7 a.m. on 15 February to 1 p.m. on 17 February. Firm load shed peaked at 20,000 MW at 7 p.m. on 15 February. Early that morning, system frequency stayed below the generator-trip threshold for more than four minutes, close to a total blackout. More than 4.5 million people in Texas lost power, and Texas health officials later confirmed 246 storm-related deaths. The Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT) ordered on 15 February that prices reflect the value of lost load while load was being shed. That set real-time energy prices at the USD 9,000/MWh cap from late 15 February until early 19 February. The independent market monitor later said that keeping prices at the cap for about two days after load shed ended added about USD 16 billion in costs. For project finance, Uri is the reference case for hedge shape and volume risk. A generator that had sold a fixed hourly volume, through a fixed-shape hedge, a proxy-style swap or a retail load obligation, but could not produce had to buy the shortfall at or near USD 9,000/MWh. Wind projects (Clearway reported about USD 50 million of settlements) and gas-fired generators that lost fuel (Vistra, NRG) all took large losses. A wholesale cooperative, Brazos Electric, filed for Chapter 11 on 1 March 2021.

## Verified facts

1. The FERC-NERC staff report (November 2021) covers a severe cold-weather event in Texas and the South Central US between 8 and 20 February 2021. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. During the event, 1,045 bulk electric system generating units, with 192,818 MW of combined nameplate capacity, had 4,124 outages, derates or failures to start. By fuel, the units were 58% natural gas, 27% wind, 6% coal, 2% solar, 7% other and under 1% nuclear. Freezing issues (44.2%) and fuel issues (31.4%) caused 75% of the outages, derates and failures to start. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. ERCOT averaged 34,000 MW of unavailable generation (against expected capacity) from 7 a.m. on 15 February to 1 p.m. on 17 February 2021. The report says this was nearly half of ERCOT's all-time winter peak demand. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. ERCOT ordered its first 1,000 MW of load shed at 1:20 a.m. on 15 February. Operators at one point had nine minutes to prevent about 17,000 MW of generation from tripping on underfrequency relays. Frequency stayed below the trip level for more than four minutes before recovering. Firm load shed lasted nearly three consecutive days and peaked at 20,000 MW at 7 p.m. on 15 February. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Combined manual firm load shed across the region was 23,418 MW, the largest controlled firm load shed event in US history. Unplanned generation outages (65,622 MW) were more than four times those of the previous largest event, in 2011 (14,702 MW). More than 4.5 million people in Texas lost power. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. The Texas Department of State Health Services confirmed 246 winter-storm-related deaths (as of 27 October 2021, report dated 30 December 2021). The FERC-NERC report cites "at least 210" deaths. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
7. On 15 February 2021 the PUCT issued an emergency order requiring ERCOT energy prices to reflect the value of lost load while load was being involuntarily curtailed under Energy Emergency Alert 3 (EEA3, ERCOT's highest emergency level). This effectively set energy prices at USD 9,000/MWh. The PUCT also temporarily suspended the Low System-Wide Offer Cap. On 16 February it largely reaffirmed the order but removed its retroactive application. Prices stayed at USD 9,000/MWh from late 15 February to early 19 February, when ERCOT declared EEA3 over. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
8. On 4 March 2021 Potomac Economics, ERCOT's independent market monitor, said ERCOT had kept prices too high for nearly two days after widespread outages ended late on 17 February. It put the result at about USD 16 billion of extra costs. It recommended that the PUCT direct ERCOT to remove the pricing interventions. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
9. Vistra appealed the PUCT's orders of 15 and 16 February to the Third Court of Appeals in Austin in March 2021. It also disputed a PUCT verbal order of 5 March 2021 and raised settlement disputes with ERCOT. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
10. Vistra reported a net loss of USD 2.040 billion for the first quarter of 2021. It attributed the loss to Uri: it had to buy power at or near the price cap because its gas plants produced less, owing to gas deliverability problems, and fuel costs were extreme. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
11. NRG reported a pre-tax loss of USD 967 million from Uri in the first quarter of 2021. The total included:
    - uplift and ancillary service charges of USD 395 million;
    - a credit-loss provision of USD 393 million for bilateral financial hedging;
    - a credit-loss provision of USD 109 million for business counterparty risk.

    NRG expected a total cash impact of USD 500–700 million over time. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
12. Clearway Energy said that in February 2021 certain of its Texas wind projects "were unable to operate". Including third-party investors' share, it recorded a reduction of approximately USD 50 million in revenue in the first quarter of 2021 "to settle obligations for wind facilities that were unable to produce the required output". The cash impact to Clearway after tax-equity investor contributions was approximately USD 25 million. [confidence: high] [source: 7, 8]
13. On 1 March 2021 Brazos Electric Power Cooperative, a Texas wholesale power cooperative, filed for Chapter 11. Its lender National Rural Utilities Cooperative Finance Corporation (CFC) reported USD 85 million of unsecured exposure under a USD 500 million syndicated revolver administered by Bank of America. CFC also said Texas cooperatives had been forced to buy power at peak prices. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
14. Following Uri, ERCOT's Low System-Wide Offer Cap was amended on 24 June 2021 to USD 2,000/MWh, which applies once a set peaker net-margin threshold is reached. NRG reports that the threshold (USD 315,000/MW-year) was reached on 16 February 2021. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

- 8–13 February 2021: freezing and fuel problems begin; gas production shut-ins from about 7 February (FERC-NERC).
- 15 February, 1:20 a.m.: ERCOT orders its first 1,000 MW of load shed. Near-collapse of frequency. Load shed peaks at 20,000 MW at 7 p.m. PUCT emergency pricing order.
- 16 February: PUCT reaffirms the order (no retroactive application).
- Late 17 February: widespread outages end (per Potomac Economics).
- Early 19 February: ERCOT ends EEA3; prices leave USD 9,000/MWh.
- 1 March: Brazos Electric files for Chapter 11.
- 4 March: market monitor's USD 16 billion finding.
- March: Vistra appeal filed.
- May 2021: first-quarter filings disclose losses (NRG, Vistra, Clearway).
- 24 June 2021: Low System-Wide Offer Cap amended to USD 2,000/MWh.
- November 2021: FERC-NERC final report.

## Financing and structure details

How the hedge losses arose (mechanics, not deal-specific claims):
- A fixed-shape or fixed-volume hedge (a financial swap settled at a hub or node) obliges the project to deliver, or pay for, a set MWh in each hour, whatever it actually generates. The settlement each hour is (fixed price − floating price) × contracted volume. Any generation is sold at the node.
- Illustration (hypothetical): a 100 MW-per-hour hedge at USD 25/MWh, with zero output and a USD 9,000/MWh hub price, costs (25 − 9,000) × 100 = −USD 897,500 per hour. Over 80 hours that is about −USD 71.8 million, against an annual hedge revenue of about USD 21.9 million if the full 100 MW were settled every hour (100 × 8,760 × 25).
- As-produced or pay-as-generated PPAs pass volume risk to the buyer. Proxy-generation structures take volume risk from the resource but leave it with the asset for availability failures. Whether an outage qualifies as force majeure under the hedge is a matter of drafting.
- Clearway's disclosure (fact 12) confirms that obligations were settled where wind facilities were "unable to produce the required output". Part of the cost fell on tax-equity investors.
- Thermal generators and retail sellers faced the same exposure through retail load and fuel deliverability (Vistra, NRG) and through counterparty defaults (NRG's USD 393 million provision for bilateral hedging credit losses).

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The FERC-NERC report puts the root cause on generating units' freezing (inadequate winterization) and natural gas fuel-supply problems, which were linked to each other. [source: 1]
- The market monitor argued that the administrative price was held too long after load shed ended, which made the financial transfer worse. [source: 4]
- From the project-finance view, the structures that failed were those that sold a volume the asset could not guarantee in a tail weather event, at a cap price many times higher than normal wholesale prices. These losses were concentrated in fixed-shape hedges, retail obligations and gas-dependent plants without firm fuel. Losses then spread through the chain as counterparties defaulted (Brazos, NRG's credit provisions). [source: 5, 6, 7, 9]
- After the event: the high offer cap was lowered and reliability reforms followed. [source: 10]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 20 (merchant revenue and hedges): This is the reference case. Walk through a fixed-shape swap settlement hour by hour at USD 9,000/MWh with zero output. Compare it with as-produced PPAs, proxy revenue swaps and floors. Show how a small, low-probability correlated event (no output exactly when prices are highest) can wipe out years of hedge margin. Discuss the drafting levers: force majeure, volume caps, price caps in the swap, and collateral and credit support.
- Ch 70 (onshore wind and solar): Lenders' sizing must stress the joint distribution of output and price, not each on its own. Cold-weather packages and icing risk belong in technical diligence. Covenants and reserves should cover hedge-settlement liquidity, and tax-equity allocations decide who bears the loss (Clearway's revenue-versus-cash split shows this). Use Clearway's disclosed USD 50 million revenue reduction and USD 25 million cash impact as the anchor figures.

## Do not state

- Specific Uri loss figures for Innergex, Ørsted, Avangrid or other renewable owners, or the outcome of hedge litigation (for example cases involving wind farms named Flat Top, Shannon or Foard City). These were not verified for this sheet.
- That wind outages "caused" the blackout. FERC-NERC data show gas-fired units were the majority of units affected, and freezing and fuel problems dominated.
- An exact figure of "four minutes and 37 seconds" from blackout. The FERC-NERC report says frequency stayed below the trip level "for over four minutes". Use that wording.
- That the PUCT "repriced" or definitively "refused to reprice", or any later court outcomes of the Vistra appeal. Not verified here.
- Death tolls other than DSHS's 246 confirmed and FERC-NERC's "at least 210". Higher academic or press estimates exist but are not official.
- Details of the Texas securitization statutes passed after Uri (bill numbers, amounts). Not verified for this sheet.
- That the USD 16 billion was "overcharged" in a legal sense. It is the market monitor's estimate of extra costs.
