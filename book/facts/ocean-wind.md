# Orsted Ocean Wind 1 and 2 cancellation (2023)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: New Jersey Board of Public Utilities vacates the project approvals, 14 August 2024)

## Summary

Ocean Wind 1 (1,100 MW) and Ocean Wind 2 (1,148 MW) were offshore wind projects off southern New Jersey, developed by Ørsted on federal lease OCS-A 0498. In June 2019 the New Jersey Board of Public Utilities (BPU) awarded Ocean Wind 1 the state's first offshore renewable energy certificate (OREC) contract, at a first-year price of $98.10/MWh. Ocean Wind 2 won a second-round OREC award in 2021. These were fixed-price, long-term revenue contracts priced before the post-COVID wave of cost inflation, before US long-dated rates rose about 300 basis points (Ørsted's figure), and before the supply chain bottlenecks of 2022-2023. In 2023:
- Ørsted bought out partner PSEG's 25% stake in Ocean Wind 1.
- New Jersey passed a law in July 2023 allowing Ørsted to keep the federal investment tax credits.
- Ørsted warned on 29 August 2023 of impairments on its US portfolio.

On 31 October 2023, after further supplier delays (Ørsted cites a vessel delay), Ørsted decided to cease development of both projects "in the form" awarded by the BPU. It recognized DKK 28.4 billion of impairments in its nine-month 2023 interim report. Its 2023 annual report shows impairments of DKK 26.8 billion for the year, of which DKK 19.9 billion related to Ocean Wind 1, plus DKK 9.6 billion of cancellation fees and a DKK 15.0 billion onerous-contract provision at year end, contributing to a 2023 net loss of DKK 20.2 billion. Ørsted settled with New Jersey in May 2024 by paying $125 million, against $300 million of escrow and guarantee that had been at stake. The BPU vacated the project approvals and OREC orders in August 2024. The case shows how a fixed-price offtake without inflation or interest rate indexation, signed years before FID, can make a project uninvestable once costs and rates move.

## Verified facts

1. Ocean Wind 1 had a nameplate capacity of 1,100 MW and was located about 24 km off southern New Jersey. Ocean Wind 2 was 1,148 MW. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2, 4]

The OREC price was $98.10/MWh in the first year, and the levelized net OREC cost after energy and capacity revenue credits to ratepayers was estimated at $46.46/MWh. The BPU estimated monthly bill increases of $1.46 for residential customers, $13.05 for commercial and $110.10 for industrial. It accelerated approval so the project could qualify for federal investment tax credits expiring that year. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

[confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]

## Financing and structure details

- **Ownership**: Ørsted, with PSEG at 25% of Ocean Wind 1 until the 2023 buy-out. [source: 4, 5]
- **Revenue**: New Jersey ORECs for a fixed price per MWh with fixed annual escalation, not indexed to construction cost inflation or interest rates. Energy and capacity revenues were credited back to ratepayers. [source: 3, 7]
- **Tax equity and ITC**: the business case relied on federal ITC monetization. Ørsted cited a lower anticipated probability of qualifying for additional tax credits, a lower expected value from monetizing them, and a reversed deferred tax liability related to the tax equity partnership for Ocean Wind 1. [source: 4]
- **Security to the state**: $200 million escrow plus a $100 million guarantee, contingent on completion. The settlement paid $125 million and Ørsted kept $175 million. [source: 6, 9]
- **Project finance**: no non-recourse financing had closed. The projects never reached FID, and pre-FID supply commitments drove the cancellation costs. [source: 4]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Fixed price meets cost and rate shock**: Ørsted's own analysis is that a roughly 300 bp rise in the US risk-free rate since the 2019 award, cost inflation, supply chain delays and slow permitting destroyed the value of a fixed-price award. It now prioritizes inflation protection in offtake. [source: 4]
- **Pre-FID commitments**: Ørsted says high commitments made before FID drove significant contract termination costs, totalling DKK 9.6 billion of cancellation fees plus a DKK 15.0 billion provision. This illustrates the risk of reserving vessels, turbines and cables before the economics are locked in. [source: 4]
- **Tax credit dependence**: Uncertainty over ITC adders and monetization value contributed to the August 2023 impairment warning. A state law letting Ørsted keep the credits was not enough to save the project. [source: 4, 8]
- **Offtaker leverage was limited**: Despite the governor's stated intent to keep the full $300 million, the state settled for $125 million. Developer security was small relative to the losses, so walking away was rational. [source: 2, 6]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 71 (offshore wind)**: Use Ocean Wind as the canonical "winner's curse" case for auction-awarded fixed-price offtake. Show how a revenue contract set years before FID, without capex or interest rate indexation, leaves the developer short inflation and rates across a long development period. Quantify the exposure with Ørsted's disclosed figures, and contrast it with the inflation-indexation features that later US state solicitations introduced (verify the specific mechanisms before writing; they are not covered by this sheet). Discuss pre-FID supply chain reservations, tax-equity and ITC dependence, and why low completion security made exit cheaper than completion.

## Do not state

- Do not state that the DKK 28.4 billion impairment related only to Ocean Wind. It covered the US portfolio in the nine-month report. The full-year Ocean Wind 1 impairment was DKK 19.9 billion.
- Do not give a single "total cost of cancellation" figure in US dollars. No verified figure was found, and adding up impairments, fees and provisions double-counts.
- Do not state the Ocean Wind 2 OREC price or exact award date. Neither was verified.
- Do not state that Ørsted "received" or "kept" tax credits on Ocean Wind 1. The governor said Ørsted never received any of that benefit.
- Do not state that the projects reached FID or had project finance debt in place. They did not.
- Do not state the lease's status after the February 2024 suspension, or any later re-tender of the site. These are not verified.
- Do not quote Ørsted executives beyond the exact words in source 1.
