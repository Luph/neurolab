# PNG LNG: ECA-led financing, country risk

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: restart after the 26 February 2018 Highlands earthquake, April 2018; financial completion 6 February 2015)

## Summary

PNG LNG is an integrated gas project in Papua New Guinea, operated by ExxonMobil. It runs from gas fields and a gas conditioning plant at Hides in the Southern Highlands (now Hela Province), through about 435 miles of onshore and offshore pipeline, to a two-train liquefaction plant near Port Moresby. Planned capacity was 6.6 mtpa at financing and about 6.9 mtpa by 2014. At financing in 2009 the joint venture was ExxonMobil 33.2%, Oil Search 29.0%, PNG state participants 19.6%, Santos 13.5% and Nippon Oil 4.7%. Loan agreements for USD 14 billion were signed on 15 December 2009, against a project cost of about USD 15 billion and a 70/30 debt-to-equity target. Sponsors called it the largest project financing ever in the energy sector. Six export credit agencies (ECAs) formed the core lender group: US EXIM, JBIC, China Exim, NEXI, SACE and EFIC. They provided about USD 8.3 billion through direct loans and covered commercial tranches. Uncovered commercial bank debt was about USD 1.95 billion, and ExxonMobil co-loans about USD 3.75 billion. US EXIM's USD 3 billion was the largest transaction in its history at the time. The ECAs negotiated the term sheet before commercial banks joined, at the trough of the financial crisis, and secured 15-year bank tenors. Construction costs rose to about USD 19 billion. ExxonMobil attributed the 2012 increase to currency appreciation (Australian dollar and kina), landowner disputes and work stoppages, and weather and logistics. The project still shipped its first cargo to TEPCO on 25 May 2014, ahead of schedule, and reached financial completion on 6 February 2015. Country risk was the defining theme: landowner identification and benefit-sharing disputes, community violence, governance concerns and natural hazards. A 2014 US EXIM Inspector General report criticized the bank's corruption screening and its treatment of foreign-exchange risk during construction.

## Verified facts

1. PNG LNG is a vertically integrated project: gas fields in the Highlands, an onshore and offshore pipeline of over 400 miles, a liquefaction plant on the coast and marine export facilities. ExxonMobil PNG Ltd is operator. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. At financing (March 2010 presentation), project interests were ExxonMobil 33.2%, Oil Search 29.0%, State participants 19.6% (IPBC, MRDC and Petromin), Santos 13.5% and Nippon Oil/JPP 4.7%. The project had 9.2 Tcf of 2P gas resources and 6.6 mtpa of capacity. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. The sponsors' finance plan targeted 70/30 debt to equity on a project cost of "~$15 Bln through initial completion". A jointly owned marketing and borrowing company, PNG LNG Global Company LDC (Bahamas), was created to deal with the underlying unincorporated joint venture. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
4. Negotiations and due diligence with the core ECA group ran from December 2008 to November 2009. The term sheet was concluded in July 2009, commercial banks were then invited on the ECA-agreed terms, and a bond effort was dropped once written lender commitments were received. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Loan agreements for USD 14 billion were signed on 15 December 2009. Sources were ECAs USD 8.30 billion, commercial banks USD 1.95 billion and ExxonMobil co-loans USD 3.75 billion. Lenders were six ECAs (US EXIM, JBIC, China Exim, NEXI, SACE, EFIC) and 17 European, Asian and Australian commercial banks, with ExxonMobil as co-lender. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. The sponsors' presentation claimed the "largest ever project financing in the energy sector", written commitments well above the requirement, less than 12 months from start of negotiations to signing, and "15 year tenor bank debt". [confidence: high, as sponsor claims] [source: 1]
7. US EXIM's board approved USD 3.0 billion on 3 December 2009: a USD 2.2 billion direct loan and an USD 800 million guaranteed loan, described as 17.5-year financing. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4]
    - Financial close for EXIM's facilities was 15 December 2009, and first disbursement 30 March 2010.
    - The financing supported over USD 2.4 billion of US exports and USD 578.9 million of PNG local costs.
    - EXIM called it the largest transaction in its 75-year history.
8. JBIC signed a loan of up to USD 1.8 billion on 15 December 2009 in New York, co-financed with Japanese banks (SMBC, Mizuho Corporate Bank, BTMU) insured by NEXI. JBIC said about half the LNG would supply Japan. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
9. Other ECA direct loans were China Exim USD 1.3 billion (17-year tenor) and EFIC USD 350 million. Covered commercial tranches were USD 800 million (US EXIM guaranteed), USD 900 million (SACE) and USD 950 million (NEXI). [confidence: medium, AidData compilation consistent with sponsor totals] [source: 5, 6]
10. The long-term buyers are TEPCO, Osaka Gas, Sinopec/UNIPEC and CPC (Taiwan). [confidence: high] [source: 2, 7]
11. In November 2012 the cost estimate rose from USD 15.7 billion to USD 19 billion. Press accounts of the operator's and Oil Search's statements put USD 1.4 billion down to foreign exchange, USD 1.2 billion to work stoppages and land-access issues, and USD 0.7 billion to logistics and weather. The project was then about 70% complete. [confidence: medium-high, press reporting of company statements] [source: 8]
12. The EXIM Office of Inspector General (OIG) found that foreign-exchange exposure, through construction contracts in Australian dollars and kina against a US dollar functional currency, accounted for USD 2.1 billion of the overall cost overrun. EXIM relied on three cost-overrun protections: a negotiated supplemental debt cap, completion guarantees, and the Coordinated Development and Operating Agreement among sponsors. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
13. Supplementary financing of USD 1.5 billion was committed in October 2013: a USD 600 million syndicated term loan, including China Development Bank, and a USD 900 million ExxonMobil loan. [confidence: medium] [source: 5]
14. LNG production started in April 2014. The first cargo, for TEPCO, shipped on 25 May 2014; ExxonMobil said it was ahead of schedule. ExxonMobil cited a cost of USD 19 billion, capacity of 6.9 mtpa and more than 9 Tcf to be produced over about 30 years. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
15. Financial completion was announced on 6 February 2015. All completion tests were satisfied, completion guarantees were released, loan repayments began and distributions to co-venturers became permitted. 65 cargoes had been delivered by then. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
16. The EXIM OIG report (OIG-INS-14-01, 18 June 2014) listed country risk (including PNG's recent nationalization of a mine and consideration of greater state control of extractives), a Transparency International ranking of 144 of 177, and disputes over land rights and Benefit Sharing Agreement funds. It found EXIM's standard character, reputational and transaction integrity (CRTI) screening returned "No Match" on parties for which open sources showed red flags. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
17. The OIG notes that Benefit Sharing Agreements under PNG's Oil and Gas Act 1998 were a condition precedent to financial close. It cites Jubilee Australia: the government threatened to impose terms on areas that would not sign. It also cites Human Rights Watch: in August 2011 villagers attacked two employees at the Komo airfield site, forcing ExxonMobil to halt work temporarily. [confidence: high as reported by OIG] [source: 2]
18. A magnitude 7.5 earthquake struck the Southern Highlands on 26 February 2018. ExxonMobil shut the Hides gas conditioning plant and then both LNG trains, warning repairs could take up to eight weeks. Production restarted ahead of that estimate in April 2018, with the second train back on 30 April 2018. [confidence: high] [source: 10, 11]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Right: the ECAs as anchor in a closed market.** The sponsor presentation credits the ECAs as the core negotiating group while "minimal bank lending" was happening, with US EXIM providing "balance" and helping avoid "least common denominator" responses. ECA cover let commercial banks offer 15-year tenors. [source: 1]
- **Right: sponsor co-lending.** ExxonMobil's USD 3.75 billion co-loan filled the gap and aligned the operator's interests with those of the lenders. [source: 1]
- **Wrong: currency mismatch in construction.** The EXIM OIG found FX exposure was "not separately addressed and sufficiently evaluated" and contributed USD 2.1 billion to the overrun. EXIM's management response pointed to completion guarantees as the main mitigant. [source: 2]
- **Country risk on the ground.** Land access, landowner identification and benefit-sharing disputes caused stoppages; they make up the USD 1.2 billion line in the 2012 overrun. The OIG criticized EXIM's integrity screening as missing publicly known risks. [source: 2, 8]
- **Outcome for lenders.** Despite about USD 4 billion of overrun, first LNG came early and financial completion was reached about nine months later, so lenders moved off sponsor guarantees onto project risk with an operating asset. [source: 7, 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 29 (The lenders).** PNG LNG is the archetype of an ECA-led club. Six ECAs from the sponsors' and contractors' home countries (US, Japan, China, Italy, Australia) each financed their own exports and local costs, against OECD Arrangement limits on local-cost support. Teach direct lending versus cover (EXIM's 2.2 + 0.8 split), why ECAs move first in a closed bank market, and the sponsor as co-lender. Use the OIG findings to discuss ECA due diligence duties (environmental and social, integrity screening).
- **Ch 76 (LNG liquefaction, regasification and FPSOs).** Use it as the integrated upstream-to-LNG model in a frontier country: a jointly owned borrower and marketing company, long-term Asian offtake with utilities, completion guarantees until financial completion tests are passed, and the cost-overrun anatomy (FX, land access, weather). Contrast with Sabine Pass (Ch 21/76), a brownfield tolling-style project in the US with no upstream or country risk.

## Do not state

- Exact SPA volumes per buyer (commonly cited: Sinopec 2.0, TEPCO 1.8, Osaka Gas 1.5, CPC 1.2 mtpa). Unverified here; the EXIM OIG redacted them.
- Interest margins, ECA premia or fees: none verified.
- The 2014 PNG government purchase of Oil Search shares funded by a UBS loan, and the later Ombudsman/Commission of Inquiry findings. Relevant to country risk but not verified for this sheet.
- Shareholdings after 2009 without a date. The OIG gives ExxonMobil 32.9% (2013 data); Oil Search merged into Santos in December 2021, and Kumul Petroleum holds the state stake. Verify current percentages before use.
- That the overrun came "entirely" from FX. Press breakdown of the November 2012 increase gives FX USD 1.4 billion; the OIG figure of USD 2.1 billion covers the whole overrun. Present both, attributed.
- Casualty figures for the January 2012 Tumbi quarry landslide near the project, or causal links to the project: not verified here.
- "Largest project financing ever" as fact: it is the sponsors' and ECAs' description in 2009-10.
