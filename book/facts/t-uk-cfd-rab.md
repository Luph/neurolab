# UK Contracts for Difference allocation rounds and RAB legislation (Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act 2022)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: CfD Allocation Round 8 application window, 20 July to 7 August 2026, with budget notice pending; AR7a results, 10 February 2026; AR7 offshore results, 14 January 2026)

Cross-references: for the nuclear RAB applied in practice (Sizewell C FID 22 July 2025, thresholds, NWF debt, government support package) and the Hinkley Point C CfD (£92.50/MWh, reduced to £89.50/MWh in 2012 prices), use facts/hinkley-sizewell.md. For the water-sector RAB precedent and its government support package, use facts/tideway.md. This sheet does not repeat those facts.

## Summary

Great Britain supports new low-carbon generation mainly through two-sided Contracts for Difference (CfDs). These are private-law contracts between a generator and the Low Carbon Contracts Company (LCCC), a government-owned counterparty. They pay the generator the difference between an agreed, CPI-indexed strike price and a market reference price, and require the generator to pay back when the market price is above the strike. Contracts are allocated in competitive sealed-bid auctions ("allocation rounds", AR). These were held roughly every two years from AR1 (2014-15) to AR4 (2021-22) and annually from AR5 (2023). AR5 (2023) attracted no offshore wind. AR6 (results 3 September 2024) secured 9.6 GW across 131 projects. AR7 was the largest round so far. For AR7 the government lengthened contracts from 15 to 20 years for wind and solar and split the auction in two. The offshore results (14 January 2026) awarded about 8.4 GW at £91.20/MWh (England and Wales) and £89.49/MWh (Scotland) in 2024 prices. The onshore wind, solar and tidal results (10 February 2026) awarded 6.2 GW. In total AR7 procured 14.7 GW across 201 projects. AR8 opened for applications on 20 July 2026. For assets whose main risk is construction (large nuclear, and earlier the Thames Tideway Tunnel), Parliament legislated a regulated asset base (RAB) alternative. The Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act 2022 (Royal Assent 31 March 2022) lets the Secretary of State designate a nuclear company and modify its electricity generation licence, with Ofgem as economic regulator, to earn an allowed revenue on its RAB during construction. Under the Act, revenue is collected from electricity suppliers through a revenue collection contract with a government-designated counterparty, and a special administration regime protects continuity if the company fails. Sizewell C is the first project financed under it.

## Verified facts

### A. CfD mechanism (Great Britain; scheme rules and allocation documents at gov.uk "Contracts for Difference" collection; LCCC at lowcarboncontracts.uk)

1. The LCCC is a private company owned by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ). It is the counterparty to contracts awarded in CfD allocation rounds. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. A CfD is two-sided. When the market reference price is below the strike price, the LCCC pays the generator the difference. When it is above, the generator pays the LCCC. Payments to generators are funded by a levy on electricity suppliers, which consumers pay through their bills. (For HPC's version of this, see facts/hinkley-sizewell.md, item 5.) [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
3. Until AR6, contracts paid a flat CPI-indexed rate over a 15-year term. For AR7, the government (July 2025) moved to 20-year CPI-indexed contracts for fixed-bottom and floating offshore wind, onshore wind (including repowered and remote island wind) and solar PV. The aim was to spread capital costs over a longer period and lower strike prices. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3, 4]
4. Allocation round dates published by government:
   - AR1: October 2014 to March 2015
   - AR2: March to September 2017
   - AR3: May to September 2019
   - AR4: December 2021 to July 2022
   - AR5: March to September 2023
   - AR6: March to September 2024
   - AR7: application window 7 to 27 August 2025; results January and February 2026
   Rounds became annual from AR5. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
5. Strike prices are bid and awarded in 2012 prices (indexed thereafter). Recent government results notices also show them in 2024 prices. Always state the price base when quoting a strike price. [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]

### B. Recent rounds

6. AR5 (results September 2023) awarded no offshore wind contracts. AR6 restored offshore wind. [confidence: high] [source: 7, 8]
7. AR6 results were announced on 3 September 2024: 131 projects with 9.6 GW in total. Offshore wind took about 5.3 GW, with new fixed-bottom offshore projects at a strike price of £58.87/MWh in 2012 prices. [confidence: high] [source: 7, 8]
8. AR7 offshore results, announced on 14 January 2026 (2012 prices / 2024 prices):
   - Fixed-bottom offshore wind (Pot 3): Awel y Môr (775 MW, Wales), Berwick Bank Phase B (1,380 MW, Scotland), Dogger Bank South East and West (1,500 MW each), and Norfolk Vanguard East and West (1,380 MW plus smaller units each). Strike prices were £65.45/MWh (£91.20/MWh in 2024 prices) for England and Wales and £64.23/MWh (£89.49/MWh) for Scotland. Delivery years run from 2028/29 to 2030/31.
   - Floating offshore wind (Pot 4): Erebus (100 MW, Wales) and Pentland (92.5 MW, Scotland) at £155.37/MWh (£216.49/MWh in 2024 prices). [confidence: high] [source: 5]
9. Carbon Brief's analysis of the AR7 offshore round reported these figures:
   - fixed-bottom capacity of 8,245 MW, at an average strike price of £90.91/MWh in 2024 prices;
   - total offshore capacity of about 8.4 GW, including 192.5 MW of floating wind;
   - RWE won about 6.9 GW;
   - the government raised the offshore budget from £900 million to about £1.79 billion after seeing the bids;
   - offshore strike prices were roughly 10% higher in real terms than in AR6. [confidence: high] [source: 9, 10]
10. AR7a results for onshore wind, solar and tidal stream (Pots 1 and 2) were announced on 10 February 2026: 189 projects with about 6.2 GW, made up of about 4.9 GW solar, 1.3 GW onshore wind and 21 MW tidal stream. Strike prices were:
    - solar PV: £46.82/MWh in 2012 prices (£65.23/MWh in 2024 prices);
    - onshore wind: £51.85/MWh (£72.24/MWh in 2024 prices).
    Across both parts, AR7 secured a record 14.7 GW in 201 projects. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 11]
11. AR8: DESNZ published its government response on AR8 design in July 2026. The application window opened on 20 July 2026 and closed on 7 August 2026. AR8 introduces an "Other Deepwater Offshore Wind" (ODOW) category and extends long-stop dates for floating wind. The Contract Budget Notice was expected between 17 September and 19 November 2026, depending on appeals. Results had not been announced as at 2026-10-03. [confidence: high] [source: 12, 13, 14]

### C. RAB legislation (UK; Act text at legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/15)

12. The Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act 2022 (2022 c. 15) received Royal Assent on 31 March 2022. [confidence: high] [source: 15, 16]
13. The Act has four Parts:
    - Part 1: the nuclear regulated asset base model. The Secretary of State may designate a nuclear company and modify the licences of designated and "relevant licensee nuclear companies", with information provisions.
    - Part 2: revenue collection contracts. Regulations designate a revenue collection counterparty, and electricity suppliers are obliged to make payments to it.
    - Part 3: a special administration regime for relevant licensee nuclear companies, applying and amending the Energy Act 2004.
    - Part 4: miscellaneous provisions, including on financing nuclear site decommissioning. [confidence: high] [source: 15]
14. The Act's stated purpose was to support investment in new nuclear and the government's 2020 Energy White Paper and Ten Point Plan commitments. It also provides for the special administration regime and clarifies when bodies corporate are not "associated with" site operators for decommissioning finance. [confidence: high] [source: 16]
15. The nuclear RAB copies the approach of the Thames Tideway Tunnel, which was licensed under water-sector legislation rather than this Act. See facts/tideway.md for that precedent and facts/hinkley-sizewell.md for Sizewell C's thresholds, cost sharing and support package. [confidence: high] [source: facts/hinkley-sizewell.md, facts/tideway.md]

## Financing and structure details

- **CfD-backed project finance**: the CfD fixes the price per MWh. Lenders still take volume risk (wind or solar resource, availability), basis and shape risk between the reference price and actual capture, and construction risk until the milestone and long-stop dates. Lengthening tenor from 15 to 20 years lets debt amortise over a longer contracted period. The strike is CPI-indexed, which suits index-linked or CPI-hedged structures. [source: 1, 3]
- **Budget and price discovery**: each pot has an administrative strike price (a ceiling) and a budget. In AR7 the government used its new power to see bids before fixing the budget, and roughly doubled the offshore budget. [source: 9, 10]
- **Nuclear RAB**: the allowed revenue comes from consumers through suppliers' payments to a revenue collection counterparty under Part 2 of the Act, starting in construction. Special administration under Part 3 keeps the asset running if the licensee fails. For the actual SZC terms (65% gearing cap, NWF loans, Bpifrance-covered bank debt, 50:50 sharing within thresholds), see facts/hinkley-sizewell.md. [source: 15]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **AR5 failure and AR6 recovery**: no offshore wind bid in AR5 because the administrative strike price had not kept up with cost inflation. AR6 raised ceilings and offshore returned. (The failure and recovery are verified in source 7; the cause is widely reported analysis rather than a primary-source finding. Attribute it if used.) [source: 7, 8]
- **AR7 scale**: a longer 20-year term, a larger budget set after viewing bids, and a split auction produced record volumes. Offshore strike prices still rose about 10% in real terms against AR6. This shows that support design moves both volume and price. [source: 9, 10]
- **Nuclear: why legislate a RAB at all**: HPC's CfD-only model put all construction risk on the sponsor, and no developer would repeat it. The RAB lowers the cost of capital by sharing construction risk with consumers. The National Audit Office's analysis of this is summarised in facts/hinkley-sizewell.md.

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 19 (Support schemes: FiTs, CfDs and RAB)**: Use the AR7 results notice to show students how a strike price is quoted in both 2012 and 2024 prices, and why the base year matters when comparing rounds (£58.87 in AR6 against £65.45 in AR7, both in 2012 prices). Have them model one AR7 offshore unit's revenue under a 15-year and a 20-year term to see how tenor drives debt sizing and bid price. Then set the CfD (paid on output, construction risk with the sponsor) against the Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act's RAB (paid on spend, with risk shared through regulation and special administration). Use SZC from facts/hinkley-sizewell.md as the worked example.
- **Chapter 74 (Nuclear)**: Walk through the four Parts of the 2022 Act as the legal plumbing that makes a nuclear RAB financeable: designation and licence, revenue collection from suppliers, special administration, and decommissioning finance. Explain why lenders need each piece. Cross-refer to facts/hinkley-sizewell.md for the commercial terms and to the Tideway precedent (facts/tideway.md).

## Do not state

- Do not state AR8 results, pot budgets or strike prices; they were not available as at 2026-10-03.
- Do not quote the AR8 Clean Industry Bonus budget (£515 million reported by one trade outlet) as fact without checking the gov.uk notice.
- Do not describe the reference price methodology in detail (for example, day-ahead hourly for intermittent generation, seasonal baseload for firm generation) without checking current LCCC or DESNZ allocation documents. The principle is right, but the detail was not verified here.
- Do not call AR6's offshore award "5.3 GW of new capacity". Some reports put fresh capacity lower (about 3.8 GW), with the rest being capacity extensions to earlier projects.
- Do not attribute the AR5 offshore failure to a specific cause as fact; attribute it to named analysts if used.
- Do not say that SMRs or other assets have been designated under the Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act 2022. Only Sizewell C is verified.
- Do not quote Act sections by number without checking legislation.gov.uk.
