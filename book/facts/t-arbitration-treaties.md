# Arbitration and investment treaties: ICSID, New York Convention, Energy Charter Treaty withdrawals, Achmea/Komstroy, sovereign immunity

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: ICSID caseload statistics as of 30 June 2026; U.S. Supreme Court certiorari denials of 29 June 2026)

## Summary

Project financiers rely on two enforcement regimes against states and state entities.

- **ICSID Convention (1965).** Awards are binding and must be recognized as if they were final national-court judgments, with only internal annulment as review. It had 158 Contracting States and 8 further signatories as of 30 June 2026.
- **New York Convention (1958).** National courts recognize and enforce commercial and non-ICSID awards, subject to limited refusal grounds. It has 172 parties.

Investment treaties, including bilateral investment treaties (BITs) and the multilateral Energy Charter Treaty (ECT), give investors the standing to bring claims. After the Court of Justice of the EU (CJEU) held intra-EU investor-state arbitration incompatible with EU law (Achmea, 2018; Komstroy, 2021; PL Holdings, 2021), the European order moved against intra-EU awards:

- EU states terminated intra-EU BITs (2020);
- the EU, Euratom and many Member States withdrew from the ECT, with the EU and Euratom withdrawal effective 28 June 2025 and the UK's effective 27 April 2025;
- the Commission declared payment of an ECT award (Antin v Spain) to be illegal state aid (March 2025).

The ECT's sunset clause continues protection for existing investments for 20 years after a withdrawal. Courts outside the EU have kept enforcing:

- the UK Supreme Court held on 4 March 2026 that ICSID Contracting States cannot claim immunity from registration of ICSID awards, though immunity from execution remains;
- the U.S. Supreme Court on 29 June 2026 declined to review D.C. Circuit rulings allowing enforcement of intra-EU awards against Spain;
- by contrast, the High Court of Australia held on 8 April 2026 that ratifying the New York Convention alone does not waive a state's immunity.

## Verified facts

### ICSID

1. **Convention basics.** The ICSID Convention was opened for signature on 18 March 1965 and entered into force on 14 October 1966. Its key enforcement provisions are:
   - Article 53: awards are binding and not subject to appeal except as the Convention provides;
   - Article 52: annulment by an ad hoc committee is the internal remedy;
   - Article 54(1): each Contracting State must recognize an award as binding and enforce its pecuniary obligations as if it were a final judgment of its own courts;
   - Article 55: Article 54 does not derogate from national law on immunity from execution.

   Current text: icsid.worldbank.org. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Membership and caseload (30 June 2026).** As of 30 June 2026, ICSID reported 158 Contracting States and 8 signatory States, 166 in total. India and Brazil are neither. Since 1972 ICSID has registered 1,118 arbitration and conciliation cases. In fiscal year 2026 (to 30 June 2026) the caseload was as follows:
   - 60 new cases were registered, 57 under the Convention and 3 under the Additional Facility Rules;
   - 62% of new cases invoked BITs;
   - oil and gas accounted for 25% and mining for 18% of new cases.

   [confidence: medium (ICSID statistics via ASIL summary)] [source: 2]

### New York Convention

3. **New York Convention.** The Convention on the Recognition and Enforcement of Foreign Arbitral Awards was adopted on 10 June 1958 and entered into force on 7 June 1959. According to UNCITRAL's status page, it has 172 States parties. The most recent accessions shown are Suriname (accession 10 November 2022, in force 8 February 2023), Turkmenistan (2022) and Iraq (2021). Recognition can be refused only on the grounds listed in Article V, which include incapacity or an invalid agreement, lack of notice, an excess of mandate, irregular composition, an award not binding or set aside, non-arbitrability, and public policy. Current status: uncitral.un.org. [confidence: high] [source: 3]

### Energy Charter Treaty

4. **ECT basics.** The ECT was signed in December 1994 and entered into force in April 1998. Article 47(3) (the "sunset clause") provides that investments existing when a withdrawal takes effect remain protected for 20 years from that date. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]

   Russia ended its provisional application in 2009, and Iceland notified withdrawal in 2026. [source: 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]
6. **Modernized ECT.** The Energy Charter Conference adopted amendments modernizing the ECT on 3 December 2024. They have applied provisionally since 3 September 2025 for parties that did not opt out by 3 March 2025. Entry into force requires ratification by three-quarters of Contracting Parties. Changes include:
   - coverage of CCUS, low-carbon hydrogen and synthetic fuels;
   - for EU Member States, a phase-out of protection for fossil-fuel investments made before 3 September 2025, ending by 31 December 2040;
   - a standalone right-to-regulate provision;
   - third-party funding disclosure;
   - a clarification that the ECT does not apply intra-EU.

   [confidence: high] [source: 11]

### Achmea, Komstroy and the intra-EU objection

7. **The CJEU line.** The CJEU decided three cases:
   - **Achmea (C-284/16, Grand Chamber, 6 March 2018).** Investor-state arbitration clauses in intra-EU BITs are incompatible with EU law.
   - **Komstroy (C-741/19, 2 September 2021).** Extended this to intra-EU disputes under Article 26 of the ECT.
   - **PL Holdings (C-109/20, 26 October 2021).** Member States may not conclude an ad hoc arbitration agreement with an investor that replicates an invalid intra-EU BIT clause.

   [confidence: high] [source: 12, 13]
8. **Termination Agreement.** On 5 May 2020, 23 EU Member States signed an agreement terminating approximately 130 intra-EU BITs, including their sunset clauses, and stating they cannot serve as a basis for arbitration. [confidence: high] [source: 13]
9. **Intra-EU ECT inter se agreement.** On 26 June 2024, 26 Member States and the EU signed a Declaration on the legal consequences of Komstroy, and an inter se Agreement on the interpretation and application of the ECT was initialled. The Agreement states the common understanding that Article 26 ECT cannot serve as a basis for intra-EU arbitration and seeks to disapply the sunset clause intra-EU. The EU approved it by Decision (EU) 2025/1904 of 10 September 2025. The Agreement enters into force 30 days after the depositary receives the second instrument of ratification. [confidence: high] [source: 14, 15]
10. **Antin state-aid decision.** By Commission Decision (EU) 2025/1235 of 24 March 2025 (SA.54155), the European Commission found that paying the ECT award in favor of Antin was illegal and incompatible state aid. The award was EUR 101 million plus interest and costs, for Spain's changes to its renewable support scheme. The Commission ordered Spain not to pay. [confidence: high] [source: 16, 17]

### Sovereign immunity and enforcement (2024–2026)

11. **UK Supreme Court (4 March 2026).** In *Kingdom of Spain v Infrastructure Services Luxembourg S.à r.l.* and *Republic of Zimbabwe v Border Timbers Ltd* [2026] UKSC 9, five Justices (Lord Lloyd-Jones, Lord Briggs, Lord Sales, Lord Leggatt and Lady Simler) unanimously held:
    - by agreeing to Article 54(1) of the ICSID Convention, a Contracting State submits to the jurisdiction of UK courts under section 2(2) of the State Immunity Act 1978 for registration of ICSID awards;
    - Article 55 preserves immunity from execution only.

    The awards were approximately EUR 112 million (later reduced by EUR 11 million) against Spain, and USD 124 million plus interest against Zimbabwe. [confidence: high] [source: 18]
12. **Singapore (24 February 2026).** The Singapore High Court held in *NextEra Energy Global Holdings BV v Kingdom of Spain* that Spain's accession to the ICSID Convention constituted submission to Singapore's jurisdiction for recognition of ICSID awards. [confidence: medium (single secondary source)] [source: 19]
13. **High Court of Australia (8 April 2026).** In *CCDM Holdings LLC v Republic of India* [2026] HCA 9, the Court unanimously held that India's ratification of the New York Convention did not in itself waive India's immunity in proceedings to enforce an award. This affirmed the Full Federal Court. In 2023 the same court had held (*Kingdom of Spain v Infrastructure Services Luxembourg*) that ICSID ratification waives immunity from recognition but not execution. [confidence: high for 2026; medium for the 2023 description] [source: 19, 20]
14. **United States.** Three developments:
    - **NextEra v Spain (D.C. Circuit, August 2024, 112 F.4th 1088).** The court held the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act (FSIA) arbitration exception applied to intra-EU ECT awards. Disputes about the scope of the arbitration agreement go to enforceability on the merits, not to jurisdiction.
    - **Certiorari denied (29 June 2026).** The U.S. Supreme Court denied certiorari in *Kingdom of Spain v. Blasket Renewable Investments LLC* (No. 24-1130) and *Russian Federation v. Stabil LLC* (No. 25-1093).
    - **CC/Devas v Antrix (5 June 2025).** The Supreme Court held unanimously that the FSIA does not require a showing of minimum contacts. Personal jurisdiction exists where an FSIA immunity exception applies and service is made. The Court left the constitutional due-process question open.

    [confidence: high for Devas and the D.C. Circuit holding; medium for the cert-denial date (two law-firm sources)] [source: 21, 22, 23]

## Financing and structure details

Several practical consequences follow for project finance, drawn from the sources above.

- **Treaty-structuring vehicles.** Sponsors often hold project companies through a holding company in a state with a favorable BIT or ECT coverage. After Achmea and Komstroy, an EU-domiciled holding company investing in another EU state cannot rely on intra-EU treaty arbitration before EU courts. Awards may still be enforceable in the U.S., UK and Australia, but EU states face state-aid and Commission constraints on paying. [source: 13, 16, 18, 21]
- **ICSID versus New York Convention.** ICSID awards carry a stronger route through registration: in the UK no immunity defense to registration, and no set-aside in national courts. Execution against state assets still depends on local immunity law, for example the commercial-use exceptions. [source: 1, 18]
- **Express waiver.** For New York Convention awards against states, the Australian 2026 ruling confirms the value of an express waiver of immunity in the concession or government support agreement. [source: 20]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Spain's renewable subsidy cuts (2013 onward)** produced dozens of ECT claims by EU and non-EU investors. EU law (Komstroy) and the Commission's state-aid decision have blocked payment within the EU. Investors have instead pursued enforcement in the U.S., UK, Australia and Singapore, with favorable appellate outcomes in 2024–2026. [source: 16, 18, 19, 21]
- **The withdrawal wave** was driven by climate-policy concerns. Ireland's minister described the ECT as not aligned with the Paris Agreement. Under the sunset clause, existing investments keep protection for 20 years, so the practical effect is mainly on new investments. [source: 5, 7]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 54 (Governing law, disputes and investment protection).** Choose the forum by working backwards from enforcement. Ask where the counterparty's assets are, whether the state is an ICSID Contracting State, and whether the contract contains an express waiver of immunity from suit and from execution. Use [2026] UKSC 9 and CCDM v India as the paired lesson: ICSID ratification waives adjudicative immunity in the UK and Australia, New York Convention ratification alone does not, and execution immunity survives both. For treaty structuring, explain the timing problem: protection depends on a treaty being in force when the investment is made and on the sunset period. Explain why an intra-EU structure is now weak, and why the modernized ECT and the withdrawals change new-build energy projects in Europe. For the Case P dispute clause, have the reader draft the arbitration agreement (seat, rules, ICSID versus UNCITRAL), the waiver of immunity and the consent to ICSID jurisdiction.
