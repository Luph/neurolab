# Rating agency project finance methodologies (S&P, Moody's, Fitch)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest verified methodology event: Fitch "Infrastructure & Project Finance Rating Criteria" republished 14 November 2025; latest regulatory filings used: Fitch, S&P and Moody's annual Form NRSRO certifications, March 2026)

## Summary

Project bonds, and many project loans that want access to institutional investors, are rated by one or more of the three global agencies: S&P Global Ratings, Moody's Ratings (Moody's Investors Service) and Fitch Ratings. All three are SEC-registered nationally recognized statistical rating organizations (NRSROs). Each must file an annual Form NRSRO certification with an exhibit describing its procedures and methodologies.

The research for this sheet could verify the methodology architecture from primary sources only for Fitch. Fitch rates project finance under a "master" Infrastructure & Project Finance (IPF) Rating Criteria, last republished on 14 November 2025 (earlier versions: 24 March 2020, 23 August 2021, 20 July 2022, 17 May 2023 and 8 January 2025). A cross-sector Completion Risk Rating Criteria (latest 10 July 2025) and sector criteria add to it, including Renewable Energy Project, Thermal Power Project, Availability-Based Projects, Toll Roads, Ports, Airports, Public-Sector Counterparty Obligations in PPP Transactions and, since August 2025, Project Finance Digital Infrastructure.

In its own regulatory description, Fitch's qualitative analysis covers completion risk, operation risk, revenue risk, debt structure, counterparty risk and country and event risk. Its quantitative metrics are the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR), leverage (net debt to cash flow available for debt service (CFADS) or to EBITDA), the project life coverage ratio (PLCR), the loan life coverage ratio (LLCR), interest coverage, maximum annual debt service (MADS) and net debt to regulated asset base. Fitch's project finance obligation ratings address probability of default only. Separate recovery ratings are assigned to infrastructure and project finance obligations only on request.

S&P and Moody's publish analogous project finance methodologies. Their current titles, dates and scoring mechanics could not be accessed from primary sources during this research (both agencies' sites blocked automated access). Writers must verify them before stating specifics; see "Do not state".

### Fitch: criteria architecture and update history (global; current criteria at fitchratings.com, criteria section)

2. Fitch's 2026 Form NRSRO lists the following IPF-related criteria publications in 2025 [confidence: high] [source: 1]:

   - Infrastructure & Project Finance Rating Criteria (master), 8 January 2025 and 14 November 2025.

   - Completion Risk Rating Criteria (cross-sector), 10 July 2025.
   - An exposure draft on climate vulnerability in IPF ratings (2025).
3. Earlier Fitch filings record the update history. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5, 6, 7]
   - Master IPF criteria: 24 March 2020, 23 August 2021, 20 July 2022 (exposure draft 13 June 2022) and 17 May 2023.
   - Completion Risk Rating Criteria: 24 March 2020, 16 December 2020 and 5 July 2023.
   - Renewable Energy Project Rating Criteria: 25 March 2020, 26 January 2021, 23 August 2021 and 6 February 2023.
   - Thermal Power Project Rating Criteria: 24 March 2020, 27 May 2020 and 9 June 2021.
   - Availability-Based Projects Rating Criteria: 24 March and 27 May 2020.
   - Toll Roads, Bridges and Tunnels: 24 March and 26 June 2020.
   - Ports: 24 March and 15 October 2020.
   - Airports: 24 March and 22 October 2020.
4. Fitch's NRSRO description of its IPF qualitative analysis. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
   - It evaluates completion risk, meaning the risk that the facility is not completed on time, on budget or to the performance standards assumed for the operating period, where material to the rating.
   - It evaluates operating cost, demand, revenue and infrastructure renewal risks.
   - It evaluates debt structure: payment waterfall ranking, refinancing risk, covenants, structural features, hedging, liquidity and reserves.
   - It evaluates financial flexibility under stresses expected over the forecast period.
   - It assesses counterparty risk (offtakers, concession grantors, warranty providers) for each risk factor.
   - It accounts for country risk, industry risk and event risk in the final rating.
5. Fitch's NRSRO description of its IPF quantitative metrics. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
   - DSCR.
   - Leverage: net debt to CFADS, or net debt to EBITDA.
   - PLCR: the net present value (NPV) of CFADS over the remaining project life divided by principal outstanding on the rated debt plus all equal-ranking and senior debt.
   - LLCR: the NPV of CFADS from the calculation date to maturity of the rated debt.
   - Also interest coverage, MADS and net debt to regulated asset base.
6. Fitch states that ratings of infrastructure and project finance obligations on the long-term scale "only consider the obligations' relative vulnerability to default". Fitch assigns Recovery Ratings only to infrastructure and project finance obligations, on request; they "typically do not influence the obligation rating". Fitch also assigns Issuer Default Ratings to certain infrastructure and project finance entities. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
7. Fitch's long-term scale definitions include the following. 'AAA' denotes "the lowest expectation of default risk". 'BBB' indicates default risk expectations are "currently low", with payment capacity "adequate" but more likely to be impaired by adverse conditions. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
8. Fitch rating decisions are taken by rating committee, which "may adjust, or vary, the application of" criteria, subject to disclosure. [confidence: medium; paraphrase of the NRSRO procedures text, exact scope of the variation rule not reviewed] [source: 1]

### Moody's and S&P: what was verifiable

9. Moody's long-term rating scale runs from Aaa through Aa1-Aa3, A1-A3, Baa1-Baa3, Ba1-Ba3, B1-B3 and Caa1-Caa3 to Ca and C, as shown in its 2026 Form NRSRO performance statistics. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
10. S&P's 2026 Form NRSRO Exhibit 2 states that S&P "considers Criteria to be its methodologies". It describes a governance structure (a Methodologies group and Criteria subject-matter experts) under a Methodology Policy, but does not list sector criteria titles. [confidence: high] [source: 2]

## Financing and structure details (parties, tranches, amounts, tenors, guarantees, where verified)

Not a transaction sheet. The structural features Fitch names in its debt structure analysis are the levers that move a rating, and writers can map them to the term sheet in Chapter 30. They are: payment waterfall ranking, refinancing risk, covenant package, structural features, hedging, liquidity and reserves.

## What went wrong or right, and why (well-established analysis only, attributed to named sources)

- Fitch's framing makes two points. Completion risk is assessed separately, and only "where material to the rating". Stresses are run against the forecast period, and debt structure protections are assessed for their ability to ensure timely payment. This reflects the general project-rating logic that construction-phase and operating-phase risks are analysed separately, with structure as mitigant. [source: 1] No named commentator's critique of agency methodologies was verified for this sheet.

## Teaching angles by chapter (for each listed chapter: the transferable lesson and the angle to take, 2–4 sentences)

- **Chapter 30 (Project bonds and ratings):** Teach the rating as the agency's view of default probability, built in layers. First qualitative risk factors (completion, operation, revenue, debt structure, counterparty), then quantitative metrics (DSCR, LLCR, PLCR, leverage) under base and stress cases, then country and event caps. Use Fitch's verified architecture (a master criteria document plus cross-sector completion and counterparty criteria plus sector criteria) as the worked template. Tell the reader that S&P and Moody's follow analogous structures, and that the current document must always be read from the agency's criteria page. For the Case P refinancing bond option, show how changing reserves, covenants or tenor changes the metrics the agency weighs.

## Do not state (claims commonly repeated but unverified or wrong, and uncertain details)

- **S&P methodology titles and dates.** For example, that S&P replaced its 2013-2014 "Project Finance Framework / Construction / Operations / Transaction Structure" methodologies with a "General Project Finance Rating Methodology" and a "Sector-Specific Project Finance Rating Methodology" in December 2022. This is widely believed but not verified here (spglobal.com was inaccessible).
- **S&P scoring mechanics.** For example, an operations-phase business assessment on a 1-12 scale, DSCR-based preliminary stand-alone credit profile, downside-case resilience adjustments, or the final rating being the lower of construction and operations SACPs. Not verified.
- **Moody's methodology titles and dates.** For example "Generic Project Finance Methodology", "Power Generation Projects Methodology", "Privately Financed Public Infrastructure (PFI/PPP/P3) Projects", and "Construction Risk in Privately Financed Public Infrastructure Projects". Their publication dates and scorecard weights were not verified (ratings.moodys.com inaccessible).
- **Moody's or S&P project finance default and recovery studies.** Do not quote any figures (for example cumulative default rates or ultimate recovery rates for project finance bank loans); none were verified.
- **Fitch updates after March 2026.** These are not captured. The 2026 NRSRO filing covers only 2025 publications. Fitch criteria published in 2024 were also not captured (the 2025 filing's list could not be extracted).
- **Specific Fitch DSCR thresholds by rating level** (for example in Renewable or Thermal criteria). Not verified.
- Do not state that any agency uses DSCR "alone". Fitch lists multiple metrics.
