# SunEdison bankruptcy and TerraForm Power

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Brookfield Renewable's acquisition of the remaining TerraForm Power shares, expected to close July 31, 2020; core coverage is 2014–2017)

## Summary

SunEdison, Inc. (formerly MEMC Electronic Materials) was a US renewable energy developer. It sponsored two listed "yieldcos": TerraForm Power (NASDAQ: TERP, IPO July 2014) and TerraForm Global (NASDAQ: GLBL, IPO August 2015). The model had three parts. SunEdison developed projects, dropped operating assets down to the yieldcos, and kept control through high-vote Class B shares and incentive distribution rights (IDRs). The yieldcos raised public equity and corporate debt to buy assets, promising growing dividends. In 2015 SunEdison expanded fast with debt. It bought First Wind (USD 2.4 billion including earnouts, closed January 29, 2015, jointly with TerraForm Power), funded partly by a USD 410 million margin loan secured on TERP shares. It also agreed in July 2015 to buy Vivint Solar, with TerraForm Power to take the operating rooftop portfolio. By September 30, 2015 SunEdison reported total assets of USD 20.7 billion and total liabilities of USD 16.1 billion on a consolidated basis. As yieldco share prices fell, margin-loan collateral calls followed, and the Vivint deal collapsed (terminated March 7, 2016). SunEdison filed for Chapter 11 in the Southern District of New York on April 21, 2016, with up to USD 300 million of new DIP financing. The yieldcos were not part of the filing, but their audited 2015 accounts carried a going-concern explanatory note. They also faced late filings and NASDAQ delisting threats, governance fights and claims from the SunEdison estate. In March 2017 Brookfield Asset Management agreed to become TerraForm Power's sponsor and 51% owner (closed October 16, 2017), under a settlement with the SunEdison debtors, and to buy TerraForm Global for USD 5.10 per share in cash (closed December 28, 2017). SunEdison's plan was confirmed July 28, 2017 and became effective December 29, 2017. In 2020 Brookfield Renewable acquired the rest of TERP.

## Verified facts

1. TerraForm Power's IPO took place on July 23, 2014. Its Class A shares began trading on NASDAQ under "TERP" on July 18, 2014. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. After the IPO, TerraForm Power owned 30.3% of Terra LLC (the operating holding LLC) and consolidated it. SunEdison held a 63.9% interest and Riverstone 5.8%, shown as non-controlling interests. TerraForm Power received USD 533.5 million of net IPO proceeds, plus USD 65.0 million from private placements at the IPO price. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. The structure was an "Up-C." TerraForm Power, Inc. (the listed company) held Class A units of Terra LLC. SunEdison held Class B units with corresponding high-vote Class B common stock, and SunEdison affiliates held the IDRs of Terra LLC. The IDRs entitled their holders to a growing share of distributions above set thresholds. Class A and Class B1 units had a Minimum Quarterly Distribution of USD 0.2257 per unit. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. At the IPO TerraForm Power signed a Support Agreement. Under it SunEdison had to offer qualifying "Call Right Projects" from its pipeline representing at least USD 175.0 million of cash available for distribution. An Intercompany Agreement added call rights over certain First Wind pipeline projects. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
5. After a January 22, 2016 conversion and sale of 12,161,844 shares, SunEdison held 48,202,310 Class B shares. Those gave it 84.1% of the voting interest in TerraForm Power but only 34.5% of the economic interest in Terra LLC. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
6. TerraForm Power raised more equity on January 22, 2015 (net USD 390.6 million) and June 24, 2015 (net USD 667.6 million). Part of each raise was used to repurchase Class B units from SunEdison. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
7. Terra Operating LLC issued USD 800.0 million of 5.875% senior notes due 2023 on January 28, 2015 (at 99.214%) to help fund First Wind. It added USD 150.0 million in June 2015 and issued USD 300.0 million of 6.125% senior notes due 2025 on July 17, 2015 to help fund the Invenergy Wind acquisition. The indentures required timely SEC filing. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
8. On January 29, 2015, SunEdison and a TerraForm Power subsidiary completed the acquisition of First Wind Holdings for total consideration of USD 2.4 billion. That comprised USD 1.0 billion upfront, including USD 361.0 million of assumed debt, and an expected USD 510.0 million of earnouts over two and a half years. TerraForm bought 521 MW of operating assets (500 MW wind, 21 MW solar). SunEdison bought the development platform (1.6 GW of pipeline and backlog). [confidence: high] [source: 4]
9. On January 29, 2015, a SunEdison subsidiary borrowed USD 410 million in term loans under a margin loan agreement, with Deutsche Bank AG as administrative agent and a SunEdison guarantee, to fund the First Wind acquisition. The loan required a loan-to-value ratio no higher than 40%, based on the value of TERP Class A stock. SunEdison had to deposit USD 152 million of extra collateral in Q3 2015 and another USD 91 million in October 2015. The rate at September 30, 2015 was 6.25%. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
10. As of September 30, 2015, SunEdison reported consolidated total assets of USD 20,714 million and total liabilities of USD 16,141 million. Total debt was USD 11,672 million, much of it non-recourse yieldco and project debt. The net loss for the nine months was USD 1,008 million. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
11. On July 20, 2015, TerraForm Power obtained commitments for a bridge facility of up to USD 960.0 million to buy operating assets it expected to acquire from SunEdison in connection with SunEdison's purchase of Vivint Solar. On December 9, 2015 the commitment was cut to up to USD 795.0 million when the purchase agreement was amended. Vivint Solar terminated the SunEdison/Vivint merger agreement on March 7, 2016, which ended TerraForm Power's obligation to buy 479.3 MW (estimated USD 814.8 million). [confidence: high] [source: 3]
12. On November 20, 2015, SunEdison exercised its designation rights to reshape TerraForm Power's board. TerraForm's CEO (Carlos Domenech Zornoza) and CFO (Alejandro Hernandez) were removed. Members of the Corporate Governance and Conflicts Committee were replaced, and three directors resigned, citing disagreement with these actions. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
13. On April 21, 2016, SunEdison and certain subsidiaries filed for Chapter 11 in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York. SunEdison had secured commitments for up to USD 300 million of new-money DIP financing from first and second lien lenders. TerraForm Power and TerraForm Global were not part of the filing. Advisors were Rothschild, McKinsey Recovery & Transformation Services and Skadden. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
14. SunEdison pledged its Class B shares, Class B units and the IDRs to its lenders as security under the DIP facility, the first and second lien credit facilities and the second lien secured notes. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
15. TerraForm Power's 2015 financial statements carried a going-concern explanatory note. The note cited the risk that TERP would be substantively consolidated with SunEdison in the bankruptcy, along with project-level default risks. The FY2015 Form 10-K was filed late, on December 5, 2016. TERP's 2016 Forms 10-Q were also delayed, and NASDAQ granted an extension to March 10, 2017 to regain compliance. Material weaknesses in internal control were reported as of December 31, 2015. [confidence: high] [source: 3, 7]

The announced terms were: Brookfield to own 51%; a price per Class A share of USD 11.46, derived from a pre-settlement price of USD 12.00, structured as a USD 1.94 special dividend plus either USD 9.52 in cash or one post-closing share (subject to proration); a USD 500 million sponsor equity line; a ROFO portfolio of approximately 3,500 MW; implied equity value of USD 1.7 billion; and implied enterprise value of USD 6.6 billion. Brookfield would receive management fees and IDRs under a Master Services Agreement. [confidence: high] [source: 8]

Under the Settlement Agreement, SunEdison exchanged its 48,202,310 Class B units for Class A shares and received extra Class A shares to hold 36.9% of the fully diluted share count before the merger. It transferred the IDRs to a Brookfield affiliate. All agreements between TerraForm Power and the SunEdison debtors were deemed rejected without further liability (with limited exceptions), and intercompany claims were released. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

Under it Brookfield Renewable would acquire all TERP shares other than the approximately 62% already owned by Brookfield Renewable and its affiliates, with closing expected July 31, 2020. ## Financing and structure details

- Sponsor-yieldco architecture (TERP): the listed Class A company owned an economic minority of Terra LLC, while the sponsor held high-vote Class B shares and units. The sponsor also held IDRs and a Support Agreement promising pipeline drop-downs (Call Right Projects) worth at least USD 175 million of CAFD. Voting control (84.1%) far exceeded economic ownership (34.5%) by early 2016. [source: 3]
- Yieldco funding: public equity raises plus high-yield senior notes at Terra Operating LLC (5.875% due 2023; 6.125% due 2025), a revolver and term loans, with project-level non-recourse debt beneath. The indentures carried reporting covenants, so late SEC filings threatened defaults. [source: 3]
- Sponsor holdco leverage: a USD 410 million margin loan secured on TERP shares, with a 40% LTV trigger. Falling TERP prices forced USD 243 million of cash collateral postings in 2015. SunEdison also pledged its Class B shares, units and IDRs to its corporate lenders. [source: 3, 5]
- Bankruptcy financing: up to USD 300 million new-money DIP from first and second lien lenders. [source: 6]
- Resolution: a new sponsor (Brookfield) bought 51% of TERP and the IDRs. A settlement released estate claims, and SunEdison's estate monetized its stake. GLBL was sold outright for cash. [source: 1, 8, 9]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The sponsor's financial distress spread into separate listed vehicles through control and contracts, not through guarantees. TERP's own 10-K identifies the main risks: substantive consolidation into the SunEdison bankruptcy, loss of sponsor services and pipeline, governance conflict from SunEdison's 84.1% voting control, and covenant risk from late filings. [source: 3]
- The yieldco growth model depended on rising share prices. Drop-downs and acquisitions were funded with new equity, and the sponsor's margin loan was secured on that same equity. When TERP's price fell, collateral calls drained sponsor liquidity (USD 152 million plus USD 91 million) just as it was trying to close the Vivint deal. [source: 3, 5]
- What worked: the project-level non-recourse structure and the yieldcos' separateness largely held. Neither yieldco was consolidated into the bankruptcy, and both were sold as going concerns, letting the estate monetize them. [source: 1, 3, 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 32 (Equity): Use TERP as the reference case for listed yieldcos. Cover the Up-C structure, high-vote Class B shares, IDRs, call-right pipelines and why they create sponsor–public holder conflicts (84.1% votes against 34.5% economics). Teach that yieldco "cost of equity" depends on share price and growth expectations. When the sponsor needs liquidity, the vehicle's independence, governance protections and ring-fencing become the real equity story.
- Chapter 63 (Refinancing, repricing and secondary sales): Use the margin loan to teach holdco leverage secured on listed shares: LTV triggers, cash collateral calls and the reflexive loop between share price and leverage. Use the Brookfield transactions to teach a distressed secondary sale. A new sponsor buys control plus IDRs, a settlement releases estate claims, and a cash/stock election with proration sets the price. Contrast this with the outright cash sale of GLBL.

## Do not state

- That TerraForm Power or TerraForm Global filed for bankruptcy. They did not.
- That SunEdison had "USD 16.1 billion of debt." USD 16.1 billion was total consolidated liabilities at September 30, 2015. Total debt was USD 11.7 billion, much of it non-recourse yieldco and project debt.
- Specific share price peaks and troughs, market capitalization figures, or percentage declines for SUNE, TERP or GLBL. None were verified here.
- The Vivint headline value split. The July 2015 deal is widely reported at about USD 2.2 billion and TERP's asset purchase at USD 922 million (later amended to about USD 799 million). Only the TERP bridge amounts (USD 960 million, then USD 795 million) and the USD 814.8 million / 479.3 MW commitment are verified from filings.
- The details of SEC investigations, class action settlements or the outcome of the Domenech whistleblower complaint. None were verified.
- The amount of claims recovered by SunEdison creditors, and the total value of asset sales in the bankruptcy (a reported figure of "more than USD 2.3 billion" was not verified against court filings).
- TerraForm Global's IPO size, and the price paid in the 2020 Brookfield Renewable buy-in.
