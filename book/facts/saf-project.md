# Sustainable aviation fuel project financing: Montana Renewables (Calumet) and its USD 1.44 billion DOE loan guarantee

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Calumet 8-K of 1 September 2026 announcing the amended DOE loan guarantee and revised MaxSAF plan; Calumet 10-Q for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, filed 7 August 2026)

Cross-references: facts/t-us-tax-credits.md (the broader OBBBA changes and transferability); facts/t-hydrogen-support.md (45V, relevant because MRL makes renewable hydrogen on site).

## Summary

Montana Renewables, LLC (MRL) is an unrestricted, non-recourse subsidiary of Calumet, Inc. In 2022–2023 it converted part of Calumet's Great Falls, Montana refinery to produce renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) from seed oils, tallow and similar feedstocks, and it was among North America's largest SAF producers in 2024. In August 2022 Warburg Pincus bought USD 250 million of preferred units in MRL's parent, Montana Renewables Holdings LLC (MRHL). On 10 January 2025 MRL signed a USD 1.44 billion loan guaranteed by the US Department of Energy (DOE). The loan has a 15-year tenor at the US Treasury rate plus 3/8%, with principal and interest deferred until the "MaxSAF" expansion is commissioned. The first tranche of USD 781.8 million was disbursed on 18 February 2025 and used mainly to refinance MRL's existing, more expensive debt. Up to about USD 658 million more was to fund construction towards roughly 300 million gallons a year of SAF capacity by 2028. The plan then came under strain. MRL needed two DOE waivers in 2026 (February and June) of an event of default tied to a "Base Cash Equity Reserve Account". On 1 September 2026 Calumet announced a much smaller expansion that repurposes equipment from the adjacent asphalt refinery: about 200 million gallons a year of SAF by end-2028 for USD 137 million of remaining capex. The DOE commitment was cut to a single final draw of USD 34 million, and the March 2029 first servicing date and December 2039 maturity were unchanged. Federal policy shifted at the same time. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act ended the SAF blenders' credit after 30 September 2025, removed the extra SAF rate under the 45Z clean fuel credit from 2026, and extended 45Z through 2029 with a North American feedstock rule.

## Verified facts

1. MRL is an unrestricted subsidiary of Calumet, Inc. (NASDAQ: CLMT). DOE loan borrowings are obligations of MRL and MRHL only and are non-recourse to Calumet and its restricted subsidiaries. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 4]
2. Calumet reports that in 2023 it commissioned a renewable hydrogen plant, a feedstock pre-treatment unit and a SAF unit at Montana Renewables, making it "one of the largest SAF producers in the western hemisphere in 2024". [confidence: high] [source: 5]
3. On 5 August 2022 MRHL sold 12,500,000 preferred units to an affiliate of Warburg Pincus LLC for USD 250 million. The units carry a minimum return threshold, partly expressed as an internal rate of return. After the fifth anniversary, absent an IPO or change of control, Warburg can initiate an IPO or change-of-control transaction. Calumet accounts for the units as a redeemable noncontrolling interest in temporary equity. [confidence: high (the precise preferred-return formula is truncated in the extract read)] [source: 5]
4. Before the DOE loan, MRL financed itself with a term loan agreed on 19 April 2023 with lenders including I Squared Capital, asset financing arrangements, a revolving credit agreement and a supply and offtake agreement. All were terminated or repaid from the first DOE tranche, and Calumet booked debt extinguishment costs of about USD 34 million (asset financing) and about USD 12 million (term loan). [confidence: high for the facilities and their repayment; medium for the rounded extinguishment amounts, which are truncated in the extract] [source: 4, 5]
5. On 16 October 2024 Calumet announced a DOE Loan Programs Office conditional commitment for a loan guarantee of up to USD 1.44 billion. The planned structure was:
   - an initial tranche of about USD 778 million expected in Q4 2024, with the balance as a delayed-draw construction facility through completion of MaxSAF in 2028;
   - a 15-year tenor at the Treasury rate plus 3/8% (then about 4 3/8%), with debt service deferred until MaxSAF was commissioned;
   - a USD 150 million equity investment at first closing, and MRL retained earnings to maintain 55/45 debt to equity during construction.

   The target capacity was about 300 million gallons a year of SAF and 330 million gallons of SAF plus renewable diesel. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. The Loan Guarantee Agreement was signed on 10 January 2025 between MRL and DOE (as guarantor and loan servicer). The first tranche of USD 781.8 million was disbursed on 18 February 2025, and Calumet made the additional USD 150 million equity investment. Up to about USD 658.0 million remained available as a delayed-draw construction facility. A Sponsor Support Agreement was dated 28 January 2025 among DOE, the sponsors (MRHL, Calumet and WPGG 14 United Aggregator, L.P.) and Citibank, N.A. as collateral agent. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4, 6]
7. The agreement allows up to USD 232.8 million of interest to be capitalised. By 30 June 2026, USD 53.4 million had been capitalised, and the DOE loan balance was USD 835.2 million (USD 815.4 million at 31 December 2025). Cash restricted under the DOE loan is reported separately. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
8. Waivers: on 10 February 2026 and again on 30 June 2026, DOE agreed limited conditional waivers of the event of default under Section 10.01(z) (Base Cash Equity Reserve Account) of the Loan Guarantee Agreement and of the related sponsor obligation (Sponsor Support Agreement §2.02(a)(ii)). The second waiver runs from 11 July 2026 to (but excluding) 1 January 2027. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
9. On 1 September 2026 Calumet announced a revised MaxSAF plan and an amended Loan Guarantee Agreement:
   - about 200 million gallons a year of SAF and 17,000 barrels a day of total product sales by year-end 2028;
   - USD 137 million of remaining project capital, against the USD 1.2 billion "megaproject" originally contemplated;
   - the plan repurposes a hydrotreater, hydrogen plant and naphtha splitter from the adjacent Calumet Montana Refining (asphalt) facility, leased long-term to MRL, and is executed as six smaller projects;
   - Phase 2 DOE funding falls from up to USD 658 million to a single final draw of USD 34 million, with the balance self-funded from MRL earnings and no third-party equity requirement (the original agreement had required full project equity to be committed before Phase 2);
   - the tenor, the Treasury + 3/8% rate, deferral to commissioning, the March 2029 first servicing date and the December 2039 maturity are unchanged, and debt is to remain below 55% of eligible spending.

   Calumet described the lender as DOE's "Office of Energy Dominance Financing (EDF)". [confidence: high] [source: 3]
10. Operating status per the same release: after the spring 2026 turnaround, MRL was running at a 60 million gallon a year SAF rate. It expected more than 80 million by year-end 2026, more than 120 million by spring 2027 and about 200 million by year-end 2028. The tie-in turnaround is scheduled for Q4 2026. [confidence: high (company statements, forward-looking)] [source: 3]
11. Policy changes in Public Law 119-21 (4 July 2025) relevant to SAF economics:
    - the §6426(k) SAF credit does not apply to any sale or use after 30 September 2025;
    - for fuel produced after 31 December 2025, §45Z no longer has a separate higher SAF rate (the special USD 1.75 SAF amount was struck);
    - §45Z fuel must be derived exclusively from feedstock produced or grown in the United States, Mexico or Canada;
    - emissions rates cannot be negative (except animal manure fuels) and exclude indirect land-use change;
    - §45Z is extended to fuel produced through 31 December 2029 (previously 2027). [confidence: high] [source: 7]
12. Treasury and IRS published proposed §45Z regulations on 4 February 2026 (91 FR 5160). [confidence: high (existence and date only)] [source: 8]

## Financing and structure details

- Borrower: Montana Renewables, LLC. Direct parent: Montana Renewables Holdings LLC. Sponsors: MRHL, Calumet, Inc. and WPGG 14 United Aggregator, L.P. (Warburg). Guarantor and loan servicer: US DOE. Collateral agent: Citibank, N.A. [source: 6]
- Debt: a DOE-guaranteed loan of USD 1.44 billion at signing. Tranche 1 was USD 781.8 million (refinancing and recapitalisation). Tranche 2 was up to USD 658 million for construction, amended to USD 34 million. The rate is Treasury + 3/8% over 15 years, with interest capitalisation up to USD 232.8 million until commissioning and the first servicing date in March 2029. [source: 2, 3, 4]
- Equity and quasi-equity: Calumet's USD 150 million at first funding; Warburg's USD 250 million preferred units (2022); and retained MRL earnings to keep debt at or below 55% during construction. [source: 1, 2, 3, 5]
- Ring-fencing: MRL and MRHL are unrestricted subsidiaries and the DOE loan is non-recourse to Calumet. That protected Calumet's other creditors but put the equity-reserve covenant on MRL. [source: 4, 6]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Refinance first, build later: about USD 782 million of the first tranche refinanced costs already incurred and replaced commercial debt, as Calumet disclosed. Most of the guaranteed loan was therefore drawn before any Phase 2 construction. [source: 2, 4]
- Covenant pressure: two DOE waivers in 2026 of the event of default tied to the Base Cash Equity Reserve Account show that the sponsors could not meet a cash-equity reserve requirement on the original timetable. The waiver documents do not say why. [source: 6]
- Re-scoping instead of default: the September 2026 amendment cut both capex and DOE exposure. Calumet's own figures are about 70% of the originally expected benefit for about 15% of the originally expected Phase 2 capital (company claim). The DOE kept the tenor and pricing. [source: 3]
- Policy drift: the changes to the SAF blenders' credit and 45Z (including the end of the SAF premium and the feedstock-origin rule) changed the revenue stack while the project was in development. [source: 7]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 83 (Hydrogen and derivatives, CCS and sustainable fuels):** Use MRL to show how a biofuel project's revenue is stacked from the fuel price plus credits (45Z, state LCFS and RINs; the latter two are not covered in this sheet's sources). One law (OBBBA) can remove a layer. Then show the financing response: a cheap, long, deferred-service public loan, ring-fenced from the parent, with equity-reserve covenants. Close with the 2026 amendment as a case of a lender and sponsor shrinking a project to fit cash flow instead of enforcing.

## Do not state

- That the full USD 1.44 billion was disbursed. Only USD 781.8 million was disbursed, plus capitalised interest; after the September 2026 amendment only USD 34 million more is available.
- That the January 2025 closing press release is dated 2024. The exhibit carries a typographical "January 10, 2024" dateline, but the agreement is dated 10 January 2025.
- Specific offtake contracts, airline customers, LCFS or RIN revenues, or SAF prices (not verified here).
