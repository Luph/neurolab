# Large data-center financings 2024-2025 (e.g., Meta Hyperion JV with Blue Owl)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Meta's 10-Q for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, filed 30 July 2026, including its July 2026 El Paso venture; and Oracle's 10-K for the fiscal year ended 31 May 2026)

## Summary

In October 2025 Meta Platforms and funds managed by Blue Owl Capital formed a joint venture (the "Venture") to develop the Hyperion AI data-center campus in Richland Parish, Louisiana. Blue Owl funds took 80% and Meta 20%. The Venture's total estimated development cost is about USD 27 billion, funded pro rata by the members. Meta contributed about USD 4.3 billion of land and construction in progress and took out a one-time cash distribution of about USD 2.55–2.6 billion. Blue Owl contributed USD 7.0 billion in cash. The debt was raised in a private securities offering anchored by PIMCO and other bond investors, with Morgan Stanley as sole bookrunner. The notes, issued by Beignet Investor LLC, are senior secured Rule 144A notes maturing in 2049. Meta acts as construction, administrative and property manager. It will lease the buildings from 2029 under four-year initial terms renewable up to 20 years, with an aggregate initial lease commitment of about USD 12.31 billion. It also gives a residual value guarantee (RVG) whose threshold starts at about USD 28 billion and declines over time. Meta does not consolidate the Venture, because it lacks power over the activities that most affect its economics (chiefly remarketing). Its disclosed maximum exposure to loss was USD 46.03 billion at 30 June 2026. The deal is a template for hyperscaler off-balance-sheet build-to-suit finance: debt investors underwrite a short lease plus an investment-grade residual guarantee, not a long-term contract. Contrasting 2024–2026 examples are Blue Owl/Crusoe build-to-suit JVs leased to Oracle in Abilene, Texas; Oracle's USD 260 billion of not-yet-commenced data-center leases with 15–19-year terms; and Meta's July 2026 agreement for a second 20% venture in El Paso with a USD 13 billion RVG.

The deciding factor was that remarketing decisions (negotiating with future tenants and selling individual properties) were judged to have the most significant impact on economic performance, and Meta does not direct them. Meta accounts for its interest under the equity method. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4]

Its maximum exposure to loss (equity carrying value plus lease commitments, estimated future funding and the maximum RVG threshold) was USD 45.95 billion and USD 46.03 billion respectively. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 4]

## Financing and structure details

- Vehicle: an unconsolidated JV (VIE). Blue Owl funds 80% (cash equity USD 7.0 billion at close); Meta 20% (USD 4.3 billion in-kind; USD 2.55–2.6 billion distributed back). [source: 1, 2]
- Debt: private 144A senior secured notes via Beignet Investor LLC, maturing 2049, anchored by PIMCO; Morgan Stanley sole bookrunner. [source: 3, 5]
- Credit support to debt: Meta operating leases (four-year initial terms from 2029, renewable to 20 years; about USD 12.31 billion initial commitment) plus a declining RVG for 16 years (threshold initially about USD 28 billion). [source: 1, 2]
- Sponsor services: Meta is construction manager and property manager, so it carries delivery execution without owning the asset. [source: 2]
- Accounting outcome: Meta records an equity-method investment (USD 2.92 billion at 30 June 2026), not the asset or the debt. Maximum loss exposure disclosed: USD 46.03 billion. [source: 4]
- Comparators: Oracle's long leases (15–19 years) against developer-owned JVs (Abilene); Meta's El Paso JV (20%, USD 13 billion RVG). [source: 4, 10, 11]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The deal is too recent for outcomes. The only established analysis is what the issuers' own filings disclose. Meta's filings set out the logic: the Venture gives "strategic optionality and flexibility" as AI demand and technology develop. Meta keeps operational control through the services agreements, while short lease terms and a declining RVG limit its fixed commitment. [source: 2, 4]
- What lenders are underwriting: debt service depends on Meta renewing four-year leases or on the RVG covering any value shortfall over 16 years. The credit is therefore closer to Meta corporate risk plus residual-value risk on specialized buildings than to a classic contracted-cash-flow project. Meta's disclosure that its maximum exposure (about USD 46 billion) far exceeds its equity carrying value (about USD 3 billion) shows how much risk stays with the sponsor off balance sheet. [source: 2, 4]
- Contrast: Oracle takes the opposite approach, committing to long 15–19-year leases that are disclosed but sit off balance sheet until commencement. [source: 11]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 82 (Telecoms, fiber, towers and data centers): Teach the hyperscaler build-to-suit JV as a project-finance variant. The angle is to compare four ways a lender can get comfortable with a specialized asset: (a) a long, creditworthy lease (Oracle's 15–19 years); (b) a short lease plus an RVG (Meta Hyperion); (c) developer-owned JV build-to-suit (Crusoe/Blue Owl for Oracle); and (d) corporate bonds (Meta's USD 25 billion May 2026 issue). Map who carries construction, power-supply (Entergy CCGTs online 2028–2029) and remarketing risk in each.
- Ch 88 (The frontier): Use Hyperion to frame the debates over AI-driven capex and private credit. Points to cover: how off-balance-sheet structures shift accounting leverage without removing economic exposure (USD 46 billion maximum loss); the dependence of residual values on technology obsolescence; the scale of private-credit and insurance-capital demand (a PIMCO-anchored 2049 private bond); and power procurement as a gating item. Teach readers to read VIE and commitment notes, not headlines.

## Do not state

- The bond size (widely reported as about USD 27.3 billion), the equity amount (reported as about USD 2.5 billion), the coupon (press commonly cites 6.58%, while an N-PORT holdings record shows 6.85%) or the credit rating (reported A+). None was verified against an offering document or rating report. Say "a private bond offering anchored by PIMCO, maturing 2049".
- That it was "the largest private credit deal ever" or "the largest-ever 144A bond". These are press characterizations and were not verified.
- That Meta "guarantees the bonds". Meta provides lease commitments and an RVG to the Venture, not a debt guarantee.
- That the RVG "covers the entire two-decade period". Meta's filings say the first 16 years, with a threshold that declines.
- The campus's compute capacity (2 GW by 2030; 5 GW eventual) as a fact. These come from executive statements and press, not filings.
- Total debt sizes for the Abilene/Stargate financings, the Vantage, xAI or other 2025 mega-financings. Not verified here.
- That the July 2026 increase to above USD 50 billion is funded by the Blue Owl JV. Not disclosed.
- The identity of Meta's El Paso venture partner. Not disclosed in the 10-Q.
