# COVID-19 and toll roads: traffic collapse, force majeure and relief (Transurban, 407 ETR, Atlantia/Abertis)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: end of the 407 ETR COVID-19 force majeure with the 1 February 2024 toll increase; core coverage 2020–2021)

## Summary

COVID-19 lockdowns in spring 2020 produced the sharpest traffic falls toll roads have recorded, but the falls were brief and recovery was fast once restrictions eased. That pattern separated toll roads from airports, whose passenger traffic fell by two-thirds or more for the year. Transurban's group average daily traffic (ADT) fell 34.6% in the June 2020 quarter and 8.6% over its year to June 2020. Melbourne fell 44.1% in the quarter and the US Express Lanes 51.5% in ADT (76.7% in toll revenue). Transurban kept its BBB+/Baa1/A- corporate ratings, raised AUD 8.6 billion of debt and added AUD 1.5 billion of bilateral facilities to strengthen liquidity. It paid a reduced FY20 distribution of 47.0 cents. In Quebec, A25 tolls were suspended from 25 March to 24 May 2020 with compensation agreed with the Ministry of Transport. Atlantia reported 2020 ADT falls of 27.3% in Italy (ASPI), 30.8% for Abertis in Spain and 24.6% in France, against 76.8% for Rome airports. At Toronto's 407 ETR, Ferrovial's factbook shows 2020 revenue down about 40% and EBITDA down about 43% from 2019. Ontario agreed that COVID-19 was a force majeure event under the concession, which suspended "Schedule 22" congestion payments until traffic regained 2017–2019 levels or 407 ETR raised tolls. The force majeure ran until the February 2024 rate increase. Dividends from 407 ETR were reduced, and other Ferrovial assets (Heathrow, AGS) stopped paying dividends under lender waivers. The lessons: liquidity and distribution lock-ups protected senior debt; contractual traffic thresholds needed force majeure relief; and relief by statute or negotiation (Quebec's compensation, Spain's RDL 8/2020 in facts/covid-transport.md, Italy's RAB-based reset for ASPI) mattered as much as the original clauses.

## Verified facts

1. Transurban FY20 (year to 30 June 2020): group ADT fell 8.6% and proportional toll revenue fell 3.4% to AUD 2,492 million. Proportional EBITDA before significant items fell 6.4% to AUD 1,888 million. Transurban reported a statutory loss of AUD 153 million. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Transurban June 2020 quarter, change against the prior-year quarter: Sydney toll revenue -16.1% and ADT -25.5%; Melbourne -38.2% and -44.1%; Brisbane -24.1% and -26.5%; North America -76.7% and -51.5%; group -32.6% and -34.6%. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
3. Transurban said traffic impacts were observable from early March 2020 and improved from mid-April as restrictions eased. Renewed restrictions held back Melbourne, and the Greater Washington Express Lanes recovered more slowly. Large vehicles proved more resilient than cars (Melbourne FY20 car traffic -14.6%, large vehicles -0.7%). [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
4. Tolls on Transurban's A25 (Montreal) were suspended from 25 March to 24 May 2020, with compensation agreed with Quebec's Ministry of Transport. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. Transurban's FY20 distribution totalled 47.0 cents per security (AUD 1.3 billion). It guided that FY21 distributions would be in line with Free Cash excluding Capital Releases. Some distributions from non-100%-owned assets were deferred. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
6. Transurban raised AUD 8.6 billion of debt in FY20, including an extra AUD 1.5 billion of corporate bilateral facilities in the second half "to further strengthen available Group liquidity". Its corporate ratings were BBB+/Baa1/A- (S&P/Moody's/Fitch) at both June 2019 and June 2020. FFO/debt moved from 8.7% to 7.0% and gearing from 32.0% to 35.8%. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
7. Atlantia's 2020 preliminary results (5 February 2021) show average daily traffic for 1 January to 31 December 2020 against 2019: Italy (ASPI) -27.3%, Spain (Abertis) -30.8%, France (Abertis) -24.6%, Brazil -8.8%, Chile -26.3% (also affected by protests since late 2019) and Mexico (Abertis) -12.1%. Passenger traffic fell 76.8% at Aeroporti di Roma and 68.4% at Nice. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
8. Atlantia said Abertis sought economic compensation for business disruptions from grantors, cut costs by about EUR 140 million and delayed capex by about EUR 250 million in 2020, and issued EUR 2.0 billion of hybrid bonds (November 2020 and January 2021). ADR's airport concession was extended by two years to 2046 by law, and ADR invoked its contract's force majeure rebalancing protections. ASPI's new draft economic and financial plan included a RAB-based tariff with an additional charge for 2020 and later COVID-19 revenue losses, subject to government approval. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
9. 407 ETR (Ferrovial factbook, C$ million, 2019 then 2020): total revenue 1,505 to 909; EBITDA 1,309 to 740; dividends 1,050 to 563. The trips series fell from 125.1 to 71.5, and average revenue per trip rose from C$11.86 to C$12.55. Computed changes: revenue -39.6%, EBITDA -43.5%, dividends -46.4%, trips -42.8%. [confidence: high] (figures; the unit of the trips series is not stated clearly in the factbook) [source: 4]
10. Under the 407 ETR concession, if annual traffic on segments falls below thresholds in Schedule 22 while tolls exceed a toll threshold, payments are due to Ontario. In April 2020, 407 ETR paid CAD 1,775,000 for 2019. Ferrovial states that Ontario agreed COVID-19 was a force majeure event under the concession. No payments were therefore due for 2020 and until the event ended, which would be when traffic reached the 2017–2019 average or 407 ETR raised tolls. The force majeure continued through 2021–2023. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
11. 407 ETR announced on 29 December 2023 a new toll schedule effective 1 February 2024, ending the force majeure. Schedule 22 payments could therefore apply from 2025, with a potential first payment in early 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 4, 5]
12. Ferrovial states that, because of COVID-19, 407 ETR paid reduced dividends of EUR 164 million in 2021 and EUR 237 million in 2022. Heathrow and AGS (airports) paid none in 2021 or 2022. HAH obtained a waiver of the Heathrow Finance interest cover covenant in 2021 that bars dividends until the regulatory asset ratio falls below 87.5%, and AGS amended and extended its facility with a dividend block. [confidence: high] [source: 5]

## Financing and structure details

- Liquidity buffers: pre-emptive bank facilities (Transurban AUD 1.5 billion bilateral) and hybrid capital (Abertis) bridged the trough. [source: 2, 3]
- Distribution tests: cash was trapped at asset level (deferred distributions from Transurban's non-100% assets; 407 ETR reduced dividends; dividend blocks at Heathrow and AGS under waivers). [source: 2, 5]
- Contractual relief: force majeure for traffic-threshold payments (407 ETR); compensation for government-ordered toll suspension (A25); statutory or regulatory rebalancing (Spain RDL 8/2020; ASPI RAB charge; ADR's two-year extension). [source: 1, 3, 5]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Right: Transurban's own data show toll-road traffic recovering quickly after restrictions, unlike airports (Atlantia: ADR -76.8% for 2020 against -24.6% to -30.8% for European motorways). Diversified, essential-use urban networks kept investment-grade corporate ratings unchanged through June 2020. [source: 1, 2, 3]
- Pressure points: assets with high commuter or managed-lane exposure fell hardest (US Express Lanes -76.7% in June-quarter revenue), and trucks held up better than cars. [source: 1]
- Contract design: threshold-based payments to grantors, such as 407 ETR's Schedule 22, became live risks once traffic fell and depended on the grantor agreeing to force majeure treatment. [source: 5]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 79 (Toll roads, bridges and tunnels): Use the June 2020 quarter figures to stress-test Case T. A 35–50% one-quarter traffic fall with fast recovery is survivable if there is liquidity and distributions are locked up, and fatal if the senior debt was sized with no reserve. Then teach three relief channels: force majeure (407 ETR), grantor compensation for ordered suspensions (A25), and statutory or regulatory rebalancing (Spain; ASPI RAB). Pair with facts/covid-transport.md and the ratings sheets.
