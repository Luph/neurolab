# UK alternative fibre networks: CityFibre's financing (2021–2025) and altnet consolidation and distress (2024–2026)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: CMA interim report on nexfibre/Substantial, 2 October 2026; CityFibre sale of Entanet, 1 July 2026; CityFibre/BDUK Project Gigabit re-scope, 26 May 2026)

Cross-references: facts/t-digital-norms.md (US fibre ABS terms, a refinancing route UK altnets have not yet used at scale; see Do not state).

## Summary

The UK broadband market is built around Openreach (BT) and Virgin Media O2's cable network. Since about 2018, dozens of "altnets" have raised private equity and debt to build full fibre (FTTP). CityFibre is the largest independent wholesale-only platform. It raised about GBP 1.425 billion of equity in 2021–2022 (Mubadala, Interogo, existing owners Antin and Goldman Sachs). In April 2022 it closed a GBP 4.9 billion bank debt package. Seven underwriters (NatWest, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole CIB, BBVA, Intesa Sanpaolo, ING and SEB) were joined by ABN AMRO, Lloyds, M&G and the new UK Infrastructure Bank as core lenders. The aim was to fund a build to about 8 million premises. As build costs, interest rates and slow take-up squeezed the sector, CityFibre turned consolidator. It bought Lit Fibre (completed May 2024, a share deal) and Connexin's network (March 2025; PATRIZIA's fund took CityFibre shares). In July 2025 it agreed a GBP 2.3 billion round: GBP 500 million of new equity, a GBP 960 million expansion of existing debt with the National Wealth Fund among the lenders, and an GBP 800 million accordion for acquisitions. Its 2025 results show the operating turn: revenue GBP 170 million, adjusted EBITDA GBP 29 million, 848,000 customers on 4.7 million premises passed, and more than 20% penetration. The distress side is visible in the CMA's 2026 inquiry into nexfibre's purchase of Substantial (Netomnia, Brsk and YouFibre). The CMA found that Substantial's "financial outlook has deteriorated" and that its owners ran a 2025 sale process with nexfibre and CityFibre as bidders. On 2 October 2026 the CMA provisionally found that the deal may substantially lessen wholesale competition, with CityFibre as the likely buyer otherwise.

## Verified facts

### CityFibre financing

1. On 16 September 2021 CityFibre announced a GBP 1.125 billion capital raise. It included new equity from Mubadala Investment Company and Interogo Holding, joining existing investors Antin Infrastructure Partners and the infrastructure business of Goldman Sachs Asset Management. Separately, CityFibre secured a GBP 300 million extension of its existing GBP 820 million bank facilities (Crédit Agricole CIB and National Australia Bank joining). Its April 2022 release describes the GBP 1.125 billion as "equity investments". Mubadala's was the first capital deployed under the infrastructure vertical of the UAE–UK Sovereign Investment Partnership. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. On 21 March 2022 Mubadala committed a further GBP 300 million of equity, building on its initial GBP 500 million (September 2021). CityFibre said this took the total raised in six months to GBP 1.425 billion, then the largest UK full-fibre financing. At that point CityFibre had passed 1.5 million homes and was running a GBP 4 billion programme to reach up to 8 million homes by 2025. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. CityFibre then confirmed a GBP 4.9 billion debt package (press release dated 25 April 2022):
   - underwritten by NatWest, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole CIB, BBVA, Intesa Sanpaolo, ING and SEB;
   - ABN AMRO, Lloyds Bank, M&G Investments and the UK Infrastructure Bank (UKIB) joined as core lenders;
   - NatWest and Société Générale were financial advisers, and Latham & Watkins legal adviser;
   - the package was said to fully fund the rollout to 8 million homes, 800,000 businesses, 400,000 public-sector sites and 250,000 5G access points, and to let CityFibre increase its participation in BDUK's Project Gigabit;
   - UKIB called itself a "cornerstone investor". [confidence: high for content; medium for the exact date] [source: 3]
4. Lit Fibre: completed 14 May 2024 as a share-based acquisition from Newlight Partners, which became a minority shareholder. Lit had a footprint of more than 220,000 premises, expected to reach up to 300,000, and about 10,000 retail customers. CityFibre called it "the first of several deals" it expected over two years. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
5. Connexin: on 24 March 2025 CityFibre announced it would acquire Connexin's full-fibre infrastructure (more than 80,000 premises built and 20,000 in progress, up to 185,000 potential, centred on Hull and East Riding), plus a Project Gigabit contract for over 34,000 premises. Connexin's shareholder, PATRIZIA's European Infrastructure Fund II, became a minority shareholder in CityFibre. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
6. FY2024 (released 11 February 2025): revenue GBP 134 million (+34%); adjusted EBITDA GBP 5 million, CityFibre's first full year of profitability since its scale rollout; 518,000 live customers; more than 4.3 million premises passed; take-up over 40% in mature cohorts; nine BDUK lots with subsidies over GBP 865 million; a long-term agreement for Sky to launch on its network in 2025. [confidence: high (company-reported)] [source: 6]
7. On 14 July 2025 CityFibre agreed a GBP 2.3 billion financing round with shareholders and existing lenders:
   - GBP 500 million of new equity from Goldman Sachs Alternatives (infrastructure), Antin, Mubadala and Interogo;
   - a committed GBP 960 million expansion of existing debt facilities, with lenders including ABN AMRO, BBVA, Crédit Agricole CIB, ING, Intesa Sanpaolo IMI CIB, Lloyds, the National Wealth Fund, NatWest, SEB and Société Générale;
   - an GBP 800 million accordion "to help drive… the acquisition of full fibre network assets". [confidence: high] [source: 7]
8. FY2025 (released 22 January 2026; management accounts pending audit): revenue GBP 170 million (+27%); consumer revenue GBP 114 million; adjusted EBITDA GBP 29 million (+480%); 330,000 consumer connections in the year and 848,000 in total; 4.7 million premises passed and 4.5 million ready for service; penetration above 20% of the consumer footprint, targeted to exceed 30% by end-2026. Sky launched across the network in July 2025. [confidence: high (company-reported)] [source: 8]
9. On 26 May 2026 CityFibre and BDUK agreed to re-scope CityFibre's Project Gigabit contracts because commercial build had reached many subsidised premises. CityFibre now expects 450,000 rural premises by 2030, of which 226,000 are subsidised. It had already delivered nearly 150,000, including over 70,000 subsidised. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
10. On 1 July 2026 CityFibre agreed to sell Entanet (its wholesale aggregator) to Tom O'Hagan, founder of Virtual1, to focus only on services over its own network. Entanet becomes a wholesale customer. [confidence: high] [source: 10]

### Consolidation and distress: nexfibre / Substantial (CMA)

11. Parties:
    - nexfibre (Opal JVco Limited) is a wholesale-only FTTP joint venture of Liberty Global, Telefónica (together the owners of Virgin Media O2, VMO2) and InfraVia. VMO2 is its only customer, and its FTTP network covers about 2.6 million premises;
    - Substantial Topco Limited owns Netomnia and Brsk (network builders) and YouFibre (a retail ISP);
    - Substantial's UK turnover exceeds GBP 100 million. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
12. CMA process: invitation to comment from 23 April 2026; Phase 1 launched 29 June 2026; referred on 1 July 2026 to an in-depth (Phase 2) investigation under the fast-track procedure at the parties' request; statutory deadline 15 December 2026. The CMA accepted interim undertakings from Substantial on 17 July 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
13. Interim report summary (2 October 2026):
    - a sale process for Substantial was held in 2025, with nexfibre and CityFibre as bidders;
    - "Substantial's financial outlook has deteriorated in recent years". Remaining standalone was viable but "not attractive compared to a sale at an acceptable valuation";
    - CityFibre would likely have been able to fund an acquisition, with its shareholders prepared to invest equity;
    - the most likely counterfactual is acquisition by CityFibre, with YouFibre sold to a third-party ISP;
    - the deal would cut the share of VMO2/nexfibre's network facing three wholesale networks (Openreach, VMO2/nexfibre and CityFibre) from about 32% to about 18%;
    - provisional finding: a substantial lessening of competition in wholesale fixed broadband. Responses were due by 23 October 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

## Financing and structure details

- Capital stack: CityFibre relies on sponsor equity (Antin, Goldman Sachs infrastructure, Mubadala, Interogo, and since 2024–2025 also Newlight and PATRIZIA through share-based acquisitions). A large syndicated bank facility is expanded in stages (GBP 820 million, +GBP 300 million, a GBP 4.9 billion package, +GBP 960 million) with participation from a state bank (UKIB, now the National Wealth Fund). An acquisition accordion is added once the business moves from build to consolidation. Margins, covenants and tenors were not disclosed in the sources. [source: 1, 3, 7]
- Paying for acquisitions in shares (Lit Fibre, Connexin) keeps the cash cost of consolidation down for an equity-funded buyer. The sellers' funds become minority holders in a larger platform instead of exiting for cash. [source: 4, 5]
- The operating metric lenders watch is penetration (customers divided by premises passed). CityFibre reports over 20% overall and over 40% in mature cohorts. [source: 6, 8]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- CMA (2026): Substantial's outlook deteriorated and its shareholders preferred a sale. That is direct regulatory evidence of altnet financial pressure, though the CMA does not publish the numbers. [source: 11]
- CityFibre's own releases show the shift from "build to 8 million" (2021–2022) to "consolidate the sector" (2024–2026). Penetration growth after Sky's July 2025 launch was a turning point in its reported numbers. [source: 7, 8]
- Public subsidy can be overtaken by commercial build: the Project Gigabit re-scope cut CityFibre's subsidised premises target to 226,000. [source: 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 82 (Telecoms, fiber, towers and data centers):** Use CityFibre to teach the financing life cycle of an overbuilder. It starts with equity-heavy funding for a build that has no customers yet, followed by a large bank facility sized to a rollout plan. The test is then penetration, because revenue depends on take-up, not homes passed. The outcome is consolidation, financed by an accordion and paid for in shares. Use the CMA's nexfibre/Substantial findings to show that when altnets run short of capital the exit is a sale, and that competition law then decides who may buy. Ask students what covenant (penetration, leverage on contracted revenue) they would set at each stage.
