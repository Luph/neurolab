# Wind and solar repowering and life extension: practice, economics, US tax treatment (80/20 rule), EU permitting (RED III), lender consents

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: WindEurope 2025 statistics, published February 2026; IEC TS 61400-28, published March 2025; IRS Notice 2025-42, 15 August 2025; US 4 July 2026 beginning-of-construction deadline for wind and solar)

## Summary

Repowering means renewing an operating renewable plant by replacing some or all of its equipment to restore or increase capacity or efficiency. Life extension means keeping the original equipment running past its design life, supported by inspection, refurbishment and a remaining-useful-life assessment. In the United States, partial repowering of wind farms grew because of tax law. Under the "80/20 rule", a retrofitted facility counts as newly placed in service if the used parts make up no more than 20% of its total value. A new 10-year production tax credit (PTC) period then starts. Berkeley Lab says the main motive for partial repowering has been to requalify for the PTC while also raising output and extending useful life. The rule began with Rev. Rul. 94-31, was applied to wind by Notice 2016-31, and is now written into the final §45Y/§48E regulations (§1.45Y-4(d) and §1.48E-4(c), January 2025). Notice 2025-42 applies it to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) deadline for wind and solar. In Europe, repowering is driven by ageing fleets and support contracts that are running out. WindEurope counts 34 GW of wind already more than 20 years old and 65 GW by 2030. Europe commissioned about 2 GW of repowered capacity in 2025, 1.5 GW of it in Germany. The amended Renewable Energy Directive (RED III, Directive (EU) 2023/2413) sets maximum permitting times for repowering: 6 months inside renewables acceleration areas and 12 months outside, each extendable. Member states had to transpose these permitting articles by 1 July 2024. For project finance, a repowering is a new construction project carried out inside an existing financing. It normally needs consent from existing lenders and tax equity investors. It may also need new or amended permits, leases, grid agreements and offtake contracts.

## Verified facts

### A. Definitions and engineering norms

1. EU legal definition (RED II, Directive (EU) 2018/2001, Article 2(10)): "repowering" means renewing power plants that produce renewable energy, including the full or partial replacement of installations or operation systems and equipment, to replace capacity or to increase the efficiency or capacity of the installation. This definition is current EU law; the consolidated text is on EUR-Lex. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. The IEC published IEC TS 61400-28:2025, "Wind energy generation systems – Part 28: Through-life management and life extension of wind power assets" (Edition 1.0), in March 2025. It was prepared by IEC Technical Committee 88. It covers assessing current condition and remaining useful life to justify operation beyond design life, and managing a wind farm throughout its life. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. Berkeley Lab's 2019 survey of US wind professionals (survey distributed August 2019 to staff at 23 organisations) found that assumed project lives rose:
   - from about 20 years in the early 2000s;
   - to about 25 years by the mid-2010s;
   - to about 30 years recently.
   Current assumptions ranged from 25 to 40 years, averaging 29.6 years. Respondents noted that turbine design certifications are often 20 years, though some manufacturers offer 25, 30 or 40 years. An independent engineer named foundation design as often the governing factor. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. The same Berkeley Lab report found that lenders focus on repayment within the PPA term, before merchant exposure. Tax equity investors focus on roughly the first 10 years, so useful life is "less often a governing factor" for them. One financier said the incremental value of years 25 to 30 is generally quite low in present-value terms, especially if O&M or refurbishment costs rise. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
5. WindEurope says the oldest wind farms typically operate for 15 to 25 years, and that newer turbines will likely last longer. It defines:
   - "capacity under repowering": the original capacity being replaced;
   - "repowered capacity": the capacity of the new wind farm;
   - "fully decommissioned capacity": capacity removed and not replaced.
   [confidence: high] [source: 4]

### B. United States: tax treatment (federal; Internal Revenue Code as amended by Public Law 119-21; Treasury Regulations, 26 CFR §§1.45Y-4 and 1.48E-4; IRS notices)

6. The 80/20 rule: a facility may qualify as originally placed in service even though it contains some used property, provided the fair market value of the used property is not more than 20% of the facility's total value. Total value is the cost of the new property plus the value of the used property. The IRS cites Rev. Rul. 94-31 and Notice 2008-60 as its origin. Notice 2016-31 (2016) applied the rule to wind PTC/ITC beginning-of-construction. It also applied the rule turbine by turbine (each facility within a single project) and counted only the cost of new property toward the Five Percent Safe Harbor. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. The Notice 2016-31 worked example uses a 13-turbine wind farm in which each facility is worth USD 1 million.
   - The owner retrofits 11 turbines at USD 1.4 million each, leaving USD 100,000 of original components in each.
   - Used value is USD 100,000 of USD 1.5 million, which is not more than 20%, so each retrofitted turbine is newly placed in service.
   - The two turbines that were not upgraded earn no new credit.
   [confidence: high] [source: 5]
8. Final §45Y/§48E regulations (Treasury Decision published January 2025; eCFR shows §1.45Y-4 dated 15 January 2025). Under §1.45Y-4(d), a retrofitted facility that meets the 80/20 rule is treated as originally placed in service on the date its new components are placed in service. It may claim §45Y without any increase in capacity. In the regulation's Example 1, a wind facility retrofitted on 1 February 2026 for USD 2 million, with USD 400,000 of remaining original components, earns §45Y for 10 years from 1 February 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
9. Rules on cost and scope:
   - Under §1.48E-4(c), the 80/20 rule also applies to energy storage technology for the investment credit.
   - Only the cost of new components counts toward the §48E credit base.
   - "Cost of new components" means all costs properly included in their depreciable basis.
   - The test is applied to the "unit of qualified facility", meaning functionally interdependent property, not to the whole project.
   [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]
10. Separately, §1.45Y-4(c) (the "Incremental Production Rule") lets a new unit or added capacity at a facility placed in service before 1 January 2025 earn §45Y. The credit applies only to the increased electricity produced, measured under prescribed standards. This is the route for uprates that do not meet the 80/20 rule. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
11. Notice 2025-42 (15 August 2025) section 5.04 applies the 80/20 rule to retrofitted "applicable wind or solar facilities" for the OBBBA termination rules. For a retrofit, the Physical Work Test applies only to work on, or amounts paid for, new components. A retrofit claiming §45Y/§48E as a new facility is therefore subject to the same deadline as new wind and solar: begin construction by 4 July 2026, or be placed in service by 31 December 2027. See t-us-tax-credits.md for the deadline rules. [confidence: high for the notice text; the deadline consequence follows from the rule] [source: 8; cross-ref t-us-tax-credits.md]
12. Berkeley Lab data on US wind partial repowering:
    - 2022: 1.7 GW of projects partially repowered.
    - 2023: 0.6 GW (seven projects, 630 MW before repowering and 640 MW after), mostly through new rotors (blades) and nacelle components such as gearboxes and generators. Average specific power fell from 325 to 213 W/m².
    Berkeley Lab says the primary motivations were to requalify for the PTC while increasing energy production and extending useful life. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
13. Practitioner commentary from 2017 (Norton Rose Fulbright) describes a turbine as new under the 80/20 rule if at least four times the value of retained equipment is spent on improvements. It notes that some tax equity investors wanted a business justification beyond requalifying for the credit. [confidence: medium (law-firm commentary)] [source: 10]

### C. Europe: market data and permitting (EU law: Directive (EU) 2018/2001 as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/2413, Articles 16a–16f; national law, e.g. Germany's BImSchG §16b)

14. WindEurope 2025 statistics (published February 2026):
    - Europe decommissioned just over 0.9 GW of wind in 2025 (Germany 631 MW) and commissioned at least 2 GW of repowered capacity.
    - Repowering by country: Germany 1.5 GW, Austria 180 MW, UK 80 MW, Italy 76 MW, France 48 MW, Spain 24 MW, Switzerland 2 MW.
    - In Germany, almost a third of new onshore capacity came from repowering.
    - On average, repowering triples electricity output while cutting turbine numbers by around a third.
    [confidence: high] [source: 4]
15. WindEurope outlook and fleet age:
    - About 16 GW is expected to be decommissioned in 2026–2030. About 8 GW of that should be repowered, eventually producing 17 GW of repowered capacity; the other 8 GW will be fully removed.
    - 34 GW of Europe's wind fleet is already more than 20 years old, rising to 65 GW by 2030.
    - Support contracts usually last about 20 years.
    - Germany has 22 GW and Spain 20 GW older than 15 years.
    [confidence: high] [source: 4]
16. RED III Article 16a(2), inside renewables acceleration areas: permitting for repowering (and for grid connection) must not exceed six months, or 12 months for offshore wind. In extraordinary circumstances it may be extended by up to three months (six for offshore wind), for example where a repowering has a substantial impact on the grid or on the plant's original capacity, size or performance. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
17. RED III Article 16b(2), outside acceleration areas: permitting for repowering, including environmental assessments and grid connection, must not exceed 12 months, or two years for offshore. It may be extended by up to three months in extraordinary circumstances. New projects outside acceleration areas get two years (three for offshore) under Article 16b(1). [confidence: high] [source: 11]
18. RED III Article 16c:
    - Where repowering raises capacity by no more than 15%, grid-connection permitting must not exceed three months, unless there are justified safety concerns or technical incompatibility.
    - Any environmental screening or assessment of a repowering is limited to the impact of the change compared with the original project.
    - Solar repowering that uses no additional space and complies with the original mitigation measures is exempt from screening and EIA.
    [confidence: high] [source: 11]
19. Transposition: the general deadline for RED III was 21 May 2025, but Articles 16, 16b, 16c, 16d, 16e and 16f had to be transposed by 1 July 2024. Directive (EU) 2023/2413 was adopted on 18 October 2023. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
20. Enforcement status:
    - In early 2026 WindEurope reported that national implementation lags in most member states, and that the Commission had referred Sweden to the CJEU over permitting transposition.
    - Germany is "one of the few countries" broadly meeting the deadlines, with average approval in 17 months in 2025 (23 months in 2024) and a record 20.8 GW of onshore wind permitted.
    - Spain still averages four to five years of procedures.
    [confidence: high (as WindEurope's report)] [source: 4]
21. Germany, BImSchG §16b (Federal Immission Control Act): in a repowering modification permit, the authority examines only the adverse effects relative to the existing plant. A full replacement qualifies if two conditions are met:
    - the new turbine is built within 48 months of dismantling the old one (extendable for good cause);
    - it stands within five times the new turbine's total height of the old one.
    A repowered turbine may not be refused solely for exceeding TA Lärm noise values if its noise contribution is lower than the turbine it replaces and it is state of the art. [confidence: high] [source: 12]

### D. Financing and contractual issues

22. Herbert Smith Freehills (March 2025, UK focus) lists the issues a repowering raises:
    - If existing debt is in place, lender consent will usually be required, and early engagement with lenders is essential.
    - New planning permission is often needed, because original consents are usually time-limited and fix parameters such as tip height.
    - Landowner consents, lease amendments or new leases may be needed.
    - Grid connection agreements may need amending, or a new one may be needed, if capacity or technology changes.
    [confidence: medium (law-firm commentary seen via a search summary; full text not accessed)] [source: 13]

## Timeline

- 1994: Rev. Rul. 94-31 establishes the 80/20 rule for used property.
- 2016: IRS Notice 2016-31 applies the 80/20 rule to wind and other retrofits for PTC/ITC beginning-of-construction.
- 2017–2022: wave of US partial repowerings driven by PTC requalification (1.7 GW partially repowered in 2022).
- September 2019: Berkeley Lab survey puts average assumed US wind project life at 29.6 years.

- 4 July 2025: OBBBA enacted.
- 15 August 2025: Notice 2025-42 applies the 80/20 rule to the wind/solar termination.

- 4 July 2026: last date for wind/solar (including retrofits) to begin construction and avoid the 2027 placed-in-service cliff.

## Financing and structure details

- **US partial repowering inside a partnership flip**: the retrofit creates a new credit stream and new depreciable basis. The existing tax equity investor's consent is needed under the partnership agreement. Some investors require a business case beyond the credits [10]. Back-leverage or project lenders must consent to the capital spending, the replacement of collateral and any change to the O&M and turbine supply contracts.
- **Credit sizing**: only the cost of new components counts toward the ITC base, and the 80/20 test runs turbine by turbine (unit of qualified facility). One turbine that fails the test loses its new credit without disqualifying the rest [5, 6].
- **EU**: repowering normally coincides with the end of the original support contract (about 20 years) and therefore with a new revenue contract: a new auction, CfD or PPA. That makes it a new financing rather than an amendment. Faster permitting under RED III shortens the development period and so the bridge-to-financing risk.
- **Lender consent points (general practice; verify against the specific facility agreement)**: restrictions on capital expenditure, asset disposals and changes to project documents; collateral over the replaced equipment; completion risk during the outage; DSCR tests while production is lost; and reserve accounts.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- Berkeley Lab attributes the US partial-repowering wave chiefly to PTC requalification, with higher output and longer life as co-benefits. That explains why volumes rose and fell with the credit calendar [9].
- WindEurope argues that repowering sites have the best wind resources, long operating data and existing infrastructure, and attract less local opposition than greenfield projects. It says slow national transposition of the EU permitting rules is the main brake [4].
- Berkeley Lab respondents cited partial repowering, together with better O&M, as a reason for growing confidence in 30-year lives [3].

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Chapter 62 (Operating the project)**: Treat repowering as a capital project inside a live financing. Walk through the 80/20 arithmetic: with USD 400,000 of retained value and USD 2 million of new spend, used value is 16.7% of the total. Then map the consents needed from lenders, tax equity, landowners, the grid operator and the offtaker. Contrast this with the Incremental Production Rule for uprates that do not meet 80/20. Stress that the tax answer is set turbine by turbine and is fixed at the date the new parts are placed in service.
- **Chapter 65 (End of life)**: Present the end-of-life decision as a three-way choice: life extension (IEC TS 61400-28-style remaining-life assessment), full repowering, or decommissioning. Use WindEurope's split of 16 GW expected to be decommissioned in 2026–2030 (8 GW repowered, 8 GW removed) and the 34 GW over 20 years old. Show why lenders give little value to years 25–30 (Berkeley Lab), and how RED III's 6- and 12-month permitting caps and Germany's BImSchG §16b change the option value of a site.
