# Copper concentrate offtake: benchmark TC/RCs 2021–2026, payables and penalty elements

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: Hudbay 2025 annual MD&A filed 27 March 2026, which reports the 2026 Chinese benchmark at $0/0¢; Antofagasta Annual Report 2025)

Cross-references: for Cobre Panamá's closure (cited by Hudbay as a cause of concentrate tightness), use facts/cobre-panama.md. For Oyu Tolgoi concentrate sales, use facts/oyu-tolgoi.md. For mining loan margins, use facts/t-market-norms-2.md.

## Summary

A copper mine that produces concentrate does not sell copper. It sells a mineral product to a smelter. The smelter pays for an agreed percentage of the contained metal (the "payable"), priced on LME averages over a quotational period after shipment. From that it deducts a treatment charge (TC, USD per dry tonne of concentrate) and a refining charge (RC, US cents per pound of payable copper), plus penalties for deleterious elements such as arsenic, and pays for by-product gold and silver at their own payable rates. Many long-term contracts reference an annual "benchmark" TC/RC, which major miners (Antofagasta has typically led) settle with Chinese smelters for the following year. The benchmark moved as follows: $59.5/t and 5.95¢/lb in 2021; $65/6.5¢ in 2022; $88/8.8¢ in 2023; $80/8.0¢ in 2024; a record low of $21.25/2.125¢ in 2025; and $0/0¢ for 2026. The collapse reflects smelter capacity growth, mostly in China, far outpacing mine supply, together with supply losses such as the late-2023 closure of Cobre Panamá. Spot terms fell further: Hudbay reported spot charges "trading at negative triple digits" in early 2026, meaning miners were in effect being paid by traders to supply concentrate. For miners this was a revenue gain. Antofagasta's TC/RC deductions on copper concentrate fell from $185.3 million in 2024 to $20.3 million in 2025. For non-integrated custom smelters it was a margin crisis: Teck reports global smelter utilisation of about 75%. For project finance, TC/RC assumptions sit in the net smelter return and in the lenders' base case, and the swings since 2023 show they are a market variable to be sensitised, not a constant.

## Verified facts

### A. Benchmark TC/RCs (annual copper concentrate benchmark agreed with Chinese smelters; USD per dry metric tonne / US cents per lb payable copper)

1. 2021: $59.5/5.95¢, described by Hudbay as "a low point of a multiyear cycle". [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. 2022: $65/6.5¢. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
3. 2023: $88/8.8¢. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
4. 2024: $80/8.0¢. After it was set, the concentrate market tightened sharply, reflecting the closure of Cobre Panamá, and spot TC/RCs fell as low as $25/2.5¢ (Hudbay, March 2024). [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
5. 2025: $21.25/2.125¢, agreed with Chinese smelters and described by Hudbay as a record low and "a dramatic fall" from $80/8¢. Hudbay warned that many non-integrated smelters in the West and smaller custom smelters in China would be under pressure to cut output. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. 2026: Chinese smelters agreed 2026 benchmark TC/RCs at $0/0¢, "yet another record low". Spot copper TC/RCs were "trading at negative triple digits for sales from miners to traders" (Hudbay, filed 27 March 2026). [confidence: high] [source: 5]
7. Drivers (Teck 2025 MD&A): global mine production grew only 1.3% in 2025 (to about 23.4 Mt), well below smelter capacity additions. This produced "historically low spot treatment and refining charges" and smelter capacity utilisation of about 75%, the lowest in recent history. China's concentrate imports rose 7.9% to over 7.5 Mt of contained copper. Hudbay attributes the tightness to "a global oversupply of smelting capacity relative to mine production". [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6]

### B. Contract mechanics as disclosed by producers

8. Freeport-McMoRan (2025 Form 10-K): concentrate sales are provisionally priced at shipment and finally priced on LME monthly average settlement prices for a specified future month, generally one to four months after shipment. Revenue from South American concentrate sales is recorded net of royalties and treatment charges ("fees paid to smelters that are generally negotiated annually"). It is also recorded net of "allowances for unrecoverable metals, which are a negotiated term of the contracts". Freeport says treatment charges vary with market conditions, sales volumes and the copper price. Lower treatment charges added $333 million to its 2025 revenue compared with 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 7]
9. Antofagasta (Annual Report 2025): TC/RCs for copper concentrate fell by $165.0 million to $20.3 million in 2025 (2024: $185.3 million), "reflecting lower average TC/RC rates". The company defines TC/RCs as charges "normally set on either an annual or spot basis". The realised copper price is computed after adding back TC/RCs for concentrate sales. Under the industry definition, unit cash costs include TC/RCs. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
10. Hudbay: "a portion of our copper concentrate sales are made under multi-year contracts, with a link to annual benchmark treatment and refining charges". [confidence: high] [source: 3, 4]
11. Payables (worked example from a regulator-filed technical report): the Escondida (Chile) SEC S-K 1300 Technical Report Summary used average payable rates of 96.2% for copper in concentrate, 100% for copper cathodes, and 90% for gold and silver, in its economic analysis. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
12. Penalty elements (same report): arsenic, cadmium, lead, zinc, bismuth and antimony are treated as impurities that attract penalties "if it exceeds the permitted limit values". Gold and silver add value. At Escondida, arsenic occurs as enargite in polymetallic veins, with "only limited impact upon long term concentrate quality". [confidence: high] [source: 9]

- Late 2023: Cobre Panamá closes. By March 2024: 2024 benchmark $80/8¢ "recently established"; spot falls to about $25/2.5¢.
- By March 2025: 2025 benchmark $21.25/2.125¢.
- By March 2026: 2026 benchmark $0/0¢; spot negative.

## Financing and structure details

- Net smelter return (NSR) per tonne of concentrate = (payable % × contained Cu × LME price) − TC − (RC × payable lb) − penalties + payable by-product credits − freight. Lenders' base cases use a TC/RC assumption, usually a long-run consensus, and run downside cases with higher charges. The 2023–2026 record shows how far realised charges can depart from long-run assumptions in both directions.
- Offtake agreements for financed projects typically fix tonnage and the quotational-period mechanics. They let TC/RC float to the annual benchmark or a spot formula, so the price risk on processing margin stays with the project. (General structure, consistent with Hudbay's description; specific contracts were not reviewed.)
- Provisional pricing creates mark-to-market receivables. Antofagasta and Freeport both report provisional-pricing adjustments.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- For miners, 2024–2026 was a windfall on the processing line. Antofagasta saved about $165 million and Freeport reported $333 million from lower treatment charges in 2025, because smelter capacity growth, largely in China, outran concentrate supply. [source: 7, 8, 6]
- For smelters, the same forces caused losses and cutbacks (Teck: about 75% utilisation). This creates counterparty risk for a mine's offtaker. A project lender should ask whether a weak custom smelter will perform over the life of a long-term offtake. (Analytical inference.)

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 21: Build an NSR waterfall from the Escondida payables (96.2% Cu; 90% Au/Ag) and the benchmark series. Show that TC/RC is a margin paid to the processor whose sign can flip: benchmark $0, spot negative. Explain the quotational period and provisional pricing as a separate price risk.
- Ch 78: In mining project finance, use the 2023 ($88/8.8¢) versus 2026 ($0/0¢) swing as a sensitivity case for the lenders' model. Discuss penalty elements (arsenic and others) as reserve and product-quality risks that technical due diligence must address, and offtaker credit when smelters are distressed.
