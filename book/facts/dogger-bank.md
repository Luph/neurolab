# Dogger Bank offshore wind financing

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: turbine installation at Dogger Bank B, reported through July 2026)

## Summary

Dogger Bank Wind Farm is a 3.6 GW offshore wind project in the UK North Sea, built in three phases of 1.2 GW each (Dogger Bank A, B and C). SSE Renewables and Equinor took it into construction as 50:50 sponsors; each sold down to 40%, and the remaining 20% now sits with Vårgrønn (a Plenitude/HitecVision joint venture), after Eni bought in in 2021-22. All three phases won 15-year Contracts for Difference (CfDs) in the UK's 2019 Allocation Round 3. Strike prices were GBP 39.65/MWh for A and GBP 41.61/MWh for B and C, in 2012 prices and CPI-indexed. Phases A and B reached financial close together on 26 November 2020. Their debt package of about GBP 4.8 billion of senior facilities plus about GBP 0.7 billion of ancillary facilities, from 29 banks and three export credit agencies (ECAs), was announced as the largest offshore wind project financing to that date. Phase C closed on 2 December 2021, with about GBP 2.5 billion of senior debt plus about GBP 435 million of ancillary facilities from 28 banks and three ECAs. The structure geared the generation assets at about 65-70%. The transmission assets were geared separately at 90% of forecast proceeds from their mandatory sale to an Offshore Transmission Owner (OFTO). Construction ran late. Weather, vessel availability, supply chain problems and blade incidents in 2024 pushed full completion of Dogger Bank A from 2024-25 into 2026. All 95 Dogger Bank A turbines were installed by February 2026, and Dogger Bank B turbine installation was expected to run into the second quarter of 2027.

## Financing and structure details

- **Ring-fencing by phase.** Each 1.2 GW phase is a separate project company with its own CfD. A and B were financed in one transaction; C closed about a year later in its own deal. [source: 1, 2]
- **Two-part gearing.** Generation assets: about 65-70% (A and B), about 70% (C). Transmission assets: 90% of forecast OFTO sale proceeds. Under the UK regime the transmission link must be sold to an OFTO after construction, so the debt on that tranche is effectively a bridge to a regulated sale. [source: 1, 2]
- **Facilities.** A and B: about GBP 4.8 billion senior plus about GBP 0.7 billion ancillary. C: about GBP 2.5 billion senior plus about GBP 435 million ancillary. The ancillary facilities were not itemized in the sponsor releases. [source: 1, 2]
- **Lenders.** A and B: 29 banks plus Bpifrance Assurance Export, EKN and GIEK. C: 28 banks plus Bpifrance (in the main package), with EKN- and Eksfin-supported separate facilities. Reported mandated lead arrangers on C included ABN AMRO, BNP Paribas, Bank of China, Deutsche Bank, DNB, ICBC, ING, Korea Development Bank, Lloyds, MUFG, Santander, Société Générale and Standard Chartered. [source: 1, 2, 4]
- **Revenue support.** 15-year CPI-indexed CfDs at GBP 39.65/MWh (A) and GBP 41.61/MWh (B, C) in 2012 prices. [source: 1, 2]
- **ECA logic.** Trade press reported that Bpifrance insured a portion of the debt linked to exports of GE Haliade-X turbines. The Swedish and Norwegian ECAs supported other supply content. The exact mapping of each ECA to specific contracts was not verified beyond this. [source: 2, 4]
- **Equity sell-downs.** Sponsors recycled capital by selling 20% to Eni at or around each financial close; Eni's share later passed to Vårgrønn. [source: 2, 6, 7]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Right: scale and bankability.** The sponsors financed the then-largest offshore wind project on non-recourse terms. A low but fixed, CPI-indexed CfD gave lenders a contracted, inflation-linked revenue line, and ECA cover widened the lender pool. [source: 1, 2]
- **Right: the OFTO structure as a financing tool.** The sponsors geared transmission at 90% of forecast OFTO proceeds, which matches debt to a known exit. [source: 1, 2]
- **Wrong: construction schedule.** SSE attributed the slip in A's completion to weather, vessel availability and supply chain delays. Two blade incidents in 2024 added to the problems, and first-of-a-kind use of very large turbines (the 13 MW Haliade-X) carried technology risk. [source: 9]
- **Cost exposure.** SSE said returns on A were not expected to be materially affected by the 2024 slip. No verified public figure for total overrun or lender waivers was found. [source: 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 71 (Offshore wind).** Use Dogger Bank as the anchor case for UK CfD-backed offshore wind finance: phase-by-phase ring-fencing, the split between generation and OFTO-bound transmission with different gearing (about 65-70% against 90% of OFTO proceeds), ECA participation tied to turbine supply, and sponsor sell-downs at financial close to recycle equity. Then show the construction risk side through the A delays (weather, vessels, supply chain, blade incidents) and ask how the CfD start-date mechanics, delay LDs and contingency would have cushioned lenders. Students can reconcile the GBP 5.5 billion headline with the capex and gearing as an exercise. The headline is senior plus ancillary, against about GBP 6 billion of A and B capex; the gearing figures apply to the generation assets only.

## Do not state

- Loan tenors, margins, hedging terms, debt service coverage ratio covenants or the split of debt by ECA. None was verified in public sources.
- That Dogger Bank was the first UK offshore wind farm to use high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission, or that A is "fully operational" or "complete". As of the latest verified report (Feb-Jul 2026), commissioning was expected later in 2026.
- Any total cost overrun figure, lender waiver or CfD amendment. Not verified.
- That Eni paid GBP 405 million at closing for A and B. That figure was widely reported around the announcement, but the closing consideration reported was GBP 206.4 million, and the reason for the difference was not verified.
- Turbine rating for C as 14.7 MW (seen in secondary sources). Use "Haliade-X 14 MW" per the sponsor release, or check current sponsor materials.
- The number of homes powered (various figures circulate).
- Details of a proposed Dogger Bank D extension (consultation reported in 2026). Not verified from primary sources. Do not confuse Dogger Bank Wind Farm with RWE's Sofia or the RWE/Masdar Dogger Bank South projects, which are separate.
- Any quotation from SSE, Equinor or lender executives.
