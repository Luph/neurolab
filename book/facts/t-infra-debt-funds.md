# Infrastructure debt funds and private credit in project finance (2020–2026)

As of: 2026-10-03. Latest data points: SEQI monthly factsheet, 31 August 2026; Ares Q4 2025 earnings release, 5 February 2026; Brookfield Asset Management 2025 Form 10-K, filed March 2026.

## Summary

Infrastructure debt funds are closed-end (and increasingly open-ended or listed) vehicles that lend to infrastructure projects and companies. They compete with or sit behind bank and DFI lenders. Strategies split into two families:
- **Investment-grade senior debt:** long-dated, fixed-rate, matched to insurers' and pension funds' liabilities. These investors more often lend directly or through private placements, such as the Rio Grande LNG notes in t-project-bonds.md.
- **Sub-investment-grade or mezzanine debt:** holdco loans, junior tranches and construction or "transition" debt, aiming for high-single to low-double-digit gross returns.

Three verified data points show the high-yield end:
- **Brookfield Infrastructure Debt.** Brookfield describes this series as investing in "mezzanine debt investments" in infrastructure, renewable power and transition assets. Its third vintage had a final close in 2023. Brookfield said the vintage was more than double its predecessor and the strategy's commitments exceeded USD 6 billion. Fund IV raised over USD 4 billion at first close (October 2025) and USD 3.3 billion during 2025.
- **Ares Infrastructure Debt Fund V.** Ares took this platform over in its February 2022 acquisition of AMP Capital's infrastructure debt business. The fund (2020 vintage, USD 4.6 billion of commitments) showed a 12.7% gross and 10.0% net IRR for its USD-hedged parallel fund at end-2025. Ares's sixth fund took USD 2.5 billion of equity commitments and USD 0.5 billion of debt commitments during 2025.
- **Sequoia Economic Infrastructure Income Fund (SEQI).** This London-listed fund holds a GBP 1.3 billion private infrastructure debt portfolio. It shows a 9.73% portfolio yield-to-worst (August 2026), a 39% average equity cushion and a 3.0-year average life. Its mix is 59% senior, 10% mezzanine and 31% holdco; 88% operational; 56% fixed rate.

At the riskiest end, private credit lends to single-asset mining projects at about SOFR + 7.5–9.5% with warrants (Cariboo, in t-market-norms-2.md). Market-wide fundraising totals and return-target surveys (Preqin, Infrastructure Investor, IJGlobal) were not accessible and are not stated.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Cash-yield returns.** The Ares IDF V figures move from 12.4% to 12.7% gross IRR as realised income builds, with MoIC only 1.1–1.3x. That is the signature of a debt fund earning contractual interest rather than capital gains. [source: 7, 8]
- **Currency share classes matter.** The spread between IDF V's USD-hedged (10.0% net) and EUR-unhedged (8.1% net) parallel funds shows that hedging cost and base-rate differentials drive investor outcomes in multi-currency infrastructure debt funds. [source: 7]
- **Holdco and mezzanine dominate the high-return end.** SEQI's 31% holdco and 10% mezzanine exposure, and Brookfield's mezzanine mandate, show where funds earn their premium over bank senior debt: structural subordination. SEQI's 39% equity cushion is the protection it reports. [source: 1, 10]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 29 (The lenders):**
  - Add infrastructure debt funds as a distinct lender class with three styles:
    - long-dated senior (insurer-like, often through private placements);
    - mezzanine and holdco (Brookfield; SEQI's holdco book);
    - diversified multi-currency closed-end funds (Ares IDF V).
  - Use the verified numbers to calibrate the risk ladder students should hold in mind: bank senior at SOFR + about 2%, mezzanine and holdco funds targeting about 10–13% gross, and private-credit mining at SOFR + 7.5–9.5% with warrants.
  - Stress that fund returns are reported gross and net of fees, by currency share class, and that IRR differs from portfolio yield-to-worst.
  - For Case P, show where a debt fund could enter: holdco debt at refinancing, or a mezzanine tranche in construction.
