# Pillar Two global minimum tax: status as of 2026

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: OECD guidance of 11 September 2026 on explicitly conditional taxes and national bills implementing the January 2026 Side-by-Side Package, through late September 2026)

## Summary

Pillar Two is the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework's global minimum tax, also called the GloBE (Global Anti-Base Erosion) rules. Its aim is that multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million pay an effective tax rate of at least 15% in every jurisdiction where they operate. Any shortfall ("top-up tax") is collected under an agreed rule order:

- first, a domestic minimum top-up tax (QDMTT) levied by the low-tax jurisdiction itself;
- next, an income inclusion rule (IIR) at the level of the parent;
- last, the undertaxed profits rule (UTPR) as a backstop.

The EU adopted the rules by Council Directive (EU) 2022/2523, and the UK adopted them as Multinational Top-up Tax and Domestic Top-up Tax. Both apply from periods beginning on or after 31 December 2023, with the UTPR following from 31 December 2024. The United States did not adopt the GloBE rules. After a G7 statement of 28 June 2025, the Inclusive Framework agreed a "Side-by-Side" (SbS) Package on 5 January 2026. The package does five things:

- exempts groups with a parent in a qualifying jurisdiction (so far only the United States) from the IIR and UTPR;
- adds a permanent Simplified ETR Safe Harbour;
- adds a Substance-based Tax Incentive Safe Harbour;
- adds an Ultimate Parent Entity (UPE) Safe Harbour;
- extends the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour by one year.

The first GloBE Information Returns (GIRs) were due by 30 June 2026. During 2026 the UK, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden and others published legislation to implement the January 2026 package. For project finance the main effects are on the value of tax holidays and income-based incentives, on how tax credits are treated, and on the tax profile of a project held by a large multinational sponsor. Funds, pension funds and government entities as ultimate parents fall outside the rules.

## Verified facts

### Core rules

1. **Scope threshold.** The global minimum tax applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenues of EUR 750 million in at least two of the last four years. The OECD's April 2026 FAQ says current implementation, including eligible SbS regimes, covers more than 90% of in-scope MNE groups. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. **Minimum rate and test.** The minimum rate is 15%. The rules apply an effective tax rate (ETR) test jurisdiction by jurisdiction, using a common tax base and a common definition of Covered Taxes. Top-up tax is due where a group's jurisdictional ETR is below 15%. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 4]
3. **Charging rules and rule order.** Jurisdictions may implement:
   - a domestic minimum top-up tax on local entities;
   - an IIR on the subsidiaries of an in-scope group held through a local holding company;
   - a UTPR as a backstop.

   The local jurisdiction's domestic top-up tax has the primary right. The IIR at the ultimate parent, or the highest IIR jurisdiction in the ownership chain, comes next, and the UTPR comes last. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
4. **Excluded entities.** Government entities, international organisations, non-profit organisations, pension funds and investment funds that are the Ultimate Parent Entity of a group are excluded, as are certain holding vehicles of such entities. The exclusion does not reach every entity they own. A group owned by an excluded entity that meets the revenue threshold stays in scope. This matters for infrastructure funds and pension-fund-owned project companies. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
5. **Substance-based Income Exclusion (SBIE).** The SBIE removes from the GloBE base 5% of the carrying value of tangible assets plus 5% of eligible payroll costs in the jurisdiction. A 10-year transition starts at 8% for tangible assets and 10% for payroll and declines to 5% by 2033. For capital-intensive infrastructure the tangible-asset carve-out can be large. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
6. **QRTCs and MTTCs.** Qualified Refundable Tax Credits (QRTCs, refundable within four years) and Marketable Transferable Tax Credits (MTTCs) count as GloBE Income, in the ETR denominator. They do not reduce Covered Taxes. This is the more favourable treatment compared with non-refundable credits, which reduce Covered Taxes. [confidence: high] [source: 1]

### EU and UK implementation

7. **EU directive.** Council Directive (EU) 2022/2523 of 14 December 2022 required transposition by 31 December 2023. It requires the IIR for fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2023 and the UTPR for fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2024. [confidence: high] [source: 5]
8. **UK.** UK Multinational Top-up Tax (the IIR) and Domestic Top-up Tax apply to accounting periods beginning on or after 31 December 2023. The UTPR applies within Multinational Top-up Tax for periods beginning on or after 31 December 2024. The rules sit in Finance (No. 2) Act 2023 Part 3, as amended, with HMRC guidance in the "Multinational Top-up Tax and Domestic Top-up Tax" manual. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]

### The Side-by-Side Package (January 2026)

9. **G7 statement.** On 28 June 2025 the G7 published a shared understanding that a "side-by-side" system could exclude US-parented groups from the IIR and UTPR. The statement referred to the removal of the proposed section 899 ("retaliatory" tax) from the draft One Big Beautiful Bill Act. [confidence: high] [source: 8, 9]
10. **Agreement.** On 5 January 2026 the OECD announced that the Inclusive Framework had agreed the "Side-by-Side Package" in the form of Administrative Guidance, to be built into the Commentary to the GloBE Model Rules. Contemporaneous reporting puts the number of agreeing Inclusive Framework members at 147. The package has five components:
    - a Simplified ETR Safe Harbour;
    - a one-year extension of the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour;
    - a Substance-based Tax Incentive (SBTI) Safe Harbour;
    - an SbS Safe Harbour;
    - a UPE Safe Harbour.

    The agreement also reaffirmed the primacy of QDMTTs and set an evidence-based stocktake. [confidence: high for the date and components; medium for the 147 count] [source: 2, 3, 10, 11]
11. **SbS Safe Harbour.** Groups whose UPE is in a jurisdiction with a "Qualified SbS Regime" can treat IIR and UTPR top-up tax as zero, for both domestic and foreign operations, for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2026, if they make a valid election. As of 5 January 2026 the United States was the only jurisdiction listed as having a Qualified SbS Regime. QDMTTs in other countries still apply to US groups' local profits. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
12. **SbS qualifying criteria.** As summarised by Sullivan & Cromwell and EY, a jurisdiction qualifies if it has:
    - an eligible domestic tax system with a statutory rate of at least 20%, plus a corporate alternative minimum tax or a QDMTT at 15% or more;
    - a comprehensive worldwide tax system that addresses BEPS risks;
    - credit for QDMTTs;
    - "no material risk" of domestic ETRs falling below 15%. [confidence: medium] [source: 2, 3]
13. **UPE Safe Harbour.** This safe harbour applies for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2026. Where it applies, UTPR top-up tax for constituent entities in the UPE jurisdiction is zero. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
14. **Simplified ETR Safe Harbour.** The OECD describes this as a permanent simplification. It computes a jurisdictional ETR from consolidation data with few mandatory adjustments and uses a 15% threshold with no buffer. A group can elect it again after failing it. EY reports that it applies for fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2026, and that jurisdictions may allow it one year earlier. Sullivan & Cromwell's summary refers to availability for 2026–2027. [confidence: high for the design; medium for the start date] [source: 1, 3, 2]
15. **Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour extension.** The safe harbour uses country-by-country report data as a proxy test. Its ETR threshold rises each year, reaching 17% for fiscal years beginning in 2026. EY reports that the extension covers fiscal years beginning on or before 31 December 2027, excluding fiscal years ending after 30 June 2029. [confidence: high for 17% in 2026; medium for the extension dates] [source: 1, 3]
16. **SBTI Safe Harbour.** This safe harbour is available for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2026 by annual election. It treats "Qualified Tax Incentives" as additions to Covered Taxes, up to a Substance Cap. The cap is the greater of 5.5% of eligible payroll costs or tangible-asset depreciation, or optionally 1% of the carrying value of eligible tangible assets. QTIs cover only:
    - expenditure-based incentives, proportionate to and capped at expenditure;
    - certain production-based incentives, based on the volume of tangible production in the jurisdiction.

    Income-based incentives such as tax holidays, reduced rates, exemptions and IP boxes are excluded. A QRTC or MTTC that meets the QTI definition may be elected into QTI treatment. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
17. **Stocktake.** EY reports that the Inclusive Framework committed to an evidence-based stocktake to conclude by 2029. [confidence: medium] [source: 3]

### First filings and 2026 implementation

18. **First GIR deadline.** The first GloBE Information Return deadline was 30 June 2026. Centrally filed GIR information is to be exchanged between implementing jurisdictions by 31 December 2026. OECD guidance of 18 May 2026 addressed central GIR filing and penalty relief, the Transitional UTPR Safe Harbour for 52–53-week years, and an updated Central Record. That record lists 37 jurisdictions that had implemented a qualified IIR and/or QDMTT from 2024 fiscal years. [confidence: medium (secondary source summarising OECD guidance)] [source: 12]
19. **UK draft legislation.** On 13 July 2026 HMRC published draft Finance Bill 2026-27 legislation to implement the Side-by-Side Package. The draft covers:
    - the permanent Simplified ETR Safe Harbour;
    - the extension of the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour;
    - treatment of qualifying substance-based tax incentives;
    - the SbS and UPE Safe Harbours, effective for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2026.

    Other technical amendments mainly apply from periods beginning on or after 31 December 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 13, 14]
20. **EU approach.** The EU has not amended Directive 2022/2523 for the package. The European Commission published a notice supporting the package, and member states are amending national laws, for example Luxembourg (draft published July 2026) and the Netherlands (bill presented September 2026). [confidence: medium] [source: 15, 16]
21. **Accounting.** In May 2023 the IASB amended IAS 12 with a temporary exception from recognising and disclosing deferred tax on Pillar Two income taxes, together with targeted disclosures. [confidence: high] [source: 17]

## Financing and structure details

Not a transaction. Structuring points that the sources establish:

- **Sponsor size decides scope.** A project company owned by a sponsor group above EUR 750 million revenue is a constituent entity whose jurisdictional ETR is tested. A project owned by an excluded UPE (pension fund, investment fund, government entity) may be outside scope, subject to the limits in fact 4. [source: 1]
- **Joint ventures.** Joint-venture holdings below consolidation are dealt with by specific JV rules in the Model Rules. The detail is not verified here, so treat them as "check the Model Rules Article 6.4" without stating outcomes.
- **Tax holidays.** Income-based tax holidays offered to projects in many emerging markets can generate top-up tax, collected locally if the host country has a QDMTT or elsewhere under the IIR/UTPR. The new SBTI Safe Harbour does not rescue them; only expenditure- or volume-based incentives qualify. [source: 1]
- **Capital-intensive projects.** The tangible-asset SBIE (8% declining to 5% of carrying value) can shelter a large share of income for capital-intensive projects with modest returns on assets. [source: 1]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **OECD position.** The OECD argues that the Simplified ETR Safe Harbour responds to complaints about compliance burden. It says the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour is less accurate and more open to manipulation than a GloBE-based calculation, which is why it was temporary. [source: 1]
- **The QTI line.** The OECD's rationale is that expenditure-based incentives offer better value for money and a closer link to substance than income-based incentives, which reward profits rather than activity. [source: 1]
- **Commentary on the SbS Package.** Some EU-law commentators argue that the package is hard to reconcile with EU law, citing for example the Kluwer International Tax Blog post "A Structural Breach of EU Tax Law". This is commentary, not settled law. [source: 15]

## Teaching angles by chapter

**Chapter 67 (Tax structuring).** Teach Pillar Two as a constraint on the classic emerging-market tool kit: tax holidays, low-tax holding companies and income exemptions. Walk through:

1. whether the sponsor group is in scope (EUR 750 million revenue);
2. the jurisdictional ETR;
3. the SBIE carve-out;
4. which jurisdiction collects the top-up tax (QDMTT first);
5. whether the incentive is a QTI.

Show that a host-country holiday may simply move tax from the host treasury to the sponsor's home treasury unless the host enacts a QDMTT. Make clear that the rules are moving fast: give the as-of date and point readers to the OECD Central Record, national legislation and HMRC's MTT manual.

## Do not state

- That Pillar Two applies to all companies or all project companies. It applies only to groups at or above EUR 750 million and excludes certain UPEs.
- That the United States has enacted the GloBE rules, or that US groups are wholly exempt. QDMTTs in host countries still apply to them.
- A precise count of jurisdictions "implementing Pillar Two" other than as given by a dated OECD Central Record. Counts differ by source and date.
- The October 2021 two-pillar statement's exact date and signatory count (often given as 8 October 2021 and 136 jurisdictions). Not verified for this sheet.
- The precise first year of the Simplified ETR Safe Harbour. Sources differ: fiscal years beginning on or after 31 December 2026 (EY), or availability for 2026–2027 (S&C). Check the Administrative Guidance text.
- That the EU directive has been amended for the SbS Package. It has not as of the sources consulted.
- Any statement about the 2025 US presidential memorandum on the OECD deal. Not verified here.
- Specific outcomes of the JV and minority-owned entity rules for project companies. Not verified.
