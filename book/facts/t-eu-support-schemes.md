# EU renewable support schemes: Germany's sliding market premium (EEG) and the 2024 EU requirement for two-way CfDs

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: Bundesnetzagentur results for the 1 July 2026 solar auction, published 18 August 2026; 1 May 2026 onshore wind auction results; Irish RESS 6 held in 2026)

Cross-references: for the GB CfD (LCCC, allocation rounds, AR7 prices) use facts/t-uk-cfd-rab.md. For Poland's 25-year offshore CfD (Baltic Power) see facts/t-market-norms-2.md, item 13. For Spain's retroactive cuts see facts/spain-renewables.md.

## Summary

Germany's Renewable Energy Sources Act (Erneuerbare-Energien-Gesetz, EEG 2023) supports most new wind and solar plants through a one-sided "sliding" market premium. The generator sells its power in the market itself, usually through a direct marketer (Direktvermarktung). The grid operator then pays a premium equal to the plant's "value to be applied" (anzulegender Wert, AW) minus a technology-specific market value, set at zero if that difference is negative. For plants commissioned or awarded from 1 January 2023 the market value is calculated annually, not monthly. The AW is set by pay-as-bid auctions run by the Bundesnetzagentur (BNetzA). Recent volume-weighted award values were 5.06 ct/kWh for onshore wind (May 2026 round) and 4.79 ct/kWh for ground-mounted solar (July 2026 round). Support lasts 20 years. For plants commissioned from 25 February 2025, no support is paid in any quarter-hour with a negative spot price; instead the support period is extended by the lost time. Because the premium never goes below zero, generators keep all the upside when prices are high, as many did in 2022. The EU's 2024 electricity market design reform, Regulation (EU) 2024/1747, changes this. From 17 July 2027 (17 July 2029 for offshore hybrid assets), direct price support for new wind, solar, geothermal, run-of-river hydro and nuclear must take the form of two-way CfDs "or equivalent schemes with the same effects". These must include an upward limit on revenues, and member states must pass the clawed-back revenues to final customers. Ireland's RESS already works this way: its auction price is "a guaranteed minimum, and also maximum".

## Verified facts

### A. Germany: EEG 2023 sliding market premium (current law at gesetze-im-internet.de/eeg_2014; consolidated text last amended by the Act of 18 December 2025)

1. Payment entitlement: operators of plants using only renewable energy or mine gas have a claim against the grid operator for (1) the market premium under § 20, (2) a feed-in tariff in defined cases (mainly small plants), or (3) a tenant-electricity supplement (§ 19(1)). [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Conditions for the market premium (§ 20): the power must be sold directly (direct marketing); the operator must let the grid operator label it as EEG-supported renewable power; and it must be balanced in a balancing group that contains only such power. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
3. Formula (§ 23a and Annex 1): market premium MP = AW − MW (monthly market value) for plants commissioned or awarded before 1 January 2023; MP = AW − JW (annual market value) for later plants. In both cases, if the result is negative, MP is set to zero. For wind and solar, the market value is technology-specific: each quarter-hour's spot price is weighted by that technology's national generation (from the online projection) and the total is divided by total generation. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
4. Duration (§ 25): market premiums and feed-in tariffs are paid for 20 years from commissioning, unless the Act provides otherwise. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
5. Negative prices (§ 51, current text): for periods when the spot market price is negative, the AW falls to zero. Exemptions cover plants under 100 kW until the end of the year in which a smart meter is fitted, and plants under 2 kW. Transitional rule (§ 100(46)): plants commissioned, or awarded in auctions, between 1 January 2023 and 24 February 2025 keep the §§ 51 and 51a rules in force on 24 February 2025. Older plants may opt in to the new rule after a smart meter is fitted and then get a 0.6 ct/kWh higher AW (§ 100(47), subject to EU state-aid approval under § 101). [confidence: high] [source: 5, 6, 7]
6. Compensation for negative-price hours (§ 51a): the support period is extended by the number of quarter-hours with zero AW in the commissioning year and the following 19 years, rounded up to whole days. For solar, the lost quarter-hours are multiplied by 0.5 and converted into "full-load quarter-hours" using monthly values set in the Act (for example 87 for January and 508 for June), so the extension is weighted towards months when solar produces. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
7. Onshore wind auction, bid date 1 May 2026 (BNetzA): volume 2,494,569 kW; ceiling price 7.25 ct/kWh; 270 bids awarded totalling 2,499,468 kW; award values ranged from 4.44 to 5.19 ct/kWh; volume-weighted average 5.06 ct/kWh. The 2026 onshore wind volume is 10,000 MW in total, spread over four bid dates. [confidence: high] [source: 9]
8. Solar (first segment, ground-mounted) auction, bid date 1 July 2026 (BNetzA, announced 18 August 2026): volume 2,134,567 kW; ceiling price 5.90 ct/kWh; 261 bids awarded totalling 2,134,657 kW; award values from 4.38 to 4.97 ct/kWh; volume-weighted average 4.79 ct/kWh. The 2026 volume is 9,900 MW, spread over three bid dates. The maximum bid size stayed at 20 MW because the 50 MW limit introduced by Solarpaket I still lacked EU state-aid approval. [confidence: high] [source: 10]
9. Several EEG provisions may be applied only after, and in line with, European Commission state-aid approval (§ 101). This shows that the EEG operates under EU state-aid control. [confidence: high] [source: 7]

### B. EU: Regulation (EU) 2024/1747 (electricity market design reform)

10. Regulation (EU) 2024/1747 of 13 June 2024 amends Regulations (EU) 2019/942 and 2019/943. It was published in the Official Journal on 26 June 2024 and entered into force on the twentieth day after publication (that is, 16 July 2024). It inserts new Articles 19a to 19e into Regulation 2019/943. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
11. Article 19d(1): direct price support schemes for investment in new power-generating facilities using the listed sources "shall take the form of two-way contracts for difference or equivalent schemes with the same effects". This applies to contracts concluded on or after 17 July 2027, or 17 July 2029 for offshore hybrid asset projects connected to two or more bidding zones. Participation is voluntary for market participants. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
12. Article 19d(4) sources: wind, solar, geothermal, hydropower without reservoir, and nuclear. Recitals explain the limit to low-carbon, non-fossil technologies with low and stable operating costs that typically do not provide flexibility. Storage and support not linked to generation, such as upfront grants, tax measures or green certificates, are not covered. Member states may exempt small-scale installations and demonstration projects (Article 19d(6)). They may also apply two-way CfDs to substantial repowering or life extension. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
13. Article 19d(2) design principles: preserve incentives to operate and participate efficiently in markets; avoid distorting operation, dispatch, maintenance or bidding in day-ahead, intraday, ancillary and balancing markets; align the minimum remuneration and the upward limit with investment cost and market revenues to avoid overcompensation; set remuneration through open, transparent, non-discriminatory competitive bidding where possible; and include penalty clauses for undue unilateral early termination. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
14. Recitals say two-way CfDs "could vary in duration" and may include injection-based CfDs with one or several strike prices, a floor price, or capability or yardstick CfDs. This allows designs that are not tied to metered output, which avoids distorting dispatch. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
15. Article 19d(5): revenues from two-way CfDs, or their financial equivalent, must be distributed to final customers. They may also fund the cost of the support schemes or investment to reduce final customers' electricity costs. Distribution must keep incentives to cut or shift consumption. [confidence: high] [source: 11]
16. The same Regulation (Article 19a) requires member states to promote PPAs and to ensure instruments such as guarantee schemes at market prices are available, to reduce the financial risks of offtaker payment default. Recitals describe PPAs and two-way CfDs as complementary. [confidence: high] [source: 11]

### C. Examples of two-way CfDs in Europe

17. Ireland, Renewable Electricity Support Scheme (RESS): simple sealed-bid auctions run by EirGrid (lowest-priced bids selected first until the quantity sought is reached); payments are made through the PSO levy, administered by the CRU. The government's description: the bid price "is a guaranteed minimum, and also maximum". When the market price is below the bid, the project is topped up; when it is above, the project pays the difference back. Six auctions have been held: RESS 1 (2020), 2 (2022), 3 (2023), 4 (2024), 5 (2025) and 6 (2026). RESS 1 support lasts up to 16 years; RESS 2–5 projects have a maximum of 16.5 years for early commercial operation and a minimum of 14 years. The residential PSO levy for 2026/27 is €0.51 a month. [confidence: high] [source: 12]
18. Great Britain's CfD (two-sided since AR1, 2014) and Poland's 25-year offshore CfD (Baltic Power) are covered in the cross-referenced sheets. [confidence: high] [source: see cross-references]

## Financing and structure details

- German EEG project finance: the lender's downside floor is the AW (the award value) times eligible output, less the direct marketer's fee and minus negative-price hours. Upside above the AW is fully merchant, because the premium is one-sided. Lenders therefore sized debt to the AW and treated upside as equity value. Since 2023 the annual market value, and since 2025 zero payment in negative hours, have pushed more shape and cannibalisation risk onto solar projects. An intra-year fall in solar capture prices is no longer offset month by month.
- Example (illustrative, computed): a solar plant with AW 4.79 ct/kWh in a year when the annual solar market value JW is 3.50 ct/kWh receives a premium of 1.29 ct/kWh on top of its market sales. If JW is 6.00 ct/kWh, the premium is zero and the plant keeps the full 6.00 ct/kWh. Under a two-way CfD at 4.79 ct/kWh it would pay back 1.21 ct/kWh.
- Two-way CfD counterparties vary: a state-owned company (GB's LCCC), a levy fund (Ireland's PSO via suppliers), or a TSO-administered account (Poland). Counterparty credit and change-in-law protection are central to bankability.

## What went wrong or right, and why

- The EU legislators' stated rationale (recitals of Regulation 2024/1747): two-way CfDs give revenue certainty for investment and an "upward limitation" of market revenues, so in high-price periods they become a source of revenue for member states that can be passed to consumers. This responded to 2021–2023, when one-sided premiums left windfall gains with generators. [source: 11]
- German negative-price reform: the 2025 change removed support in every negative quarter-hour for new plants, but compensated by extending the support period, which shifts value to the end of the asset life. That later-dated value is worth less to lenders than revenue during the debt tenor. (This is an analytical inference, not a quoted source.)

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Ch 19: Put the three payoff profiles side by side: a fixed feed-in tariff, a one-sided sliding premium (EEG: floor at AW, merchant upside) and a two-way CfD (RESS/GB: floor and cap at strike). Use the EEG annual-versus-monthly reference value and the negative-price rule to show that basis between the plant's capture price and the reference price stays with the generator under any premium. Close with the EU 2027 deadline: new support must cap upside, so sponsors will look to corporate PPAs (Ch 20) for upside or for unsupported volumes.

- That Germany has already legislated two-way CfDs for onshore wind and solar: the consolidated EEG examined (amended to 18 December 2025) still provides the one-sided premium. The content and status of any 2026 German CfD reform bill were not verified.
