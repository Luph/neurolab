# Snowy 2.0 pumped-hydro expansion (Australia): cost growth, reset and financing

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest events covered: Snowy Hydro FY2025 Annual Report, published February 2026; cost reassessment launched 3 October 2025; ANAO performance audit, June 2026; Snowy Hydro progress release, 20 September 2026)

## Summary

Snowy 2.0 is a pumped-hydro expansion of the Snowy Mountains Scheme in New South Wales. Owner and developer is Snowy Hydro Limited, a company wholly owned by the Commonwealth of Australia. The design:

- links the existing Tantangara (upper) and Talbingo (lower) reservoirs through about 27 km of tunnels;
- adds a new underground power station with six pump-turbines, roughly 800 m underground;
- provides 2,200 MW of capacity (raised by 10 percent in the 2023 reset) and 350,000 MWh (350 GWh) of storage, about 160 hours at full output;
- has a 150-year design life.

Construction history:

- **2019.** Shareholder ministers approved the project in February 2019. Snowy Hydro appointed the Future Generation Joint Venture (FGJV) as principal contractor under a single wrapped EPC contract. FGJV is Salini Impregilo (now Webuild), Clough and Lane, with Voith Hydro supplying the generating equipment.
- **Problems.** Bushfires, COVID-19, supply chain inflation, design issues and poor geology followed. The tunnel boring machine "Florence" was bogged in soft ground soon after launch in 2022, and a sinkhole formed above the tunnel in December 2022.
- **2023 reset.** Snowy Hydro reset the project. Estimated total delivery cost rose to AUD 12 billion, first power was targeted for the second half of 2027 and full commercial operation for December 2028. The FGJV contract moved to an incentivised target-cost model that settled all outstanding claims.
- **2025 reassessment.** On 3 October 2025, with the project 67 percent complete, Snowy Hydro directed FGJV to carry out a line-by-line cost reassessment, expected to take up to nine months, while holding the December 2028 date.

The project is financed on Snowy Hydro's corporate balance sheet, not as non-recourse project finance. The funding comprises:

- Commonwealth equity injections;
- bank facilities (AUD 7.675 billion of total financing facilities at June 2025);
- an AUD 4.5 billion shareholder construction loan finalised in March 2025.

Snowy Hydro is rated BBB+ by S&P. Snowy 2.0 is the leading recent example of construction risk in large pumped storage: geology, tunnelling productivity, contract form, and transmission dependence (HumeLink, Sydney Ring South, VNI West).

## Verified facts

1. Snowy 2.0 links Tantangara and Talbingo through 27 km of tunnels and a new underground power station. Snowy Hydro began construction in 2019. [confidence: high] [source: 1]
2. Shareholder approval came in February 2019; shareholder ministers issued an updated Statement of Expectations on 26 February 2019. Snowy Hydro appointed Future Generation, a joint venture of Clough, Salini Impregilo and Lane, as principal contractor under a single wrapped EPC contract. Future Generation leads civil works and Voith Hydro delivers the hydro-generation technology. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3]
3. The power station has six pump-turbines about 800 m underground. As of June 2022 three 11 m diameter TBMs were operating. The third, "Florence", was excavating a headrace tunnel of about 15 km (later described as about 17 km). [confidence: high] [source: 3, 6]
4. In April 2019 Snowy Hydro signed a Snowy 2.0 contract with a euro-denominated component and hedged it with forward contracts. EUR 275.2 million was outstanding at 30 June 2022 and EUR 55.9 million at 30 June 2025, with hedges to January 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 3]
5. **The 2023 reset**, from the FY2023 Annual Report:
    - The FGJV EPC contractor was "significantly impacted by external events, comprising bushfires, the COVID-19 pandemic, global supply chain disruption and inflation", and by "design and the impact of variable site and geological conditions", causing delays and cost impacts "beyond the project contingency allowed".
    - After review, estimated total cost was revised to AUD 12 billion.
    - The target for commercial operation of all units became December 2028, with first power in the second half of 2027.
    - The FGJV contract was being revised to an incentivised target-cost model that would settle all outstanding claims.
    - Capacity rose 10 percent to 2,200 MW, with 350,000 MWh of storage, or 160 hours at maximum output.

    [confidence: high] [source: 4]
6. In the same reset, the Hunter Power Project (Kurri Kurri, 660 MW gas) expected cost rose to AUD 950 million. [confidence: high] [source: 4]
7. **Early cost estimates:**
    - The feasibility study released on 21 December 2017 estimated a cost of AUD 3.8–4.5 billion.
    - The original rough cost cited when the project was announced in 2017 was about AUD 2 billion.

    On these figures the AUD 12 billion reset estimate is roughly 2.7 to 3.2 times the feasibility range. [confidence: medium for the 2017 figures (secondary source; not checked against the feasibility study itself)] [source: 7]
8. **FY2025 Annual Report** (year to 30 June 2025):
    - Snowy 2.0 "is facing persistent and material cost pressures", and Snowy Hydro was reassessing the cost to complete.
    - Contractual commitments reflected "the Shareholder's approved target cost limit".
    - A fourth TBM received planning approval, to tunnel through the Long Plain Fault Zone.
    - Work was disrupted by protected industrial action and safety matters.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
9. **Financing at June 2025:**
    - Total interest-bearing liabilities were AUD 6,504.1 million, up AUD 1,705.4 million from AUD 4,798.7 million a year earlier.
    - Total financing facilities were AUD 7,675 million (AUD 6,409 million used), up from AUD 5,900 million. They comprise bank debt and shareholder loans maturing between 2026 and 2031.
    - In March 2025 Snowy Hydro finalised an AUD 4,500 million shareholder construction loan and drew AUD 150 million in April 2025. The remaining AUD 4,350 million is to be drawn at AUD 1,450 million a year until June 2028.
    - Contributed equity rose by AUD 1,625 million through issuing shares to the Commonwealth (FY2024: AUD 277 million).
    - The S&P rating stayed at BBB+.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]
10. In FY2022 contributed equity rose by AUD 902 million through shares issued to the Commonwealth. [confidence: high] [source: 3]
11. **Safety disruptions:**
    - On 27 February 2025, after an underground fan failed and lost part of a blade (no injuries), Snowy Hydro sought an independent safety review and required changes to FGJV's leadership. Underground work stopped and restarted progressively.
    - Snowy Hydro agreed to pay AUD 400,000 under an enforceable undertaking with the NSW EPA over a 2023 environmental incident, with FGJV contributing half.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1, 6]
12. **The 2025 reassessment** (release of 3 October 2025). Snowy Hydro directed FGJV to undertake a "comprehensive line-by-line reassessment" of its costs, expected to take up to nine months and verified by independent cost experts. Status and causes as Snowy Hydro stated them:
    - The project was 67 percent complete.
    - Delivery had nearly doubled from 0.57 to 0.91 percent a month on average since the reset, with 0.98 percent in August 2025.
    - Contractor productivity targets were not realised.
    - The fourth TBM could no longer be absorbed within project costs.
    - There were unforeseen supply chain cost increases on bespoke procurement, including major offshore purchases for the power station.

    [confidence: high] [source: 5]
13. The Australian National Audit Office (ANAO) published a performance audit, "Delivery of Snowy 2.0", in June 2026. Snowy Hydro agreed with four of five recommendations and partly agreed with the one on public reporting. It said many matters reflected "deliberate commercial positions" taken to hold the principal contractor accountable. An earlier 2022 ANAO audit (governance of early implementation) found appropriate governance arrangements. [confidence: high for Snowy Hydro's response; the ANAO report text was not accessible] [source: 3, 8]
14. **Transmission dependence** (FY2025 Annual Report):
    - HumeLink (approved December 2024) is needed to connect Snowy 2.0 to the NSW grid.
    - Sydney Ring South has an expected completion of September 2028.
    - VNI West's timeline was revised to 2030.

    [confidence: high] [source: 1]

## Financing and structure details

- **Borrower and recourse:** Snowy Hydro Limited, a corporate borrower 100 percent owned by the Commonwealth. Debt is unsecured credit facilities (bank debt plus shareholder loans). This is corporate finance, not limited recourse. [1]
- **Equity:** repeated Commonwealth equity subscriptions (AUD 902 million in FY2022, AUD 277 million in FY2024, AUD 1,625 million in FY2025). [1, 3]
- **Shareholder construction loan:** AUD 4.5 billion, drawn in instalments to June 2028. [1]
- **Contract:** originally a single wrapped EPC with FGJV (civil) and Voith (electromechanical). It became an incentivised target-cost contract in 2023. [2, 4]
- **Hedging:** euro forward contracts on equipment purchases. [1, 3]
- **Rating:** S&P BBB+ (stable through FY2025). [1]

## What went wrong or right, and why

- **Snowy Hydro's explanation (FY2023):** external shocks (bushfires, COVID-19, supply chain, inflation), design challenges and variable geology caused delays and cost beyond contingency. The reset moved to a target-cost contract to align incentives and settle claims. [4]
- **Snowy Hydro's explanation (October 2025):** the reset improved productivity but not enough. Geological de-risking (TBM4) and bespoke supply chain costs could no longer be absorbed. [5]
- **Snowy Hydro's case for value:** it argues the project remains economic and critical for multi-day storage. It cites Baringa Partners modelling (released June 2026) commissioned by Snowy Hydro. This is the owner's position, not independent consensus. [1, 9]

## Teaching angles by chapter

- **Ch 73 (Storage, transmission and interconnectors):** Use Snowy 2.0 to teach why pumped storage is a tunnelling and geotechnical project as much as an energy project. The cost moved from a feasibility range of about AUD 3.8–4.5 billion to AUD 12 billion, and that figure was itself under reassessment by late 2025. Show how revenue value depends on transmission that someone else builds (HumeLink, Sydney Ring South, VNI West). Discuss why the project was funded with state equity and shareholder loans rather than non-recourse debt, and what a lender would have needed: a geotechnical baseline, a contingency size, a contract form, and completion support from the shareholder.

## Do not state

- Any revised total cost figure above AUD 12 billion, or a new completion date. As of the latest Snowy Hydro documents reviewed, the reassessment outcome had not been published.
- The 2017 AUD 2 billion and AUD 3.8–4.5 billion figures as primary-verified. They come from Wikipedia citing contemporary reports.
- The original EPC contract price (often reported as about AUD 5.1 billion) and any 2022 interim estimate (often reported as AUD 5.9 billion). Not verified here.
- The ANAO 2026 audit's findings or quotations. The ANAO site was unreachable; only Snowy Hydro's response was reviewed.
- That Snowy 2.0 has bank project finance or green loans specific to the project. Facilities are corporate.
- Any "cost per MWh" or benefit-cost claim except as Snowy Hydro's own or its consultant's position.
