# Sadara Chemical Company (Jubail, Saudi Arabia)

As of: 2026-10-03 (latest event covered: Dow Inc. Form 10-Q for the quarter ended 30 June 2026, filed in 2026; production at Sadara halted since 31 March 2026, restart not verified)

## Summary

Sadara Chemical Company is a joint venture formed in 2011 by Saudi Aramco (65 percent) and The Dow Chemical Company (35 percent) to build and operate a 26-unit integrated chemicals complex in Jubail Industrial City II, Saudi Arabia, producing about 3 million tonnes a year of specialty chemicals and performance plastics. Its approximately USD 12.5 billion of senior debt, the largest project financing closed in the Middle East at the time, combined a SAR 7.5 billion (USD 2 billion) musharaka-based project sukuk closed on 2 April 2013 with a main financing of about USD 10.5 billion signed on 16 June 2013: a direct loan of approximately USD 4.975 billion from US Ex-Im (its largest commitment ever), direct loans from Spain's FIEM, Korea's K-Exim and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), ECA-covered commercial tranches backed by COFACE, Euler Hermes, K-Exim, K-Sure and UK Export Finance, uncovered commercial loans in US dollars and Saudi riyals, and Islamic istisna-ijara and wakala-ijara facilities. A "split-closing" let the sukuk close ten weeks before the rest of the senior debt. The sponsors gave completion guarantees, released when Sadara reached project completion in November 2020. Weak results led to a March 2021 debt reprofiling (final maturity extended from 2029 to 2038, principal grace period to June 2026, new USD 3.7 billion pro rata sponsor guarantee). Sadara's accumulated losses reached SAR 44.2 billion by end-2025, and on 31 March 2026 it halted all production because of supply-chain disruption from the Middle East conflict; Dow recorded a USD 298 million increase in its guarantee liability in the first quarter of 2026.

## Verified facts

1. Sadara was formed in 2011 as a joint venture between a Dow subsidiary (Dow Saudi Arabia Holding B.V.) and a Saudi Aramco subsidiary (Excellent Performance Chemicals Company) to build and operate a petrochemical complex at Jubail Industrial City II in the Eastern Province. The joint venture was announced on 25 July 2011; the sponsors had signed a memorandum of understanding on 12 May 2007. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
2. Ownership is Saudi Aramco 65 percent and Dow 35 percent; Dow's 2026 filings continue to describe a 35 percent interest. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 6, 7]
3. The complex has 26 manufacturing units, with a mixed-feed cracker (ethane and naphtha) and an aromatics plant as the key feed units, and was designed to produce over 3 million tonnes a year of high value-added chemical products and performance plastics. It achieved full commercial operations in 2017. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
4. Total project cost was approximately USD 19–20 billion (PFI/Milbank: approximately USD 19 billion; other trade sources: USD 19.3 billion or USD 20 billion). [confidence: medium; use "approximately USD 20 billion"] [source: 2, 4, 5]
5. Total senior debt raised at the 2013 closings was approximately USD 12.5 billion: the USD 2 billion sukuk plus about USD 10.5 billion (SAR 39.37 billion) of main financing. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 3, 5]
6. The sukuk was sized at SAR 7.5 billion (USD 2 billion equivalent), priced at 95 basis points over six-month SAIBOR, with a tenor of 15.75 years, and was 2.6 times oversubscribed. It was the second project finance sukuk in Saudi Arabia, after the USD 1 billion Satorp sukuk of 2011. It was issued on 31 March 2013 (per the issuer's restructuring notice) and closed on 2 April 2013, with scheduled maturity on 15 December 2028 before the 2021 reprofiling. [confidence: high] [source: 2, 8]
7. The sukuk used a musharaka (Sharia-compliant partnership) between Sadara and a special-purpose issuer: the issuer contributed the sukuk proceeds and Sadara contributed its rights under the land lease with the Royal Commission for Jubail and Yanbu; Sadara procured project assets under a procurement agreement and leased them under a forward lease; rentals flowed to certificate holders. Certificates benefited from the sponsors' completion guarantees before completion. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
8. Under the "split-closing" regime, the sukuk closed on 2 April 2013, after the sponsors had commitments from commercial banks but more than two months before the remaining senior debt was signed (16 June 2013) and closed (30 June 2013). The certificate holders' consent was not required to amend and restate the finance documents at the second signing, subject to safeguards: redemption at par if the rest of the senior debt was not signed within a specified period, protection of certain key intercreditor rights, and limits on adverse amendments. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
9. In September 2012 US Ex-Im agreed its largest-ever commitment, approximately USD 4.975 billion, for Sadara. ECA direct loans were also provided by FIEM (Spain) and K-Exim (Korea), and PIF provided a direct loan. COFACE, Euler Hermes, K-Exim, K-Sure and UK Export Finance (through ECGD) provided ECA cover for commercial bank tranches in US dollars and in some cases Saudi riyals; commercial banks also provided uncovered loans in the same currencies, including a letter of credit facility in the riyal facility. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
10. Reported amounts (trade press, June 2013): US Ex-Im about USD 5 billion; PIF USD 1.3 billion; commercial bank lending about USD 2 billion, of which about USD 1.1 billion from US banks in dollars and the rest from Saudi banks in riyals; and about USD 1.8 billion from other ECAs, including USD 700 million from ECGD (UKEF), USD 500 million from K-Sure and USD 425 million from Hermes. [confidence: medium; single trade-press source for the split] [source: 3]
11. UKEF's guarantee of USD 700 million supported loans by Bank of Tokyo-Mitsubishi UFJ, HSBC, JP Morgan, Mizuho and SMBC and was described as UKEF's largest project finance facility to that date. [confidence: medium] [source: 4]
12. The main financing included Islamic istisna-ijara and wakala-ijara facilities, with 11 Islamic finance institutions as mandated Islamic lead arrangers. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
13. Financial advisers to the sponsors were Royal Bank of Scotland and Riyad Bank; Greengate advised the ECAs and PIF; Milbank was common counsel to all creditors and to the sukuk lead managers; White & Case advised Saudi Aramco and Shearman & Sterling advised Dow. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
14. Process: the sponsors released a 400-page draft financing term sheet to the ECAs and PIF in August 2011; term-sheet negotiations were largely concluded in three all-party meetings between February and April 2012; a PIF bridge financing was signed in May 2012; the term sheet went to the commercial bank market on 28 May 2012. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
15. Intercreditor voting included an "ECA majority" concept, a simple majority of the ECAs that generally had to include US Ex-Im, for a range of qualified-majority decisions that would customarily be super-majority or all-lender decisions. The sukuk holders received a more limited range of voting rights than the ECAs and PIF. Additional senior debt was permitted subject to ratio tests. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
16. The completion test grouped the 26 units into three "envelopes" subject to integrated performance testing and a 60-day completion, operation and reliability test within a 90-day testing period. The scheduled project completion date was 31 December 2017. Pre-completion net revenues could, subject to conditions, pay project costs, refinance equity and fund shareholder distributions. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
17. Sponsor commitments: Saudi Aramco and Dow were required to keep their 65:35 ownership until completion (an IPO of up to 30 percent of Sadara was envisaged), and after completion generally to maintain at least 20 percent each. The Saudi Industrial Development Fund (SIDF) could join later on its customary terms (first-ranking security, restricted tenor) subject to arrangements on residual security proceeds. [confidence: high] [source: 2]
18. A Dow subsidiary initially had the exclusive right and obligation to market Sadara's products outside the Middle East region. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 2]
19. Sadara achieved project completion in November 2020, releasing about USD 10 billion of shareholder completion guarantees (per Saudi Aramco's 2021 disclosure as reported). [confidence: medium] [source: 9]
20. Sukuk holders were asked to vote on 26 January 2021 (with a postponed date of 8 February 2021 if quorum failed) on reprofiling the SAR 7.5 billion sukuk; Sadara had sought consent in November 2020. [confidence: high] [source: 8]
21. On 25 March 2021 Sadara entered an amended and restated shareholders' agreement and finalized a debt restructuring with its agency creditors and commercial lenders: final maturity extended from 2029 to 2038; principal grace period until June 2026 (15 June 2026 per Aramco's announcement); a new USD 3.7 billion pro rata sponsor guarantee of principal, plus all interest due during the grace period, in proportion to ownership (approximately USD 2.405 billion Aramco, USD 1.295 billion Dow). Marketing of finished products was to transfer from Sadara to Dow and Aramco in proportion to ownership, phased over five years from mid-2021, with Sadara paying marketing fees; Aramco agreed to increase ethane and natural gasoline feedstock supply. [confidence: high] [source: 1, 9, 10]
22. As part of the reprofiling, Sadara established a USD 500 million revolving credit facility guaranteed by Dow to fund Dow's pro rata share of any shortfall. Sadara drew USD 80 million under it in 2025; it expired in the second quarter of 2026 and Dow paid the USD 80 million guarantee obligation. In the second quarter of 2026 Dow issued an USD 86 million letter of credit under the reprofiling commitments, expiring in the fourth quarter of 2026. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
23. Dow currently guarantees approximately USD 1.2 billion of principal (and related interest) of Sadara's project financing debt, and states that there is a more than remote probability that some future performance under the guarantee may be required. [confidence: high] [source: 6]
24. Sadara reported a 2025 net loss of SAR 5.8 billion (2024: SAR 4.2 billion), revenue of SAR 9.9 billion (down 14.8 percent), and accumulated losses of SAR 44.2 billion, 124 percent of share capital; shareholders approved continuing operations. [confidence: medium; single trade-press source reporting the company's disclosure] [source: 11]
25. On 31 March 2026 Sadara disclosed to the Saudi Exchange that it had halted production at the Jubail complex because of disruption to its supply chains (reported as feedstock and supply disruption through the Strait of Hormuz during the conflict involving Iran), with restart depending on domestic and international factors and no estimate given. [confidence: high for the halt and date; medium for the specific causal detail] [source: 12, 13]
26. In the first quarter of 2026 the fair value of Dow's Sadara guarantee liability increased by USD 298 million (reported as a USD 292 million significant item), attributed to adverse business conditions including the conflicts in the Middle East; Dow suspended recognition of its share of Sadara equity losses because its Sadara-related liabilities reached its relevant obligations and commitments. At 30 June 2026 Dow's estimated guarantee liability was USD 450 million and its negative investment balance in Sadara USD 793 million. [confidence: high] [source: 6, 7]

## What went wrong or right, and why

Right, at financing: Milbank's account in PFI emphasizes the sponsors' use of ECA-led direct lending to anchor the deal, the split-closing that let the sukuk price first without making later senior debt hostage to a certificate-holder vote, and an ECA-majority voting construct that gave US Ex-Im's large commitment a decisive voice while preserving defined protections for sukuk holders. [source: 2]

Wrong, in operations: the March 2021 restructuring was disclosed by Dow as part of shareholder actions "to improve Sadara operating results," with repayments "better aligned" to expected cash flows, and paired with commercial changes (sponsor marketing, more Aramco feedstock). This indicates that cash flow after completion did not support the original 2029 amortization. [source: 1]

The 2026 production halt shows a different risk: physical supply-chain dependence on maritime routes during regional conflict. Dow attributes the 2026 increase in its guarantee liability to adverse business conditions including the Middle East conflicts. [source: 6, 13]

No independent evaluator has published a full analysis of Sadara's underperformance; present causes cautiously and attribute them to company disclosures.

## Teaching angles by chapter

- Chapter 33 (Islamic project finance): Sadara is the reference case for a project sukuk sitting alongside ECA, PIF and conventional bank debt. Walk through the musharaka/procurement/forward-lease chain, the completion guarantees supporting certificate holders before completion, and the split-closing safeguards (par redemption if the rest of the debt is not signed, entrenched intercreditor rights). Add the 2021 reprofiling as the case where a listed sukuk required a formal certificate-holder vote, which is slower than bank-lender consent.
- Chapter 53 (Intercreditor arrangements): Use the ECA-majority concept (simple majority of ECAs including US Ex-Im for qualified-majority matters), the narrower voting rights of sukuk holders, and the ratio-tested permission for additional senior debt (and potential SIDF participation with first-ranking security) to show how an intercreditor agreement balances a dominant lender, a dispersed bondholder class and future dilution.
- Chapter 77 (Refining, petrochemicals and manufacturing): Use Sadara to teach why petrochemicals project finance depends on sponsor integration (Aramco feedstock, Dow technology and marketing) and on completion guarantees tested by a demanding reliability test across 26 units. Then follow the credit through weak operations to the 2021 extension and sponsor guarantee, and the 2026 shutdown, to show that a "sponsor-supported" industrial project financing carries market and supply-chain risk that the contract set did not transfer.
