# Unit u12 glossary (Chapters 57 to 60)

Terms owned by Chapters 57, 58, 59 and 60. Home sections refer to the locked tables of contents in `u12.md`. Overlaps flagged in the u12 change requests (unitary charge, political risk insurance, minimum revenue guarantee) await the glossary canon's decision.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| value for money | VfM | The margin by which the risk-adjusted present cost of delivering a project through a PPP is lower than the risk-adjusted present cost of delivering the same output through conventional public procurement. | 57.2.1 |
| public sector comparator | PSC | The risk-adjusted present cost of delivering a PPP's output specification through conventional public procurement, used as the benchmark in a value-for-money test. | 57.2.2 |
| reference project | – | The most efficient form of public delivery of the required output that the public sector comparator costs. | 57.2.1 |
| financing premium | – | The extra cost, per year or in present value, of financing an asset at the private cost of capital rather than at the government's borrowing rate. | 57.1.2 |
| competitive neutrality | – | An adjustment to the public sector comparator that removes advantages or disadvantages the public sector has only because it is public, such as tax and insurance differences. | 57.2.2 |
| transferable risk | – | A risk the public sector would bear under conventional delivery that the PPP contract passes to the private party, priced into the public sector comparator at its expected cost. | 57.2.2 |
| retained risk | – | A risk the public sector keeps under both conventional delivery and the PPP, included on both sides of a value-for-money test. | 57.2.2 |
| shadow bid | – | The public sector's own estimate of what a private bidder would charge for the reference project, used to test value for money before real bids arrive. | 57.2.2 |
| optimism bias | – | The systematic tendency of project appraisals to understate cost and duration and overstate benefits, corrected by evidence-based uplifts. | 57.2.3 |
| affordability | – | Whether a project's payments, indexed over every budget year, fit within the budget envelope or ceiling the government has set. | 57.3.1 |
| PPP commitment ceiling | – | A government-set limit on total annual PPP payments, usually expressed as a share of revenue or of a budget. | 57.3.2 |
| statistical classification | – | The national-accounts decision on whether a PPP asset and its liability sit on or off the government's balance sheet. | 57.4.1 |
| contingent liability | – | An obligation of the government that becomes payable only if a specified event occurs, such as a guarantee call or an early termination. | 57.5.1 |
| minimum revenue guarantee | MRG | A government undertaking to pay a concessionaire the shortfall between actual revenue and a guaranteed floor. | 57.5.1 |
| implicit contingent liability | – | A payment the government is expected to make to prevent the failure of a service, without any legal obligation to do so. | 57.5.1 |
| PPP unit | – | A government body that screens, advises on, standardizes, and often procures and manages public–private partnerships. | 57.6.1 |
| unsolicited proposal | – | A project proposal submitted to a government by a private party without a government request. | 57.7.1 |
| Swiss challenge | – | A regime in which an unsolicited proposal is tendered and the original proponent may match the best competing bid. | 57.7.2 |
| bonus system | – | A regime in which the proponent of an unsolicited proposal receives a stated scoring or price advantage in a competitive tender. | 57.7.2 |
| best-and-final-offer system (unsolicited proposals) | – | A regime in which the proponent of an unsolicited proposal is guaranteed a place in the final round of a competitive tender. | 57.7.2 |
| Private Finance Initiative | PFI | The UK government's PPP program from 1992, in which private project companies designed, built, financed and maintained public assets in return for a performance-related unitary charge; closed to new projects in 2018. | 57.8.1 |
| Private Finance 2 | PF2 | The 2012 reform of the PFI, with a public minority equity stake and equity-return transparency, used for six projects before its abolition in 2018. | 57.8.2 |
| design, build, finance and maintain | DBFM | A PPP model in which the private party designs, builds, finances and maintains an asset that the public sector operates. | 58.1.1 |
| design, build, finance, operate and maintain | DBFOM | A PPP model in which the private party also operates the asset and delivers the service for the contract term. | 58.1.1 |
| build, operate, transfer | BOT | A model in which the private party builds and operates an asset under a concession and transfers it to the public sector at the end of the term. | 58.1.1 |
| build, own, operate, transfer | BOOT | A BOT in which the private party owns the asset during the term before transferring it. | 58.1.1 |
| build, own, operate | BOO | A model in which the private party builds, owns and operates the asset with no obligation to transfer it. | 58.1.1 |
| availability-based PPP | – | A PPP in which the public sector pays for the asset being available and performing to standard, so the private party carries no demand risk. | 58.1.2 |
| user-pay PPP | – | A PPP in which the private party is paid by users through tolls or fares and carries demand risk. | 58.1.2 |
| shadow toll | – | A payment by the public sector to a concessionaire per vehicle using the road, with no charge to users. | 58.1.3 |
| least present value of revenue | LPVR | A concession awarded to the bidder asking the lowest present value of revenue, with the term ending when that value has been collected. | 58.1.3 |
| request for qualifications | RFQ | The invitation in which an authority asks interested parties to show they meet the criteria to bid. | 58.2.1 |
| prequalification | – | The stage in which an authority selects the bidders allowed to receive the request for proposals. | 58.2.1 |
| request for proposals | RFP | The tender document that sets the contract, the requirements and the evaluation rules on which shortlisted bidders submit proposals. | 58.2.1 |
| competitive dialogue | – | A procurement procedure in which the authority discusses solutions with shortlisted bidders before inviting final tenders. | 58.2.2 |
| best and final offer | BAFO | The final bids requested from remaining bidders after evaluation or dialogue, on which the preferred bidder is chosen. | 58.2.1 |
| preferred bidder | – | The bidder selected to negotiate and sign the PPP contract, subject to conditions, before financial close. | 58.2.1 |
| bid security | – | A guarantee or bond a bidder posts to secure its commitment to sign on its bid terms if selected. | 58.2.3 |
| funding competition | – | A competition run by a preferred bidder, often required by the authority, to set the price of its debt or equity. | 58.2.3 |
| abnormally low bid | – | A bid priced so far below the reference or other bids that the authority must test whether it can be delivered. | 58.2.4 |
| unitary charge | UC | The single periodic payment an authority makes under an availability PPP, reduced by deductions for unavailability and poor performance. | 58.3.1 |
| payment mechanism | – | The contract schedule that sets the unitary charge, its indexation, and the deductions for unavailability and poor performance. | 58.3.1 |
| functional unit | – | A defined part of a PPP asset whose availability is measured separately for deductions, such as a ward, a theatre or a lane-kilometer. | 58.3.2 |
| rectification period | – | The time a private party has to fix a failure before a deduction starts to accrue. | 58.3.2 |
| deduction | – | A reduction in the unitary charge for unavailability or a service failure. | 58.3.2 |
| service failure points | – | Points accrued for performance failures that convert into deductions and count toward warning and termination thresholds. | 58.3.3 |
| ratchet (performance regime) | – | A rule that raises deductions or points for repeated failures of the same type. | 58.3.3 |
| benchmarking | – | A periodic comparison of the price of a service in a PPP with market prices, leading to a price adjustment. | 58.4.2 |
| market testing | – | Retendering a service within a PPP to reset its price. | 58.4.2 |
| relief event | – | An event that excuses the private party from termination and deductions for the period it lasts, without compensation. | 58.4.3 |
| compensation event | – | An event, usually caused by the authority, that entitles the private party to time and money. | 58.4.3 |
| retendering procedure | – | A contractor-default compensation method in which the authority retenders the contract and pays the private party the adjusted highest compliant tender price less retendering costs. | 58.5.2 |
| debt floor | – | A minimum compensation on termination, usually a share of senior debt, paid regardless of the cause of termination. | 58.5.2 |
| refinancing gain | – | The increase in the present value of distributions to equity caused by a refinancing, measured at the base-case equity IRR. | 58.6.2 |
| handback requirements | – | The condition and residual-life standards an asset must meet when it returns to the public sector at the end of a PPP. | 58.7.1 |
| handback reserve | – | A reserve account funded in the final years of a PPP to pay for works needed to meet the handback requirements. | 58.7.2 |
| geotechnical risk band | – | A contract mechanism that divides the cost of unforeseen ground conditions between the parties in layers, with a termination right above the top layer. | 58.8.1 |
| country risk | – | The risk that conditions in a country, including its economy, currency regime, institutions and politics, prevent a project or its counterparties from paying. | 59.1.1 |
| sovereign risk | – | The risk that a government fails to pay or perform its own obligations, including guarantees. | 59.1.1 |
| transfer risk | – | The risk that a borrower holding local currency cannot send foreign currency out of the country because the authorities restrict transfers. | 59.2.1 |
| convertibility risk | – | The risk that local currency cannot be exchanged into foreign currency through legal channels. | 59.2.1 |
| devaluation risk | – | The risk that the local currency loses value against the currency of a project's debt or costs. | 59.2.1 |
| FX allocation queue | – | A central-bank system that rations foreign exchange by making buyers wait for allocations. | 59.2.1 |
| currency mismatch | – | A gap between the currency in which a project earns revenue and the currency of its debt or costs. | 59.2.2 |
| sovereign ceiling | – | The practice of rating agencies and lenders of rarely rating a domestic borrower's foreign-currency debt above its sovereign's rating. | 59.1.2 |
| convertibility and transfer undertaking | – | A host-government or central-bank commitment that a project company will be able to convert local currency and transfer it abroad for specified purposes. | 59.3.3 |
| offshore account | – | A bank account held outside the host country into which a project's converted revenues are paid and from which debt is serviced. | 59.3.4 |
| devaluation case | – | A sizing or stress case in which the local currency falls by a stated amount. | 59.3.5 |
| standby letter of credit | SBLC | A bank's undertaking to pay a beneficiary on demand if the applicant fails to pay, used as payment security for an offtaker. | 59.5.1 |
| confirmation (of an LC) | – | A second bank's undertaking to pay under a letter of credit, adding its credit to the issuing bank's. | 59.5.1 |
| replenishment | – | The obligation to restore a letter of credit to its required amount after a drawing. | 59.5.1 |
| escrow account | – | An account controlled by an agent under agreed instructions, used to ring-fence collections or payments. | 59.5.2 |
| liquidity facility | – | A committed facility, often supported by a DFI, that funds an offtaker's payment shortfall or reimburses an LC bank for a limited period. | 59.5.4 |
| netting agreement | – | An agreement that sets off amounts owed between parties, such as an offtaker's arrears against a project's payables to a state supplier. | 59.5.5 |
| payment security stack | – | The layered set of liquidity, credit and termination protections behind an offtaker's payment obligations. | 59.4.2 |
| months of runway | – | The number of months a project can meet its cash needs with zero receipts from its offtaker, using its LC, reserves and cash. | 59.5.6 |
| arrears | – | Amounts overdue beyond the contractual payment terms. | 59.4.1 |
| political risk | – | The risk that actions by a host or home state, or political violence, reduce a project's value or its ability to pay. | 60.1.1 |
| expropriation | – | The taking of a project's assets or ownership rights by a state, directly or through measures with the same effect. | 60.1.1 |
| creeping expropriation | – | A series of state measures, none decisive alone, that together deprive an investor of its investment's value. | 60.1.2 |
| political violence | – | War, revolution, insurrection, terrorism, sabotage or civil disturbance causing loss to a project. | 60.1.1 |
| breach of contract cover | – | Political risk insurance that pays when a state breaches or repudiates a contract with the investor and the investor cannot obtain or enforce an award. | 60.4.2 |
| non-honoring cover | – | Political risk insurance that pays when a sovereign or state-owned entity fails to pay an unconditional financial obligation, without requiring an award. | 60.4.2 |
| denial of recourse | – | A trigger for breach-of-contract cover in which government interference prevents the investor from obtaining an arbitral award. | 60.4.2 |
| political risk insurance | PRI | Insurance against losses from specified political perils, sold by MIGA, ECAs and private insurers to lenders and equity investors. | 60.4.1 |
| waiting period | – | The time that must pass after a covered event before an insured may claim under a political risk policy. | 60.4.2 |
| subrogation | – | The insurer's right, after paying a claim, to pursue the insured's rights against the party that caused the loss. | 60.4.4 |
| obsolescing bargain | – | The shift of bargaining power from investor to host state once the investor's capital is sunk. | 60.2.1 |
| multilateral halo | – | The deterrent effect a multilateral institution's participation has on a host state's willingness to act against a project. | 60.5.1 |
| local content | – | Obligations or commitments to use host-country labor, suppliers, equity or training in a project. | 60.3.3 |
| Foreign Corrupt Practices Act | FCPA | The US law that prohibits bribery of foreign officials by covered persons and imposes accounting and internal-control obligations on issuers. | 60.6.2 |
| UK Bribery Act | UKBA | The UK's 2010 anti-bribery statute, including the corporate offence of failing to prevent bribery by associated persons. | 60.6.2 |
| facilitation payment | – | A small payment to an official to speed up a routine action, permitted under the FCPA's narrow exception and prohibited under the UK Bribery Act. | 60.6.2 |
| adequate procedures | – | The anti-bribery policies and controls that give a company a defense to the UK Bribery Act's failure-to-prevent offence. | 60.6.2 |
| sanctions | – | Legal restrictions imposed by a state or group of states on dealings with designated persons, sectors or countries. | 60.7.1 |
| Specially Designated Nationals list | SDN list | The US Treasury list of persons whose property is blocked and with whom US persons may not deal. | 60.7.1 |
| 50 percent rule | – | The US rule that an entity owned 50% or more, in aggregate, by blocked persons is itself blocked. | 60.7.1 |
| secondary sanctions | – | Sanctions that penalize non-US persons for dealings with targets of US sanctions. | 60.7.1 |
