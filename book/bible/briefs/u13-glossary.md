# Unit u13 glossary (Chapters 61 to 65)

Terms owned by Chapters 61 to 65, with canonical definitions and home sections. Abbreviation "–" means none.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| drawdown request | – | The project company's notice asking lenders to fund a stated amount on a stated date, with the supporting invoices, certificates and confirmations the finance documents require. | Section 61.2.1 (Ch 61) |
| drawdown certificate | – | The independent engineer's certificate that the work and costs supporting a drawdown request have been performed or incurred and that the stated conditions are met. | Section 61.2.1 (Ch 61) |
| cost-to-complete test | – | A condition to each drawdown that the funds still available to the project company equal or exceed the independent engineer's estimate of all remaining costs to completion. | Section 61.2.3 (Ch 61) |
| funding shortfall | – | The amount by which the independent engineer's cost to complete exceeds undrawn commitments plus cash in the construction accounts. | Section 61.2.3 (Ch 61) |
| owner's contingency | – | The unallocated budget line held by the project company for cost increases outside the EPC lump sum, drawn under lender controls. | Section 61.3.1 (Ch 61) |
| risk-weighted exposure | RWE | The sum of probability times cost across the open items on a construction risk register, used to test the adequacy of remaining contingency. | Section 61.3.2 (Ch 61) |
| earned value | EV | The budgeted value of the work actually performed to date, measured as the assessed percent complete times the contract price. | Section 61.4.2 (Ch 61) |
| planned value | PV | The budgeted value of the work scheduled to have been performed by a given date. | Section 61.4.2 (Ch 61) |
| schedule performance index | SPI | Earned value divided by planned value; below 1.00 indicates work behind plan. | Section 61.4.2 (Ch 61) |
| payments ahead of progress | – | Certified payments to the contractor in excess of earned value, which is the owner's unsecured exposure if the contractor fails. | Section 61.4.2 (Ch 61) |
| concurrent delay | – | A period of delay to completion caused by two or more events at the same time, at least one at each party's risk. | Section 61.6.1 (Ch 61) |
| prolongation costs | – | The contractor's additional time-related costs claimed when completion is delayed by an event at the owner's risk. | Section 61.5.2 (Ch 61) |
| delay cash bridge | – | The book's framework that sets the project company's daily cost of delay against the LDs, insurance and relief that fund it, and cumulates the gap (Framework 61.1). | Section 61.6.3 (Ch 61) |
| subrogation | – | An insurer's right, after paying a claim, to pursue in the insured's name the party that caused the loss. | Section 61.7.3 (Ch 61) |
| replacement contractor | – | A contractor engaged to complete works abandoned by a defaulting or insolvent EPC contractor, usually at a premium reflecting latent-defect and productivity risk. | Section 61.8.3 (Ch 61) |
| latent defect | – | A defect in completed or part-completed work that is not discoverable by reasonable inspection when the work is taken over. | Section 61.8.3 (Ch 61) |
| lenders' completion | – | The point, defined in the finance documents and certified by the independent engineer, at which the project has met the physical, operational, financial and documentary tests that release sponsor completion support; also called financial completion or project completion. | Section 61.11.1 (Ch 61) |
| reliability run | – | A continuous operating test of stated duration and minimum availability that a plant must pass before taking-over or lenders' completion. | Section 61.11.2 (Ch 61) |
| completion long-stop date | – | The latest date by which lenders' completion must occur, after which an event of default arises. | Section 61.11.2 (Ch 61) |
| buy-down LDs | – | Performance liquidated damages paid for a permanent shortfall in output or efficiency and applied, under the finance documents, to prepay debt so that cover ratios are restored. | Section 61.11.3 (Ch 61) |
| operating covenant calendar | – | The book's framework listing every recurring reporting, testing, approval and renewal obligation of an operating project company with its date, owner, reviewer and consequence of a miss (Framework 62.1). | Section 62.1.2 (Ch 62) |
| operating report | – | The periodic report to lenders on generation or throughput, availability, outages, revenue, receivables, costs, incidents and compliance. | Section 62.2.1 (Ch 62) |
| compliance certificate | – | A certificate signed by an authorized officer of the project company stating the financial ratios for a test date, the calculations behind them, and that no default is continuing. | Section 62.2.2 (Ch 62) |
| test date | – | The date at the end of a calculation period on which financial covenants and distribution conditions are measured. | Section 62.4.1 (Ch 62) |
| calculation period | – | The period, usually the 12 months ending on a test date, over which historic ratios are computed. | Section 62.4.1 (Ch 62) |
| operating budget | – | The annual budget of operating and maintenance costs that the project company submits for lender review or approval under the finance documents. | Section 62.3.1 (Ch 62) |
| permitted variance | – | The tolerance, in aggregate or by line, by which actual operating costs may exceed the approved budget without lender consent. | Section 62.3.2 (Ch 62) |
| default budget | – | The budget that applies when a new budget has not been approved, usually the prior year's budget indexed or capped. | Section 62.3.2 (Ch 62) |
| trapped cash | – | Cash that the waterfall would otherwise distribute but that stays in the project company's accounts because a distribution condition has failed. | Section 62.4.3 (Ch 62) |
| availability headroom | – | The margin between actual availability and the contractual target within which outages do not reduce capacity payments. | Section 62.5.1 (Ch 62) |
| waiver | – | Lenders' agreement not to treat a specified breach as a default or not to exercise a specified right, without changing the underlying term. | Section 62.7.1 (Ch 62) |
| consent | – | Lenders' permission for an action the finance documents prohibit or restrict unless approved. | Section 62.7.1 (Ch 62) |
| amendment | – | A change to the terms of a finance document that operates for the future. | Section 62.7.1 (Ch 62) |
| waiver fee | – | A fee, usually a percentage of outstanding debt, paid to lenders for granting a waiver or amendment. | Section 62.7.3 (Ch 62) |
| margin uplift | – | A temporary increase in the loan margin agreed as a condition of a waiver or amendment. | Section 62.7.3 (Ch 62) |
| reservation of rights | – | A statement by lenders that, by acting or not acting, they do not give up any right in respect of a default. | Section 62.7.4 (Ch 62) |
| uprate | – | An increase in a plant's rated output or efficiency through equipment upgrades or control changes. | Section 62.8.1 (Ch 62) |
| repowering | – | Replacing the main generating equipment of an existing plant, usually on the same site and grid connection, to restore or increase output and extend life. | Section 62.8.1 (Ch 62) |
| hybridization | – | Adding a different generation or storage technology behind an existing plant's grid connection, such as a battery at a solar plant. | Section 62.8.1 (Ch 62) |
| permitted additional debt | – | Debt that the finance documents allow the project company to incur, usually subject to purpose, ranking and pro forma ratio tests. | Section 62.8.2 (Ch 62) |
| refinancing | – | Replacing some or all of a project's existing debt with new debt on different terms. | Section 63.1 (Ch 63) |
| refinancing gain | – | The increase in value to equity from a refinancing, measured net of breakage, make-whole, transaction costs, tax effects and any share paid to a public counterparty. | Section 63.2.1 (Ch 63) |
| refinancing gain bridge | – | The book's framework that decomposes a refinancing gain into margin saving, rate reset, swap unwind, breakage, costs, tax and gain share (Framework 63.1). | Section 63.2.1 (Ch 63) |
| breakage costs | – | The amounts payable when debt or hedges are terminated early: lenders' funding break costs, swap close-out amounts and bond make-whole or call premiums. | Section 63.2.2 (Ch 63) |
| make-whole premium | – | The amount by which the present value of a bond's remaining payments, discounted at a reference yield plus a spread, exceeds par when the issuer redeems early. | Section 63.2.2 (Ch 63) |
| repricing | – | Changing the margin or fees on existing debt by amendment, without replacing the debt. | Section 63.3.1 (Ch 63) |
| yank-the-bank | – | A provision allowing the borrower to replace or prepay a lender that refuses a consent most other lenders have given. | Section 63.3.1 (Ch 63) |
| soft call protection | – | A premium, usually a small percentage of par for a limited period, payable if an institutional term loan is repriced or refinanced to lower its cost. | Section 63.3.2 (Ch 63) |
| re-gearing | – | Increasing a project's debt after completion, usually at a refinancing, to release cash to equity; also called equity release refinancing. | Section 63.4.1 (Ch 63) |
| capital recycling | – | A sponsor's sale of stakes in operating projects to fund new development. | Section 63.6.1 (Ch 63) |
| minimum holding requirement | – | A covenant requiring a named sponsor to keep at least a stated shareholding in the project company for a stated period. | Section 63.6.3 (Ch 63) |
| stake-sale consent map | – | The book's framework listing every consent, right and approval that the sale of a stake in a project-financed company requires (Framework 63.2). | Section 63.6.3 (Ch 63) |
| holdco distribution coverage | – | The ratio of distributions expected from operating companies to holdco debt service. | Section 63.7.1 (Ch 63) |
| margin loan | – | A loan secured on listed shares whose terms require more collateral or repayment if the loan-to-value ratio rises above a set level. | Section 63.7.2 (Ch 63) |
| loan-to-value ratio | LTV | Loan outstanding divided by the market value of the collateral securing it. | Section 63.7.2 (Ch 63) |
| liquidity runway | – | The number of months for which cash and reserves can cover the shortfall between debt service and CFADS. | Section 64.2.1 (Ch 64) |
| distress early-warning dashboard | – | The book's framework of financial, commercial and structural indicators with thresholds that trigger a lender response (Framework 64.1). | Section 64.2.3 (Ch 64) |
| coordinating committee | – | A small group of creditors appointed to negotiate a restructuring on behalf of a larger lender group, usually with advisers paid by the borrower. | Section 64.3.2 (Ch 64) |
| standstill agreement | – | An agreement under which creditors suspend enforcement and acceleration for a period while the borrower provides information and the parties negotiate. | Section 64.3.3 (Ch 64) |
| amend-and-extend | A&E | An amendment that extends a loan's maturity, usually in exchange for a fee, higher margin and tighter terms. | Section 64.4.2 (Ch 64) |
| relevant alternative | – | What would most likely happen to creditors if a proposed restructuring did not proceed; the benchmark for the no-worse-off test in a Part 26A plan and, by extension, for any negotiation. | Section 64.6.1 (Ch 64) |
| recovery waterfall | – | The allocation of a project's value to classes of claims in order of priority. | Section 64.6.2 (Ch 64) |
| sustainable debt | – | The debt a project can service on a conservative case at a target cover ratio, used to size the new senior instrument in a restructuring. | Section 64.7.1 (Ch 64) |
| write-down | – | The part of a creditor's claim cancelled or converted in a restructuring; also called a haircut. | Section 64.7.1 (Ch 64) |
| debt-for-equity swap | – | The conversion of part of a creditor's claim into shares of the debtor. | Section 64.7.2 (Ch 64) |
| restructuring surplus | – | The excess of the value a restructuring delivers to existing creditors over the value they would receive in the relevant alternative. | Section 64.7.2 (Ch 64) |
| payment-in-kind interest | PIK | Interest paid by adding it to principal instead of in cash. | Section 64.7.3 (Ch 64) |
| debtor-in-possession financing | DIP | New-money financing to a company in Chapter 11, usually with super-priority. | Section 64.7.3 (Ch 64) |
| credit bid | – | A bid in a sale of collateral in which a secured creditor offsets its claim against the purchase price. | Section 64.8.1 (Ch 64) |
| prepackaged Chapter 11 | – | A Chapter 11 filing in which the plan has been negotiated and votes solicited before the filing. | Section 64.8.2 (Ch 64) |
| receivership | – | A common-law enforcement remedy in which a receiver appointed under security takes control of charged assets to realize them for the secured creditor. | Section 64.9.2 (Ch 64) |
| scheme of arrangement | – | An English court-sanctioned compromise under Part 26 of the Companies Act 2006, approved by 75% in value and a majority in number of each class, with no cross-class cram-down. | Section 64.10.2 (Ch 64) |
| restructuring plan | – | An English court-sanctioned compromise under Part 26A of the Companies Act 2006, available to a company in financial difficulty, approved by 75% in value of each voting class and capable of cross-class cram-down. | Section 64.10.2 (Ch 64) |
| cross-class cram-down | – | Court sanction of a restructuring over the objection of a whole class of creditors, subject to statutory tests. | Section 64.10.2 (Ch 64) |
| no-worse-off test | – | The condition that no member of a dissenting class is worse off under the plan than in the relevant alternative. | Section 64.10.2 (Ch 64) |
| absolute priority rule | – | The rule that a dissenting senior class must be paid in full before any junior class receives value. | Section 64.10.1 (Ch 64) |
| fair allocation | – | The English courts' requirement, developed in Adler, Thames Water and Petrofac, that a plan imposed by cram-down allocate the benefits of the restructuring fairly among classes. | Section 64.10.2 (Ch 64) |
| decommissioning obligation | – | A project owner's duty, under permit, lease, statute or contract, to remove a facility and restore or remediate its site at the end of its life. | Section 65.2.1 (Ch 65) |
| gross decommissioning cost | – | The estimated cost of removal, restoration and remediation before any credit for salvage. | Section 65.2.2 (Ch 65) |
| net decommissioning cost | – | Gross decommissioning cost less the estimated salvage value of recovered equipment and materials. | Section 65.2.2 (Ch 65) |
| salvage value | – | The amount expected from selling equipment and materials recovered when a facility is decommissioned. | Section 65.2.2 (Ch 65) |
| decommissioning security | – | A bond, letter of credit, guarantee or funded reserve that assures payment of decommissioning costs if the owner does not pay. | Section 65.2.3 (Ch 65) |
| sinking fund | – | A reserve built by periodic contributions that, with interest, reaches a target amount on a stated date. | Section 65.2.3 (Ch 65) |
| handback conditions | – | The contractual standards an asset must meet when it transfers to the authority or offtaker at the end of a concession or BOOT contract. | Section 65.3.1 (Ch 65) |
| residual life requirement | – | A handback condition stating the minimum remaining useful life of a component or asset at transfer. | Section 65.3.1 (Ch 65) |
| handback survey | – | A joint inspection, usually several years before expiry, that measures the asset against the handback conditions and fixes the works required. | Section 65.3.2 (Ch 65) |
| handback reserve | – | A reserve account funded in the final years of a contract to pay for the works the handback survey requires. | Section 65.3.3 (Ch 65) |
| handback bond | – | A bond or guarantee that the authority can call if the handback conditions are not met. | Section 65.3.3 (Ch 65) |
| life extension | – | Investment that keeps an existing plant operating beyond its original design or contract life. | Section 65.4.1 (Ch 65) |
| end-of-life option tree | – | The book's framework comparing retirement, extension, repowering, transfer and sale at a project's contract or technical end (Framework 65.2). | Section 65.4.2 (Ch 65) |
| handback readiness timeline | – | The book's framework of milestones from seven years before expiry to the release of handback security (Framework 65.1). | Section 65.3.2 (Ch 65) |
| tail value | – | The part of a project's value generated after its main contract or its debt ends. | Section 65.5.1 (Ch 65) |
