# Unit u05 glossary (Chapters 17 to 22)

Terms owned by Chapters 17 to 22, with canonical definitions and home sections (labels per D-008). Compiled from the chapter briefs in `u05.md`, October 3, 2026.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| concession agreement | – | A contract by which a public authority grants a private company the right to build or take over, operate and earn revenue from an asset or service that the authority would otherwise provide. | ssec:17.1.1 |
| implementation agreement | IA | A contract between the host government and a project company that sits beside the offtake contract and records the undertakings only the government can give, such as site rights, incentives, convertibility and termination support. | ssec:17.1.1 |
| project agreement | – | The generic name, common in water and port projects, for the principal contract between the contracting authority and the project company. | ssec:17.1.1 |
| administrative contract | – | In civil-law systems, a contract with a public body that gives the body prerogatives to vary or end it in the public interest, balanced by a duty to restore the private party's economic position. | ssec:17.1.3 |
| economic equilibrium | – | The principle that a concessionaire is entitled to have its financial position restored when the authority's acts or specified events upset the balance agreed at signing. | ssec:17.1.3 |
| convertibility and transfer undertaking | – | A government promise that the project company may convert local currency into foreign currency and transfer it abroad to pay debt service, costs and distributions. | ssec:17.2.2 |
| change in law | – | A change after signing in the laws, regulations or their official interpretation that affects the project company's costs or revenues, classified as discriminatory, specific or general. | ssec:17.3.1 |
| discriminatory change in law | – | A change in law that applies to the project, its sector peers of the same kind, or its investors and not to businesses generally. | ssec:17.3.1 |
| compensation event | – | An event, usually caused by the authority, that entitles the project company to relief from its obligations and to compensation that leaves it no better and no worse off. | ssec:17.3.2 |
| material adverse government action | MAGA | Action by a government entity, other than a lawful general change in law, that materially harms the project company's ability to perform or its economics. | ssec:17.3.2 |
| relief event | – | An event that excuses the project company from the consequences of delay or non-performance, without entitling it to compensation. | ssec:17.3.2 |
| political force majeure | – | Force majeure caused by war, civil disturbance, expropriation or other acts in the government's sphere, for which the government side usually bears the cost. | ssec:17.3.3 |
| natural force majeure | – | Force majeure from natural events or other causes outside both parties' control and outside the government's sphere, for which losses are usually shared. | ssec:17.3.3 |
| termination compensation | – | The amount a contract requires one party to pay the other when the contract ends early, set by the ground of termination. | ssec:17.4.2 |
| debt floor | – | The minimum share of senior debt that termination compensation must pay, even when the project company is the party in default. | ssec:17.4.2 |
| put and call option agreement | PCOA | An agreement under which, on early termination, the project company may require the government to buy the plant or its shares for a price at least equal to outstanding debt, and the government may require the sale. | ssec:17.4.5 |
| sovereign guarantee | – | An undertaking by the state, usually through its finance ministry, to pay specified obligations of a state entity if that entity does not. | ssec:17.5.2 |
| counter-guarantee | – | A guarantee by one government entity of another government entity's guarantee, such as a federal guarantee behind a state guarantee. | ssec:17.5.1 |
| letter of support | – | A letter in which a government states its support for a project and its intention to see an entity perform, without a legal obligation to pay. | ssec:17.5.3 |
| comfort letter | – | A letter from a government or parent company acknowledging a project or a subsidiary's obligations, intended to reassure but generally not enforceable as a payment obligation. | ssec:17.5.3 |
| letter of undertaking | – | A government letter containing specific, enforceable undertakings short of a guarantee, such as to procure that an entity has the means to pay. | ssec:17.5.1 |
| power purchase agreement | PPA | A long-term contract under which a buyer, usually a utility, pays a generator for capacity, energy or both at prices set by formula. | sec:18.1 |
| two-part tariff | – | A tariff with a capacity payment that recovers fixed costs and an energy payment that recovers variable costs. | ssec:18.1.1 |
| capacity payment | – | The fixed monthly payment for making contracted capacity available, adjusted for availability. | ssec:18.1.1 |
| energy payment | – | The payment per unit of energy delivered, covering fuel and variable operating costs. | ssec:18.1.1 |
| required commercial operation date | RCOD | The date by which a PPA requires the plant to reach commercial operation, after which delay liquidated damages are payable to the offtaker. | ssec:18.1.3 |
| contracted capacity | – | The capacity the offtaker pays for, set at signing and reset by capacity tests. | ssec:18.2.1 |
| target availability | – | The availability level at which a PPA pays the full capacity payment. | ssec:18.2.2 |
| outage allowance | – | The hours of planned and forced outage a PPA permits each year without reducing payments. | ssec:18.2.2 |
| deemed availability | – | Treatment of the plant as available during an event for which the offtaker's side is responsible, so that capacity payments continue. | ssec:18.2.3 |
| dispatch | – | The offtaker's or system operator's instruction setting when and at what output the plant runs. | ssec:18.3.1 |
| contracted heat rate | – | The heat rate written into the PPA that determines how much fuel the offtaker pays for per unit of energy. | ssec:18.3.2 |
| heat-rate headroom | – | The margin by which the contracted heat rate exceeds the EPC contractor's guaranteed heat rate. | ssec:18.3.2 |
| part-load heat-rate curve | – | A schedule of contracted heat rates at loads below full load, so that the offtaker pays for the extra fuel its dispatch causes. | ssec:18.3.2 |
| fuel pass-through | – | A tariff mechanism that charges the offtaker for fuel at its actual or formula price. | ssec:18.3.2 |
| take-or-pay | – | An obligation to pay for a contracted quantity whether or not it is taken. | ssec:18.4.1 |
| take-and-pay | – | An obligation to pay only for what is actually taken. | ssec:18.4.1 |
| deemed energy | – | Energy a plant could have delivered but for an event on the offtaker's side, paid for as if delivered. | ssec:18.4.2 |
| curtailment | – | An instruction to reduce output below what the plant could produce, for grid, economic or system-security reasons. | ssec:18.4.3 |
| late payment interest | – | Interest a contract charges on amounts paid after their due date. | ssec:18.6.1 |
| tolling agreement | – | A contract under which the buyer supplies the fuel and takes the output, paying the project company a fee for conversion capacity and variable costs. | ssec:18.7.1 |
| tolling fee | – | The capacity-style payment a toller makes for the right to convert its fuel at the plant. | ssec:18.7.1 |
| support scheme | – | A government mechanism that gives a generator or network asset revenue certainty without a commercial offtaker. | ssec:19.1.1 |
| feed-in tariff | FiT | A fixed price per unit of energy, set by statute or regulation, paid to eligible generators for a set term. | ssec:19.2.1 |
| feed-in premium | – | A payment on top of the market price, either fixed or sliding to top revenue up to a reference level. | ssec:19.2.2 |
| sliding premium | – | A feed-in premium that varies so that market revenue plus premium reaches a set reference price. | ssec:19.2.2 |
| contract for difference | CfD | A contract that pays a generator the difference between an agreed strike price and a market reference price; in a two-sided CfD the generator pays the difference back when the reference exceeds the strike. | ssec:19.4.1 |
| strike price | – | The fixed price per unit of energy in a contract for difference against which the reference price is settled. | ssec:19.4.1 |
| reference price | – | The market price index against which a contract for difference settles. | ssec:19.4.1 |
| difference amount | – | The payment under a contract for difference for a period: the strike price minus the reference price, multiplied by the metered output. | ssec:19.4.1 |
| negative-price rule | – | A support-scheme rule that suspends support payments in periods when the market reference price is below zero. | ssec:19.4.3 |
| long-stop date | – | The last date by which a project must start delivering under a support contract before the counterparty may terminate it. | ssec:19.4.4 |
| allocation round | – | A competitive round in which a government awards support contracts within a budget, usually by sealed bid. | ssec:19.4.7 |
| administrative strike price | – | The maximum strike price a government will accept for a technology in an allocation round. | ssec:19.4.7 |
| cap-and-floor regime | – | A revenue regime under which revenue below a floor is topped up and revenue above a cap is returned, assessed over set periods. | ssec:19.6.1 |
| regulated asset base | RAB | The regulator's measure of the capital a regulated company has invested, on which it earns an allowed return. | ssec:19.7.1 |
| allowed revenue | – | The revenue a regulator permits a regulated company to recover in a period: return on the RAB, regulatory depreciation and allowed operating costs. | ssec:19.7.1 |
| allowed return | – | The rate, usually a real weighted average cost of capital set by the regulator, applied to the RAB to compute the return element of allowed revenue. | ssec:19.7.1 |
| cost-sharing threshold | – | A level of project spend at which the sharing of further overruns between investors and consumers or the state changes. | ssec:19.7.2 |
| government support package | GSP | A set of contingent government instruments that cover tail risks investors cannot price, such as extreme overruns or uninsurable losses. | ssec:19.7.3 |
| merchant revenue | – | Revenue from selling output at market prices without a long-term price contract. | ssec:20.1.1 |
| shape risk | – | The risk that the hourly profile of a project's output differs from the profile a contract assumes, so that output is short or long in hours with different prices. | ssec:20.1.1 |
| basis risk | – | The risk that the price at the point where a project sells its output differs from the price at the point where its contract settles. | ssec:20.1.1 |
| corporate PPA | – | A long-term power purchase agreement between a generator and a non-utility company that uses or wants credit for the power. | sec:20.2 |
| sleeved PPA | – | A physical corporate PPA in which a retailer or utility takes delivery from the generator and passes the power to the corporate buyer for a fee. | ssec:20.2.1 |
| virtual PPA | vPPA | A financially settled corporate PPA in which the parties exchange the difference between a fixed price and a market price on the project's output, while the generator sells its power in the market. | ssec:20.2.2 |
| renewable energy certificate | REC | A tradable certificate representing the environmental attributes of one MWh of renewable generation. | ssec:20.2.2 |
| fixed-volume swap | – | A financial hedge that exchanges a fixed price for a market price on a set quantity in each hour, regardless of the project's output. | ssec:20.3.1 |
| fixed-shape hedge | – | A financial hedge on a quantity that varies hour by hour according to a pre-agreed profile. | ssec:20.3.2 |
| unit-contingent | – | A hedge or sale whose quantity is reduced when a named generating unit is unavailable. | ssec:20.3.3 |
| as-produced hedge | – | A hedge or PPA that settles on the project's actual output, so the buyer takes shape and resource volume risk. | ssec:20.4.1 |
| proxy revenue swap | PRS | A hedge in which a provider pays a fixed annual amount and receives the revenue a project would have earned at full availability, computed from measured resource and a market price. | ssec:20.4.2 |
| revenue floor | – | An option that tops a project's market revenue up to a set level in exchange for a premium. | ssec:20.5.1 |
| collar | – | A combination of a bought floor and a sold cap that bounds revenue between two levels. | ssec:20.5.2 |
| battery toll | – | A contract under which a buyer pays a fixed fee for the right to dispatch a battery's capacity, with the owner guaranteeing availability and capacity. | ssec:20.6.1 |
| de-rating factor | – | The share of a resource's nameplate capacity that a capacity market counts toward its obligations. | ssec:20.6.2 |
| revenue stacking | – | Earning several revenue streams, such as capacity, ancillary services and arbitrage, from the same storage asset. | ssec:20.6.3 |
| credit support | – | Collateral or guarantees a party provides to secure its obligations under a hedge or contract. | ssec:20.7.1 |
| first-lien hedge | – | A hedge whose provider shares the senior lenders' security instead of receiving cash collateral. | ssec:20.7.1 |
| fixed fee | – | A payment owed for reserved capacity regardless of whether the capacity is used. | ssec:21.1.1 |
| sale and purchase agreement | SPA | A long-term contract for the sale of LNG or another commodity, setting quantities, delivery terms and price. | sec:21.2 |
| free on board | FOB | A delivery term under which the buyer takes the cargo at the loading port and arranges shipping. | ssec:21.2.1 |
| delivered ex ship | DES | A delivery term under which the seller delivers the cargo to the buyer's receiving terminal. | ssec:21.2.1 |
| annual contract quantity | ACQ | The quantity a buyer contracts to take in a contract year. | ssec:21.2.1 |
| cargo cancellation right | – | A buyer's right under an LNG SPA to decline a scheduled cargo with notice, while still paying the fixed fee. | ssec:21.2.2 |
| liquefaction tolling agreement | – | A contract under which a customer supplies its own feed gas and pays a fee to have it liquefied. | ssec:21.2.3 |
| firm transportation | – | Pipeline service on reserved capacity that the transporter must provide except in force majeure. | ssec:21.3.1 |
| reservation charge | – | A fixed charge per unit of reserved pipeline capacity, payable whether or not the capacity is used. | ssec:21.3.1 |
| commodity charge | – | A pipeline charge per unit of gas actually transported. | ssec:21.3.1 |
| ship-or-pay | – | A shipper's obligation to pay for reserved transportation capacity whether or not it ships. | ssec:21.3.1 |
| payable metal | – | The share of the metal contained in a concentrate for which the buyer pays. | ssec:21.4.1 |
| treatment charge | TC | A smelter's charge per dry metric tonne of concentrate for processing it. | ssec:21.4.1 |
| refining charge | RC | A refiner's charge per unit of payable metal for refining it. | ssec:21.4.1 |
| penalty element | – | A deleterious element in a concentrate, such as arsenic, above a threshold for which the buyer deducts a charge. | ssec:21.4.1 |
| quotational period | QP | The period over which the metal price used for final invoicing is averaged. | ssec:21.4.1 |
| net smelter return | NSR | The value of payable metal in a concentrate less treatment and refining charges, penalties and, depending on the definition, transport. | ssec:21.4.1 |
| royalty | – | A payment to a resource owner or royalty holder equal to a share of production value. | ssec:21.5.1 |
| stream | – | A contract under which a financier pays an upfront deposit and a fixed ongoing price per unit in exchange for a share of a mine's future production of a metal. | ssec:21.5.2 |
| availability payment | AP | A periodic payment by a public authority for making an asset available to a specified standard, reduced by deductions. | ssec:21.6.1 |
| unitary charge | – | The single periodic payment under an availability-based PPP, before deductions. | ssec:21.6.1 |
| availability deduction | – | A reduction in the availability payment for parts of the asset that are unavailable, weighted by time and location. | ssec:21.6.1 |
| maximum toll | – | The highest toll a concessionaire may charge under its concession, which it may discount. | ssec:21.7.1 |
| revenue leakage | – | Toll revenue lost to unpaid trips, enforcement failures and billing errors. | ssec:21.7.1 |
| single till | – | Airport regulation that counts commercial revenue against the revenue requirement, lowering aeronautical charges. | ssec:21.8.1 |
| dual till | – | Airport regulation that sets aeronautical charges without counting commercial revenue. | ssec:21.8.1 |
| landlord port | – | A port model in which the authority owns the land and basic infrastructure and leases terminals to private operators. | ssec:21.8.2 |
| minimum annual guarantee | MAG | A concessionaire's commitment to pay a port authority a minimum fee or guarantee a minimum throughput each year. | ssec:21.8.2 |
| power usage effectiveness | PUE | The ratio of a data center's total energy use to the energy used by its IT equipment. | ssec:21.9.1 |
| residual value guarantee | RVG | A guarantee that pays the shortfall if an asset's value at the end of a lease falls below an agreed threshold. | ssec:21.9.2 |
| lump-sum turnkey | LSTK | An EPC contract under which one contractor delivers a complete, operating facility for a fixed price. | ssec:22.1.1 |
| date-certain | – | Describes a contract that fixes a completion date, with liquidated damages for delay. | ssec:22.1.1 |
| single point responsibility | – | The principle that one contractor is liable for the whole of design, procurement and construction, so that the owner need not prove which party caused a defect. | ssec:22.1.1 |
| employer's requirements | – | The owner's specification of what the EPC contractor must deliver. | ssec:22.1.2 |
| fitness for purpose | – | A contractor's obligation that the works will be fit for the purpose stated in the contract, stricter than a duty of reasonable skill and care. | ssec:22.1.2 |
| employer risk event | – | An event for which the owner, not the contractor, bears the risk under the EPC contract, entitling the contractor to time and often money. | ssec:22.1.3 |
| limited notice to proceed | LNTP | An instruction that lets the EPC contractor start specified early work before full notice to proceed. | ssec:22.2.1 |
| guaranteed completion date | – | The date by which the EPC contractor must achieve completion, adjusted only by extensions of time. | sec:22.3 |
| delay liquidated damages | delay LDs | A sum per day that the contractor pays for each day completion is late, up to a cap. | ssec:22.3.1 |
| performance guarantee | – | The contractor's guarantee that the completed plant meets specified output, efficiency, reliability or emissions levels. | ssec:22.4.1 |
| minimum performance level | – | The level below which the owner may reject the works instead of accepting performance liquidated damages. | ssec:22.4.1 |
| performance liquidated damages | buy-down LDs | Amounts the contractor pays per unit of performance shortfall, calibrated to the owner's lost value over the plant's life. | ssec:22.4.1 |
| correction curve | – | A schedule that adjusts test results to reference ambient conditions so that guaranteed and tested performance can be compared. | ssec:22.4.2 |
| reliability run | – | A completion test requiring the plant to operate continuously at a set availability for a set period. | ssec:22.4.2 |
| taking-over | – | The point at which the owner takes control of the completed works and the defects liability period starts. | ssec:22.4.2 |
| variation | – | A change to the scope of works, instructed by the owner or proposed by the contractor, valued under the contract. | ssec:22.5.1 |
| extension of time | EOT | An adjustment of the guaranteed completion date for delay caused by events at the owner's risk or by force majeure. | ssec:22.5.2 |
| concurrent delay | – | Delay to completion caused at the same time by an event at the contractor's risk and an event at the owner's risk. | ssec:22.5.2 |
| prolongation costs | – | The contractor's additional time-related costs caused by an extension of time for which it is compensated. | ssec:22.5.2 |
| time bar | – | A contractual rule that a claim is lost if not notified within a set period. | ssec:22.5.3 |
| aggregate LD cap | – | The maximum total of delay and performance liquidated damages under an EPC contract. | ssec:22.6.1 |
| performance bond | – | A bank or surety instrument for a share of the contract price that the owner may call if the contractor defaults. | ssec:22.6.2 |
| advance payment guarantee | APG | A guarantee securing repayment of the contractor's advance, reducing as the advance is recovered. | ssec:22.6.2 |
| retention | – | A percentage of each payment withheld from the contractor until completion or the end of the defects period. | ssec:22.6.2 |
| retention bond | – | A bond that replaces cash retention. | ssec:22.6.2 |
| parent company guarantee | PCG | A guarantee by the contractor's parent of the contractor's obligations under the EPC contract. | ssec:22.6.2 |
| joint and several liability | – | Liability under which each member of a consortium is liable for the whole of the consortium's obligations. | ssec:22.6.2 |
| defects liability period | – | The period after taking-over during which the contractor must remedy defects at its cost. | ssec:22.7.1 |
| serial defect | – | A defect that recurs across several identical components, triggering wider remedial obligations. | ssec:22.7.1 |
