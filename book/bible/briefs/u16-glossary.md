# Unit u16 glossary (Chapters 77 to 83)

Terms owned by Chapters 77 to 83. Home section numbers refer to the brief's locked tables of contents (sec:N.M and ssec:N.M.K). One entry (bank price deck) is conditional on change request CR-u16-1.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| spread business | – | A plant whose revenue depends on the difference between the price of what it sells and the price of what it buys, rather than on a contracted payment for capacity or service. | 77.1.1 |
| crack spread | – | The difference between the value of refined products and the cost of the crude oil used to make them, per barrel of crude. | 77.2.1 |
| 3-2-1 crack spread | – | A benchmark crack spread that assumes three barrels of crude yield two barrels of gasoline and one of diesel. | 77.2.1 |
| gross refining margin | GRM | A refinery's product revenue less crude and other feedstock cost, per barrel processed, before operating costs. | 77.2.1 |
| capture rate | – | The share of a benchmark margin that a specific plant actually realizes, after its own yields, crude slate, product discounts and losses. | 77.2.3 |
| Nelson complexity index | – | A measure of a refinery's conversion capability that weights each secondary unit's capacity by its relative cost against crude distillation. | 77.2.2 |
| turnaround | – | A scheduled multi-week shutdown of a refinery or chemical plant for inspection and major maintenance. | 77.2.3 |
| feedstock advantage | – | The cost edge a petrochemical plant gains from a cheaper feedstock, measured as the margin difference per tonne of main product. | 77.3.1 |
| co-product credit | – | The value of secondary products from a cracker or refinery, deducted from feedstock cost when computing the margin on the main product. | 77.3.1 |
| yield (manufacturing) | – | The share of units started that pass final quality tests and can be sold. | 77.4.1 |
| breakeven yield | – | The manufacturing yield at which revenue from good units equals variable and fixed cash costs at a given throughput. | 77.4.1 |
| capacity reservation fee | – | A fixed payment from a customer to reserve a share of a plant's output, owed whether or not the customer takes product. | 77.4.3 |
| processing agreement | – | An industrial tolling arrangement in which a toller supplies feedstock, owns the product, and pays the project company a fee per unit processed. | 77.5.2 |
| margin protection ladder | – | Framework 77.1: a ranking of revenue arrangements for spread businesses by how firmly they protect the project company's margin. | 77.5.1 |
| process performance guarantee | – | A licensor's guarantee that its technology will achieve stated capacity, yields and consumption when built and run to its design. | 77.7.2 |
| envelope (completion testing) | – | A group of interdependent units in a complex that is performance-tested together for completion purposes. | 77.7.5 |
| reliability run | – | A completion test period during which a plant or envelope must operate continuously at or above a stated output and specification. | 77.7.5 |
| cost curve | – | A ranking of all producers of a commodity by unit cost, plotted against cumulative output, used to judge which mines survive low prices. | 78.1.1 |
| C1 cash cost | C1 | Site operating costs plus treatment, refining and freight charges, less by-product credits, per unit of payable main metal. | 78.1.1 |
| all-in sustaining cost | AISC | C1 cash cost plus sustaining capital, royalties and corporate costs needed to keep the mine producing, per unit of payable metal. | 78.1.1 |
| by-product credit | – | Revenue from secondary metals in a mine's output, deducted from costs when computing unit cost of the main metal. | 78.1.1 |
| strip ratio | – | Tonnes of waste rock moved per tonne of ore mined in an open pit. | 78.1.2 |
| critical mineral | – | A mineral that a government designates as essential to its economy or security and exposed to supply concentration or disruption. | 78.2.1 |
| bank price deck | – | The set of commodity prices, set by lenders below consensus forecasts, on which debt is sized and tested. | 78.7.1 (conditional: only if Chapter 75 does not own it; CR-u16-1) |
| reserve tail | – | The share of proven and probable reserves that must remain unmined when the debt is fully repaid. | 78.7.2 |
| four-part completion test | – | Framework 78.1: mechanical, physical, metallurgical and operating, and financial tests that a mine must pass before sponsor completion support is released. | 78.7.3 |
| debt cap | – | The maximum senior debt the finance documents allow, including any supplemental tranches that may be added later on ratio tests. | 78.7.4 |
| carried interest (state) | – | A host state's equity share in a mine whose capital contributions are funded by the other shareholders, usually repayable from the state's future dividends. | 78.6.2 |
| fiscal stability clause | – | A host-agreement provision that freezes or compensates for changes in the tax and royalty regime applying to a project. | 78.6.1 |
| profit-based royalty | – | A royalty calculated on a measure of mine profit rather than on gross revenue or volume. | 78.5.4 |
| care and maintenance | – | The state of a mine that has stopped production but is kept safe and capable of restart, at a continuing cost. | 78.3.3 |
| deck-tail-test triangle | – | Framework 78.2: the check that sizing deck, reserve tail and financial completion test use consistent assumptions. | 78.7.3 |
| value of time | VOT | The money a traveler is willing to pay to save one hour of travel time, used to predict route choice. | 79.1.1 |
| generalized cost | – | The total cost of a trip as a traveler perceives it: money cost plus travel time valued at the value of time. | 79.1.1 |
| diversion rate | – | The share of corridor trips that choose the tolled route over the free alternative. | 79.1.1 |
| toll elasticity | – | The percentage change in traffic for a one percent change in the toll. | 79.1.2 |
| revenue-maximizing toll | – | The toll at which further increases lose more revenue from lost traffic than they gain from higher price. | 79.1.2 |
| shadow toll | – | A per-vehicle payment by the government to a road concessionaire in place of tolls charged to drivers. | 79.3.1 |
| managed lanes | – | Tolled lanes alongside free lanes on the same road, priced (often dynamically) to keep traffic flowing. | 79.3.2 |
| leakage | – | Toll revenue lost because transactions are not identified or not collected, expressed as a share of gross revenue. | 79.3.3 |
| minimum revenue guarantee | MRG | A government undertaking to pay a concessionaire the shortfall when revenue falls below a stated floor. | 79.4.1 |
| revenue-sharing band | – | A contract mechanism that shares revenue above an upper threshold (and sometimes below a lower one) between concessionaire and government. | 79.4.1 |
| least present value of revenue | LPVR | A flexible-term concession awarded to the bidder asking the lowest present value of revenue, ending when that value is collected. | 79.4.2 |
| economic equilibrium (rebalancing) | – | The civil-law principle that a concession's financial balance is restored, by term or tariff change, after defined events. | 79.4.3 |
| ramp-up | – | The period after opening during which traffic grows toward its mature level as users learn the new route. | 79.5.1 |
| structural shortfall | – | A traffic shortfall caused by a permanently wrong assumption, which does not close with time. | 79.5.1 |
| timing shortfall | – | A traffic shortfall caused by an event arriving later than forecast, which closes when the event arrives. | 79.5.1 |
| competing facility | – | A road or crossing built or improved by the government that draws traffic from a concession road. | 79.6.2 |
| demand-risk sharing menu | – | Framework 79.1: the structured choice among ways of allocating traffic risk between government and concessionaire. | 79.4.4 |
| ramp-up diagnosis | – | Framework 79.2: a procedure for classifying a traffic shortfall as timing, structural or policy-fixable and drawing the debt consequences. | 79.5.1 |
| farebox recovery ratio | – | Fare revenue as a share of a transit service's operating cost. | 80.1.1 |
| vertical separation | – | The division of a railway into separate infrastructure and train-operating businesses. | 80.1.2 |
| gross-cost contract | – | A transit operating contract in which the authority keeps fare revenue and pays the operator its costs plus a fee. | 80.1.2 |
| rolling stock lease | – | A lease of trains from a lessor that owns them, with rentals set over a term shorter than the trains' life. | 80.1.4 |
| aeronautical revenue | – | An airport's revenue from charges on airlines and passengers for using runways, terminals and related services. | 80.2.1 |
| commercial revenue (airport) | – | An airport's revenue from retail, food, parking, property and other non-aeronautical sources. | 80.2.1 |
| single till | – | Airport regulation that counts commercial revenue toward the allowed revenue, lowering aeronautical charges. | 80.2.2 |
| dual till | – | Airport regulation that sets aeronautical charges on aeronautical assets and costs only, leaving commercial profit to the airport. | 80.2.2 |
| landlord port | – | A port model in which a public authority owns land and basic infrastructure and leases terminals to private operators. | 80.3.1 |
| twenty-foot equivalent unit | TEU | The standard measure of container volume, equal to one 20-foot container. | 80.3.2 |
| transshipment | – | Moving containers between ships at an intermediate port without their entering the port's hinterland. | 80.3.2 |
| minimum annual guarantee | MAG | A concessionaire's undertaking to pay concession fees on at least a stated throughput or amount each year. | 80.3.3 |
| footloose-demand test | – | Framework 80.1: a scoring of how easily a transport asset's demand decision-makers can move elsewhere. | 80.4.1 |
| anchor-line agreement | – | A terminal services agreement under which a shipping line commits volumes to a terminal, often in return for tariffs or berthing priority. | 80.6.3 |
| social infrastructure | – | Publicly used buildings and facilities such as hospitals, schools, prisons and courts, typically paid for by government rather than users. | 81.1.1 |
| hard facilities management | hard FM | Maintenance of a building's fabric and systems, including planned and reactive repairs. | 81.1.3 |
| soft facilities management | soft FM | Building services such as cleaning, catering, portering and security. | 81.1.3 |
| lifecycle cost | – | The cost of replacing building components and systems at the end of their useful lives during a concession. | 81.1.3 |
| deduction passdown | – | The contractual transfer of availability or performance deductions from the project company to the subcontractor responsible, up to a cap. | 81.5.3 |
| affermage | – | A water-sector lease in which a private operator runs public assets and earns a share of tariffs, while the public owner funds major investment. | 81.2.1 |
| non-revenue water | NRW | Water produced but not billed, because of leaks, theft or metering errors, as a share of production. | 81.2.2 |
| reverse osmosis | RO | Desalination by forcing seawater through semi-permeable membranes at high pressure. | 81.3.1 |
| multi-stage flash | MSF | Thermal desalination that evaporates seawater in successive low-pressure stages using heat, often from a power plant. | 81.3.1 |
| water purchase agreement | WPA | A long-term contract under which a single buyer pays a water project company capacity and output charges for desalinated or treated water. | 81.3.2 |
| independent water project | IWP | A privately financed stand-alone water plant selling to a single buyer under a water purchase agreement. | 81.3.3 |
| gate fee | – | The price per tonne a waste supplier pays a treatment plant to accept waste. | 81.4.1 |
| put-or-pay | – | A waste supplier's obligation to deliver a minimum tonnage or pay as if it had. | 81.4.2 |
| net calorific value | NCV | The usable heat released by burning a unit of fuel or waste, net of the heat used to evaporate its water. | 81.4.2 |
| bottom ash | – | The solid residue left on the grate after waste combustion. | 81.4.3 |
| air-pollution-control residues | APCr | Hazardous fly ash and reagent residues captured from a WtE plant's flue gas. | 81.4.3 |
| payment-chain trace | – | Framework 81.1: tracing a project's revenue from the end-payer through each collecting or appropriating intermediary to find the weakest link. | 81.5.1 |
| tenancy ratio | – | The average number of paying tenants per tower in a portfolio. | 82.2.1 |
| master lease agreement | MLA | A framework contract under which a mobile operator leases space on many towers from one tower company on common terms. | 82.2.2 |
| colocation (towers) | – | Adding a second or later tenant's equipment to an existing tower. | 82.2.1 |
| homes passed | – | The number of premises a fiber network can connect without further street works. | 82.3.2 |
| penetration (take-up) | – | Connected subscribers as a share of homes passed. | 82.3.2 |
| average revenue per user | ARPU | Revenue per subscriber per month. | 82.3.2 |
| overbuild | – | Construction of a competing network over an area already served, splitting the customer base. | 82.3.2 |
| indefeasible right of use | IRU | A long-term, usually prepaid, exclusive right to use specified fiber capacity or strands. | 82.3.1 |
| hyperscale data center | – | A very large data center built for, and usually leased whole to, one major cloud or AI company. | 82.4.1 |
| colocation (data centers) | – | A data center leasing space, power and cooling to several customers who install their own equipment. | 82.4.1 |
| IT load | – | The electrical power consumed by computing equipment in a data center, the unit in which capacity is sold. | 82.4.1 |
| power usage effectiveness | PUE | Total data-center energy use divided by IT equipment energy use. | 82.4.1 |
| triple-net lease | NNN | A lease under which the tenant pays rent plus the property's operating costs, taxes and insurance (and, for data centers, power). | 82.4.3 |
| build-to-suit | – | A facility designed and built for a specific tenant who commits to lease it on completion. | 82.4.3 |
| residual value guarantee | RVG | A tenant's or sponsor's guarantee that an asset will be worth at least a stated amount at a stated date, paying the shortfall. | 82.6.3 |
| lease-life gap test | – | Framework 82.1: comparison of firm lease term, debt tenor, technology life and power-contract life to find unsupported debt. | 82.5.1 |
| electrolyzer | – | Equipment that splits water into hydrogen and oxygen using electricity. | 83.2.1 |
| levelized cost of hydrogen | LCOH | The constant price per kilogram of hydrogen that recovers a plant's capital, operating and power costs at a stated cost of capital over its life. | 83.2.1 |
| stack replacement | – | Periodic replacement of an electrolyzer's cell stacks, whose efficiency degrades with operating hours. | 83.2.1 |
| green ammonia | – | Ammonia made from hydrogen produced by electrolysis powered by renewable electricity. | 83.2.2 |
| take-if-tendered | – | An offtake obligation to pay for product only when the seller tenders it for delivery, without a payment obligation for product the seller cannot deliver. | 83.2.3 |
| carbon capture and storage | CCS | Capturing CO2 from an emitter, transporting it and injecting it into a geological formation for permanent storage. | 83.3.1 |
| cross-chain risk | – | The risk that failure or delay in one link of a multi-party chain (capture, transport, storage) strands the investment in the other links. | 83.3.1 |
| transport and storage company | T&S | The entity that owns and operates the CO2 pipelines or ships and the storage site in a CCS chain. | 83.3.3 |
| storage as a service | – | A CCS business model in which a storage operator contracts with several emitters to take their CO2 for a fee. | 83.3.3 |
| sustainable aviation fuel | SAF | Jet fuel made from non-fossil feedstocks or captured carbon that meets jet fuel specifications for blending. | 83.4.1 |
| hydroprocessed esters and fatty acids | HEFA | A SAF and renewable-diesel pathway that hydrotreats waste oils and fats. | 83.4.1 |
| green premium | – | The extra price a buyer pays for a low-emissions product over its conventional equivalent. | 83.4.2 |
| market-risk holder trace | – | Framework 83.1: identifying, link by link, which party bears the price and volume risk of a new molecule and whether that party can carry it for the debt's life. | 83.5.1 |
| role concentration | – | One party acting in several project roles at once, such as sponsor, offtaker and EPC contractor. | 83.5.2 |
