# Unit u07 glossary (Chapters 29 to 34)

Terms owned by Part V chapters, with canonical definitions and home sections. Writers use these definitions exactly; bold (`\term{}`) appears only at the home section. Ownership questions are listed in u07.md, "Change requests" items 6, 7, and 9.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| risk-adjusted return on capital | RAROC | A bank's after-tax income from a loan, net of funding cost, operating cost, and expected loss, divided by the capital the bank allocates to it. | Section 29.2.1 |
| liquidity premium | – | The extra funding cost a lender pays to borrow for longer, which it passes into the margin on long-tenor loans. | Section 29.2.2 |
| hold level | – | The amount of a loan a bank intends to keep on its own balance sheet after syndication. | Section 29.2.3 |
| OECD Arrangement on Officially Supported Export Credits | the Arrangement | A non-binding agreement among eleven Participants that caps the generosity of official export credit support, including down payment, support percentage, local costs, tenor, repayment profile, minimum interest rates, and minimum premia. | Section 29.3.2 |
| export contract value | – | The value of the goods and services in the export contract, including third-country content and excluding local costs, on which ECA support limits are calculated. | Section 29.3.2 |
| local costs | – | Goods and services bought in the buyer's country, which ECAs may support only up to a cap expressed as a share of the export contract value. | Section 29.3.2 |
| starting point of credit | – | The date from which an ECA-supported loan's repayment term and first-installment deadline are measured, usually linked to delivery, commissioning, or acceptance. | Section 29.3.2 |
| Commercial Interest Reference Rate | CIRR | The minimum fixed interest rate an official lender may charge under the Arrangement, built from government bond yields plus a margin. | Section 29.3.2 |
| minimum premium rate | MPR | The lowest premium an ECA may charge for credit risk under the Arrangement, set by country risk category, risk horizon, buyer risk, cover, and mitigants. | Section 29.3.5 |
| Climate Change Sector Understanding | CCSU | The annex to the Arrangement that allows longer tenors and more flexible repayment for listed climate-related sectors. | Section 29.3.2 |
| pure cover | – | ECA support given as a guarantee or insurance to commercial lenders who fund the loan themselves. | Section 29.3.3 |
| direct lending | – | ECA support in which the ECA or an affiliated official lender funds the loan itself. | Section 29.3.3 |
| comprehensive cover | – | ECA cover against both commercial and political causes of non-payment. | Section 29.3.3 |
| political-only cover | – | ECA or insurer cover against non-payment caused by defined political events only. | Section 29.3.3 |
| national content rule | – | An ECA's own rule on how much of a contract must originate in its country for the contract to qualify for support. | Section 29.3.4 |
| weighted average life | WAL | The average time to repayment of a loan's principal, each installment weighted by its share of principal. | Section 29.3.6 |
| additionality | – | The contribution a DFI makes that private markets would not provide on reasonable terms, financial or non-financial, which the DFI must show to justify its participation. | Section 29.4.2 |
| preferred creditor status | PCS | The convention under which multilateral development banks' loans are serviced in priority during a country's foreign-exchange crisis and excluded from general sovereign reschedulings. | Section 29.4.3 |
| A loan | – | The part of a DFI loan of record that the DFI funds and holds for its own account. | Section 29.4.4 |
| B loan | – | The part of a DFI loan of record funded by commercial participants who share its privileges through a participation agreement. | Section 29.4.4 |
| lender of record | – | The institution named as lender in the loan agreement, which holds and administers the loan for itself and its participants. | Section 29.4.4 |
| participation agreement | – | The contract by which a B-loan participant funds part of a loan of record and takes its risk, without becoming a lender to the borrower. | Section 29.4.4 |
| parallel loan | – | A loan made directly by a separate lender alongside a DFI loan, usually under a common terms agreement, without sharing the DFI's privileges contractually. | Section 29.4.4 |
| public development lender | – | A state-owned bank or credit program that lends to domestic infrastructure on policy terms, such as a national development bank or a federal infrastructure credit program. | Section 29.5 |
| infrastructure debt fund | – | A pooled investment vehicle that lends to infrastructure projects on behalf of institutional investors. | Section 29.7.1 |
| private credit | – | Lending by non-bank funds and investors through privately negotiated loans or notes rather than syndicated bank loans or public bonds. | Section 29.7.1 |
| project bond | – | A debt security issued by or for a project company, usually secured and amortizing, and sold to investors rather than lent by banks. | Section 30.1.1 |
| indenture | – | The contract between a bond issuer and a trustee acting for bondholders that sets out the bond's terms, covenants, and remedies (a trust deed in English-law practice). | Section 30.1.1 |
| bond trustee | – | The institution that holds the bond's rights and security for the bondholders and acts on their instructions. | Section 30.1.1 |
| Rule 144A | 144A | The US securities rule that permits resale of privately placed securities to qualified institutional buyers without registration. | Section 30.2.1 |
| Regulation S | Reg S | The US securities rule under which securities sold outside the United States need not be registered. | Section 30.2.1 |
| offering memorandum | OM | The disclosure document used to sell a privately placed or 144A bond. | Section 30.2.1 |
| US private placement | USPP | Notes sold directly to a small group of institutional investors, mainly US insurers, under a negotiated note purchase agreement. | Section 30.2.2 |
| private activity bond | PAB | A US tax-exempt bond issued by a public conduit to fund a privately operated project of public benefit. | Section 30.2.3 |
| yield to maturity | YTM | The single discount rate at which a bond's scheduled payments equal its price; the bond's internal rate of return. | Section 30.3.1 |
| issue price | – | The price, as a percentage of face value, at which a bond is first sold. | Section 30.3.1 |
| original issue discount | OID | The amount by which a bond's or loan's issue price falls below face value. | Section 30.3.1 |
| negative carry | – | The cost of paying interest on debt proceeds that are raised before they are spent, net of what the unspent proceeds earn. | Section 30.3.3 |
| delayed-draw bond | – | A bond whose investors commit at pricing but fund in several tranches on later dates, reducing negative carry. | Section 30.3.3 |
| make-whole | – | A prepayment amount equal to the present value of a bond's remaining payments discounted at a benchmark yield plus a fixed spread, if higher than par. | Section 30.3.4 |
| non-call period | – | The period in which a bond may not be redeemed early at the issuer's option. | Section 30.3.4 |
| consent solicitation | – | A formal request to bondholders to approve an amendment or waiver, often with a fee paid to those who consent. | Section 30.4.2 |
| credit rating | – | An agency's opinion of an issuer's or instrument's creditworthiness, expressed on a letter scale. | Section 30.5.1 |
| investment grade | – | A credit rating of BBB- or Baa3 or higher, the threshold many investors require. | Section 30.5.1 |
| rating case | – | The set of conservative assumptions an analyst uses to test whether debt can be serviced, distinct from the sponsor's base case. | Section 30.5.3 |
| breakeven analysis | – | Finding the value of one variable at which cash just covers debt service. | Section 30.5.3 |
| sovereign ceiling | – | The practice of capping an issuer's foreign-currency rating at or near its country's sovereign rating because of transfer and convertibility risk. | Section 30.5.4 |
| credit wrap | – | A financial guarantee of a bond's scheduled payments by a third party, historically a monoline insurer, which lends the bond the guarantor's rating. | Section 30.5.4 |
| mezzanine debt | – | Debt that ranks behind senior debt and ahead of equity in the same borrower, priced between the two. | Section 31.2.1 |
| contractual subordination | – | Subordination created by agreement, under which a junior creditor agrees not to be paid or to enforce until senior creditors are paid or consent. | Section 31.2.2 |
| payment blockage | – | A junior lender's agreement to stop receiving payments while a senior default or blockage notice is outstanding. | Section 31.2.2 |
| standstill period | – | The period during which a junior or other creditor agrees not to accelerate or enforce after a default. | Section 31.2.2 |
| turnover | – | A junior creditor's obligation to pass to senior creditors any amount it receives in breach of the subordination terms. | Section 31.2.2 |
| payment-in-kind interest | PIK | Interest paid by adding it to the loan's principal instead of in cash. | Section 31.2.3 |
| equity kicker | – | A lender's share in equity upside, such as warrants or a success fee, granted alongside a loan. | Section 31.2.3 |
| holdco debt | – | Debt borrowed by a company whose main asset is its shares in a project company, repaid from that company's distributions. | Section 31.3.1 |
| structural subordination | – | The junior position of a holding company's creditors, who can reach a subsidiary's assets only after all the subsidiary's creditors are paid. | Section 31.3.1 |
| term loan B | TLB | A floating-rate institutional term loan with minimal amortization, typically sold to funds and collateralized loan vehicles. | Section 31.3.3 |
| back-leverage | – | Sponsor-level debt secured on the sponsor's interest in a project partnership, ranking behind the project's tax equity investor. | Section 31.3.4 |
| equity bridge loan | EBL | A loan that funds sponsors' equity contributions during construction and is repaid from the sponsors' committed equity at or near COD. | Section 31.4 |
| VAT facility | – | A short-term facility, usually in local currency, that funds value-added tax paid during construction until refunds are received. | Section 31.5.1 |
| working-capital facility | – | A revolving facility that funds the timing gap between paying costs and collecting revenues. | Section 31.5.2 |
| letter-of-credit facility | LC facility | A facility under which a bank issues letters of credit for the project company, which must reimburse the bank for any drawing. | Section 31.6 |
| standby facility | – | Committed debt available only after construction contingency is used, to fund cost overruns, usually drawn with contingent equity in a fixed ratio. | Section 31.7 |
| cost-overrun facility | – | A committed facility dedicated to funding construction cost overruns, provided by lenders or sponsors. | Section 31.7 |
| share capital | – | Money subscribed for shares in the project company, ranking last in insolvency and returned only through dividends or capital reductions. | Section 32.2.1 |
| shareholder loan | SHL | A loan from a sponsor to the project company, subordinated to senior debt, that lenders count as equity. | Section 32.2.2 |
| back-ended equity | – | Equity contributed at or near the end of construction, after debt has funded earlier costs, usually against letters of credit. | Section 32.3.1 |
| pro rata funding | – | Funding construction costs with equity and debt drawn together in the agreed ratio. | Section 32.3.1 |
| equity letter of credit | equity LC | A letter of credit from a bank that lenders may draw if a sponsor fails to contribute committed equity. | Section 32.3.2 |
| contingent equity | – | Equity a sponsor commits to contribute only if defined events occur, such as cost overruns beyond contingency. | Section 32.3.3 |
| development fee | – | A fee paid to a developer at financial close for development work, funded from the project budget. | Section 32.4.1 |
| development premium | – | The amount an incoming investor pays a developer above its share of development costs, reflecting value created before financial close. | Section 32.4.2 |
| farm-down | – | A sponsor's sale of part of its equity in a project to another investor. | Section 32.5.1 |
| lock-in | – | A finance-document requirement that a sponsor keep a minimum shareholding for a stated period. | Section 32.5.2 |
| yieldco | – | A listed company that owns operating infrastructure assets and pays out most of its cash available for distribution as dividends, usually with a sponsor that supplies new assets. | Section 32.7 |
| cash available for distribution | CAFD | The cash a yieldco or holding company can pay to shareholders after debt service and reserves at every level below it. | Section 32.7 |
| incentive distribution rights | IDRs | Rights that entitle a sponsor to a rising share of distributions above set thresholds. | Section 32.7 |
| Up-C structure | – | A structure in which a listed company owns units of an operating partnership alongside a sponsor that holds units with high-vote shares. | Section 32.8 |
| tax equity | – | Investment by a taxpayer in a project partnership in exchange mainly for the project's tax credits and tax losses. | Section 32.9.1 |
| partnership flip | – | A tax equity structure in which most tax benefits go to the investor until it reaches a target return, after which allocations flip mostly to the sponsor. | Section 32.9.2 |
| flip date | – | The date on which a partnership flip's investor reaches its target return and allocations change. | Section 32.9.2 |
| tax credit transfer | – | The sale for cash of an eligible US federal energy tax credit to an unrelated taxpayer under §6418 of the Internal Revenue Code. | Section 32.9.3 |
| hybrid tax equity | – | A partnership flip in which the partnership also sells some of its tax credits to a third party. | Section 32.9.3 |
| elective pay | – | The US rule under which tax-exempt and governmental entities receive certain energy credits as a refundable payment. | Section 32.9.3 |
| prohibited foreign entity | PFE | Under US federal law as amended in 2025, an entity whose ownership, debt, or control links to specified foreign entities is denied energy tax credits. | Section 32.9.4 |
| Sharia | – | Islamic law as applied to commercial and financial dealings. | Section 33.1.1 |
| riba | – | Any predetermined return on a loan of money, which Islamic finance prohibits. | Section 33.1.1 |
| gharar | – | Excessive uncertainty in the terms or subject of a contract, which Islamic finance prohibits. | Section 33.1.1 |
| maysir | – | Gambling or speculation, which Islamic finance prohibits. | Section 33.1.1 |
| Sharia board | – | The panel of scholars that reviews and certifies a financial institution's products and transactions as compliant. | Section 33.1.2 |
| fatwa | – | A Sharia board's or scholar's formal opinion that a transaction or document complies. | Section 33.1.2 |
| AAOIFI | AAOIFI | The Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions, which issues Sharia, accounting, and governance standards. | Section 33.1.2 |
| istisna'a | – | A contract to manufacture or build an asset for future delivery at an agreed price, which may be paid in stages. | Section 33.2.1 |
| ijara | – | A lease under which the financier owns an asset and earns rent from the lessee's use of it. | Section 33.2.2 |
| forward ijara | – | A lease agreed before the leased asset exists, with advance rentals payable during construction. | Section 33.2.2 |
| advance rental | – | Rent paid under a forward ijara before the asset is delivered, credited against later rent or refunded if delivery fails. | Section 33.2.2 |
| wakala | – | An agency in which a principal appoints an agent to act, invest, or procure on its behalf. | Section 33.2.3 |
| murabaha | – | A sale at cost plus an agreed profit, with payment deferred. | Section 33.2.4 |
| commodity murabaha | – | A murabaha over traded commodities used to provide cash liquidity, the buyer reselling the commodities spot. | Section 33.2.4 |
| musharaka | – | A partnership in which parties contribute capital and share profits by agreement and losses by contribution. | Section 33.2.5 |
| diminishing musharaka | – | A musharaka in which one partner buys out the other's share in stages. | Section 33.2.5 |
| service agency agreement | – | The agreement under which the lessee performs, as the lessor's agent, the ownership duties (major maintenance, insurance) that the lessor must bear. | Section 33.3.1 |
| purchase undertaking | – | The lessee's promise to buy the leased asset from the financier at a set price on maturity, default, or other events. | Section 33.3.1 |
| total loss event | – | The destruction of a leased asset, which ends the lease and triggers payment from insurance proceeds. | Section 33.3.2 |
| sukuk | – | Certificates representing undivided ownership in assets, usufructs, or a venture, whose returns come from those assets rather than from interest. | Section 33.4 |
| wa'd | – | A unilateral promise, used to build Islamic hedging and undertaking structures. | Section 33.5.2 |
| blended finance | – | The use of public or philanthropic money to change a project's risk or return so that commercial money will invest alongside it. | Section 34.1 |
| concessional finance | – | Lending on terms more favorable than the market offers, through lower rates, longer tenors, longer grace periods, or grants. | Section 34.2.1 |
| grant element | – | The share of a loan's face value that is a gift, measured as one minus the present value of its debt service at a market rate divided by the face value. | Section 34.2.1 |
| climate fund | – | A pooled public fund that provides concessional finance, grants, or guarantees for climate mitigation or adaptation projects. | Section 34.2.2 |
| on-lending | – | Borrowing from one lender and lending the proceeds to another borrower, often passing the original terms through. | Section 34.2.4 |
| first-loss capital | – | A tranche or guarantee that absorbs losses before other investors or lenders do. | Section 34.3.1 |
| minimum concessionality | – | The principle of giving no more concession than is needed to make a project financeable. | Section 34.3.2 |
| crowding out | – | Displacing commercial finance that would have been available without public support. | Section 34.3.2 |
| partial risk guarantee | PRG | A guarantee by a DFI to lenders or an LC bank against losses caused by a government's or state entity's failure to meet defined obligations, backed by a government indemnity. | Section 34.4.1 |
| liquidity guarantee | – | A partial risk guarantee that backs an LC bank's claim for reimbursement after the LC has been drawn to cover an offtaker's missed payments. | Section 34.4.1 |
| debt-mobilization guarantee | – | A partial risk guarantee that covers commercial lenders' debt service if a defined government obligation is not met. | Section 34.4.1 |
| partial credit guarantee | PCG | A guarantee covering a defined part of debt service, whatever the cause of default, used to extend tenor or lift a rating. | Section 34.4.2 |
| indemnity agreement | – | The agreement under which a host government promises to repay a DFI any amount the DFI pays under a guarantee. | Section 34.4.3 |
| viability-gap funding | VGF | A public capital grant that closes the gap between a project's cost and the amount its revenues can finance. | Section 34.5 |
| local-currency financing | – | Debt denominated in the currency in which a project earns its revenue. | Section 34.6.1 |
| inflation-linked bond | – | A bond whose principal or payments are indexed to an inflation measure, such as Colombia's UVR unit. | Section 34.6.3 |
| cross-currency swap | – | A swap that exchanges payments in one currency for payments in another, converting a loan's currency. | Section 34.6.4 |
