# Unit u15 glossary: Chapters 69 to 76

Terms owned by the Part XVI sector chapters in this unit. Home section numbers refer to the locked tables of contents in `bible/briefs/u15.md`. Terms flagged in change request CR-u15-3 (turbine supply agreement, balance of plant, terminal use agreement, independent water and power project, field life cover ratio) move to an earlier home if the glossary canon assigns one.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| screening curve | – | A plot of each generating technology's total cost per kW-year (or per MWh) against hours run, used to find the least-cost technology at each capacity factor. | 69.1.2 (Chapter 69) |
| baseload, mid-merit and peaking plant | – | Plants classified by the share of hours they run: baseload most hours, mid-merit for daily and seasonal swings, peaking only in the highest-demand or scarcity hours. | 69.1.3 (Chapter 69) |
| firming capacity | – | Dispatchable capacity contracted or held to cover periods when variable renewable output is low. | 69.1.3 (Chapter 69) |
| independent water and power project | IWPP | A privately financed plant that sells both electricity and desalinated water to a state single buyer under a long-term purchase agreement. | 69.3.2 (Chapter 69) |
| spark spread | – | The power price minus the fuel cost of generating one MWh in a gas-fired plant at its heat rate. | 69.3.3 (Chapter 69) |
| clean spark spread | – | The spark spread minus the cost of the carbon allowances needed to generate one MWh. | 69.3.3 (Chapter 69) |
| dark spread | – | The power price minus the fuel cost of generating one MWh in a coal-fired plant. | 69.3.3 (Chapter 69) |
| heat-rate headroom | – | The difference between the heat rate at which a PPA pays for fuel and the heat rate the plant actually achieves, which turns into a fuel gain or loss for the project company. | 69.4.2 (Chapter 69) |
| fuel-chain lock-in | – | Dependence of a plant on one fuel type, quality and supply route because its boilers or turbines were designed for them. | 69.2.3 (Chapter 69) |
| inverter loading ratio | ILR | The ratio of a solar plant's DC module capacity to its AC inverter capacity, also called the DC/AC ratio. | 70.2.2 (Chapter 70) |
| clipping loss | – | Energy a solar plant's modules could produce but its inverters cannot deliver because DC output exceeds AC capacity. | 70.2.2 (Chapter 70) |
| hybrid plant | – | A wind or solar plant with co-located storage behind a shared grid connection. | 70.2.3 (Chapter 70) |
| turbine supply agreement | TSA | The contract under which a turbine manufacturer supplies, delivers, erects and commissions wind turbines, separate from the balance-of-plant works. | 70.5.1 (Chapter 70) |
| balance of plant | BOP | All works of a wind or solar project other than the turbines or modules: foundations, roads, collection system, substation and grid works. | 70.5.1 (Chapter 70) |
| full-service agreement | FSA | A long-term turbine maintenance contract from the manufacturer covering scheduled and unscheduled service, parts and an availability guarantee. | 70.5.1 (Chapter 70) |
| module supply agreement | – | The contract for supply of solar modules with product and performance warranties. | 70.5.2 (Chapter 70) |
| nodal decoupling | – | A persistent gap between prices at a generator's injection node and its customers' withdrawal node, caused by transmission congestion. | 70.4.2 (Chapter 70) |
| resource-to-revenue bridge | – | Framework 70.1: the step-by-step conversion of a resource estimate into captured revenue with each loss and price adjustment assigned to a risk owner. | 70.9 (Chapter 70) |
| array cable | – | A medium-voltage cable linking offshore wind turbines to each other and to the offshore substation. | 71.2.1 (Chapter 71) |
| export cable | – | The high-voltage cable carrying power from an offshore substation to the onshore grid connection. | 71.2.1 (Chapter 71) |
| offshore substation | – | A platform that collects array-cable output and steps up its voltage for export to shore. | 71.2.1 (Chapter 71) |
| offshore transmission owner | OFTO | In Great Britain, the licensed owner to which an offshore wind farm's transmission assets must be sold after construction, earning a regulated revenue. | 71.7.1 (Chapter 71) |
| offshore renewable energy certificate | OREC | A certificate representing one MWh of offshore wind output, bought by a US state program at a contracted price. | 71.3.2 (Chapter 71) |
| transport and installation contract | T&I contract | The contract under which a marine contractor transports and installs turbines, foundations or cables with specialized vessels. | 71.6.1 (Chapter 71) |
| weather window | – | A period in which sea and wind conditions allow a given marine operation to proceed safely. | 71.4.1 (Chapter 71) |
| marine warranty surveyor | MWS | An independent surveyor appointed for insurers who approves marine operations before they proceed. | 71.6.2 (Chapter 71) |
| serial defect | – | A design or manufacturing defect that recurs across many units of the same component, triggering wider warranty remedies. | 71.4.3 (Chapter 71) |
| pre-FID exposure clock | – | Framework 71.1: a map of the windows in which a developer's revenue price is fixed but costs, rates or tax benefits remain open. | 71.5.1 (Chapter 71) |
| run-of-river plant | – | A hydro plant that generates from the river's natural flow with little or no storage. | 72.1.2 (Chapter 72) |
| storage hydro plant | – | A hydro plant with a reservoir that stores water to generate when it is most valuable. | 72.1.2 (Chapter 72) |
| trans-basin diversion | – | A hydro scheme that diverts water from one river basin to another to gain head and generate. | 72.1.2 (Chapter 72) |
| firm energy | – | The energy a hydro plant can deliver with high reliability in dry conditions, often contracted as primary energy. | 72.1.2 (Chapter 72) |
| secondary energy | – | Energy above firm energy, available in average or wet years and sold at a lower or contingent price. | 72.1.2 (Chapter 72) |
| hydrology risk | – | The risk that river flows, and so a hydro plant's output, fall below forecast. | 72.2.1 (Chapter 72) |
| geotechnical baseline | – | A contractual description of expected ground conditions against which unforeseen conditions are measured for time and cost relief. | 72.3.2 (Chapter 72) |
| confirmation drilling | – | Drilling after exploration that tests whether a geothermal reservoir can supply enough steam for a plant of a given size. | 72.5.1 (Chapter 72) |
| make-up well | – | A geothermal well drilled during operations to replace declining steam supply. | 72.5.3 (Chapter 72) |
| reinjection | – | Returning spent geothermal fluid to the reservoir to sustain pressure and dispose of brine. | 72.5.3 (Chapter 72) |
| joint operation contract | JOC | In Indonesian geothermal practice, the contract under which a private operator develops a concession holder's field and plant. | 72.5.4 (Chapter 72) |
| duration (storage) | – | The number of hours a storage asset can discharge at its rated power, equal to energy capacity divided by power rating. | 73.1.1 (Chapter 73) |
| augmentation | – | Adding battery modules during operation to restore usable energy capacity lost to degradation. | 73.2.2 (Chapter 73) |
| capacity maintenance agreement | – | A contract under which a battery supplier or integrator keeps usable capacity at a guaranteed level, by augmentation or replacement, for a fee. | 73.2.1 (Chapter 73) |
| thermal runaway | – | A self-sustaining overheating reaction in a battery cell that can spread to adjacent cells and modules and cause fire. | 73.2.3 (Chapter 73) |
| long-duration energy storage | LDES | Storage that can discharge at rated power for many hours (in the GB scheme, eight hours or more). | 73.3.3 (Chapter 73) |
| pumped storage hydro | – | Storage that pumps water to an upper reservoir when power is cheap and generates when it is valuable. | 73.3.3 (Chapter 73) |
| annual value (transmission) | – | A fixed yearly payment, set by competitive bid and usually indexed, paid to a transmission concessionaire for an available line. | 73.4.2 (Chapter 73) |
| merchant transmission | – | A transmission line or interconnector whose revenue depends on price differences or congestion rents rather than regulated or contracted payments. | 73.4.5 (Chapter 73) |
| interconnector | – | A transmission link between two power markets or countries that earns from flows driven by price differences. | 73.5.1 (Chapter 73) |
| HVDC converter station | – | The installation at each end of a high-voltage direct current link that converts between alternating and direct current. | 73.5.3 (Chapter 73) |
| overnight cost | – | The cost of building a plant as if it were built instantly, excluding financing costs during construction. | 74.1.1 (Chapter 74) |
| first-of-a-kind | FOAK | The first build of a design in a market, carrying design maturity, supply-chain and learning risks that later units do not. | 74.1.2 (Chapter 74) |
| nth-of-a-kind | NOAK | A build of a design after several units, when costs and schedules reflect learning. | 74.1.2 (Chapter 74) |
| small modular reactor | SMR | A nuclear reactor of up to a few hundred megawatts designed for factory fabrication and serial deployment. | 74.3.5 (Chapter 74) |
| funded decommissioning programme | FDP | An arrangement requiring a nuclear operator to accumulate a segregated fund during operations to pay for decommissioning and waste. | 74.4.1 (Chapter 74) |
| special administration regime | – | A statutory insolvency regime that keeps an essential asset operating under an administrator if its owner fails. | 74.3.2 (Chapter 74) |
| government support package (nuclear) | – | The set of state instruments (shutdown compensation, overrun backstops, debt support, uninsurable-risk cover) that make a nuclear project financeable. | 74.3.2 (Chapter 74) |
| vendor equity | – | An equity stake taken by the reactor vendor in the project or its operator to align the vendor with long-term performance. | 74.3.4 (Chapter 74) |
| nuclear risk-bearer map | – | Framework 74.1: a table assigning each nuclear risk to the party that bears it under a given financing model. | 74.3 (Chapter 74) |
| borrowing base | – | The maximum amount available under a reserve-based loan, recalculated periodically from the value of the borrower's reserves. | 75.3.2 (Chapter 75) |
| borrowing-base assets | – | The fields and interests whose reserves and cash flows are included in the borrowing-base calculation. | 75.3.2 (Chapter 75) |
| bank price deck | – | The commodity price forecast set by the lenders and used to value reserves in a borrowing-base calculation. | 75.3.2 (Chapter 75) |
| redetermination | – | The periodic recalculation of a borrowing base, usually semiannual. | 75.3.3 (Chapter 75) |
| borrowing-base deficiency | – | The amount by which loans and other utilizations exceed a newly redetermined borrowing base, which the borrower must cure. | 75.3.3 (Chapter 75) |
| field life cover ratio | FLCR | In reserve-based lending, the NPV of a borrowing base's cash flows over the remaining field life divided by the debt. | 75.3.2 (Chapter 75) |
| tail test | – | A reserve-based lending condition that a minimum share of reserves remains to be produced after final maturity. | 75.3.2 (Chapter 75) |
| production sharing contract | PSC | A contract under which the state owns the resource and a contractor recovers costs and shares profit oil with the state. | 75.2.2 (Chapter 75) |
| cost oil | – | The share of production a PSC contractor receives to recover its costs, usually subject to a cap. | 75.2.2 (Chapter 75) |
| profit oil | – | Production remaining after cost oil, split between the state and the contractor. | 75.2.2 (Chapter 75) |
| cost-of-service tariff | – | A regulated tariff set to recover an asset's operating costs, depreciation and an allowed return on its rate base. | 75.4.2 (Chapter 75) |
| host government agreement | HGA | An agreement between a cross-border pipeline's owners and each host or transit state setting the project's legal and fiscal terms. | 75.5.1 (Chapter 75) |
| liquefaction train | – | One processing unit of an LNG plant that cools natural gas into liquid; plants are built and financed train by train. | 76.1.1 (Chapter 76) |
| integrated LNG project | – | An LNG project in which the same owners develop the gas fields, pipeline and liquefaction plant and sell LNG through a joint marketing company. | 76.2.1 (Chapter 76) |
| marketing company (LNG) | – | The entity, often jointly owned by an LNG project's participants, that signs sale and purchase agreements and sells the project's LNG. | 76.2.1 (Chapter 76) |
| financial completion | – | The point in an LNG or other large financing at which physical, operating and financial tests are met and sponsors' completion guarantees are released. | 76.4.3 (Chapter 76) |
| floating storage and regasification unit | FSRU | A ship that stores LNG and regasifies it for delivery into a pipeline, usually chartered to the importer. | 76.7.2 (Chapter 76) |
| terminal use agreement | TUA | A contract reserving capacity at an LNG terminal for a fee payable whether or not the capacity is used. | 76.7.1 (Chapter 76) |
| floating production, storage and offloading unit | FPSO | A vessel that processes oil and gas from subsea wells, stores oil and offloads it to tankers. | 76.8.1 (Chapter 76) |
| lease-and-operate contract | – | A charter under which an FPSO owner leases and operates the unit for an oil company for a day rate. | 76.8.1 (Chapter 76) |
| day rate (capital and operating elements) | – | The daily charter payment for a floating asset, split into an element that repays capital and an element that covers operating costs. | 76.8.2 (Chapter 76) |
| early termination fee | – | The amount a charterer pays if it ends a charter before the firm period expires, usually scheduled to cover outstanding debt and an equity return. | 76.8.3 (Chapter 76) |
