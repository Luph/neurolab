Terms owned by Chapters 23 to 28, with canonical definitions and home sections. Home sections refer to the locked tables of contents in `bible/briefs/u06.md`. "–" means no abbreviation.

|---|---|---|---|
| wrap premium | – | The extra price a single contractor charges for taking interface, coordination and integration risk across all the works. | 23.1.1 |
| split contract | – | A construction arrangement in which the works are divided between two or more contracts, often an offshore supply contract and an onshore construction contract, tied together by a coordination agreement. | 23.2.1 |
| coordination agreement | – | The umbrella agreement that makes split contracts behave as one, through a single schedule, aggregate LDs and caps, cross-default and a cross-guarantee. | 23.2.1 |
| multi-contracting | – | Delivering a project through several separate packages contracted directly by the owner, with the owner acting as integrator. | 23.2.2 |
| free-issue equipment | – | Equipment bought by the owner and handed to an installing contractor, so that the owner answers to the installer for its timely delivery and fitness. | 23.2.3 |
| interface | – | A point where the work, design, schedule or testing of one contractor depends on another. | 23.3.1 |
| interface matrix | – | A table listing each interface, the parties on each side, the deliverable, the date and the contractual remedy if it fails. | 23.3.1 |
| interface agreement | – | A contract among the owner and its contractors that sets cooperation duties, a single schedule and direct liability between contractors for interface failures. | 23.3.3 |
| wrap | – | An undertaking by one party to take responsibility for all or part of the works performed by others. | 23.4.1 |
| performance wrap | – | A wrap that guarantees the completed plant's output and efficiency across packages without taking delay risk. | 23.4.1 |
| wrap guarantee | – | The instrument by which a lead contractor, its parent or a specialist wrapper gives the wrap. | 23.4.1 |
| engineering, procurement, and construction management | EPCM | A delivery model in which a contractor designs, procures and manages construction as the owner's agent for a fee, while the owner contracts with every trade and supplier and bears price and schedule risk. | 23.5.1 |
| trade contract | – | A contract between the owner and a specialist contractor or supplier under an EPCM model. | 23.5.1 |
| target cost contract | – | A contract that pays actual cost plus fee and shares any overrun or underrun against an agreed target between owner and contractor. | 23.5.3 |
| alliance contract | – | A contract in which owner and contractors share the project's outcome collectively, with pain-share and gain-share replacing individual liability. | 23.5.3 |
| design and construction contract | D&C contract | The contract by which a PPP concessionaire passes its design and construction obligations to a contractor on terms that mirror the concession. | 23.6.1 |
| construction joint venture | – | Two or more contractors contracting together, usually jointly and severally liable, with an internal agreement that splits work and results. | 23.6.2 |
| FIDIC | – | The International Federation of Consulting Engineers, publisher of the most widely used international standard construction contracts. | 23.7.1 |
| NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract | NEC4 ECC | A standard form built around early warnings and compensation events, with options ranging from priced to cost-reimbursable. | 23.7.2 |

| price adjustment clause | – | A clause that changes the contract price by reference to a published cost index. | 23.9.2 |
| slot reservation agreement | – | An agreement, usually with a fee, that secures a supplier's manufacturing or vessel capacity for a future period. | 23.9.1 |
| supplier credit exposure | – | The value, at a point in time, of the supplier's remaining obligations to the owner that would be lost if the supplier failed. | 23.10.2 |
| operation and maintenance agreement | O&M agreement | The contract under which an O&M operator runs and maintains the plant for the project company to a stated standard and budget. | 24.2.1 |
| prudent utility practice | – | The standard of skill, care and diligence expected of a competent operator of similar plant, used to measure O&M performance. | 24.2.1 |
| operating budget | – | The annual plan of operating and maintenance spending prepared by the operator, approved by the owner and reviewed by the lenders. | 24.2.1 |
| fixed-fee O&M | – | An O&M agreement in which the operator is paid a set, indexed fee and keeps any cost saving or overrun. | 24.2.2 |
| cost-plus O&M | – | An O&M agreement in which the operator is reimbursed actual costs plus a fee, so the owner bears cost variation. | 24.2.2 |
| bonus and malus | – | Payments to or from an operator for performance above or below a guaranteed level. | 24.2.3 |
| deadband | – | A range of performance around the target within which no bonus or malus is paid. | 24.2.3 |
| affiliated operator | – | An O&M operator or service provider owned by or related to a sponsor of the project company. | 24.2.5 |
| long-term service agreement | LTSA | A contract under which the equipment manufacturer supplies parts, repairs and field services for scheduled inspections of its equipment over many years. | 24.3.1 |
| full-service agreement | – | A wind-turbine maintenance contract under which the manufacturer performs all scheduled and unscheduled service and guarantees availability. | 24.3.1 |
| independent service provider | ISP | A maintenance company other than the equipment manufacturer. | 24.3.1 |
| equivalent operating hours | EOH | Operating hours plus a contractual hour-equivalent for each start and other wear events, used to schedule inspections and charge LTSA fees. | 24.3.2 |
| combustion inspection | – | The shortest scheduled gas turbine inspection, covering the combustion system. | 24.3.2 |
| hot gas path inspection | – | A scheduled gas turbine inspection of the combustion system and turbine section components exposed to hot gases. | 24.3.2 |
| major inspection | – | The longest scheduled gas turbine inspection, opening the whole machine. | 24.3.2 |
| asset management agreement | – | A contract under which a manager administers the project company's contracts, compliance, budgets and reporting on the owner's behalf. | 24.4 |
| major maintenance | – | Large, infrequent maintenance events, such as overhauls and component replacements, planned years ahead. | 24.5.1 |
| lifecycle cost | – | The cost of renewing and replacing an asset's components over a concession term to keep it at the required standard. | 24.5.3 |
| gas sale agreement | GSA | The contract under which a seller supplies natural gas to the project company in stated quantities, quality and price over a term. | 25.2.1 |

| maximum daily quantity | MDQ | The most gas the buyer may nominate on any day. | 25.2.2 |

| take-or-pay quantity | – | The share of ACQ the buyer must pay for whether or not it takes the gas. | 25.2.3 |
| deficiency payment | – | The buyer's payment for the shortfall between its takes and the take-or-pay quantity. | 25.2.3 |
| make-up gas | – | Gas already paid for through a deficiency payment that the buyer may take later, within a recovery period. | 25.2.3 |
| carry-forward | – | A credit for takes above a year's obligation that reduces the buyer's take-or-pay obligation in a later year. | 25.2.3 |
| deliver-or-pay | – | The seller's obligation to compensate the buyer when it fails to make nominated gas available. | 25.2.4 |
| gross calorific value | GCV | The energy released by burning a fuel including the latent heat of water vapor; the basis on which gas is usually sold. | 25.2.6 |
| reserves dedication | – | A seller's commitment of specified field reserves to a supply contract. | 25.2.7 |
| coal supply agreement | – | A fuel supply contract for coal, with quality bands and price adjustments for calorific value and contaminants. | 25.2.8 |
| reserved capacity | – | The pipeline capacity a shipper books and pays for whether or not it uses it. | 25.3.1 |
