# Unit u01 glossary (Chapters 1 to 4)

Terms whose home definition is in Chapters 2 to 4. Chapter 1 owns no glossary term (change request CR-u01-2); it owns the framework name "deal on a page" (fw:deal-on-a-page, ssec:1.1.2). Home sections use the anchor labels of `bible/briefs/u01.md`.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| project finance | PF (avoid in prose) | Financing in which lenders are repaid from the cash flows of a single ring-fenced project and secured on its assets and contracts, with limited or no recourse to the sponsors. | ssec:2.1.1 |
| special purpose vehicle | SPV | A company created to own and operate one project and to do nothing else, so that the project's assets, contracts and debts are held apart from its owners'. | ssec:2.1.1 |
| project company | – | The special purpose vehicle that owns the project, signs its contracts and borrows its debt (the book's canonical term). | ssec:2.1.1 |
| sponsor | – | A company that develops a project and owns equity in its project company, carrying the first loss. | ssec:2.1.1 |
| offtaker | – | The party that buys the project's output under a long-term contract. | ssec:2.3.1 |
| ring-fencing | – | The legal and contractual separation that keeps a project's assets and cash flows apart from its sponsors' other businesses, protecting each from the other's failure. | ssec:2.1.2 |
| single-purpose undertaking | – | A covenant by the project company to carry on no business other than the project. | ssec:2.1.2 |
| non-recourse debt | – | Debt whose lenders can look only to the project company's assets and cash flows, never to the sponsors. | ssec:2.2.1 |
| limited recourse debt | – | Debt for which the sponsors give defined, capped support (usually until completion) beyond their equity. | ssec:2.2.1 |
| full recourse debt | – | Debt for which the borrower's owner is liable without limit. | ssec:2.2.1 |
| sponsor support | – | Any undertaking by the sponsors to provide funds or performance to the project company beyond their base equity. | ssec:2.2.2 |
| contractual web | – | The set of interlocking contracts that fixes a project's revenues, costs and risks and so takes the place of a corporate balance sheet as the lenders' protection. | ssec:2.3.1 |
| risk allocation principle | – | The rule that each risk should be borne by the party best able to control it, price it and absorb its loss. | ssec:2.3.2 |
| corporate finance | – | Borrowing by a company on the strength of its whole balance sheet and all its cash flows (also balance-sheet financing). | ssec:2.4.1 |
| asset finance | – | Debt or leasing secured on a specific movable asset and repaid by the user's general credit. | ssec:2.4.2 |
| finance lease | – | A lease that transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of owning the asset to the lessee. | ssec:2.4.2 |
| operating lease | – | A lease under which the lessor keeps the main risks and rewards of ownership. | ssec:2.4.2 |
| reserve-based lending | RBL | Revolving debt to oil and gas producers whose amount is set by the value of proved reserves and redetermined periodically. | ssec:2.4.3 |
| acquisition finance | – | Debt raised to buy a company or asset, repaid from the target's cash flows and often the buyer's. | ssec:2.4.4 |
| securitization | – | Financing by selling or pledging a pool of many similar receivables to a vehicle that issues debt repaid from the pool. | ssec:2.4.5 |
| structured finance | – | Financing that uses vehicles, tranching and credit enhancement to shape risk for investors, not tied to a single operating project. | ssec:2.4.5 |
| debt capacity | – | The largest amount of debt a borrower can carry within its lenders' and rating agencies' limits. | ssec:2.5.1 |
| agency cost | – | The loss of value that arises when managers or one set of owners act in their own interest at the expense of other investors. | ssec:2.5.2 |
| off-balance-sheet financing | – | Financing that does not count as the sponsor's debt for covenant, rating or (where the tests are met) accounting purposes. | ssec:2.5.1 |
| net debt to EBITDA | – | A leverage ratio: borrowings less cash, divided by annual earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization. | ssec:2.5.1 |
| production payment | – | A right to a share of a resource's output, or its proceeds, until a fixed sum plus a return has been received, with no claim on the producer beyond that output. | ssec:3.1.1 |
| independent power producer | IPP | A privately owned generator that sells power to a utility or market rather than to its own customers. | sec:3.2 |
| Public Utility Regulatory Policies Act | PURPA | The 1978 US law that required utilities to buy power from qualifying independent generators (definition subject to the planned fact sheet). | sec:3.2 |
| Private Finance Initiative | PFI | The UK government's 1992 to 2018 program of privately financed public infrastructure paid for through performance-related unitary charges. | sec:3.5 |
| PF2 | – | The 2012 reform of the PFI, with a minority public equity stake, used six times and closed to new projects in 2018. | sec:3.5 |
| monoline insurer | – | A financial guarantor that, before 2008, wrapped bonds (including project bonds) by guaranteeing their debt service. | ssec:3.6.1 |
| debt trap | – | The compounding of capitalized interest when a heavily geared project is delayed, so that its debt grows faster than its future revenue can repay. | sec:3.4 |
| strategic sponsor | – | A sponsor that invests mainly to sell a product or service to, or buy output from, the project, as well as for dividends. | ssec:4.1.1 |
| financial sponsor | – | A sponsor, such as an infrastructure fund or pension fund, that invests for the equity return alone. | ssec:4.1.2 |
| developer | – | A company that originates and develops projects, taking early risk and usually selling some or all of its stake at or after financial close. | ssec:4.1.3 |
| mandated lead arranger | MLA | The bank appointed by the project company to structure, underwrite or arrange and syndicate the debt. | ssec:4.3.1 |
| institutional investor | – | An insurer, pension fund or asset manager that lends to or invests in projects from long-term savings. | ssec:4.3.4 |
| private credit | – | Lending by non-bank funds negotiated directly with borrowers rather than syndicated or issued in public markets. | ssec:4.3.4 |
| host government | – | The government of the country where the project is located, acting through its ministries, agencies and state companies. | sec:4.5 |
| contracting authority | – | The public body that signs a PPP or concession contract with the project company. | sec:4.5 |
| regulator | – | A public body that licenses the project or sets or approves its tariffs. | sec:4.5 |
| EPC contractor | – | The contractor that designs, procures and builds the plant under an engineering, procurement and construction contract. | ssec:4.6.1 |
| original equipment manufacturer | OEM | The maker of a project's main equipment, such as turbines or modules. | ssec:4.6.1 |
| O&M operator | – | The company that operates and maintains the plant under an O&M agreement. | ssec:4.6.2 |
| LTSA provider | LTSA | The equipment maker that maintains major equipment under a long-term service agreement. | ssec:4.6.2 |
| input supplier | – | A party that supplies fuel, feedstock, water or another input the project needs to operate. | sec:4.7 |
| hedge provider | – | A bank or other counterparty that enters into interest rate, currency or commodity hedges with the project company. | sec:4.7 |
| independent engineer | IE | The technical advisor appointed by the lenders to review the project's design, costs, schedule and performance and to certify drawdowns and completion. | sec:4.8 |
| financial adviser | – | An advisor, usually to the sponsors, that designs the financing strategy and runs the process with lenders. | sec:4.8 |
| model auditor | – | An independent firm that checks the financial model's arithmetic, logic and consistency with the documents for the lenders. | sec:4.8 |
| facility agent | – | The bank that administers a loan for the lenders: it collects and distributes payments and passes information and instructions. | sec:4.8 |
| intercreditor agent | – | The agent that collects votes and acts for the lender groups under an intercreditor agreement. | sec:4.8 |
| security agent | – | The party that holds the security for all secured creditors and enforces it on their instructions. | sec:4.8 |
| account bank | – | The bank that holds the project company's accounts under the accounts agreement. | sec:4.8 |
| bond trustee | – | The party that represents bondholders and acts for them under a bond indenture or trust deed. | sec:4.8 |
| rating agency | – | A firm that assigns credit ratings to debt, including project bonds and loans. | sec:4.8 |
| origination | – | The stage in which a project opportunity is identified and a sponsor decides to pursue it. | ssec:4.10.1 |
| development | – | The stage from origination to financial close in which the site, permits, contracts and financing are secured at the sponsors' risk. | ssec:4.10.2 |
| financial close | – | The date on which the finance documents are signed (or already signed) and all conditions to the first drawdown are satisfied or waived, so that debt can be drawn (mechanics Ch 55). | ssec:4.10.3 |
| completion | – | The point at which the plant passes its contractual completion tests and the lenders' completion conditions are met, releasing construction-phase sponsor support (mechanics Ch 61). | ssec:4.10.4 |
| commercial operation date | COD | The date from which the plant is commercially operating under its revenue contract and earns its full revenue. | ssec:4.10.4 |
| refinancing | – | Replacing all or part of a project's debt with new debt on different terms. | ssec:4.10.6 |
| decommissioning | – | Dismantling a plant and restoring its site at the end of its life. | ssec:4.10.7 |
| handback | – | Transfer of an asset to the host government or contracting authority at the end of a concession, in a specified condition. | ssec:4.10.7 |
