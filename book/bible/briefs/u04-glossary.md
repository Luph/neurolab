# Unit u04 glossary (Chapters 14, 15, 16)

Terms owned by Chapters 14 to 16, with canonical definitions and home sections. Source: the glossary tables in `bible/briefs/u04.md` (items 14.O, 15.O, 16.O). Bold `\term{}` appears only at the home section.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk (project finance sense) | – | A possible variation in a project's cash flow, its timing or the value of the claims on it, which some party must absorb. | 14.1.1 |
| Exposure | – | The amount and form of loss a particular party would suffer if a risk occurs, as distinct from the hazard that causes it. | 14.1.1 |
| Trigger | – | The event that starts a risk's transmission to cash flow (for example, a devaluation or a failed test). | 14.1.2 |
| Transmission path | – | The sequence of contracts and parties through which a risk event passes before it reaches a line of the project company's cash flow. | 14.1.2 |
| Risk bearer | – | The party that finally absorbs the loss when a risk occurs, after all contractual transfers are applied and honored. | 14.1.2 |
| Risk card | – | The book's five-field description of a risk: trigger, transmission path, cash-flow line hit, phase and duration, typical bearer (Framework 14.1). | 14.1.2 |
| Risk register | – | A list of a project's identified risks, each described in enough detail to be owned, assessed and tracked; the starting artifact of risk management. | 14.21 |
| Lifecycle risk profile | – | The way the amount and kind of risk in a project changes from development through construction, operations and end of life, peaking around completion. | 14.2 |
| Development risk | – | The risk that a project fails to reach financial close, losing the development spending. | 14.3.1 |
| Permitting risk | – | The risk that a permit or consent needed to build or operate is refused, delayed, conditioned or overturned after grant. | 14.3.2 |
| Financing risk (pre-close) | – | The risk that debt or equity cannot be raised on workable terms, or that a committed funder withdraws, before financial close. | 14.3.1 |
| Construction risk | – | The risk that the asset costs more, takes longer or performs worse at completion than the financing assumed. | 14.4 |
| Cost overrun risk | – | The construction risk that final capital cost exceeds the budget. | 14.4.1 |
| Delay risk | – | The construction risk that completion comes later than scheduled, costing revenue, interest and fixed costs for each day of delay. | 14.4.2 |
| Performance risk | – | The risk that the completed asset's output, efficiency or availability falls short of the levels the revenue and financing assume. | 14.4.3 |
| Technology risk | – | The risk that a design, piece of equipment or process does not perform as expected because it is new, scaled up or unproven in the conditions of use. | 14.5.1 |
| First-of-a-kind | FOAK | A technology, design or configuration with no commercial-scale operating reference. | 14.5.1 |
| Resource risk | – | The risk that the natural resource feeding the project (wind, sun, water, steam) is smaller or more variable than forecast. | 14.6.1 |
| Reserves risk | – | The risk that recoverable quantities of a mineral or hydrocarbon deposit are smaller, poorer or costlier to extract than forecast. | 14.6.1 |
| Demand risk | – | The risk that users buy less of the project's service than forecast at the price charged. | 14.7.1 |
| Volume risk | – | The risk that the project produces or is allowed to deliver less output than forecast, whatever the demand. | 14.7.1 |
| Operating leverage | OL | The ratio of revenue to revenue minus fixed operating costs; the multiplier by which a percentage revenue shortfall becomes a larger percentage fall in operating cash flow. | 14.7.1 |
| Price risk | – | The risk that the price received for output differs from the price assumed. | 14.7.2 |
| Market risk | – | Price and volume risk arising from selling into a traded market rather than under a fixed contract. | 14.7.2 |
| Merchant exposure | – | The part of a project's revenue sold at market prices without a contract or hedge. | 14.7.2 |
| Input supply risk | – | The risk that fuel, feedstock, water or other inputs are unavailable, of the wrong quality, late or dearer than assumed. | 14.8.1 |
| Operating risk | – | The risk that availability, performance or operating cost during operations differs from the assumptions, including catastrophic loss. | 14.9.1 |
| Counterparty credit risk | – | The risk that a party owing the project money or performance fails to pay or perform, and the cost of replacing it. | 14.10.1 |
| Concentration risk | – | Exposure to a single counterparty, site, market or event large enough that its failure alone threatens debt service. | 14.10.4 |
| Interface risk | – | The risk of loss where the scope, timing or performance of two parties or two pieces of work meet and neither contract assigns the gap. | 14.11.1 |
| Interest rate risk | – | The risk that changes in interest rates raise debt service, or reduce value, relative to revenue that does not move with them. | 14.12.1 |
| Inflation risk | – | The risk that costs and revenues are indexed differently, or not at all, so that inflation changes the margin between them. | 14.12.2 |
| Refinancing risk | – | The risk that debt due before the end of the asset's cash flows cannot be refinanced when due, or only on worse terms. | 14.12.3 |
| Liquidity risk | – | The risk that cash is not available on a payment date even though the project is solvent over time. | 14.12.4 |
| Currency risk | – | The risk of loss from the exchange rate between the currencies of revenue, costs and debt, or from the inability to exchange or move currency. | 14.13 |
| Devaluation risk | – | The currency risk that the revenue currency loses value against the debt or cost currency. | 14.13.1 |
| FX breakeven rate | – | The exchange rate at which local-currency cash after local costs exactly covers foreign-currency costs and debt service. | 14.13.1 |
| Convertibility risk | – | The risk that local currency cannot be exchanged into foreign currency through legal channels. | 14.13.2 |
| Transfer risk | – | The risk that converted foreign currency cannot be paid out of the host country. | 14.13.2 |
| Political risk | – | The risk of loss from acts or omissions of a government or from political events, including expropriation, political violence, breach of contract, change in law, non-honoring of sovereign obligations and sanctions. | 14.14 |
| Expropriation | – | Taking of the project or its value by the state, directly or through a series of measures with equivalent effect (creeping expropriation). | 14.14.1 |
| Political violence | – | War, insurrection, terrorism, sabotage or civil disturbance that damages the project or stops its operation. | 14.14.2 |
| Breach of contract (political) | – | Failure by a government or state entity to honor its contractual obligations to the project, with no effective remedy. | 14.14.3 |
| Change in law risk | – | The risk that new or changed law raises the project's costs or reduces its revenue. | 14.14.3 |
| Non-honoring of sovereign financial obligations | NHSFO | Failure of a government or state entity to pay an unconditional financial obligation, such as a guarantee, when due. | 14.14.3 |
| Sanctions risk | – | The risk that sanctions imposed on a country, person or sector prohibit or penalize the project's financing, supply, sales or payments. | 14.14.4 |
| Integrity risk | – | The risk of loss from corruption, fraud or other misconduct by or around the project's parties. | 14.14.4 |
| Regulatory risk | – | The risk that a regulator, acting within its powers, changes tariffs, rules or licences in a way that reduces value. | 14.15.1 |
| Legal risk | – | The risk that a contract, concession or permit is invalid or is interpreted against the project. | 14.15.2 |
| Enforceability risk | – | The risk that rights under contracts, security or arbitral awards cannot be enforced in practice. | 14.15.2 |
| Environmental and social risk | E&S risk | The risk that environmental damage or harm to people, or opposition from affected communities, causes cost, delay, liability or loss of financing (standards in Chapter 50). | 14.16.1 |
| Natural force majeure | – | An event outside the parties' control arising from nature, such as storm, flood or earthquake, that prevents performance. | 14.17.1 |
| Political force majeure | – | A force majeure event caused by government action or political events, usually given wider compensation than natural force majeure. | 14.17.1 |
| Sponsor risk | – | The risk that a sponsor cannot or will not fund commitments, manage the project, or avoid conflicts between its interests and the project's. | 14.18.1 |
| Physical climate risk | – | The risk of loss from acute weather events or chronic climate shifts that damage assets or reduce output. | 14.19.1 |
| Transition risk | – | The risk of loss from policy, technology or market changes made in the move to a low-carbon economy. | 14.19.2 |
| Cyber risk | – | The risk of loss from attacks on, or failures of, the project's control systems and information systems. | 14.19.3 |
| Decommissioning risk | – | The risk that dismantling, clean-up and site restoration cost more, arrive earlier or fall on a party less able to pay than planned. | 14.19.4 |
| Model and forecast risk | – | The risk that decisions rest on a financial model or forecast that is wrong in its logic or biased in its assumptions. | 14.3.1 |
| Risk cluster | – | A set of risks set off by a common trigger, which therefore occur together. | 14.20.1 |
| Wrong-way risk | – | The situation in which a protection's provider is most likely to fail in the same circumstances in which the protected loss occurs. | 14.20.2 |
| Local currency (generic) | LCY | The book's label for an unspecified local currency in illustrative examples. | 14.1.1 |
| Risk management cycle | – | The seven-step method of identifying, analyzing, quantifying, allocating, mitigating, pricing and monitoring a project's risks, repeated at each deal stage (Framework 15.1). | 15.1.1 |
| Pre-mortem | – | An identification exercise in which the team assumes the project has failed and writes the most plausible story of how. | 15.1.3 |
| Expected loss | EL | The probability-weighted average loss from a risk, the sum of each outcome's probability times its loss. | 15.3.1 |
| Tail loss | – | The size of a risk's loss in its worst outcomes, measured by a high percentile or by the average loss in the worst cases. | 15.3.1 |
| Three-point estimate | – | A low, most likely and high estimate of a quantity, used to build a simple probability distribution. | 15.3.2 |
| Joint scenario | – | A scenario in which correlated risks occur together with consistent assumptions, used instead of adding independent losses. | 15.3.3 |
| Risk matrix | – | A table that scores each risk's likelihood and impact on defined scales; drawn as a heat map. | 15.4.1 |
| Heat map | – | The grid form of a risk matrix, with likelihood on one axis, impact on the other and risks placed in cells. | 15.4.1 |
| Allocation matrix | – | A table recording, for each risk, its bearer, the instrument that allocates it, any cap, the residual bearer, its bankability rung and its monitoring indicator. | 15.4.2 |
| Allocation test | – | The book's four questions for choosing a risk's bearer: control, capacity, cost and collectability (Framework 15.2). | 15.5.1 |
| Collectability | – | The extent to which a party allocated a risk will actually pay when the loss occurs, given its credit and the correlation between its distress and the loss. | 15.5.1 |
| Pass-through | – | Allocation of a project company's cost or loss onward to the offtaker, users or the state through the revenue mechanism. | 15.5.2 |
| Back-to-back | – | Allocation of an obligation the project company owes upstream (to the offtaker or authority) downstream to a contractor or supplier on matching terms. | 15.5.3 |
| Risk-sharing band | – | An allocation that divides a risk's loss into layers carried by different parties, often with a first-loss layer for the party that controls the risk. | 15.5.4 |
| Residual risk | – | The risk that remains with the project company after all allocations and mitigations, borne by equity first and then by lenders. | 15.6.2 |
| First loss | – | The layer of a loss absorbed before any other party bears anything. | 15.6.1 |
| Mean-preserving spread | – | A change that widens the range of outcomes without changing their average; it lowers the value of a capped claim such as debt and raises that of a residual claim such as equity. | 15.7.1 |
| Risk premium | – | The amount a bearer charges for taking a risk above its expected loss. | 15.8.2 |
| Cost of retention | – | The expected loss of a retained risk plus the cost of the capital or support that must stand behind its tail (Equation 15.5). | 15.8.2 |
| Bankable | – | Describes a project whose risks, as allocated and mitigated, leave a residual that lenders will finance on non-recourse terms the project can afford, in the market at the time. | 15.9.1 |
| Bankability ladder | – | The book's seven-rung scale (0 to 6) placing a risk allocation from unfinanceable to capital-markets terms; a project sits on its weakest material rung (Framework 15.3). | 15.9.2 |
| Key risk indicator | KRI | A measurable quantity tracked after financial close that gives early warning that a risk is materializing. | 15.11.1 |
| Mitigation | – | Action that reduces a risk's probability or severity, transfers it to another party, or provides resources to absorb it. | 16.1.1 |
| Risk response | – | One of four ways of dealing with a risk: avoid, reduce, transfer or absorb. | 16.1.1 |
| Mitigant | – | A specific contract term, policy, guarantee, reserve, hedge, structural feature or support undertaking that mitigates a risk. | 16.1.2 |
| Deductible | – | The part of an insured loss the insured bears before the insurer pays; may be an amount or, for business interruption, a period. | 16.3.2 |
| Carrying cost of a reserve | – | The annual cost of holding a funded reserve: its amount times the difference between the cost of the money used to fund it and the interest it earns (Equation 16.1). | 16.5.2 |
| Natural hedge | – | An offset between exposures within the project itself, such as revenue and debt in the same currency, that reduces risk without a derivative. | 16.6.1 |
| Forward points (currency) | – | The difference between a forward exchange rate and spot, set mainly by the interest differential between the two currencies. | 16.6.2 |
| Structural mitigant | – | A feature of the financing's structure, such as gearing, tenor, sculpting, sweeps, lock-ups and covenants, that protects lenders without a third party. | 16.7 |
| Sponsor support | – | A sponsor's commitment, beyond its base equity, to provide funds or performance if defined events occur (detail in Chapter 26). | 16.8.1 |
| Limited recourse | – | Recourse to sponsors restricted by amount, time or trigger, as distinct from full recourse to their balance sheets. | 16.8.2 |
| Credit enhancement | – | Support from a third party, such as a guarantor, insurer, public lender or concessional funder, that improves the credit quality of the project's debt. | 16.9.1 |
| Monoline insurance (financial guarantee) | – | Insurance by a specialist guarantor of scheduled payments on bonds, which transfers the bonds' credit risk to the guarantor. | 16.9.1 |
| Layered loss stack | – | The ordered set of resources that absorb a loss, from the project's own margins to lenders' capital (Framework 16.2). | 16.10.1 |
| Mitigation selection grid | – | The book's tool for matching a risk's features (frequency and severity, controllability, insurability, tradability, correlation) to tool families (Framework 16.1). | 16.11.1 |
| Mitigation cost-benefit test | – | The book's test that a mitigant's annual cost is below its benefit in reduced expected loss and better financing, adjusted for collectability (Framework 16.3). | 16.11.2 |
| Common-mode failure | – | The failure of several mitigants at once because their providers depend on the same party or event. | 16.12.1 |
| Basis (mitigation) | – | A mismatch between the trigger, amount or timing of a mitigant's payment and the loss it is meant to cover. | 16.12.2 |
| Mitigation plan | – | A document listing each residual risk, the tools chosen, their cost and provider, the remaining exposure and the post-mitigation bankability rung. | 16.13 |
