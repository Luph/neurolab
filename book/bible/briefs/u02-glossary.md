# Unit u02 glossary: terms owned by Chapters 5 to 9

Canonical one-sentence definitions. Home section is where the term is defined in full and set in bold with `\term{}`. Terms flagged "see CR" depend on a change request in `u02.md`.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| time value of money | – | The principle that an amount of money is worth more the sooner it is received, because it can earn a return, because people prefer it sooner, and because inflation and risk erode later amounts. | 5.1.1 |
| simple interest | – | Interest calculated on the original principal only, so it does not itself earn interest. | 5.1.1 |
| compound interest | – | Interest calculated on the principal plus all interest already added, so interest earns interest. | 5.1.1 |
| compounding frequency | – | The number of times a year interest is added to the balance, such as annually, semiannually or monthly. | 5.1.2 |
| effective annual rate | EAR | The annual rate that, compounded once a year, gives the same result as a quoted rate compounded at its stated frequency. | 5.1.2 |
| continuous compounding | – | The limiting case of compounding infinitely often, under which an amount grows by the factor e raised to the rate times time. | 5.1.2 |
| rule of 72 | – | A shortcut that estimates the years needed to double money as 72 divided by the percentage interest rate. | 5.1.3 |
| future value | FV | The amount a sum invested today grows to after a stated number of periods at a stated rate. | 5.1.1 |
| present value | PV | The amount that, invested today at the discount rate, would grow to equal a stated future cash flow. | 5.2.1 |
| discount rate | r | The rate per period used to convert future cash flows into present values, reflecting the return available on investments of similar risk. | 5.2.1 |
| discount factor | DF | The present value of one unit of currency received at time t, equal to one divided by (1 + r) to the power t. | 5.2.1 |
| opportunity cost of capital | – | The return an investor gives up by committing money to one investment rather than the best alternative of similar risk. | 5.2.3 |
| annuity | – | A series of equal cash flows paid at equal intervals for a fixed number of periods, with payments at the end of each period unless stated otherwise. | 5.3.1 |
| annuity due | – | An annuity whose payments fall at the start of each period. | 5.3.1 |
| annuity factor | – | The present value of one unit of currency paid at the end of each of n periods at rate r. | 5.3.1 |
| growing annuity | – | A series of cash flows over a fixed number of periods that grows at a constant rate each period. | 5.3.2 |
| perpetuity | – | A level cash flow paid every period forever, whose present value is the payment divided by the discount rate. | 5.3.2 |
| growing perpetuity | – | A cash flow paid forever that grows at a constant rate below the discount rate. | 5.3.2 |
| levelized price | – | The constant (or constant-escalating) price per unit of output whose present value over the project's life equals the present value of its costs or of a varying price stream; levelized cost and levelized tariff are its two common uses (see CR-u02-1). | 5.3.3 |
| net present value | NPV | The sum of the present values of all of a project's cash flows, including the initial investment, at a stated discount rate. | 5.4.1 |
| NPV profile | – | A graph or table of a project's NPV across a range of discount rates. | 5.4.2 |
| internal rate of return | IRR | The discount rate at which the NPV of a series of cash flows equals zero. | 5.5.1 |
| multiple IRRs | – | The existence of more than one discount rate that sets NPV to zero, which can occur when cash flows change sign more than once. | 5.6.1 |
| reinvestment assumption | – | The implicit assumption in comparing IRRs that interim cash flows can be reinvested at the IRR itself. | 5.6.2 |
| modified internal rate of return | MIRR | A rate of return that compounds interim inflows at a stated reinvestment rate and discounts outflows at a stated finance rate, giving a single answer. | 5.6.2 |
| incremental IRR | – | The IRR of the difference between two mutually exclusive projects' cash flows, used to judge whether the larger project's extra investment earns enough. | 5.6.3 |
| crossover rate | – | The discount rate at which two projects' NPVs are equal and above or below which their ranking reverses. | 5.6.4 |
| XNPV | – | The net present value of cash flows on specific dates, discounted using the actual number of days elapsed divided by 365. | 5.7.1 |
| XIRR | – | The internal rate of return of cash flows on specific dates, consistent with XNPV. | 5.7.2 |
| payback period | – | The time until a project's cumulative undiscounted cash flows turn positive. | 5.8.1 |
| discounted payback | – | The time until a project's cumulative discounted cash flows turn positive. | 5.8.1 |
| inflation | – | A sustained rise in the general level of prices, measured as the percentage change in a price index. | 5.9.1 |
| consumer price index | CPI | A published index of the prices of a fixed basket of consumer goods and services, used to measure inflation and to index contract prices. | 5.9.1 |
| base date | – | The date at which a contract price or an index is fixed as the reference for later indexation. | 5.9.1 |
| rebasing | – | Rescaling an index so that its value at a chosen base date equals 100. | 5.9.1 |
| nominal value | – | An amount expressed in the money of the day it is paid, including the effect of inflation. | 5.9.2 |
| real value | – | An amount expressed in the purchasing power of a stated base year, with inflation removed. | 5.9.2 |
| Fisher relation | – | The identity linking nominal and real rates: (1 + nominal) = (1 + real) × (1 + inflation). | 5.9.2 |
| indexation | – | Adjustment of a contract price by the change in a stated index between a base date and an adjustment date. | 5.10.1 |
| indexation factor | IF | The ratio of an index's value at the adjustment date (after any lag) to its value at the base date. | 5.10.1 |
| partial indexation | – | Indexation of only a stated share of a price, with the remainder fixed in nominal terms. | 5.10.1 |
| fixed escalator | – | An increase in a contract price by a fixed percentage each period, regardless of actual inflation. | 5.10.1 |
| indexation lag | – | The gap between the index month used and the date the indexed price applies. | 5.10.2 |
| debt | – | Money borrowed under a contract that obliges the borrower to repay principal and pay interest on stated dates, whatever its results. | 6.1.1 |
| principal | – | The amount borrowed and outstanding under a loan, on which interest is calculated. | 6.1.1 |
| interest | – | The price a borrower pays a lender for the use of money over time, expressed as a rate on the principal. | 6.1.1 |
| facility | – | A lender's commitment to lend up to a stated amount on stated terms, from which loans are drawn. | 6.1.1 |
| commitment | – | The maximum amount a lender has agreed to lend under a facility. | 6.1.1 |
| availability period | – | The period during which a borrower may draw loans under a facility. | 6.1.1 |
| drawdown | – | A borrowing of part of a facility's commitment, also called a utilisation in English-law documents. | 6.1.1 |
| upfront fee | – | A one-time fee paid to lenders at signing or first drawdown, expressed as a percentage of the commitment. | 6.1.2 |
| commitment fee | – | A periodic fee on the undrawn part of a commitment, compensating lenders for holding the funds available. | 6.1.2 |
| interest period | – | The period over which interest on a loan accrues at one rate before it is paid and reset. | 6.2.1 |
| business-day convention | – | The rule that moves a payment date falling on a non-business day, such as following or modified following. | 6.2.1 |
| day-count convention | – | The rule for counting days in an interest period and in a year to compute the year fraction used for interest. | 6.2.2 |
| Actual/360 | – | A day-count convention that divides the actual days in the period by 360, used for USD loans, SOFR and euro money-market rates. | 6.2.2 |
| Actual/365 | – | A day-count convention that divides the actual days in the period by 365, used for sterling and SONIA. | 6.2.2 |
| 30/360 | – | A day-count convention that treats every month as 30 days and the year as 360, common for fixed-rate bonds and swap fixed legs. | 6.2.2 |
| repayment profile | – | The schedule of principal repayments over a loan's life. | 6.3.1 |
| bullet repayment | – | Repayment of all principal in one payment at maturity. | 6.3.1 |
| annuity repayment | – | A repayment profile in which principal plus interest is the same in every period. | 6.3.1 |
| straight-line repayment | – | A repayment profile in which the same amount of principal is repaid in every period. | 6.3.1 |
| weighted average life | WAL | The average time to repayment of a loan's principal, weighting each repayment date by the share of principal repaid then. | 6.3.2 |
| fixed rate | – | An interest rate set for the life of a loan or bond. | 6.4.1 |
| floating rate | – | An interest rate reset each interest period as a reference rate plus a margin. | 6.4.1 |
| reference rate | – | A published benchmark interest rate on which floating rates are set, also called the base rate. | 6.4.2 |
| margin | – | The fixed spread over the reference rate that a lender charges for credit risk, capital and profit. | 6.4.2 |
| basis point | bp | One hundredth of one percentage point (0.01%). | 6.4.2 |
| London Interbank Offered Rate | LIBOR | A former benchmark of the rates at which panel banks said they could borrow unsecured, which ceased to be published after September 30, 2024. | 6.5.1 |
| risk-free rate (reference-rate sense) | RFR | An overnight benchmark based on transactions with little or no credit risk, such as SOFR, SONIA and €STR (see CR-u02-8; compare risk-free rate, finance sense). | 6.5.2 |
| Secured Overnight Financing Rate | SOFR | The US dollar overnight rate measuring the cost of borrowing cash secured by US Treasury securities, published daily by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. | 6.5.2 |
| Term SOFR | – | A forward-looking SOFR-based rate for a stated term, set in advance of the interest period and derived from SOFR derivatives. | 6.5.3 |
| Sterling Overnight Index Average | SONIA | The sterling overnight unsecured rate administered by the Bank of England. | 6.5.2 |
| euro short-term rate | €STR | The euro overnight unsecured rate published by the European Central Bank. | 6.5.2 |
| Euro Interbank Offered Rate | EURIBOR | The euro term benchmark administered by the European Money Markets Institute, set for tenors of one week to twelve months. | 6.5.2 |
| in advance | – | Describes a floating rate known at the start of the interest period. | 6.5.3 |
| in arrears | – | Describes a floating rate calculated from daily rates observed during the interest period and known only near its end. | 6.5.3 |
| lookback | – | The number of business days by which daily rates used for an in-arrears calculation are taken from earlier dates, to allow time for payment. | 6.5.3 |
| observation shift | – | A variant of the lookback in which the day weights also come from the earlier observation period. | 6.5.3 |

| fallback | – | The contractual or statutory replacement rate that applies when a reference rate stops being available. | 6.5.4 |
| interest rate floor | – | A minimum level for a reference rate or an interest rate under a loan. | 6.5.4 |
| yield curve | – | The set of interest rates for different maturities at a given date. | 6.6.1 |
| zero rate | – | The annual rate for discounting a single cash flow at a stated maturity. | 6.6.1 |
| forward rate | – | The rate for a future period implied by today's curve, computed from two discount factors. | 6.6.2 |
| bond | – | A tradable debt security on which the issuer pays coupons and repays face value at stated dates. | 6.7.1 |
| coupon | – | The periodic interest a bond pays, as a percentage of its face value. | 6.7.1 |

| par, premium and discount | – | A bond trades at par when its price equals face value, at a premium above it, and at a discount below it. | 6.7.1 |

| amortizing swap | – | A swap whose notional falls over time in line with a loan's repayments. | 6.8.4 |
