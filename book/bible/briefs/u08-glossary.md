# Unit u08 glossary (Chapters 35 to 38)

Canonical definitions for terms owned by Chapters 35 to 38. Writers use these words; other chapters cross-reference the home section. "Ch" is the owning chapter.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| cash flow available for debt service | CFADS | The cash the project company has in a period, after paying operating costs, cash taxes, maintenance capital spending not funded from a reserve or from equity, and net transfers to the major maintenance reserve, that is available to pay senior debt service. | 35.1.1 (Ch 35) |
| debt service cover ratio | DSCR | CFADS for a period divided by the senior debt service (interest, scheduled principal, periodic fees, and net hedge payments) due in that period. | 35.2.1 (Ch 35) |
| minimum DSCR | – | The lowest period DSCR from the first repayment date to final maturity on a stated case. | 35.2.2 (Ch 35) |
| average DSCR | – | Total CFADS divided by total senior debt service over the repayment periods (the weighted form this book uses); the simple average of period DSCRs is reported only with that label. | 35.2.2 (Ch 35) |
| historic DSCR | – | The DSCR for the 12 months ending on a test date, calculated from actual cash flows. | 35.2.3 (Ch 35) |
| projected DSCR | – | The DSCR for the 12 months following a test date, calculated from the projections agreed under the finance documents. | 35.2.3 (Ch 35) |
| sizing ratio | – | The ratio level, on a stated case, to which the amount and repayment profile of debt are set at financial close. | 35.2.4 (Ch 35) |
| covenant ratio | – | A ratio level written into the finance documents whose breach on a test date triggers a consequence such as a distribution lock-up or an event of default. | 35.2.4 (Ch 35) |
| loan life cover ratio | LLCR | The present value of CFADS from a date to final maturity, discounted at the weighted average all-in senior interest rate, plus the DSRA balance at that date, divided by senior debt outstanding at that date. | 35.3.1 (Ch 35) |
| project life cover ratio | PLCR | The LLCR calculation extended to the end of the revenue contract or concession: present value of CFADS to that date plus the DSRA balance, divided by senior debt outstanding. | 35.4.1 (Ch 35) |
| gearing | – | Senior debt divided by the sum of senior debt and equity (share capital plus shareholder loans), measured on the total funding requirement at financial close, including financing costs and initial reserve funding. | 35.5.1 (Ch 35) |
| base case | – | The sponsor's best-estimate projection, usually on P50 resource or demand and the contracted terms, from which the financing is first designed. | 35.6.1 (Ch 35) |
| banking case | – | The lenders' projection: the base case with the adjustments the lenders and their advisors require (for example ten-year P90 resource, lower dispatch, higher costs), used for sizing or testing. | 35.6.1 (Ch 35) |
| downside case | – | A projection in which one or more drivers are set worse than the banking case, run with debt service held fixed to test whether the debt is still paid. | 35.6.2 (Ch 35) |
| break-even case | – | A projection in which one driver is moved until a ratio reaches a stated level, usually a minimum DSCR of 1.00x, to measure how far that driver can fall. | 35.6.3 (Ch 35) |
| headroom | – | The fall in a driver or in CFADS that the debt structure can absorb before a ratio reaches a stated trigger level. | 35.6.3 (Ch 35) |
| debt capacity | – | The largest senior debt that the CFADS of a stated case supports under all the sizing constraints at once. | 36.1.1 (Ch 36) |
| sculpting | – | Setting each period's scheduled debt service equal to that period's CFADS divided by a target DSCR, so that the DSCR on the sizing case is the same in every repayment period. | 36.2.1 (Ch 36) |
| target DSCR | DSCR* | The constant DSCR on the sizing case to which a repayment profile is sculpted. | 36.2.1 (Ch 36) |
| lesser-of rule | – | The rule that senior debt equals the smallest amount allowed by each sizing constraint (DSCR tests on each case, gearing cap, and any other limit). | 36.3.1 (Ch 36) |
| binding constraint | – | The sizing constraint that allows the smallest debt and so sets the debt amount. | 36.3.1 (Ch 36) |
| gearing cap | – | The maximum gearing the lenders allow, applied as a sizing constraint alongside the DSCR tests. | 36.3.2 (Ch 36) |
| P90 sizing | – | Sizing or testing debt on CFADS calculated from one-year or ten-year P90 resource or demand, at a DSCR set for that case. | 36.4.1 (Ch 36) |
| tenor | – | In debt sizing, the period from the first repayment (or COD) to final maturity, chosen against the length of the revenue contract and the asset's life. | 36.5.1 (Ch 36) |
| tail | – | The period between the final maturity of the senior debt and the end of the revenue contract, concession, or economic resource life, whose cash flows give lenders room to reschedule. | 36.5.2 (Ch 36) |
| grace period | – | A period after COD, or after first drawdown, in which interest is paid but no principal is due. | 36.6.1 (Ch 36) |
| balloon | – | A final principal installment much larger than the scheduled installments before it, intended to be refinanced rather than repaid from one period's cash flow. | 36.7.1 (Ch 36) |
| refinancing test | – | A sizing check that a balloon or mini-perm maturity amount could be refinanced from the cash flows after maturity at a stated DSCR and interest rate. | 36.7.1 (Ch 36) |
| mini-perm | – | A loan whose legal maturity is much shorter than the amortization profile it is sized on, so a large balance remains at maturity to be refinanced. | 36.7.2 (Ch 36) |
| hard mini-perm | – | A mini-perm whose outstanding balance must be repaid at legal maturity, so failure to refinance is an event of default. | 36.7.2 (Ch 36) |
| soft mini-perm | – | A mini-perm with a long legal maturity in which incentives (margin step-ups and cash sweeps from a set date) push the borrower to refinance, without a default if it does not. | 36.7.2 (Ch 36) |
| notional amortization profile | – | The longer repayment profile on which a mini-perm or balloon loan is sized, against which the legal maturity is shorter. | 36.7.2 (Ch 36) |
| revenue bucket sizing | – | Sizing debt by applying a separate target DSCR to the CFADS from each revenue type (contracted, hedged, merchant) and adding the resulting debt service. | 36.8.1 (Ch 36) |
| merchant tail | – | The years of a project's life after its revenue contract ends, when it sells at market prices. | 36.8.2 (Ch 36) |
| weighted average life | WAL | The average time to repayment of principal, each principal amount weighted by the time from the starting point at which it is repaid. | 36.9.1 (Ch 36) |
| debt service reserve account | DSRA | An account held for the lenders, funded at COD to a target (usually the next six months of senior debt service) and drawn to pay senior debt service when CFADS falls short. | 37.1.1 (Ch 37) |
| DSRA letter of credit | – | A letter of credit from an eligible bank, payable to the lenders on demand, that the finance documents allow in place of all or part of the DSRA cash balance. | 37.1.3 (Ch 37) |
| major maintenance reserve account | MMRA | An account into which the project company transfers, over the periods before each major overhaul, the cost of that overhaul, so that the overhaul does not fall on one period's CFADS. | 37.2.1 (Ch 37) |
| ramp-up reserve | – | A reserve funded at financial close, usually with equity, that pays interest or debt service shortfalls during the ramp-up of demand after opening. | 37.2.2 (Ch 37) |
| cash sweep | – | A requirement that a stated share of cash left after senior debt service and reserve transfers be applied to prepay senior debt instead of being distributed. | 37.3.1 (Ch 37) |
| inverse order of maturity | – | Application of a prepayment to the last scheduled installments first, shortening the loan's final maturity rather than reducing every installment. | 37.3.2 (Ch 37) |
| distribution lock-up | lock-up | The blocking of payments to shareholders when a distribution condition, usually a minimum historic or projected DSCR, is not met on a test date. | 37.4.1 (Ch 37) |
| distribution conditions | – | The conditions that must all be met on a test date before the project company may pay dividends or shareholder loan amounts (no default, reserves full, ratios at or above the lock-up levels, first repayment made, completion achieved). | 37.4.1 (Ch 37) |
| cash trap | – | Cash retained in a lenders' account after a lock-up, released only when the release conditions are met or applied to prepayment after a set number of failed tests. | 37.4.3 (Ch 37) |
| financial covenant | – | A covenant requiring a financial measure, such as historic DSCR or LLCR, to stay at or above a stated level, with defined consequences if it does not. | 37.5.1 (Ch 37) |
| default DSCR | – | The DSCR level below which a test result is an event of default, set below the lock-up level. | 37.5.2 (Ch 37) |
| equity cure | – | The sponsors' right to remedy a financial covenant breach by injecting equity, either counted as CFADS for the test period or applied to prepay debt, within stated limits on number and timing. | 37.5.3 (Ch 37) |
| non-financial covenant | – | A covenant requiring the project company to do or not do something other than meet a financial level, such as report information, maintain insurance, or not incur other debt. | 37.6.1 (Ch 37) |
| hedging requirement | – | The obligation in the finance documents to hedge a stated share of projected floating-rate debt (or of a currency or inflation exposure) for a stated tenor with eligible counterparties. | 37.7.1 (Ch 37) |
| hedge ratio | – | The hedge notional in a period divided by the projected floating-rate debt outstanding in that period. | 37.7.2 (Ch 37) |
| permitted hedging band | – | The range within which the hedge ratio must stay (for example 75% to 90%), outside which the project company must add or terminate hedges. | 37.7.1 (Ch 37) |
| accreting swap | – | An interest rate swap whose notional rises over time, used to match debt drawn during construction or debt that grows by capitalized interest. | 37.7.2 (Ch 37) |
| over-hedging | – | A hedge notional above the debt it hedges, usually after a prepayment, leaving the project company with a position that gains or loses with rates on no underlying debt. | 37.7.2 (Ch 37) |
| minimum margin | – | The margin a lender must charge to cover its funding premium, expected loss, operating cost, and required return on the capital the loan consumes. | 38.1.1 (Ch 38) |
| expected loss | EL | The average credit loss a lender expects on a loan per year, calculated as probability of default times loss given default times exposure. | 38.1.1 (Ch 38) |
| margin step-up | – | A scheduled increase in the margin at stated dates over the life of a loan. | 38.2.2 (Ch 38) |
| margin ratchet | – | A change in the margin, usually a reduction, triggered by an event such as completion, a rating, or a ratio level. | 38.2.3 (Ch 38) |
| arrangement fee | – | An upfront fee paid to the arrangers for structuring and arranging a financing, also called a structuring fee. | 38.3.1 (Ch 38) |
| underwriting fee | – | An upfront fee paid to banks that commit to provide the full facility before syndication, for taking the risk of not selling it down. | 38.3.1 (Ch 38) |
| participation fee | – | An upfront fee paid to lenders that join a syndicate, usually tiered by the size of the commitment taken. | 38.3.1 (Ch 38) |
| front-end fee | – | An upfront fee charged by a DFI or other direct lender on its own commitment, payable at or after signing. | 38.3.1 (Ch 38) |
| commitment fee | – | A fee charged on the undrawn amount of a committed facility, stated as a rate a year or as a percentage of the margin. | 38.3.2 (Ch 38) |
| agency fee | – | A fixed annual fee paid to the facility agent, security agent, or intercreditor agent for administering the financing. | 38.3.3 (Ch 38) |
| credit and execution charge | – | The amount added to the mid-market swap rate by a hedge counterparty for its credit risk, capital, and execution cost. | 38.4.1 (Ch 38) |
| withholding tax gross-up | – | The borrower's obligation to increase interest payments so that a lender receives the same amount after withholding tax as it would have without it. | 38.5.3 (Ch 38) |
| all-in cost of debt | – | The interest rate that equates the net proceeds a borrower receives with every payment it makes on the debt, including fees, premia, hedge charges, insurance, and gross-ups, stated on a named basis. | 38.6.1 (Ch 38) |
| market flex | – | The arrangers' right under an underwritten commitment to change the pricing or structure of a financing, within agreed limits, to complete syndication. | 38.8.1 (Ch 38) |
| reverse flex | – | A reduction in pricing made under a flex arrangement when syndication demand is strong. | 38.8.1 (Ch 38) |
