# Unit u10 glossary (Chapters 46 to 50)

Terms owned by Chapters 46 to 50, with canonical definitions and home sections (from `bible/briefs/u10.md`). Writers use these definitions verbatim at the home definition and the same terminology elsewhere.

| Term | Abbreviation | Definition | Home section |
|---|---|---|---|
| project IRR | – | The discount rate at which the present value of a project's unlevered cash flows (before any debt flows, stated pre- or post-tax) equals zero. | 46.1.1 |
| equity IRR | – | The discount rate at which the present value of all cash flows between the project company and its equity investors (share capital, shareholder loans, their interest and repayments, and dividends) equals zero. | 46.1.1 |
| hurdle rate | – | The minimum return an investor requires before committing capital to a given project at a given stage; the rate at which it discounts to set a price. | 46.1.2 |
| cash yield | – | Cash distributed to equity in a period divided by the equity invested, used by income investors as a measure of current return. | 46.1.3 |
| money multiple | MOIC | Total cash returned to equity divided by total equity invested, ignoring timing. | 46.1.4 |
| stage premium | – | The addition to an operating-asset hurdle that investors require for holding a project at an earlier stage of development or construction. | 46.2.2 |
| bid rate | – | The discount rate a sponsor uses to price a competitive bid, usually below the rate at which it would hold the asset, reflecting expected refinancing and sell-down gains. | 46.2.4 |
| value staircase | – | The book's framework for valuing a development project at each milestone by rolling back from financial close with probabilities, time, stage hurdles and remaining spend. | 46.3.2 |
| development value | – | The probability-weighted present value of a development project's equity at a milestone, net of the spend needed to reach financial close. | 46.3.2 |
| enterprise value | EV | The value of a project's unlevered cash flows to all capital providers, before deducting debt. | 46.4.1 |
| equity value | – | Enterprise value less net debt and other senior claims, or equivalently the present value of distributions to equity at a levered rate. | 46.4.1 |
| equity bridge | – | The reconciliation from enterprise value to equity value through debt, cash, reserves, hedges and shareholder loans. | 46.4.1 |
| risk-bucket valuation | – | Valuation that discounts contracted, hedged and merchant cash flows separately, each at a rate matched to its risk. | 46.4.2 |
| EV/EBITDA multiple | – | Enterprise value divided by a year's EBITDA, used as a transaction cross-check; misleading for assets with different remaining lives. | 46.4.4 |
| bid variable | – | The single quantity on which a competitive tender ranks compliant bids, such as the tariff, the public contribution or the upfront concession fee. | 47.1.1 |
| levelized tariff | – | The constant tariff whose present value over the contract equals the present value of the actual tariff stream, per unit of output, at a stated discount rate. | 47.1.2 |
| committed-finance letter | – | A lender's letter submitted with a bid confirming that financing is approved on stated terms, subject only to listed conditions. | 47.1.4 |
| winner's curse | – | The tendency of the winning bidder in a common-value competition to be the one that most overestimated the asset's value. | 47.2.1 |
| bid shading | – | Lowering a bid below one's own unbiased value estimate to correct for the winner's curse. | 47.2.3 |
| walk-away price | – | The highest price (or lowest tariff) a bidder's committee has approved, beyond which the team must not bid. | 47.2.4 |
| information memorandum (sale) | IM | The seller's marketing document describing an asset for sale, issued to bidders after a confidentiality agreement. | 47.3.1 |
| non-binding offer | – | A first-round indicative bid in an auction, stating price and key assumptions without commitment. | 47.3.1 |
| binding offer | – | A final-round offer in an auction with a marked-up SPA and committed financing, capable of acceptance. | 47.3.1 |
| sale and purchase agreement | SPA | The contract by which a seller transfers shares or assets to a buyer, setting price, conditions, warranties, indemnities and limitations. | 47.3.3 |
| disclosure letter | – | The seller's document that qualifies the warranties by disclosing known exceptions. | 47.3.3 |
| specific indemnity | – | A seller's promise to reimburse a known, identified risk in full, outside the warranty limits. | 47.3.3 |
| locked box | – | A price mechanism fixing the equity price on balance-sheet figures at a past date, with the buyer protected by a covenant against value leaving the company afterward. | 47.3.4 |
| leakage | – | Value transferred from the target to the seller or its affiliates after the locked-box date, such as dividends or fees, which the seller must repay unless permitted. | 47.3.4 |
| permitted leakage | – | Payments to the seller after the locked-box date that the SPA expressly allows and the price already reflects. | 47.3.4 |
| ticker | – | An amount added to a locked-box price for the period from the locked-box date to completion, compensating the seller for cash the business earns. | 47.3.4 |
| completion accounts | – | A price mechanism under which the final price is adjusted for net debt and working capital shown in accounts prepared at completion. | 47.3.4 |
| warranty and indemnity insurance | W&I | Insurance, usually bought by the buyer, that pays losses from breaches of the seller's warranties above a retention and up to a limit. | 47.3.5 |
| retention (W&I) | – | The amount of loss the insured bears before a W&I policy pays. | 47.3.5 |
| deferred consideration | – | Part of the purchase price paid after completion, often conditional on a stated event. | 47.3.6 |
| earn-out | – | Deferred consideration whose amount depends on the asset's performance after completion. | 47.3.6 |
| concentration limit | – | A cap in a fund's or lender's rules on exposure to one asset, technology, market, counterparty or country. | 47.4.2 |
| limited partner | LP | An investor in a fund partnership whose liability is limited to its commitment. | 47.5.1 |
| general partner | GP | The manager of a fund partnership, which makes investment decisions and earns fees and carried interest. | 47.5.1 |
| commitment | – | The amount an LP agrees to provide to a fund when called. | 47.5.1 |
| core, core-plus, value-add | – | Fund strategy labels ordered by rising risk and target return, from operating contracted assets to development and repositioning. | 47.5.1 |
| management fee | – | The GP's annual fee, charged on commitments during the investment period and usually on invested capital afterward. | 47.5.2 |
| preferred return | – | The compound annual return LPs must receive before the GP earns carried interest. | 47.5.2 |
| carried interest | carry | The GP's share of fund profits above the preferred return. | 47.5.2 |
| catch-up | – | The waterfall tier that pays the GP a high share of distributions after the preferred return until it has received its full carry percentage of profit. | 47.5.2 |
| European waterfall | – | A whole-fund distribution waterfall in which carry is paid only after LPs recover all contributions and the preferred return. | 47.5.2 |
| American waterfall | – | A deal-by-deal waterfall in which carry is paid on each realized investment, usually with a clawback. | 47.5.2 |
| gross IRR | – | A fund's return on its investments before fees and carried interest. | 47.5.3 |
| net IRR | – | The return to LPs after fees, expenses and carried interest. | 47.5.3 |
| net asset value | NAV | The fund's estimate of the fair value of its investments less liabilities at a reporting date. | 47.5.3 |
| due diligence | DD | The investigation lenders and investors carry out, largely through advisors, to verify a project's assumptions and identify risks before committing capital. | 48.1.1 |
| reliance letter | – | An advisor's letter allowing named lenders or investors to rely on a report prepared for another client, usually subject to a liability cap. | 48.1.1 |
| vendor due diligence | VDD | Diligence reports commissioned by a seller and made available, with reliance, to bidders and the eventual buyer. | 48.1.1 |
| bring-down letter | – | An advisor's confirmation at financial close that nothing has come to its attention since its report that changes its conclusions. | 48.1.2 |
| diligence scope matrix | – | The book's framework for planning every diligence workstream by commissioner, reliance, scope, outputs and timing. | 48.1.2 |
| report challenge protocol | – | The book's eight-step method for testing any advisor's report. | 48.1.3 |
| owner's engineer | – | The engineer retained by the project company to manage and supervise design and construction on its behalf, as distinct from the lenders' independent engineer. | 48.2.1 |
| first-of-a-kind | FOAK | A technology or configuration without a commercial-scale operating reference, for which performance cannot be benchmarked. | 48.2.2 |
| three-point estimate | – | A cost or duration estimate given as low, most likely and high values, from which a mean and spread are derived (PERT). | 48.2.3 |
| energy yield assessment | EYA | A consultant's estimate of a wind or solar plant's output, from gross output through losses to net P50 and exceedance levels. | 48.3.1 |
| loss chain | – | The ordered set of percentage losses applied to gross output to reach net output. | 48.3.1 |
| ore reserve | – | The economically mineable part of a measured or indicated mineral resource, stated as proved or probable. | 48.3.3 |
| competent person | – | A qualified professional who signs a public or lender report on resources and reserves under a recognized reporting code. | 48.3.3 |
| reserve tail | – | The share of initial reserves (or reserve life) expected to remain when a loan is fully repaid. | 48.3.3 |
| price curve | – | A market advisor's projection of future prices for a commodity or power product, usually in base, low and high cases. | 48.4.1 |
| investment-grade traffic study | – | A traffic and revenue forecast prepared to the standard lenders and rating agencies rely on, as distinct from a planning study. | 48.5.1 |
| value of time | VOT | The amount a traveler is willing to pay to save one hour of travel time, the main input to toll-choice models. | 48.5.2 |
| diversion curve | – | The relationship between the toll road's share of a trip market and the generalized-cost advantage of using it. | 48.5.2 |
| toll elasticity | – | The percentage change in toll-road traffic for a 1% change in the toll. | 48.5.2 |
| optimism bias | – | The systematic tendency of forecasts prepared to win approval or a bid to overstate benefits and understate costs. | 48.5.4 |
| legal due diligence report | – | Lenders' counsel's report on the legal validity, enforceability and risks of a project's corporate structure, contracts, permits, land and security. | 49.1.2 |
| red-flag report | – | A diligence report limited to material adverse findings, without a full description of each document. | 49.1.2 |
| red-flag grid | – | The book's framework for grading each diligence finding by severity, likelihood and cure route. | 49.1.2 |
| legal opinion | – | A law firm's formal letter to lenders on specified legal matters, such as capacity, enforceability and choice of law, given on stated assumptions and qualifications. | 49.1.5 |
| qualification (in an opinion) | – | A stated limit on a legal opinion's conclusion, such as insolvency laws or court discretion. | 49.1.5 |
| reasoned opinion | – | A legal opinion that sets out the analysis and the opinion-giver's view where the law is unsettled, instead of an unqualified conclusion. | 49.1.5 |
| lenders' insurance advisor | – | The adviser who reviews a project's insurance program for the lenders against the finance documents and the project's risks. | 49.2.1 |
| broker's letter of undertaking | – | A letter from the project company's insurance broker to the lenders undertaking to notify them of non-payment, cancellation and changes in cover. | 49.2.4 |
| model audit sign-off | – | The model auditor's letter confirming, within a stated scope, that the financial model computes as described and reflects the documents. | 49.3.2 |
| materiality threshold | – | The size of error, in debt or ratio terms, above which a diligence or audit finding must be corrected before sign-off. | 49.3.2 |
| tax leakage | – | Tax cost that reduces cash to lenders or equity, such as withholding gross-ups, disallowed interest or irrecoverable VAT. | 49.4.2 |
| gross-up | – | An obligation to increase a payment so that the recipient receives the full amount after a deduction such as withholding tax. | 49.4.2 |
| counterparty credit card | – | The book's one-page framework for assessing a counterparty's ability and willingness to perform. | 49.5.4 |
| know your customer | KYC | The checks a financial institution must make to verify the identity, ownership, control and source of funds of its customers. | 49.6.1 |
| beneficial owner | – | A natural person who ultimately owns or controls a legal entity. | 49.6.1 |
| politically exposed person | PEP | A person who holds or has held a prominent public function, or a family member or close associate of such a person. | 49.6.1 |
| sanctions screening | – | Checking parties and their owners against sanctions lists and ownership rules before and during a transaction. | 49.6.2 |
| OFAC 50 percent rule | – | The US rule that any entity owned 50% or more, in aggregate and directly or indirectly, by blocked persons is itself blocked. | 49.6.2 |
| ownership trace | – | The book's framework for mapping ownership and control to natural persons and applying each sanctions regime's tests. | 49.6.2 |
| integrity due diligence | IDD | Investigation of the reputation, conduct and connections of sponsors, partners and agents, focused on corruption, fraud and sanctions risk. | 49.6.3 |
| environmental and social | E&S | The impacts of a project on the natural environment and on people, including workers, communities and indigenous peoples, and the risks those impacts create. | 50.1.1 |
| social license to operate | – | The ongoing acceptance of a project by affected communities and the wider public, without which legal rights to operate become hard to exercise. | 50.1.3 |
| IFC Performance Standards | IFC PS | The eight IFC standards (2012 edition) on environmental and social sustainability that IFC clients must meet and that other lenders adopt as a benchmark. | 50.2.1 |
| mitigation hierarchy | – | The sequence of avoiding, then minimizing, then compensating for or offsetting adverse impacts. | 50.2.1 |
| environmental and social management system | ESMS | A project company's system of policies, procedures, people and monitoring for managing E&S risks over the project's life. | 50.2.1 |
| Equator Principles | EP | A voluntary risk-management framework under which signatory financial institutions apply E&S standards to in-scope project-related financings; the current version is EP4 (2020). | 50.2.2 |
| Equator Principles Financial Institution | EPFI | A financial institution that has adopted the Equator Principles. | 50.2.2 |
| Designated Country | – | Under EP4, a country that is both an OECD member and on the World Bank's high-income list, where host-country law is the applicable standard. | 50.2.2 |
| Category A, B, C | – | EP4 and IFC classifications of projects by the severity of potential E&S impacts, from significant and irreversible (A) to minimal (C). | 50.2.2 |
| Environmental and Social Framework | ESF | The World Bank's framework (2016) of ten Environmental and Social Standards applying to its investment project financing to governments. | 50.2.3 |
| Environmental and Social Commitment Plan | ESCP | The legally binding plan of a World Bank borrower's E&S commitments under the ESF. | 50.2.3 |
| OECD Common Approaches | – | The OECD recommendation under which export credit agencies screen, classify and review the E&S impacts of projects they support. | 50.2.4 |
| environmental and social impact assessment | ESIA | The study that identifies a project's E&S baseline, impacts, alternatives and mitigation, and is disclosed to affected people. | 50.3.1 |
| cumulative impact assessment | – | Assessment of a project's impacts in combination with other existing and planned developments affecting the same resources or communities. | 50.3.1 |
| environmental and social management plan | ESMP | The plan setting out the measures, responsibilities, schedule and monitoring that implement an ESIA's mitigation. | 50.3.2 |
| environmental and social action plan | ESAP | The lenders' list of dated actions that close gaps between a project and the applicable standards, enforced through the finance documents (EPAP under EP4). | 50.3.3 |
| independent environmental and social consultant | IESC | The adviser who reviews E&S documentation and compliance for the lenders and monitors the project after financial close. | 50.3.4 |
| stakeholder engagement plan | SEP | A project's plan for identifying stakeholders, disclosing information and consulting affected communities. | 50.4.1 |
| grievance mechanism | – | A project-level process for receiving, investigating and resolving complaints from affected communities or workers. | 50.4.2 |
| physical displacement | – | Relocation or loss of shelter caused by project land acquisition. | 50.5.1 |
| economic displacement | – | Loss of assets or access to assets that leads to loss of income or livelihood, with or without relocation. | 50.5.1 |
| full replacement cost | – | Compensation sufficient to replace lost assets at market value plus transaction costs, without deduction for depreciation. | 50.5.1 |
| resettlement action plan | RAP | The plan for compensating and relocating physically displaced people and restoring their livelihoods. | 50.5.1 |
| livelihood restoration plan | LRP | The plan for restoring the incomes of economically displaced people who are not relocated. | 50.5.1 |
| cut-off date | – | The date after which people or assets newly present in a project area are not eligible for compensation. | 50.5.2 |
| informed consultation and participation | ICP | The in-depth consultation PS7 requires with affected indigenous peoples in all cases. | 50.6.1 |
| free, prior and informed consent | FPIC | Agreement of affected indigenous peoples reached through good-faith negotiation in the circumstances PS7 specifies; it does not require unanimity and confers no individual veto. | 50.6.2 |
| critical habitat | – | Habitat of high biodiversity value under PS6, where a project may proceed only under strict conditions including net gains. | 50.7.1 |
| biodiversity offset | – | Measurable conservation gains designed to compensate for residual biodiversity losses after avoidance and minimization. | 50.7.2 |
| net gain | – | A biodiversity outcome in which gains exceed losses for the values affected, required in critical habitat under PS6. | 50.7.2 |
| human rights due diligence | – | Assessment and management of a project's actual and potential human rights impacts, by reference to the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. | 50.8.3 |
