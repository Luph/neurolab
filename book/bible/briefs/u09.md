## 0. How this unit is organized

Part VII builds one workbook, the Case P reference model, from a blank file. Chapters 39 to 43 each add sheets and rows in a fixed order; Chapter 44 audits a seeded copy of the finished model; Chapter 45 adds sector modules on Cases T and R and on illustrative assets. Because the seven chapters share one workbook, this file opens with the **Part VII model build map** (Section 0.2): the sheet list, the row map of every sheet with the chapter that builds each block, the formula for the first period column in mathematical notation and in Excel, and the macros. Each chapter brief then cites the rows it builds. Writers must not re-specify rows another chapter builds; they cross-reference them.

### 0.1 Coordination with the modeler and the companion workbook

The modeler agent is building `model/case_p.py`, `model/Case_P_Model.xlsx` and the figure ledgers at the same time as this brief. When this brief was written, `book/model/` held only the three input files; no workbook, Python mirror or ledger existed. Therefore:

1. The row map below is the book's canonical layout. It follows the style sheet (Section 5.4) exactly: sheet order Cover, Inputs, Time, Construction, Operations, Tax, Funding, Debt, Reserves, Waterfall, Financials, Ratios, Returns, Checks, Outputs; column D label, E unit, F constant or scalar result, G row total or check, H and I blank, J the first model period; flags 1 or 0 with the `Flag_` prefix; no range names except `Scenario`; no OFFSET or INDIRECT; costs stored positive and subtracted explicitly.
2. The coordinator should send Section 0.2 to the modeler with the request that `build_excel_p.py` lay out the workbook on this map. If the generated workbook differs, the workbook wins for cell addresses: each writer re-reads the row numbers from `Case_P_Model.xlsx` before quoting a cell, and keeps the sheet names, block order and formulas of this brief. Every row-number difference is reported in the writer's status note.
3. Every Case P number printed in Chapters 39 to 44 is a figure-ledger value cited by ID. This brief never states a Case P model output. Where a chapter needs a figure that has no ID, it is listed in "Case Bible flaws and requests" at the end of this file with a proposed new ID (P-F37 onward, R-F11).
4. Where the Case Bible or the JSON leaves a modeling rule open (for example, how operating years map onto semiannual periods), this brief proposes the rule, marks it "proposed rule", and lists it for the modeler. If the modeler's report adopts a different rule, the writer teaches the modeler's rule.

Equation (39.1), the period end rule:
$$e_t = \begin{cases}\text{end of the month of } s_t & s_t < \text{COD}\\ \min\left(\text{end of June or December after } s_t,\ \text{PPA expiry}\right) & s_t \ge \text{COD}\end{cases}$$
Equation (39.2), operating year (proposed rule, flaw F-01): $\mathrm{OY}_t = \lceil \text{months since COD at } e_t / 12 \rceil$. In the FC base this makes OY1 the eight months May to December 2021 and OY$k$ the calendar year $2020+k$ for $k \ge 2$, so tax years, availability cycle years and maintenance years all align with fiscal years.

### 39.K Practitioner's notebook

- Checklist: blueprint written and agreed; every input has a unit and a source; one formula per row (test: select row, Ctrl+\ finds no differences); timeline contiguous; flags sum to expected counts; master check visible on every sheet; version and change log on the Cover.
- Red flags: dates typed as values; TRUE/FALSE flags; OFFSET, INDIRECT or merged cells on calculation sheets; columns whose period length is not on a row; a model where changing COD moves revenue but not debt service; inputs on calculation sheets; the same input in two places.
- Rules of thumb and their limits: "operating period equals debt service period" (breaks for monthly-paying bonds or quarterly-paying loans, where the operating period follows the debt); "monthly construction" (quarterly is acceptable when drawdowns are quarterly and IDC is immaterial); "one formula per row" (exceptions: the first column of a corkscrew, handled with N(I...) rather than a different formula).
- Common mistakes: using `>` where `>=` is needed; a stub period treated as a full half-year in a fixed-charge row; year fractions computed inside formulas; scenario logic written into calculation formulas instead of the live column.
- Questions experts ask: Which decision is this model for? What happens to every row if COD moves three months? Where is the day count? Show me the check that proves the timeline. Who changed this input and when?

### 40.H Frameworks

- **Framework 40.1 The circularity ladder** (`fw:circularity-ladder`, original). Purpose: choose the least fragile way to resolve a loop. Steps: (1) map the loop and confirm it is real (a value depends on itself through other cells); (2) if the loop sits inside one period and is linear, solve it algebraically in the period (gross-up); (3) if it spans periods but is linear in one or two scalars, solve for the scalars in closed form or paste them; (4) paste the fewest possible values, on the Inputs sheet, in input color, with a calculated twin and a residual check; (5) run a macro that copies, recalculates and stops on tolerance or a pass limit; (6) never leave iterative calculation switched on in a model others use; if an inherited model relies on it, add a circuit-breaker switch and a convergence check before relying on its outputs. When to use: every time a model shows a circular reference warning, or a number "moves" between recalculations. Demonstrated in Example 40.3 and Section 40.5.5.

### 40.K Practitioner's notebook

- Checklist: budget lines sum to the contract budget; every timing rule an input; sources equal uses in every period; total gearing at or below the cap; ECA tranche within its cap; commitment fees stop when a tranche is fully drawn; IDC computed on opening balances; pasted values in input color with residual check; pass count logged.
- Red flags: IDC computed on closing balances (creates an in-period loop); a single blended rate for all tranches when margins differ; contingency modeled as unspent while the lenders size on full spend; ECA premium omitted from uses or from the capitalized cost; equity percentage applied to costs before financing instead of total uses; iterative calculation switched on with no convergence check.
- Rules of thumb and their limits: "IDC is roughly half the construction period's interest on the final debt" (holds for a smooth S-curve and level rates; fails for a front-loaded profile with a large advance payment, such as a 10% advance plus development costs at close); "pro rata costs the sponsor less IRR than equity first" (true when the equity hurdle exceeds the debt cost, which it nearly always does).
- Common mistakes: treating the LNTP as a new cash use at close; charging commitment fees on the standby facility after it is cancelled at COD; forgetting that a swap's fixed leg and floating leg use different day counts; converging only on debt and not on total uses.
- Questions experts ask: What is the residual after the last converge pass? Which constraint binds? How much of the funding requirement is financing cost? What happens to IDC if notice to proceed slips three months? Where is the LNTP in sources and uses?
