## Verdict: FAIL

Five statements are wrong or misattributed: the 2015 note amount, Sabine Pass capacity, SunEdison debt attribution, the referent of "it", and the Hyperion debt issuer. Several smaller sourcing, labeling and D-011 defects remain.

## Defects

1. **Opening, paragraph 2 ("Much of the USD 11.7 billion ... borrowed by the TerraForm companies and by the individual projects beneath them").** This misattributes the debt. SunEdison's Q3 2015 10-Q (Note 8) breaks the USD 11,672 million down as follows:
   - TerraForm Power segment: USD 2,548 million.
   - TerraForm Global segment: USD 1,240 million.
   - SunEdison's own Renewable Energy Development segment: USD 7,884 million. This includes recourse items: the convertible senior notes (USD 1,862 million), the exchangeable notes (USD 336 million) and the SunEdison-guaranteed margin loan (USD 404 million). It also includes non-recourse warehouse and project debt on projects SunEdison itself held.

   So the TerraForm companies and their projects held about a third of the debt (USD 3.8 billion), not "much" of it. The sentence also implies that all non-recourse debt sat beneath TerraForm. **Fix:** "About USD 3.8 billion of it had been borrowed by the TerraForm companies and the projects beneath them, and much of the rest by project companies and warehouse vehicles that SunEdison held directly, without recourse to SunEdison; lenders to those borrowers could look only to them and their projects." Add nothing beyond these 10-Q figures.

2. **Opening, paragraph 2 ("owned operating wind and solar plants that SunEdison had built or bought").** This is inaccurate as a blanket statement. TerraForm Power's FY2015 10-K says it acquired assets "from SunEdison and unaffiliated third parties", for example Invenergy Wind; First Wind was bought jointly. **Fix:** "owned operating wind and solar plants, many of them built or bought by SunEdison".

3. **Opening, paragraph 3 ("Five months before SunEdison's bankruptcy, it had used its voting control to remove TerraForm Power's chief executive...").** The antecedent of "it" is TerraForm Power, the subject of the preceding sentence. As written, the sentence says TerraForm Power removed its own officers. **Fix:** "Five months before its bankruptcy, SunEdison had used its voting control to remove ..."

4. **Opening, paragraph 2 (citation for "in 2017 Brookfield bought ... all of TerraForm Global").** This is cited only to TerraForm Power 2018, which covers TerraForm Power, not the Global sale. **Fix:** cite the Global sale to TerraForm Global, Inc., Form 8-K Exhibit 99.1, "Brookfield Closes Acquisition of TerraForm Global," December 28, 2017, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1620702/000095015717001639/ex99-1.htm (fact sheet source 9), and add it to the Sources list.

5. **ssec:2.1.3 ("in 2015 its subsidiary Terra Operating LLC issued USD 800 million of 5.875% senior notes due 2023").** This understates the 2015 issuance. A USD 150 million add-on of the 2023 notes followed in June 2015 (fact sheet item 7). The SunEdison 10-Q shows USD 1,250 million of TerraForm Power senior notes at September 30, 2015 (800 + 150 + 300). **Fix:** "issued USD 950 million of 5.875% senior notes due 2023 (USD 800 million in January and USD 150 million in June) and USD 300 million of 6.125% senior notes due 2025".

6. **ssec:2.1.3, last sentence of the governance paragraph ("SunEdison's creditors' committee pursued claims ... released only in the 2017 settlement with Brookfield").** The Settlement Agreement was between TerraForm Power and the SunEdison debtors. It was signed on March 6–7, 2017, alongside the Brookfield merger agreement, and it released the intercompany claims (fact sheet items 16–17). Brookfield was not the counterparty. The committee's step was a November 7, 2016 motion for standing to bring avoidance claims (FY2015 10-K). **Fix:** "In November 2016 SunEdison's creditors' committee sought standing to sue to unwind payments and asset transfers between SunEdison and TerraForm Power; the claims were released in TerraForm Power's March 2017 settlement with the SunEdison debtors, signed alongside the Brookfield agreement."

7. **ssec:2.1.2, final paragraph ("Common-law courts can, in rare cases, consolidate a parent and a subsidiary that were run as one business").** Substantive consolidation is a US bankruptcy-court doctrine, not a general common-law power. English courts do not pool group estates; they disregard separate personality only in the narrow cases left after *Prest v Petrodel Resources Ltd* [2013] UKSC 34. The statement also matters for the book's common-law Case T. **Fix:** "US bankruptcy courts can, in rare cases, substantively consolidate a parent and a subsidiary that were run as one business; English courts almost never disregard a subsidiary's separate personality; civil-law systems reach similar results through other doctrines."

8. **ssec:2.3.3 ("with most of its capacity reserved by Total and Chevron").** This is wrong. Total and Chevron each reserved about 1.0 Bcf/d of about 4.0 Bcf/d. The remaining about 2.0 Bcf/d was reserved by a Cheniere affiliate, and from July 2012 by SPL (fact sheet items 1–2). **Fix:** "with half of its capacity reserved by Total and Chevron under 20-year agreements paying about USD 125 million a year each, used or not, and the other half by a Cheniere affiliate."

9. **ssec:2.3.3 ("Cheniere and Bechtel reported that every train was completed ahead of its guaranteed date and within budget").** This has no source in the Sources list. **Fix:** add Cheniere Energy, "Cheniere Announces Substantial Completion of Train 6 at the Sabine Pass Liquefaction Project," February 2022, https://lngir.cheniere.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/238/cheniere-announces-substantial-completion-of-train-6-at-the (fact sheet source 15), and cite it in the text.

10. **ssec:2.4.5 ("The venture raised debt through a private bond offering anchored by PIMCO").** This is not supported. The October 21, 2025 announcement says Blue Owl raised the capital through a private securities offering, with PIMCO and select bond investors providing the debt. Holdings data show the issuer as Beignet Investor LLC, the Blue Owl side (fact sheet items 9–10). **Fix:** "Blue Owl's side of the venture raised its funding largely through a private bond offering anchored by PIMCO, maturing in 2049."

11. **sec:2.14, Solution to Exercise 2.12 (table and stem).** The stem says "Using only the facts given in this chapter", but the solution adds facts the chapter does not give, and one of them is unverified:
    - (a) Hyperion "Senior secured on the venture's assets": the sheet supports only "senior secured 144A notes" (medium confidence), and the collateral and issuer are not verified as the venture. Replace with "Senior secured notes; collateral not described in this chapter".
    - (b) Terra Operating notes "Unsecured notes of the holding company": this is correct (FY2015 10-K: "senior obligations of Terra Operating LLC ... guaranteed by Terra LLC and ... subsidiaries"), but the chapter text never says so. Either add "unsecured, guaranteed by Terra LLC and certain subsidiaries" to ssec:2.1.3, or change the cell to "Not secured on specific projects (holding-company notes)".
    - (c) Eurotunnel "Recourse to owner: None to governments, by treaty": this answers the wrong question, because the governments were not owners. Replace with "None to shareholders; no government funds or guarantees under the Treaty of Canterbury".

12. **ssec:2.4.5 ("at an estimated total cost of about USD 27 billion").** This is undated. The figure is the October 2025 estimate, and a July 2026 press report (UPI, single source, medium confidence) says Meta raised it above USD 50 billion. **Fix:** "at a total development cost then estimated at about USD 27 billion". Do not state the USD 50 billion figure as fact.

13. **Exhibit 2.2 source note ("Examples are illustrative descriptions, not specific transactions").** This is contradicted by the table. The structured-finance row ("Data-center venture with a value guarantee, US, 2025") is recognizably Hyperion, and the project-finance row is the book's fictional Llano Pardo. **Fix:** change the note to "Examples are generic illustrations, except the structured-finance row, which describes Meta's Hyperion venture (ssec:2.4.5), and the project finance row, which is the Chapter 1 case." Alternatively, make the data-center row generic, for example "Build-to-suit data-center vehicle, US".

14. **ssec:2.5.2 ("Michael Jensen called the loss of value ... an agency cost, and argued ... (Jensen 1986)").** This implies that the term and concept originate in the 1986 paper. Agency costs were defined by Jensen and Meckling (1976). Jensen (1986) contributed the free-cash-flow argument that debt reduces them. **Fix:** "Economists call that loss of value an agency cost (Jensen and Meckling 1976). Michael Jensen argued that debt reduces it because ... (Jensen 1986)." Add: Jensen, Michael C., and William H. Meckling. 1976. "Theory of the Firm: Managerial Behavior, Agency Costs and Ownership Structure." *Journal of Financial Economics* 3 (4): 305–360. In the Jensen 1986 entry, add "(Papers and Proceedings)" after the issue number.

15. **D-011 / standards §9: market norms stated without an "indicative" label, market and period.** Each of the following needs one of the two fixes below.
    - (a) ssec:2.6.1: "a loan that will take a year to close and 15 or more years to repay."
    - (b) ssec:2.6.1, after Example 2.4: "a sponsor pays roughly a percentage point a year, and waits roughly a year longer". This generalizes an illustrative example into a market claim.
    - (c) sec:2.12: "a sponsor pays about a percentage point a year, and waits about a year longer".
    - (d) Example 2.3: "GLA's facility agreements, like most, define net debt to leave out non-recourse debt". "Most" is unsourced; the writer flags it too.

    **Fix:** for (a) to (c), either tie the statement to the example ("in the Alto Huelén example, the sponsor pays about ...") or label it "indicative, contracted renewables in OECD and Latin American bank markets, 2022–2025" and have the domain reviewer sign it off. For (d), change "like most" to "like many".

16. **sec:2.14, Solution to Exercise 2.3 ("shared through a reference ground baseline").** This is not a recognized contract term. Standard practice is a geotechnical baseline report (GBR). The FIDIC Emerald Book (2019) builds risk sharing on a Geotechnical Baseline Report. **Fix:** "shared through a geotechnical baseline report, which fixes the ground conditions the contractor prices; worse ground is the owner's risk."
