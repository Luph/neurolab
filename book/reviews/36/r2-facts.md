## New defects

1. **Line 410 (tail view).** The text reads "Where ... the contract carries no volume risk (availability PPPs, Comber's feed-in tariff, the Gulf contracts above)". Two of the three examples carry volume risk:
   - Comber Wind's Ontario FIT contracts pay per MWh generated, so the project bears wind-resource volume risk (t-market-norms item 14).
   - Sakaka Solar, one of "the Gulf contracts above", is a solar PPA, which carries irradiance volume risk.

   Only availability PPPs, and capacity-based water and power contracts such as Rabigh 3, fit "no volume risk". **Fix:** "Where the payer is investment grade or state-backed and the contract fixes the price and removes demand risk (availability PPPs, Comber's feed-in tariff, the Gulf contracts above), tails of about six months to two years are accepted."

2. **Line 614.** The text says a 20-year tenor is "unavailable from a US commercial bank club in 2023 to 2026, which lends seven-year mini-perms instead". The fact sheets show seven-year bank mini-perms only for US LNG (Port Arthur, Rio Grande). They give no tenor evidence for US contracted-renewables term debt either way. The sentence turns two LNG deals into a claim about all US bank lending. **Fix:** "A 20-year tenor is available from an Indian state lender, while the US bank clubs that financed LNG export plants in 2023 lent seven-year mini-perms."

3. **Exh 36.6 note, line 395.** The note says geothermal falls under the CCSU, "which caps ECA repayment terms at 22 years". t-oecd-arrangement item 29 says maximum terms are set sector by sector in CCSU Appendix I: most sectors get 22 years, some sub-types 18. The 22-year cap for geothermal is not verified. **Fix:** "...under the climate change sector understanding ..., which allows ECA repayment terms of up to 22 years depending on the sector listed in its Appendix I, so beyond that maximum the ECA tranche could not follow...". Alternatively, verify geothermal's term in Appendix I and cite it.

4. **Solution 36.18(c), line 1125.** "Without a published worst-year record for UK onshore wind" claims that no such record exists anywhere. That is unverified, and probably wrong given published UK wind statistics. The round-1 text ("No fact sheet records...") was accurate. **Fix:** "This book has no verified worst-year record for UK onshore wind, so the one-year P99 (factor 0.777) is the defensible proxy...".
