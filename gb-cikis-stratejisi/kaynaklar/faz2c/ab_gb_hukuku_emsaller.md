## Q1. CU constraints on Türkiye while still in the CU

### 1a. Ankara Agreement (CELEX 21964A1229(01)), English text OJ L 361, 31.12.1977, p. 29 (orig. OJ 217, 29.12.1964, p. 3687)
- EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:21964A1229(01)
- Read from Cellar English PDF manifestation: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/ba1c2608-a962-42b6-b40a-eba7378e7c55.0008.03/DOC_1

- **Art 7** [VERIFIED]: "The Contracting Parties shall take all appropriate measures, whether general or particular, to ensure the fulfilment of the obligations arising from this Agreement. They shall refrain from any measures liable to jeopardize the attainment of the objectives of this Agreement."
  - Note: this is a loyalty/good-faith clause modelled on ex-Art 5 EEC (now Art 4(3) TEU). It is binding on BOTH parties; it is the textual hook the EU would invoke against Turkish acts "liable to jeopardize" CU objectives (e.g., deliberate de-alignment while still in the CU). Preparatory planning, studies, contingency legislation that does not enter into application do not obviously "jeopardize" objectives; measures that change applied tariffs or trade rules do. [PARTIAL – inference]
- **Art 2(1)** [VERIFIED]: aim "to promote the continuous and balanced strengthening of trade and economic relations between the Parties, while taking full account of the need to ensure an accelerated development of the Turkish economy..."
- **Art 21** [VERIFIED]: Parties "agree to work out a consultation procedure in order to ensure coordination of their commercial policies towards third countries and mutual respect for their interests in this field, inter alia in the event of subsequent accession to or association with the Community, by third countries."
- **Art 22** [VERIFIED]: (1) "In order to attain the objectives of this Agreement the Council of Association shall have the power to take decisions in the cases provided for therein. Each of the Parties shall take the measures necessary to implement the decisions taken. The Council of Association may also make appropriate recommendations." (2) periodic review of the functioning of the Association. (3) once the transitional stage has been embarked on, the Council "shall adopt appropriate decisions where ... attainment of an objective of this Agreement calls for joint action by the Contracting Parties but the requisite powers are not granted in this Agreement."
- **Art 23** [VERIFIED]: "The Council of Association shall act unanimously." (=> Türkiye has a veto on any new Association Council decision, incl. any amendment/termination of Decision 1/95 by decision; equally the EU has a veto.)
- **Art 24** [VERIFIED]: Association Council may set up committees (basis for Customs Cooperation Committee, Decision 2/69, and the Customs Union Joint Committee, Dec 1/95 Art 52).
- **Art 25** [VERIFIED]: (1) "The Contracting Parties may submit to the Council of Association any dispute relating to the application or interpretation of this Agreement which concerns the Community, a Member State of the Community, or Turkey." (2) "The Council of Association may settle the dispute by decision; it may also decide to submit the dispute to the Court of Justice of the European Communities or to any other existing court or tribunal." (3) "Each Party shall be required to take the measures necessary to comply with such decisions." (4) Where the dispute cannot be settled under para 2, the Council of Association "shall determine, in accordance with Article 8 of this Agreement, the detailed rules for arbitration or for any other judicial procedure..."
  - Practical meaning: no automatic, compulsory third-party adjudication under the Ankara Agreement; every step requires a unanimous Association Council decision. The only compulsory arbitration is the narrow Dec 1/95 Art 61 route (scope/duration of protection, safeguard, rebalancing measures). [VERIFIED text; PARTIAL interpretation]
- **Art 26** [VERIFIED]: "This Agreement shall not apply to products within the province of the European Coal and Steel Community." (=> steel/coal trade is regulated by the separate 1996 ECSC–Turkey FTA; see Q2.)
- **Arts 31–33** [VERIFIED]: ratification, entry into force, authentic languages (Dutch, French, German, Italian, Turkish). **No denunciation/termination clause** in the Ankara Agreement (text searched for "terminat", "denounc", "withdraw": no relevant hit). Same for the Additional Protocol (1970) and Decision 1/95. => Exit would rely on general international law (VCLT Art 54 (consent) / Art 56 (denunciation of a treaty with no termination clause: only if intended/implied; 12 months' notice) / Art 62 (fundamental change of circumstances)) or a negotiated Association Council decision (unanimity). [VERIFIED absence of clause; legal consequence PARTIAL]

### Additional Protocol (23.11.1970), CELEX 21970A1123(01), OJ L 293, 29.12.1972, p. 4
- EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:21970A1123(01) ; read via Cellar http://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/71555c02-fbc0-4033-bcf6-50a539812752.0005.01/DOC_1
- **Art 60** [VERIFIED] (safeguard, confirmed by Dec 1/95 Art 63): (1) Türkiye may take "the necessary protective measures" "If serious disturbances occur in a sector of the Turkish economy or prejudice its external financial stability, or if difficulties arise which adversely affect the economic situation in a region of Turkey"; Association Council notified immediately. (2) mirror right for the Community. (3) "preference shall be given to those which will least disturb the functioning of the Association. These measures shall not exceed what is strictly necessary to remedy the difficulties that have arisen." (4) consultations may take place.
  - Relevance: Art 60(1) is the only lawful unilateral route for Türkiye to protect a sector/region or its external financial stability while still in the CU; it is notification-based (not prior authorisation). But it triggers EU rebalancing rights (Dec 1/95 Art 64) and Art 61 arbitration on scope/duration.
- **Art 47** [VERIFIED]: intra-Association anti-dumping modalities (recommendations by Association Council; protective measures by injured party after 3 months or if dumping continues); kept in force by Dec 1/95 Art 44(2).

### 1b. Decision No 1/95 of the EC-Turkey Association Council of 22 December 1995 (96/142/EC), OJ L 35, 13.2.1996, p. 1 (CELEX 21996D0213(01)); entered into force 31 Dec 1995 (Art 65(1))
- EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:21996D0213(01) ; read via Cellar http://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/d22fdf4c-0268-473c-97c8-dbc6fdd7e799.0008.01/DOC_1
- Scope: Chapter I (free movement & commercial policy) applies to industrial goods (Art 2: products other than agricultural products as defined in Art 11 Ankara Agreement); ECSC products are outside (Ankara Art 26).
- **Art 3** [VERIFIED]: goods "coming from third countries and in free circulation in the Community or in Turkey" are covered; free circulation = import formalities complied with and duties levied, no total/partial reimbursement (drawback). (=> the A.TR / free-circulation mechanism; CU covers non-originating goods.)
- **Art 4** [VERIFIED]: abolition of import/export duties and CEEs between the parties; "shall refrain from introducing any new customs duties on imports or exports or any charges having equivalent effect". **Arts 5–6**: QRs and MEEs on imports/exports prohibited. **Art 7**: usual public-policy/health etc. exceptions, not to be "arbitrary discrimination or a disguised restriction".
- **Art 8** [VERIFIED]: (1) "Within five years ... Turkey shall incorporate into its internal legal order the Community instruments relating to the removal of technical barriers to trade." (2) list and conditions to be laid down by Association Council decision within one year (=> Decision 2/97). (4) "The Parties stress the importance of effective cooperation between them in the fields of standardization, metrology and calibration, quality, accreditation, testing and certification."
- **Art 9** [VERIFIED]: once Türkiye has put into force the Community instruments for a product, "trade in that product between the Parties shall take place in accordance with the conditions laid down by those instruments" (=> basis of mutual acceptance of conformity assessment, Turkish notified bodies).
- **Art 10** [VERIFIED]: interim duty on Türkiye not to impede EU-certified products; safeguard procedure via Customs Union Joint Committee (CUJC). **Art 11** [VERIFIED]: interim acceptance by the Community of Turkish conformity-assessment results "provided that those procedures are in conformity with the requirements in force in the Community".
- **Art 12(1)** [VERIFIED]: "Turkey shall, in relation to countries which are not members of the Community, apply provisions and implementing measures which are substantially similar to those of the Community's commercial policy set out in the following Regulations": common rules for imports (3285/94, 519/94), quota administration (520/94), anti-dumping and anti-subsidy (3283/94, 3284/94), Trade Barriers Regulation (3286/94), common rules for exports (2603/69), export credits (Decision 93/112/EEC), textiles regs. (2) textiles: "substantially the same commercial policy as the Community" under GATT Art XXIV.
- **Art 13** [VERIFIED]: "(1) Upon the date of entry into force of this Decision, Turkey shall, in relation to countries which are not members of the Community, align itself on the Common Customs Tariff. (2) Turkey shall adjust its customs tariff whenever necessary to take account of changes in the Common Customs Tariff."
- **Art 14** [VERIFIED]: (1) Türkiye to be informed in time of CCT changes; prior consultations in CUJC. (2) CUJC may grant time for alignment, but "Under no circumstances may the Customs Union Joint Committee authorize Turkey to apply a customs tariff which is lower than the Common Customs Tariff for any product." (3) If Türkiye "wishes to suspend on temporary basis or resume duties other than as envisaged in paragraph 1, Turkey will make a prompt notification to the Community"; consultations in CUJC.
  - Note: Art 14(2) bars a LOWER Turkish tariff; Art 15 allowed HIGHER duties only until 1.1.2001. Türkiye's "additional customs duties" (ilave gümrük vergisi) on third-country goods are higher-than-CCT duties: a long-running EU complaint (see Commission reports) but not lower-than-CCT. [VERIFIED text; PARTIAL on practice]
- **Art 15** [VERIFIED]: derogation allowing higher-than-CCT duties "until 1 January 2001" for agreed products (expired).
- **Art 16** [VERIFIED]: (1) "With a view to harmonizing its commercial policy with that of the Community, Turkey shall align itself progressively with the preferential customs regime of the Community within five years ... This alignment will concern both the autonomous regimes and preferential agreements with third countries. To this end, Turkey will take the necessary measures and negotiate agreements on mutually advantageous basis with the countries concerned." (2) "the granting of these tariff preferences shall be conditional on compl[i]ance with provisions relating to the origin of products identical to those governing the granting of such preferences by the Community." (3) compensatory levy mechanism (during the 5-year period) if Türkiye's tariff policy differs: trade deflection test (5 pp duty differential, "important distortion of traffic").
  - Statements by Türkiye on Art 16 [VERIFIED]: (i) Türkiye "may seek consultation within the Association Council regarding the obligations that may arise for it as the result of its membership of the Economic Cooperation Organization (ECO)"; (ii) priority list of FTAs (CEECs, Israel, Baltics, Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt).
  - Annex 10 lists the autonomous regimes and preferential agreements referred to in Art 16.
- **Art 28** [VERIFIED]: Türkiye to adopt customs-law provisions based on the Community Customs Code (origin, valuation, free circulation, procedures, customs debt, appeal); (3) "The Customs Cooperation Committee shall lay down the appropriate measures to implement paragraphs 1 and 2" (=> Decision 1/2006 "bridging legislation"; COM(2026) 477).
- **Art 31 + Annex 8** [VERIFIED]: IP protection equivalence.
- **Competition/State aid**:
  - **Art 32** (cartels) / **Art 33** (abuse of dominance) [VERIFIED]: prohibited "as incompatible with the proper functioning of the Customs Union, in so far as they may affect trade between the Community and Turkey".
  - **Art 34(1)** [VERIFIED]: "Any aid granted by Member States of the Community or by Turkey through State resources in any form whatsoever which distorts or threatens to distort competition by favouring certain undertakings or the production of certain goods shall, in so far as it affects trade between the Community and Turkey, be incompatible with the proper functioning of the Customs Union." (2)–(3) compatibility categories (social aid, natural disasters; regional aid where living standard abnormally low; IPCEI; "aid to facilitate the development of certain economic activities or of certain economic areas, where such aid does not adversely affect trading conditions ... to an extent contrary to the common interest"; time-limited 5-yr derogations for less-developed regions and structural adjustment).
  - **Art 35** [VERIFIED]: practices contrary to Arts 32–34 "shall be assessed on the basis of criteria arising from the application of the rules of Articles 85, 86 and 92 of the Treaty establishing the European Community and its secondary legislation" (=> EU State-aid acquis is the yardstick).
  - **Art 37** [VERIFIED]: implementing rules to be adopted within two years; "(2) Until these rules are adopted, ... (b) the provisions of the GATT Subsidies Code shall be applied as the rules for the implementation of Article 34." (The implementing rules on State aid have, to the best of available knowledge, never been adopted, so the WTO SCM standard is the operative implementing rule. [PARTIAL – not verified in this session])
  - **Art 38** [VERIFIED]: if a practice is incompatible with Arts 32–34 and not adequately dealt with, and "causes or threatens to cause serious prejudice ... or material injury to its domestic industry, it may take appropriate measures after consultation within the Joint Customs Union Committee or after 45 working days following referral". (2) For Art 34 practices, measures "where the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade applies thereto, only be adopted in conformity with the procedures and under the conditions laid down by the [GATT/WTO]". (=> EU countervailing duties against Turkish subsidies are WTO-disciplined.)
  - **Art 39** [VERIFIED]: Türkiye to make competition law compatible with EU law; adapt aid schemes to Community frameworks; (2)(f) "notify the Community in advance of any individual aid ... that would be notifiable under Community frameworks or guidelines had it been granted by a Member State, or of individual aid awards outside of Community frameworks or guidelines above an amount of ECU 12 million"; (4) the Community "shall have the right to raise objections against an aid granted by Turkey which it would have deemed unlawful under EC law"; if not resolved within 30 days, "the Community and Turkey shall each have the right to refer the case to arbitration".
  - **Art 41** public undertakings; **Art 42** State monopolies.
  - **Art 43** [VERIFIED]: positive comity: a party may notify anti-competitive activities on the other's territory and request enforcement; "Nothing in this Article limits the discretion of the notified Party ..." (it is a cooperation clause, not a State-aid clause).
  - => **Local-content subsidies**: an aid conditional on use of domestic over imported goods would be (i) a prohibited subsidy under WTO SCM Art 3.1(b) (applied via Dec 1/95 Art 37(2)(b)), (ii) incompatible under Art 34/35 (EU State-aid criteria), (iii) discriminatory against EU goods (GATT III / Dec 1/95 Art 50 on internal taxation). Türkiye's existing local-content-type schemes (e.g., in public procurement/YEKA, medical/pharma localisation) are already EU complaints. [Legal analysis PARTIAL]
- **Trade defence between the parties**:
  - **Art 44** [VERIFIED]: (1) Association Council may review application of TDIs "other than safeguard" between the parties, and "may decide to suspend" them "provided that Turkey has implemented competition, State aid control and other relevant parts of the acquis ... and ensured their effective enforcement". (Never suspended -> both sides still apply AD/AS to each other.) (2) Additional Protocol Art 47 AD modalities remain in force.
  - **Art 45** [VERIFIED]: CU consultation/decision procedures "shall not apply to trade defence measures taken by either Party"; endeavour to coordinate TDIs towards third countries.
  - **Art 46** [VERIFIED]: "By derogation from the principle of the free movement of goods ... where one Party has taken or is taking anti-dumping measures or other measures pursuant to trade policy instruments as referred to in Article 44 in its relations with the other Party or with third countries, that Party may make imports of the products concerned from the territory of the other Party subject to the application of those measures. In such cases it shall inform the Customs Union Joint Committee accordingly." (=> Each party already runs its own TDI system and can apply its third-country TDIs to goods coming via the other party. Türkiye's own TDI apparatus is therefore already "exit-ready".)
  - **Art 47** [VERIFIED]: origin indication on import declaration for products subject to trade-policy measures.
- **Consultation/decision-making**:
  - **Art 52** [VERIFIED]: CUJC set up under Ankara Art 24; (2) "The Parties shall consult within the Committee on any point relating to the implementation of this Decision which gives rise to a difficulty for either of them."
  - **Art 54** [VERIFIED]: (1) Turkish legislation "shall be harmonized as far as possible with Community legislation" in areas of direct relevance; (2) such areas = "commercial policy and agreements with third countries comprising a commercial dimension for industrial products, legislation on the abolition of technical barriers to trade in industrial products, competition and industrial and intellectual property law and customs legislation."
  - **Art 55** [VERIFIED]: Commission to "informally consult Turkish experts"; good-faith cooperation (4).
  - **Art 56** [VERIFIED]: Community informs Türkiye of new legislation "to allow Turkey to adopt corresponding legislation"; (2) CUJC to find a "mutually acceptable solution" if Türkiye has a problem.
  - **Art 57** [VERIFIED]: (1) Türkiye's right to amend legislation in areas of direct relevance is preserved "provided the Customs Union Joint Committee has concluded that the amended legislation does not affect the proper functioning of the Customs Union or that the procedures referred to in the paragraphs 2 to 4 of this Article have been accomplished." (2) "Where Turkey is contemplating new legislation in an area of direct relevance ... it shall informally seek the views of the Commission"; (3) consultations in CUJC once drafting is advanced; (4) "Where Turkey adopts legislation ... it shall forthwith inform the Community"; if likely to disrupt, CUJC "shall endeavour to find a mutually acceptable solution."
    - => **Procedural trap**: drafting exit-contingency legislation in CU-relevant areas (tariff, trade policy, TBT, competition, customs) technically engages the Art 57(2) duty to "informally seek the views of the Commission". Purely internal studies, capacity-building, or legislation outside the Art 54(2) list (e.g., services, investment, procurement, agriculture) do not. Contingency laws with deferred/conditional entry into force are a grey zone. [PARTIAL – inference]
- **Protection / safeguard / rebalancing / disputes (EU responses if Türkiye deviates)**:
  - **Art 58(1)** [VERIFIED]: if no solution after Art 56(2)/57(4) consultations and a party "considers that discrepancies in the legislation in question may affect the free movement of goods, deflect trade or create economic problems on its territory", referral to CUJC to "recommend appropriate ways of avoiding any injury". Same for "differences in the implementation of legislations".
  - **Art 58(2)** [VERIFIED]: "If discrepancies between Community and Turkish legislation or differences in their implementation in an area of direct relevance to the functioning of the Customs Union, cause o[r] threaten to cause impairment of the free movement of goods or deflections of trade and the affected Party considers that immediate action is required, it may itself take the necessary protection measures and notify the Customs Union Joint Committee thereof; the latter may decide whether to amend or abolish these measures. Priority should be given to measures which least disturb the functioning of the Customs Union." (=> the EU's main unilateral weapon against Turkish de-alignment: e.g., re-imposing origin controls / suspending free-circulation treatment for goods affected by trade deflection.)
  - **Art 61** [VERIFIED]: without prejudice to Ankara Art 25(1)–(3), if the Association Council fails within six months to settle a dispute "relating to the scope or duration of protection measures taken in accordance with Article 58 (2), safeguard measures taken in accordance with Article 63 or rebalancing measures taken in accordance with Article 64", either party may refer it to arbitration (Art 62: 3 arbitrators, umpire from Association Council list, sits in Brussels). "The arbitration award shall be binding on the Parties to the dispute." (Note: arbitration covers only the scope/duration of such measures, not the underlying legality of Turkish de-alignment.)
  - **Art 63** [VERIFIED]: "The Parties confirm that the mechanism and modalities of safeguard measures provided for in Article 60 of the Additional Protocol remain valid."
  - **Art 64** [VERIFIED]: "(1) If a safeguard or protection measure taken by a Party creates an imbalance between the rights and obligations under this Decision, the other Party may take rebalancing measures in respect of that Party. Priority shall be given to such measures as will least disturb the functioning of the Customs Union. (2) The procedures provided for in Article 63 shall apply."
  - **Art 66** [VERIFIED]: provisions identical in substance to EC Treaty provisions "shall be interpreted ... in conformity with the relevant decisions of the Court of Justice".
  - **Annex 7** [VERIFIED]: mutual assistance between administrative authorities in customs matters (definitions Art 1; scope Art 2: assist each other "in ensuring that customs legislation is correctly applied, in particular by the prevention, detection and investigation of operations in breach of that legislation"). This customs MAA is part of Decision 1/95 itself (see Q2).
  - No termination clause; Art 65 only on entry into force (31.12.1995).

#### Summary: what Türkiye may NOT do unilaterally while in the CU (industrial goods)
1. Apply a tariff lower than the CCT on any product (Arts 13, 14(2)); cut MFN duties unilaterally (e.g., a "Turkish Global Tariff" in force) – breach. Higher-than-CCT duties: Art 15 window closed in 2001; additional duties are contested but not expressly barred by Art 14(2) (EU treats them as contrary to Art 13). [VERIFIED text; practice PARTIAL]
2. Conclude/apply FTAs giving preferences not "aligned" with the EU's preferential regime, or with origin rules different from the EU's (Art 16(1)–(2)); negotiating with countries the EU already has FTAs with is mandated; negotiating with others (e.g., an FTA with a country the EU has none with) is not expressly prohibited for negotiation, but granting tariff preferences to a non-EU-FTA partner would deviate from the CCT (Art 13) unless the EU agrees. [VERIFIED text; inference PARTIAL]
3. Introduce intra-CU duties/QRs/MEEs (Arts 4–6); impede EU products certified under EU directives/regulations (Arts 9–10).
4. Adopt CU-relevant legislation (trade policy, TBT, competition, IP, customs) without the Art 57(2)–(4) informal consultation/notification steps.
5. Grant State aid that distorts EU–Türkiye trade (Art 34), incl. unnotified individual aid above the ECU 12m threshold (Art 39(2)(f)); local-content subsidies (WTO SCM Art 3.1(b) via Art 37(2)(b)).
6. Depart from the EU's commercial-policy regulations (Art 12) – e.g., own export controls, own import surveillance/quotas inconsistent with EU's.

#### EU's legal responses (graduated)
- Consultation in CUJC (Art 52(2)); referral and recommendation (Art 58(1)).
- Unilateral protection measures with notification (Art 58(2)) – e.g., treating goods from Türkiye as non-free-circulation / requiring origin proof; reimposing duties on deflected goods.
- Safeguards under Additional Protocol Art 60 (Dec 1/95 Art 63) and rebalancing under Art 64.
- Arbitration only on scope/duration of those measures (Arts 61–62).
- Anti-dumping/countervailing duties (Arts 44–46, 38(2) – WTO-disciplined), which the EU already uses against Turkish steel etc.
- Outside the CU texts: EU Anti-Coercion Instrument (if Turkish measures are coercive) and Enforcement Regulation 654/2014 (to enforce rulings / respond to breaches of international trade agreements) – see Q9.
- Politically: freezing high-level dialogue, IPA funds, visa liberalisation, modernisation talks (not legal remedies).

## Q10. Revised PEM rules of origin (Decision No 1/2023 of the PEM Joint Committee of 7 Dec 2023, OJ L, 2024/390, 19.2.2024; CELEX 22024D0390)
- EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec/2024/390/oj ; read via Cellar XHTML http://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/2371884d-cecb-11ee-b9d9-01aa75ed71a1.0006.03/DOC_1
- Decision Art 1(2) [VERIFIED]: "The amendments to the Convention shall enter into force on 1 January 2025." Recital (1): Convention signed 15 June 2011, in force 1 January 2012.
- Transitional parallel application of 2012 rules during 1.1.–31.12.2025 by PEM JC Decision No 2/2024 (OJ L 2025/17, 9.1.2025, per Commission matrix footnote 4) [VERIFIED via matrix/COM(2026) 477 recital 8].

### General rules (Appendix I as amended)
- **Tolerance – Art 5(1)** [VERIFIED]: non-originating materials otherwise excluded by the list rule may be used if their total "does not exceed: (a) 15 % of the net weight of the product falling within Chapters 2 and 4 to 24, other than processed fishery products of Chapter 16; (b) 15 % of the ex-works price of the product for products other than those covered by point (a)." Not applicable to Chapters 50–63 (textile tolerances of Introductory Notes 6 and 7 apply instead). Art 5(2): tolerance cannot be used to exceed any max-NOM % in the list rule. (Old 2012 PEM tolerance was 10 %.)
- **Cumulation – Art 7** [VERIFIED]: (1) diagonal cumulation of originating materials of any Contracting Party (beyond minimal operations); (3) **full cumulation**: "with the exclusion of products falling within Chapters 50 to 63 ..., working or processing carried out in a Contracting Party other than the exporting Contracting Party shall be considered as having been carried out in the exporting Contracting Party when the products obtained undergo subsequent working or processing in that exporting Contracting Party." (4) for Ch 50–63 only bilateral full cumulation with the importing Party; (5) parties may opt in to extend full cumulation to Ch 50–63 (Annex VIII list). Art 8(1): cumulation only where a GATT XXIV FTA applies between all parties involved AND identical rules of origin ("variable geometry").
- **Drawback – Art 16** [VERIFIED]: "(1) Non-originating materials used in the manufacture of products falling within Chapters 50 to 63 of the Harmonised System ... shall not be subject in the exporting Contracting Party to drawback of, or exemption from, customs duties of whatever kind." => **drawback prohibition now limited to textiles/clothing (Ch 50–63)**; drawback/inward processing is permitted for all other chapters (cars, machinery, steel, plastics, furniture). Exceptions even for Ch 50–63: (4) bilateral cumulation cases under Art 7(4)/(5); (5) "shall not apply in bilateral trade between on the one hand Switzerland (including Liechtenstein), Iceland, Norway, Türkiye or the European Union and, on the other hand, any participant in the Barcelona process other than Türkiye and Israel if the products are considered as originating ... without application of cumulation"; (6) Agadir parties.

## Q9. EU Anti-Coercion Instrument and Enforcement Regulation – triggers

### Regulation (EU) 2023/2675 of 22 November 2023 (ACI), OJ L, 2023/2675, 7.12.2023 (in force 20 days after publication = 27.12.2023) [VERIFIED; https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2675/oj]
- Art 2(1): "economic coercion exists where a third country applies or threatens to apply a third-country measure affecting trade or investment in order to prevent or obtain the cessation, modification or adoption of a particular act by the Union or a Member State, thereby interfering in the legitimate sovereign choices of the Union or a Member State."
- Art 2(2) factors: (a) intensity, severity, frequency, duration, breadth, magnitude; (b) pattern of interference; (c) encroachment on sovereignty; (d) "whether the third country is acting on the basis of a legitimate concern that is internationally recognised"; (e) prior good-faith attempts at international coordination or adjudication.
- Art 3(2) "particular act" includes "an expression of a position" by an EU/Member State institution.
- Art 8(1): response measures only if engagement failed, necessary, and in the Union interest. Annex I measures include "re-establishment of customs duties at the most-favoured-nation level ... which may amount, as necessary, to the non-performance of applicable international obligations" (i.e., suspension of CU preferences as countermeasure), import/export restrictions, procurement exclusion, services, FDI, IP, financial services, chemicals/sanitary market access, etc.
- **Turkish pre-exit actions that could trigger the ACI** [PARTIAL – analysis]: explicitly conditioning trade/investment measures (e.g., threatened tariffs, import bans, procurement exclusions, export restrictions on EU firms) on EU or Member-State political acts (visa liberalisation, Cyprus/Eastern Mediterranean, accession-chapter decisions, sanctions positions), or targeting a single Member State (e.g., Greece, Cyprus) with trade measures to change its policy. **Not** coercion: WTO-consistent diversification, negotiating FTAs, contingency planning, exit itself or a negotiated exit, legitimate safeguards/TDIs, measures pursuing internationally recognised concerns with good-faith prior consultation. Keep messaging de-linked from political demands.

### Regulation (EU) No 654/2014 (Enforcement Regulation), OJ L 189, 27.6.2014, p. 50, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2021/167 (OJ L 49, 12.2.2021, p. 1; in force 13 Feb 2021) [VERIFIED]
- Art 3 (scope), original: applies "(a) following the adjudication of trade disputes under the WTO [DSU], when the Union has been authorised to suspend concessions ...; (b) following the adjudication of trade disputes under other international trade agreements, including regional or bilateral agreements, when the Union has the right to suspend concessions or other obligations under such agreements; (c) for the rebalancing of concessions or other obligations, to which the application of a safeguard measure by a third country may give right pursuant to Article 8 of the WTO Agreement on Safeguards, or to the provisions on safeguards included in other international trade agreements, including regional or bilateral agreements; (d) in cases of modification of concessions by a WTO member under Article XXVIII of the GATT 1994, where no compensatory adjustments have been agreed."

- **Turkish pre-exit actions that could trigger it** [PARTIAL – analysis]: (i) a WTO-inconsistent Turkish measure (e.g., local-content/localisation) where Türkiye "appeals into the void" -> Art 3(aa); (ii) Turkish protection/safeguard measures under the CU (Add. ## Decision-relevant synthesis: what Türkiye can lawfully and quietly do before exit, and what would backfire

**Lawful, low-visibility, no-regret (CU-consistent):**
1. Bank the EU's pending "plumbing" upgrades now: bring the AEO MRA (CCC Decision 1/2026) into force; push adoption of the COM(2026) 477 bridging decision (e-A.TR, PEM alignment, drawback); ensure the agri (OJ L 2026/1903) and ECSC (OJ L 2026/359) dynamic-PEM protocols are applied. These reflect standard EU templates (AEO MRAs with US/China/Japan/UK; PEM protocols with all PEM partners) and are therefore re-usable in a post-CU FTA. The Swiss precedent shows the EU freezes dynamic updates once it perceives strategic drift.
2. Complete Türkiye's revised-PEM network: Türkiye still applies 2012 rules with Israel, Syria, Tunisia and has no cumulation partner status with Algeria, Jordan, Lebanon, Ukraine in the matrix. Upgrading bilateral protocols is fully consistent with Dec 1/95 Art 16 and directly raises post-exit diagonal-cumulation value (EU–PEM–Türkiye).
3. Keep the multilateral customs instruments Türkiye holds in its own name: CTC/NCTS (since 1.12.2012), SAD Convention, PEM Convention (since 1.2.2014) – all survive exit (UK precedent: joined CTC in own right 1.1.2021).
4. Protect conformity-assessment continuity: keep TÜRKAK's EA MLA status (eligibility not CU-dependent: candidate + Council of Europe member; UKAS precedent) by maintaining Reg. 765/2008-aligned accreditation law; encourage Turkish CABs to establish/obtain EU27-designated subsidiaries now (UK precedent: BSI NL 2797 etc.), since Turkish NBs' status depends entirely on Dec 1/95 Art 9 + Assoc. Council Decision 1/2006 (2006/654/EC) and would end with the CU (UK notice 22.1.2018 logic).
5. Build in-house capacity without adopting measures: shadow MFN tariff modelling (UKGT method: banding, nuisance-tariff removal, 47% zero lines), trade-remedy transition review lists (UK: 43 of 106 EU measures kept), continuity-agreement templates (incorporation by reference vs. replication; UK–Türkiye FTA Art 1.1(2) "preserve preferential conditions" + incorporation of Dec 1/95 agri components) – internal studies do not trigger Dec 1/95 Art 57(2) until "contemplating new legislation".
6. Negotiate FTAs with EU FTA partners (expressly mandated by Dec 1/95 Art 16(1)); invoke the Commission's own diagnosis of "FTA asymmetry" (SWD(2016) 475) and the EU–Chile "Declaration regarding Turkey" model to request EU support – this is CU-loyal and increases the exit payoff.

**What would backfire (legal exposure):**
7. Applying lower-than-CCT tariffs or non-aligned FTA preferences while in the CU (Dec 1/95 Arts 13–14(2), 16) -> EU Art 58(2) protection measures (e.g., denial of free-circulation/A.TR treatment, origin controls) and Art 64 rebalancing; Ankara Art 7 loyalty breach.
8. Local-content/localisation subsidies or conditions: incompatible with Dec 1/95 Art 34/35 and WTO (SCM 3.1(b), GATT III:4, TRIMs) – the EU already won DS583 (2022 Art 25 award; Türkiye complied 25.4.2023); repeat measures invite WTO litigation and, if Türkiye blocks adjudication, Enforcement Regulation 654/2014 Art 3(aa)/(ba) countermeasures.
9. Linking trade measures to political asks (visa, Cyprus, sanctions) or targeting a Member State: fits ACI Art 2(1) "economic coercion"; Annex I allows MFN re-imposition, procurement exclusion, FDI/services/IP restrictions, even "non-performance" of CU obligations as countermeasures.
10. Unilateral safeguard/protection measures as a bargaining chip (Add. Protocol Art 60; Dec 1/95 Art 58(2)) -> EU rebalancing (Dec 1/95 Art 64; Enforcement Reg. Art 3(c)); stalling the Art 61–62 arbitration -> Enforcement Reg. Art 3(ba). Also: escalating "additional duties" or TAREKS-type controls during the pre-exit phase erodes goodwill needed for a negotiated successor FTA, and the ECSC FTA's strict State-aid clause (Art 7) remains a lever on steel.
