# Extracts: Task 2 post-exit / unilateral tariff regimes -- collected 18 Oct 2026

## UK Global Tariff (announced 19 May 2020, in force 1 Jan 2021)
- Gov.uk: removes tariffs on GBP30bn of imports entering UK supply chains; tariffs <2% removed (just under 500 lines); rates rounded down to bands; ~6,000 lines changed; Meursing table dropped. https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-global-tariff-backs-uk-businesses-and-consumers
- Share of tariff-free lines: 47% vs 27% under EU CET (IBISWorld; Hansard minister "a little under 50%").
- UKTPO (20 May 2020) https://blogs.sussex.ac.uk/uktpo/2020/05/20/new-tariff-on-the-block-what-is-in-the-uks-global-tariff/ : duty-free share of imports from MFN countries 51.5% (EU CET) -> 70.3% (UKGT); only ~44% of EU imports would be tariff free under UKGT if no FTA; UKGT far less liberal than no-deal schedule (~95% of MFN imports duty-free).
## No-deal temporary tariff regime
- 13 Mar 2019: 87% of total imports by value tariff-free for up to 12 months (vs ~80% at the time); tariffs kept on beef, lamb, pork, poultry, some dairy, cars, etc.; no tariffs on goods crossing Ireland->NI. 8 Oct 2019 revision: 88%; extra clothing lines; exceptional review process. https://www.gov.uk/government/news/temporary-tariff-regime-updated ; https://www.euronews.com/2019/10/08/britain-says-88-percent-of-imports-to-face-no-tariffs-in-event-of-no-deal-brexit ; UKTPO 14 Mar 2019 https://blogs.sussex.ac.uk/uktpo/2019/03/14/what-should-we-make-of-the-uks-no-deal-tariffs/
## Brexit consumer-price evidence
- Breinlich, Leromain, Novy & Sampson, "The Brexit vote, inflation and UK living standards", International Economic Review 63(1):63-93 (2022): depreciation raised consumer prices 2.9%, cost GBP870/household/yr; aggregate ERPT 0.29. Earlier version: +1.7pp inflation within a year, GBP404/household (LSE blog Nov 2017). https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/111602
- Bakker, Datta, Davies & De Lyon, "Non-tariff barriers and consumer prices: evidence from Brexit", CEP DP 1888 (Dec 2022): TCA NTBs raised food prices ~6% (2020-21), pass-through 50-80%; households lost GBP5.84bn (~GBP210/household), producers gained GBP4.78bn, DWL GBP1.06bn; lowest-decile cost 52% higher than top. https://cep.lse.ac.uk/_new/publications/abstract.asp?index=9721 . Update (Warwick TWERP 1461, 2023): food prices +25pp Dec 2019-Mar 2023; without Brexit ~8pp (30%) smaller. https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/economics/research/workingpapers/2023/twerp_1461_-_datta.pdf
## Switzerland industrial tariff abolition (IZA), 1 Jan 2024
- SECO page: ex-ante welfare gain ~CHF860m; tariff numbers 9,114 -> 7,511. https://www.seco.admin.ch/en/industrial-tariffs-abolition
- SECO Grundlagen fuer die Wirtschaftspolitik No. 65 (29 Jan 2026) "Die Wirkungen des Industriezollabbaus auf Konsumentenpreise": 2022 industrial-goods tariff revenue CHF683.1m, collected on only ~15% of industrial imports (rest duty-free under FTAs); potential consumer savings 0.76% of consumer-goods spend (~CHF120/household); clothing/footwear AVE 2.9%, potential 3.8%. Measured (synthetic-control-type DiD vs European HICP): industrial goods prices -1.15% in first 12 months (PTR point est. 150.7%, 95% CI 13.7-287.7%); clothing/footwear -1.95% (PTR 50.9%, CI 13.6-88.1%); household saving ~CHF180/yr, total CHF724m; NO significant increase in CIF import prices (foreign exporters did not capture); survey: most firms' cost cut <=3%, ~14% passed on directly; reasons for incomplete pass-through: other input costs 28%, wages 24%, simultaneous VAT increase 15%; autos slow pass-through, independent importers gained from admin simplification. https://www.seco.admin.ch/dam/en/sd-web/tGlW1DJYcru5/wp_65_Wirkungen%20des%20Industriezollabbaus%20auf%20Konsumentenpreise.pdf  (raw copy: raw/seco/wp65.txt)
## Customs-union exit precedents
- Venezuela left CAN (notice Apr 2006; CAN tariff preferences phased out over 5 yrs to 22 Apr 2011; working group on transitional mechanism); had 4 years to adopt MERCOSUR CET after accession (USTR NTE 2007-2010). No dedicated tariff-redesign study found.
