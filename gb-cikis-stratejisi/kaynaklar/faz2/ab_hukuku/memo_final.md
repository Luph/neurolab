# MEMO: Controlled exit from the EU–Türkiye Customs Union (position at 18 Oct 2026)

Tags: [VERIFIED] = primary or official text read this session; [PARTIAL] = secondary source, official summary or my own analysis; [UNVERIFIED] = not confirmed. Sources (S#) at the end.

**Bottom line**
- No treaty provides for exit, so a lawful exit needs EU agreement. Cleanest technique: an Association Council (AC) standstill decision, then an EU-only Art 207 agreement under which the customs-union provisions "cease to have effect", as in Art 33.11 of the EU–Chile Interim Trade Agreement (ITA).
- Under Art 207 the Council votes by qualified majority (QMV), so Cyprus and Greece lack a legal veto, but not a political one: no mandate has followed the Commission's December 2016 proposal. Pending CJEU Opinion 1/26 (Mercosur) may narrow the splitting technique.
- **1 Jan 2028:** only a standstill or roll-over is realistic. **1 Jan 2029:** a goods-centred FTA at best. **DCFTA:** 2030 or later.

## 1. Exit and replacement mechanics

**Treaty status**
- **Ankara Agreement:** the AC "shall act unanimously" (Art 23). Disputes go to the AC, which may settle them or refer them to the CJEU or another tribunal (Art 25). There is no termination or denunciation clause (Arts 31–33). The Agreement excludes ECSC products (Art 26) [VERIFIED S1].
- **Decision 1/95:** in force since 31 Dec 1995 (Art 65), with no duration or termination clause [VERIFIED S2].
- **VCLT:** Türkiye is absent from the UN Treaty Collection participant list [VERIFIED S3]. Customary law mirrored in VCLT Arts 56 (denunciation only if intended or implied) and 62 (fundamental change, read narrowly) applies, so unilateral exit is legally exposed [PARTIAL].
- **Mixed agreement:** member states are parties (Arts 23, 31) [VERIFIED S1].

**Can an AC decision replace or amend Decision 1/95?**
- **Yes, for its implementing content.** The AC decides unanimously, meaning the EU side plus Türkiye. The EU's position is set under Art 218(9), with the Council voting according to the substantive legal basis. The Commission's September 2026 proposal to replace the A.TR "bridging" Decision 1/2006 rests on Arts 207(4) and 218(9), so the Council votes by QMV [VERIFIED S4]. Case C-81/13 *UK v Council*, on an EEC–Turkey AC position, confirms that the voting rule follows the legal basis [PARTIAL S5].
- **Limit 1:** Art 218(9) excludes acts "supplementing or amending the institutional framework" [VERIFIED S4].
- **Limit 2:** the customs union is a treaty-level objective (Ankara Arts 2 and 5; Additional Protocol). An AC decision can freeze, adapt or phase the regime; it cannot abolish it [PARTIAL].
- **Precedent:** the EU-only Chile ITA, Art 33.11: "Part IV of the Association Agreement, including any decisions taken under its Institutional Framework, shall cease to have effect". The 2002 Association Agreement it displaced was mixed [VERIFIED S6].
- **Applied to Türkiye:** a Türkiye ITA could likewise displace the Additional Protocol's customs-union provisions, Decisions 1/95 and 1/98, and the 1996 ECSC agreement. Member-state-competence parts would remain, such as Decision 1/80 on workers and the Art 41 standstill clause [PARTIAL].

**Veto players**
- **Unanimity routes** (Arts 217 and 212, mixed agreements) give Cyprus and Greece a veto.
- **Art 207 QMV:** Greece and Cyprus hold about 2.5% of EU population. A blocking minority needs at least four states and more than 35% of population. The Mercosur opponents, at about 31%, failed to block [PARTIAL S9]. Ankara therefore needs Germany and Italy on side.
- **Practical vetoes:**
  - The Council has never adopted negotiating directives for customs-union modernisation [VERIFIED S10]. Turkish Trade Minister Ömer Bolat said in October 2026 this was "because of the position of some member states" [PARTIAL S11].
  - Any agreement must apply to Cyprus, which reopens Türkiye's non-implementation of the 2005 Protocol extending the agreement to new member states [PARTIAL].
  - The European Parliament must consent.
  - Linkage to unanimity files, e.g. the Eastern Mediterranean drilling sanctions (CFSP 2025/2395) [VERIFIED S12].

**Mercosur precedent**
- **The split:** the Commission proposed on 3 Sep 2025 a mixed Partnership Agreement (EMPA) plus an EU-only ITA that "will be repealed and replaced by the EMPA" [VERIFIED S13].
- **Council decision:** Decision (EU) 2026/183 of 9 Jan 2026 authorised signature and provisional application under Arts 91(1), 100(2), 207(4) and 218(5), with "one or more" Mercosur states (ITA Art 23.3) [VERIFIED S7].
- **Vote:** taken by QMV. France, Poland, Austria, Hungary and Ireland voted against; Belgium abstained [PARTIAL S9]. The Cyprus presidency dropped a declaration that would have barred provisional application before Parliament's consent [PARTIAL S14].
- **Signature:** 17 Jan 2026 [VERIFIED S13].
- **European Parliament:** on 21 Jan 2026 it voted 334–324–11 to request an Art 218(11) opinion on both texts, including whether the split complies with Art 218 and the division of competences. The request is pending as Opinion 1/26, and consent is frozen until the Court rules [PARTIAL S15].
- **Agricultural safeguards:** Regulation 2026/687 [VERIFIED S12].
- **Provisional application:** from 1 May 2026 [VERIFIED S13], before Parliament's consent, for the Mercosur states that had ratified [PARTIAL]. Tariff quotas are managed under Implementing Regulation 2026/888 [VERIFIED S12].

**Chile precedent**
- Signed 13 Dec 2023 (Decision 2023/2761, same legal bases). Parliament consented on 29 Feb 2024 [PARTIAL]. In force since 1 Feb 2025 [VERIFIED S6,S16].
- The ITA lasts until the mixed Advanced Framework Agreement (AFA) enters into force (Art 33.15) and can be terminated on six months' notice (Art 33.16) [VERIFIED S6].
- Mexico copied the model: the Modernised Global Agreement and its iTA were signed on 22 May 2026 [VERIFIED S17].

**Is the split feasible for Türkiye?** Legally yes, because the trade content is exclusive EU competence. Three caveats apply [PARTIAL]:
- Opinion 1/26 may constrain the technique.
- Chile, Mexico and Mercosur were deals the EU wanted. None replaced a customs union or overrode states with a live sovereignty dispute.
- A hostile EU simply does not propose an ITA.

**Decision 1/95 Arts 61–64 and unilateral Turkish tariff changes**
- **Art 61:** arbitration only after six months in the AC, and only for disputes on Art 58(2) protection measures, Art 63 safeguards or Art 64 rebalancing.
- **Art 62:** three arbitrators; the umpire comes from a list "established by the Association Council".
- **Art 63:** confirms safeguards under Art 60 of the Additional Protocol.
- **Art 64:** if a safeguard or protection measure "creates an imbalance", the other Party "may take rebalancing measures" [VERIFIED S2].
- **The gap:** departures from the Common Customs Tariff (Art 13) are not arbitrable. They fall to the AC, which decides by unanimity (Ankara Art 25) [VERIFIED S1,S2]. I found no evidence the Art 62 list was ever established [UNVERIFIED].

EU options if Türkiye changes its tariffs unilaterally:
1. Art 58(2) protection measures, for example re-imposing origin controls on deflected goods [VERIFIED S2].
2. Art 64 rebalancing.
3. Trade defence against Turkish goods (Art 46) [VERIFIED S2].
4. Countermeasures under Enforcement Regulation Art 3(ba), where adjudication "is not possible because the third country is not taking the steps… necessary" [VERIFIED S18].
5. Suspension of the agreement's application by Council decision (Art 218(9)) [VERIFIED S46].
6. After the customs union lapses, EU MFN tariffs apply. Türkiye may raise its own tariffs only up to its WTO bindings: 50.3% of lines are bound, at an average of 28.5% [PARTIAL S19].

## 4. Pan-Euro-Mediterranean (PEM) Convention

- **Türkiye's status:** the Convention has been in force for Türkiye since 1 Feb 2014 (for the EU since 1 May 2012) [VERIFIED S30].
- **Revised rules:** apply from 1 Jan 2025, with the 2012 rules running in parallel to 31 Dec 2025 [VERIFIED S31]. Partners applying revised or transitional rules with the EU [VERIFIED S31]:
  - from 1 Sep 2021: EFTA, the Faroe Islands, Albania, Georgia, Jordan and Palestine;
  - 2021–23: North Macedonia, Moldova, Serbia, Montenegro, Kosovo, Ukraine and Bosnia and Herzegovina;
  - 2025: Tunisia, Egypt and Morocco (Morocco in parallel with the old rules).
- **EU–Türkiye:** dynamic PEM references were adopted on 22 Dec 2025 for agricultural products (AC Decision 1/2025) and ECSC steel (Joint Committee Decision 1/2025) [VERIFIED S30]. Industrial goods need no origin proof inside the customs union. COM(2026) 477 would align the A.TR rules with PEM (non-alteration, full cumulation, drawback on exports outside the customs union), retroactively from 1 Jan 2026 [VERIFIED S4].
- **Diagonal cumulation after exit** has three conditions [PARTIAL]:
  - an EU–Türkiye FTA whose origin protocol refers to PEM;
  - PEM-based FTAs among every party whose materials are cumulated;
  - publication of the applicable notices.
- **Türkiye's FTA network:**
  - In force [PARTIAL]: EFTA, the Western Balkans, Georgia, Moldova, the Faroe Islands, Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt and Palestine.
  - Ukraine: FTA signed in 2022; entry into force not confirmed [UNVERIFIED].
  - Israel: trade halted by Türkiye in 2024 [PARTIAL].
  - Jordan: FTA terminated in 2018 [PARTIAL].
  - The Commission flags new Albania–, Serbia–, Moldova– and Egypt–Türkiye cumulation [VERIFIED S31].
- **No equivalence clause:** the revised Convention does not treat customs-union goods as EU-originating, unlike Andorra and San Marino [VERIFIED S31].
- **Tolerance (Art 5):** 15% of ex-works price; 15% of weight for Chapters 2 and 4–24; textiles under Notes 6–7 [VERIFIED S31].
- **Drawback (Art 16):** prohibited only for Chapters 50–63. Exceptions include EU/EFTA/Türkiye trade with Barcelona partners (other than Türkiye and Israel) where no cumulation is used [VERIFIED S31].
- **Full cumulation:** allowed except Chapters 50–63 (Art 7(3)) [VERIFIED S31].
- **A.TR on exit:** the A.TR proves free circulation, not origin, so it lapses with the customs union. It would be replaced by EUR.1/EUR-MED certificates or origin declarations (PEM Title V), plus a goods-in-transit clause modelled on TCA Art 67 (12-month window) [VERIFIED S8,S31; design PARTIAL].

## 5. Conformity assessment after exit

- **Legal basis:** Decision 1/95 Arts 8–10 (Türkiye incorporates EU technical legislation; trade then follows those instruments) [VERIFIED S2]. Decision 2/97 lists the instruments. AC Decision 1/2006 of 15 May 2006 implements Art 9 through a Customs Union Joint Committee statement and the notification of Turkish bodies [PARTIAL S32].
- **NANDO count:** my query of the Commission's NANDO index on 18 Oct 2026 [VERIFIED S33; counting method PARTIAL]:
  - 95 Turkish notified-body entries, about 81 with unexpired notifications;
  - plus 8 recognised third-party organisations (pressure equipment) and 2 technical assessment bodies (construction products);
  - designations continued into September 2026.
  - Ankara's own figure was 65 notified bodies under 16 directives in April 2025 [PARTIAL S34].
- **Sectors notified:** construction products (CPR 305/2011 and 2024/3110), machinery, lifts, PPE, pressure equipment and simple pressure vessels, gas appliances, medical devices (MDR/MDD/IVDD/AIMDD), EMC, radio equipment, ATEX, measuring and non-automatic weighing instruments, recreational craft, pyrotechnics, explosives, cableways, outdoor noise and boilers [VERIFIED S33]. Türkiye has just completed alignment with the new Machinery Regulation [VERIFIED S35].
- **TÜRKAK:** EA multilateral agreement signatory in all nine fields; status maintained in October 2024; next re-evaluation March 2028 [VERIFIED S36]. This survives exit but does not confer notified-body status.
- **UK lesson:** UK notified bodies lost EU status at end-2020. Great Britain accepts EU CE marking indefinitely for 21 regulations (from 1 Oct 2024), while UK approved-body assessments require UKCA. The TCA has no conformity-assessment MRA [PARTIAL S37].
- **Swiss lesson:** the MRA's medical-devices chapter was not updated for the MDR, so from 26 May 2021 Swiss certificates became invalid in the EU. The fix comes only through the 2026 package and its institutional protocol [PARTIAL S27,S28].
- **Implication:** without a grandfathering ACAA or MRA, Turkish notified bodies and their certificates lose EU validity on exit. The EU's price for such an agreement is dynamic alignment, market surveillance and CJEU-anchored dispute settlement [PARTIAL].

**Transition precedents**
- **UK Withdrawal Agreement Art 126:** transition period to 31 Dec 2020; the Art 132 extension option was not used [PARTIAL].
- **2019 no-deal contingency measures:** narrow, time-limited unilateral regulations, for example 2019/501 (road transport) and 2019/502 (air connectivity), with no roll-over of trade preferences [VERIFIED S12].
- **WTO Art XXIV:** an interim arrangement must lead to an FTA or customs union within a "reasonable length of time", which "should exceed 10 years only in exceptional cases" [VERIFIED S39]. A dated standstill leading to an FTA fits.
- **Useful clauses:** "until replaced" duration (Chile Art 33.15); provisional application before EP consent (TCA Art 783); goods-in-transit (TCA Art 67) [VERIFIED S6,S8].

**What is realistic**
- **By 1 Jan 2028:** the negotiating mandate is the bottleneck. What can be done in time [PARTIAL]:
  - an AC decision, adopted by QMV on the EU side (Arts 207 and 218(9)), keeping Decision 1/95 in place (tariffs, A.TR, notified-body status) to a fixed date such as 31 Dec 2029;
  - or simply postponing the exit.
  - A full FTA is not: the TCA's speed needed a ready mandate and an existential deadline.
- **By 1 Jan 2029:** a goods-centred Art 207 ITA is feasible only with a mandate by about Q3 2027 [PARTIAL]. It would cover zero industrial tariffs, roll-over of the agricultural and ECSC regimes, a PEM reference, customs and AEO cooperation, technical rules with notified-body grandfathering, trade defence, dispute settlement and light LPF. Parliament must consent before the 2029 European elections. A DCFTA is a 2030-plus project.

## 7. EU interests and leverage

- **Trade stakes:** goods trade in 2025 was €217.6bn. EU exports were €114.3bn and imports €103.3bn, an EU surplus of about €11bn. Türkiye is the EU's 5th-largest partner, and 42.7% of Turkish exports go to the EU. Services trade was €42.1bn in 2024 [VERIFIED S10].
- **Automotive:** Türkiye is the 3rd-largest market for EU new-vehicle exports (811,957 vehicles, €18.8bn, 11.1% of value). Türkiye in turn supplies 797,721 vehicles worth €18.2bn, or 21.5% of EU new-vehicle import value, including 230,833 commercial vehicles and buses (€8.2bn). EU-made cars hold 49% of Turkish sales and Chinese-made cars 8% [VERIFIED S40]. Because supply chains run both ways, PEM cumulation matters to EU manufacturers too.
- **Migration:** the 2016 EU–Turkey Statement and the Facility for Refugees in Türkiye (10th annual report, July 2026) [PARTIAL S41].
- **Defence (SAFE):** the SAFE Regulation (2025/1106, Art 17) admits candidate countries only through an EU agreement [VERIFIED S42]; with a candidate state this probably requires unanimity [PARTIAL]. Greece and Cyprus condition Turkish participation on lifting the 1995 casus belli (statements Oct 2025–Jul 2026) [PARTIAL S43]. I found no specific February 2026 blocking act [UNVERIFIED].
- **Energy:** the Southern Gas Corridor and TurkStream run through Türkiye. Regulation 2026/261 bans long-term Russian pipeline gas from 30 Sep or 1 Nov 2027, raising Türkiye's value as a transit hub and gas-origin verifier [PARTIAL S44].

Likely EU demands in a DCFTA:

| Area | Demand | Source |
|---|---|---|
| State aid / LPF | A working state-aid authority (the law's implementation is suspended "indefinitely"); TCA-style subsidy control and Art 411 rebalancing | [VERIFIED S45,S8] |
| Procurement | Opening of procurement markets; removal of local-content rules (renewables, tobacco) | [VERIFIED S45] |
| IPR / pharma | End the "artificial exchange rate" in pharma pricing; accept EU GMP certificates; resolve batch release | [VERIFIED S45,S35] |
| Market access | End "additional duties" on goods in free circulation, import surveillance and export taxes on hides | [VERIFIED S45,S35] |
| Sanctions | Anti-circumvention commitments: re-exports of critical goods, the "shadow fleet" | [VERIFIED S45,S35] |
| SPS, CBAM/ETS, Cyprus non-discrimination | Alignment and full application to all member states | [PARTIAL] |

**Balance of leverage:** the EU controls steel quotas, trade defence, CBAM, notified-body status and origin rules; Türkiye has market size, automotive integration, migration and energy transit. Under QMV, Berlin and Rome are Ankara's pivotal interlocutors.
